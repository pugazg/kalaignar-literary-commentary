# 114. Declaring the Abandonment of Modesty

**1131. For one who has suffered and grieved through love,  
there is no strong refuge other than riding the madal.**

For a lover who suffers through love, there is no powerful means of protection other than riding the madal.

**1132. My body and life, unable to bear it, will mount the madal,  
setting modesty aside.**

Because my life and body cannot bear the anguish of separation from my beloved, I have cast modesty aside and resolved to ride the madal.

**1133. Once I possessed modesty and worthy manliness; today I possess  
the madal ridden by those overcome by love.**

I, who once possessed worthy manliness and a sense of modesty, have today forgotten them and taken up riding the madal for the sake of love.

**1134. The fierce flood of love will sweep away the rafts  
called modesty and worthy manliness.**

The great flood of love is powerful enough to sweep away the boats called modesty and worthy manliness.

**1135. The maiden with slender bangles has given me, together with the madal,  
the anguish that torments me in the evening.**

The maiden who wears a waist-ornament and delicate bangles has given me both the malady of love that blossoms in the evening and the task called riding the madal.
