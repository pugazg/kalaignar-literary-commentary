# 126. Loss of Restraint

**1256. What kind of suffering is this that has come upon me,  
making me wish to follow the one who left in hostility?**

What a strange nature this sickness of love has—it has made my heart willing to bear even his rejection and separation and still follow after him.

**1257. When the one we cherish does what we desire out of love for us,  
we know nothing of a thing called modesty.**

When the one dear to us, moved by love, does what pleases us, we do not even know that there is a quality called modesty.

**1258. Is not the humble, honeyed speech of that thief skilled in many deceits  
the weapon that breaks our womanly firmness?**

Is it not the humble, sugar-sweet speech of the beloved—a thief skilled in many deceptions—that serves as the weapon which breaks the firmness called our womanhood?

**1259. I went intending to sulk; but when I saw my heart rush to unite with him,  
I forgot my resolve and embraced him.**

I went thinking that I would quarrel and remain estranged; but seeing my heart leave me and join him, I forgot my stubborn resolve and embraced him.

**1260. Can those whose hearts melt like fat placed in fire  
unite with their beloved and still stand firm in a quarrel?**

Can people whose hearts melt like fat thrown into fire remain firmly in a lover's quarrel after they have joined and delighted in one another?
