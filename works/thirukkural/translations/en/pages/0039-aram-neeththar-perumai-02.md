**26. Those who do deeds that are hard to do are the great;  
the small are those who cannot do such worthy deeds.**

Those who perform deeds that bring greatness may be defined as great people; those who do nothing beyond petty deeds and fail to perform deeds worthy of greatness may be defined as small people.

**27. The world esteems the one who understands the nature of the five—  
taste, sight, touch, sound, and smell.**

The world honours the person who understands the nature of the five senses and possesses the ability to bring them under control.

**28. The greatness of people whose words are fulfilled  
is shown upon the earth by their enduring words of aram.**

The greatness of the worthy is shown by their works on the path of aram, which remain in this world without perishing.

**29. If anger arises in those who stand upon the mountain called virtue,  
it is hard for that anger to remain even for an instant.**

If great people who are mountains of virtue become angry, that anger will not remain in their hearts even for a moment.

**30. Those called `anthanar` are people of aram,  
for they live with tender compassion toward every living being.**

Any worthy people who love all living beings and shower compassion upon them are called `anthanar`.

> Source-check note: Kural 30 keeps `அந்தணர்` as `anthanar` because Kalaignar's commentary itself defines who may be called by that term: **any worthy people who love all living beings and shower compassion upon them**. No caste- or religion-specific definition from outside this source is substituted.
