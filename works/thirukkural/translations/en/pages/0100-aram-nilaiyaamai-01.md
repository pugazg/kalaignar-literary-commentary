# 34. Impermanence

**331. To regard things that do not endure as permanent  
is the lowest kind of ignorance.**

The ignorance that believes impermanent things to be permanent is deeply degrading.

**332. Great wealth gathers like a crowd at a performance  
and departs as that crowd disperses when it ends.**

Money and property that have been accumulated leave a person just as people leave the theatre and disperse once a performance has ended.

**333. Wealth is by nature impermanent; when it comes,  
one should at once use it for deeds that endure.**

Knowing that the wealth that comes to us is impermanent, we should use it immediately to engage in lasting good deeds.

**334. What appears as a day is a sword  
that keeps cutting life away, for those who understand.**

Those who understand life know that what is called a day is a sword that continually cuts down and shortens a person's span of life.

**335. Before the tongue is stilled and the final hiccup rises,  
one should hasten to perform good deeds.**

Understanding the impermanence of life, we should come forward to perform lofty good works while life is still within us.
