# 27. Ascetic Practice

**261. To endure the suffering that comes upon oneself and cause no harm to any life—  
these alone form ascetic practice.**

Having a heart that can bear anything, and refraining from causing harm to any living being, is what is called “ascetic practice.”

**262. Ascetic practice belongs to those who possess the discipline for it;  
for those without it to undertake it is futile.**

Only those with firmness of purpose and self-control can attain the greatness of ascetic practice. Therefore, for people without disciplined conduct to undertake it is merely a futile act.

**263. Have others forgotten their own ascetic discipline  
in seeking to support those who have renounced?**

Others should not forget the ascetic discipline they themselves ought to observe merely because they wish to stand in support of renunciants.

**264. If one wills it, ascetic practice brings the power  
to bring down enemies and protect friends.**

Only when the observance called ascetic practice gains strength through firmness of mind and self-control can one, at the very thought of it, bring down enemies and protect friends.

**265. Because it enables one to attain each desire in the desired way,  
ascetic practice is pursued here with earnest effort.**

Because firm observance can enable one to attain what one desires in the manner desired, it is something to be undertaken swiftly and with effort.
