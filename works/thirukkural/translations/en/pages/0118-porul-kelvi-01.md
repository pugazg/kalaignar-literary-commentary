# 42. Listening

**411. Of all wealth, the wealth of the ear is wealth indeed;  
that wealth stands foremost among every kind of wealth.**

The wealth obtained through the ear by receiving rich ideas is the foremost of all forms of wealth.

**412. When there is no food for the ear,  
a little may then be given to the stomach.**

Only when there is no food that gives pleasure through the ear does the occasion arise to give a little food to the stomach.

**413. Those who possess the food of listening for the ear  
are, on earth, equal to the great who live on measured food.**

Those who take little physical food yet shine with abundant knowledge are matched by those who partake of the food of the ear called knowledge gained through listening.

**414. Even if one has not studied, let one listen;  
for a person it is a staff of support when the step falters.**

Even if one has not learned from books, if one listens to the learned and comes to know, that knowledge will serve as a support like a walking stick to one whose gait has become unsteady.

**415. The words spoken by people of good conduct  
are like a staff on slippery ground.**

Just as a walking stick helps one walk on slippery ground, the counsel of people who possess good conduct will be of help.
