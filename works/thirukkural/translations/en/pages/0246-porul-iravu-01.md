# 106. Begging

**1051. Beg when you find those fit to be asked; if they conceal what they have,  
the blame is theirs, not the beggar's.**

If one asks something of a person who has the means to give, and that person, though possessing it, says that he does not, the blame belongs to the person who says so, not to the one who asked.

**1052. Begging itself is a pleasure to a person  
if what is begged comes without bringing suffering.**

If an object can be obtained without causing any distress to the minds of either the giver or the receiver, then even though that object was obtained by begging, it brings pleasure.

**1053. To stand and beg before those whose hearts conceal nothing  
and who know their duty has a dignity of its own.**

Even asking out of one's poverty from a person who has an open heart and a sense of duty possesses a certain dignity.

**1054. Begging itself seems like giving  
before those who would not dream even of concealing what they have.**

Before those who would never even dream of stretching out an empty hand and saying “there is nothing” when something is there, the asking of those who have nothing possesses a greatness like giving to others.

**1055. Beggars take up begging because there are people in the world  
who do not conceal what they have.**

It is because people who do not hide what they possess but have the quality of giving it exist in this world that those who have nothing go to them and undertake begging.
