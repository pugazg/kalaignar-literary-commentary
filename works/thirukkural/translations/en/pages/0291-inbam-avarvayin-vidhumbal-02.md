# 127. Longing for the Beloved

**1266. Let my beloved who has left me wasting away come one day;  
then I shall drink in delight until all my suffering is gone.**

The beloved who has left me to waste away must come back one day. When he comes, I shall enjoy delight with him until all my suffering is completely ended.

**1267. When the beloved who is as dear as my eyes returns, shall I sulk, embrace him,  
or mingle the two? I cannot tell.**

The moment my beloved, the very jewel of my eye, returns, shall I quarrel with him because of the pain of separation? Or shall I clasp him in an embrace? Or shall I combine both sulking and union? When I think of that delight, I understand nothing at all.

**1268. Let the leader succeed in the undertaking he has begun;  
then, reunited at home, evening will be a feast of delight.**

May the leader win in the task he has undertaken; when he has won, evening with my wife at home will be a feast of delight for me.

**1269. For one who longs for the day when the beloved who went far away will return,  
a single day passes like seven days.**

For one who waits and longs for the day when the beloved who has travelled far away will return, every single day seems like an age.

**1270. What use is gaining him, what use is union, what use is meeting again,  
if the heart has already broken down?**

If the mind loses its steadiness because it cannot bear the suffering, what benefit is there afterward in meeting one another again or in meeting and uniting?
