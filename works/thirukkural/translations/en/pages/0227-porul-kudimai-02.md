**956. Those who say, “Let us live by our stainless lineage,”  
will not, for deceit, do acts without nobility.**

People who regard themselves as living with stainless character will not engage in improper deeds with deceitful intent.

**957. A fault in one of noble birth shines openly  
like a blemish on the moon in the sky.**

Even small faults in a person who adds distinction to the lineage of his birth will be plainly visible, without concealment, like the blemish on the moon in the sky.

**958. If lack of love appears amid one's goodness,  
doubt arises about the lineage from which he came.**

However much beauty and fame a person may possess, if he lacks the one quality called love, a situation arises in which even the lineage into which he was born comes under doubt.

**959. The crop reveals the nature of the soil in which it grew;  
a person's speech reveals the lineage into which he was born.**

By looking at a crop, one can know the kind of soil in which it grew. In the same way, by listening to a person's speech one can understand the kind of lineage into which he was born.

**960. If one desires goodness, one needs modesty; if one desires honour of lineage,  
one should show humility to everyone.**

Being ashamed and afraid to do an improper act, and behaving humbly toward everyone without arrogance, are qualities capable of elevating both a person's goodness and the lineage of his birth.
