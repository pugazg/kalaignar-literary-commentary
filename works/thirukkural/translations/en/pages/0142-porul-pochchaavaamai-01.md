# 54. Freedom from Forgetfulness

**531. Forgetfulness born of excessive joy is worse  
than the harm born of unrestrained anger.**

The forgetfulness that arises from inward delight is more harmful than the consequence produced by uncontrolled anger.

**532. Forgetfulness destroys fame  
as unrelenting poverty destroys knowledge.**

Just as poverty that torments a person day after day destroys knowledge, forgetfulness destroys fame.

**533. Those possessed of forgetfulness have no enduring fame;  
this is the settled conclusion of the learned everywhere.**

That people who are forgetful cannot possess unfading fame is the firm conclusion of scholars who have learned and understood all things.

**534. A fort is of no use to one ruled by fear;  
likewise, high position is of no use to one ruled by forgetfulness.**

For those who tremble in fear, even a fort built around them for protection is of no use. In the same way, however high their position may be, that position is of no use to those who are forgetful.

**535. One who fails to guard beforehand  
will later grieve over his own fault when suffering comes.**

The person who fails to think ahead and protect himself will, after suffering arrives, be forced to lament his own mistake.
