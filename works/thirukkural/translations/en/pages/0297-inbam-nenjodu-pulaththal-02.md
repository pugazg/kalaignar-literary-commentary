# 130. Sulking with the Heart

**1296. When I sit alone thinking of him,  
my heart remains as though it would devour me.**

When I sat alone thinking of my separation from the beloved, my heart was cruel to me, as though it were eating me alive.

**1297. Caught by my foolish, unworthy heart that cannot forget him,  
I have forgotten even the modesty I should never forget.**

Joined to this foolish and undistinguished heart of mine that wastes away unable to forget him, I too have forgotten the modesty that ought never to be forgotten.

**1298. Thinking that to despise him would bring disgrace upon itself,  
my heart that loves my life thinks only of his greatness.**

Because it considers that belittling the beloved who has left would itself be a humiliation, my life-loving heart goes on thinking only of his greatness.

**1299. Who can be one's companion in suffering  
if one's own heart does not stand as a companion?**

When suffering comes, if one's own heart is not there as a companion to bear it, who else can be a companion?

**1300. If one's own heart is not one's kin,  
it is only natural that strangers will not be a refuge.**

When the heart that belongs to us is itself not related to us, it is easy enough for others to remain unrelated and offer no refuge.
