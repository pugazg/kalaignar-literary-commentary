# 47. Acting After Deliberation

**461. Weigh what may be lost, what may be gained,  
and the benefit that follows; then act.**

One should enter upon an action only after calculating its consequences — how much good may come from it or how much harm may arise.

**462. For those who join with well-known, well-chosen companions,  
think carefully, and act, nothing is difficult.**

If one joins with clearly known and carefully chosen friends, examines the work to be done, thinks it through well oneself, and then acts, there is nothing that cannot be accomplished.

**463. The wise will not undertake an act that may lose the capital  
in the hope of gaining a great profit.**

People of wisdom will not undertake a matter that may make them lose even their capital merely because they expect a large gain.

**464. Those who fear the blemish of disgrace  
do not begin an action whose consequences are unclear.**

Those who are capable of fearing blemish will think through the consequences and will not enter upon an action that could bring such blemish.

**465. To rise against an enemy without fully examining every preparation  
is a way of planting and strengthening that enemy.**

To move to suppress an enemy without fully examining and making the necessary preparations becomes a way of allowing that enemy's strength to grow and become firmly established.
