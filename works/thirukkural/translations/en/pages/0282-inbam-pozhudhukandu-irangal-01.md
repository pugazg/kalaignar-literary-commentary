# 123. Lamenting at Evening

**1221. You are no evening; you are a spear that drinks the life  
of women separated from their beloved—long live, O hour!**

A greeting to you—for instead of being merely the evening hour, you have become a spear that drinks the life of women separated from their beloved!

**1222. O bewildering evening with sorrowful eyes, live on! You too suffer like us;  
is your companion, like our beloved, without compassion?**

Bewildering evening! You too seem to suffer as we do. Is your companion, like our beloved, also without compassion?

**1223. The evening that once came fearfully, budding with dew and pallor,  
now comes budding with aversion and growing ever greater in suffering.**

When my beloved was beside me, evening came timidly, with pallor; now it comes carrying suffering in such abundance that it makes me weary even of life.

**1224. When the beloved is away, evening comes  
like an enemy's raised sword on a killing field.**

When the beloved is separated from me, the evening hour comes like the sword an enemy raises and swings on a field of slaughter.

**1225. What good have I done the morning, and what wrong have I done the evening?**

Once evening arrives, the suffering of love torments me more intensely. And so the heart of one separated from the beloved laments: “What good did I do the morning? What evil did I do the evening?”
