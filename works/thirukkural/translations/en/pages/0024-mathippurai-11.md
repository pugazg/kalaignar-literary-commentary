## I. Entirely Fresh Explanation

As further examples of such interpretations fragrant with freshness:

**29. If anger arises in those who stand upon the mountain called virtue,  
it is hard for that anger to remain even for an instant.**

If great people who are mountains of virtue become angry, that anger will not remain in their hearts even for a moment.

**37. Do not define the path of aram by the difference  
between the one who bears the palanquin and the one who rides it.**

Those who walk in the path of aram will travel through life joyfully, regarding both the pleasures and sufferings that come to them as light, like people seated and travelling in a palanquin. Those who have surrendered themselves to an evil path, however, will be like people carrying the palanquin: they will find no peace even in pleasure, lack the maturity of mind to bear suffering, and regard life itself as a great burden.

**55. She who does not worship a deity, but rises worshipping her husband—  
the rain will fall when she commands, “Fall.”**

A wife who regards her husband's word as greater than the word of God and worships him alone will come to think of herself as a slave, like rain that, trembling in fear, falls as soon as it is ordered, “Fall.”

These may be taken as examples. Among them, the finesse with which meaning has been found without breaking apart or altering the words is worthy of praise.

## J. Subtle Penetrating Insight

As an example of Kalaignar's subtle penetrating insight:

**622. Flood-like adversity will perish  
within the mind of the wise when they think upon it.**
