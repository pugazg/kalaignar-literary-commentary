**656. Even to relieve the hunger of the mother who bore him,  
one must not do a deed that the worthy would condemn.**

Even for the sake of easing the suffering of one's own mother as she struggles with hunger, one must not engage in a degrading act.

**657. Better the crushing poverty of the upright  
than wealth gained by embracing disgrace.**

It is better to live as an upright person without distress even when terrible poverty strikes than to live in wealth by committing degrading acts without fear of blame.

**658. Those who refuse to avoid deeds that have been rejected as wrong  
will suffer from those deeds even if they succeed.**

For people who carry out actions that have been set aside as improper instead of abandoning them, those very actions will bring suffering even if they happen to be completed successfully.

**659. All wealth gathered by making others weep will go away amid tears;  
wealth that comes by a good path will yield benefit again even after loss.**

Wealth amassed by making others weep will itself disappear amid weeping. If wealth has come through a good path, even when it is lost it will return and bring benefit again.

**660. Gathering wealth by wrongful means and trying to preserve it  
is like pouring water into an unbaked clay pot and trying to keep it there.**

To accumulate wealth through wrong ways and then try to protect it is just like pouring water into a vessel made of green, unbaked clay and trying to preserve that water.
