**486. The restraint of one possessed of resolve  
is like a ram stepping back before it charges its foe.**

When people of firm resolve remain quiet even after witnessing cruelties, it is not from fear; it is like a ram drawing back its legs before striking its enemy.

**487. The wise do not flare up outwardly at once;  
they hold their anger within and wait for time and place.**

Though anger rises within them to defeat an enemy, the wise do not display it outwardly; they wait for both the proper place and the proper time.

**488. Bear with the enemy until the time of his end;  
when that time comes, his head will fall downward of itself.**

One must patiently bear one's hostility until the fitting time comes when the enemy reaches his end and overturns himself.

**489. When a rare opportunity arrives,  
that very moment is the time to accomplish the difficult deed.**

When a time that is hard to obtain presents itself, one should seize it and then and there complete the difficult action.

**490. Like the crane, wait folded and still until the season comes;  
when the moment is right, strike without missing the mark.**

Until the time comes together, one should wait patiently like a crane. When the opportunity arrives, one should complete the act just as the crane strikes without missing its target.
