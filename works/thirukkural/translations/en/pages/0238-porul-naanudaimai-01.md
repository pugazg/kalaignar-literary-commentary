# 102. Modesty

**1011. To feel shame for one's improper deed is shame; the modesty  
of fair-browed good women is of another kind.**

There is a great difference between the shame one feels because of one's improper conduct and the modesty that naturally arises in good women.

**1012. Food, clothing and the rest are no different for all living beings;  
modesty is the distinction of human beings.**

Food, clothing and such things are common needs for everyone; but the distinctive need is modesty—the quality of living while avoiding acts for which others would blame us.

**1013. As all life is joined to the body,  
worthiness is joined to the goodness called modesty.**

Just as life exists joined to the body, dignity consists in being joined to a sense of modesty.

**1014. Is modesty not an ornament to the worthy? Without it,  
is not a proud gait a disease?**

The feeling called modesty, by which one grieves inwardly over a wrong that has been done, shines as an ornament to the great. Without that ornament, however proudly one may walk, that gait can only be regarded as something like a disease.

**1015. Those who feel shame for others' blame as for their own—  
the world calls them the dwelling place of modesty.**

Those who grieve and feel shame not only for blame that falls upon themselves but also for blame that falls upon others become the very dwelling place of the quality called modesty.
