# 117. Languishing in Longing

**1161. I would hide this sickness, but the more I hide it,  
the more it swells like spring water as it is drawn.**

Like water from a spring that increases the more it is drawn, the sickness of love grows the more I try to hide it from others.

**1162. I cannot hide this sickness; yet shame keeps me  
from telling the one who caused it.**

I cannot conceal the sickness of love; nor, because of modesty, can I tell the beloved who caused it.

**1163. Love and modesty hang at either end of the carrying-pole of my life,  
within this body that cannot bear the pain.**

My body, in which life throbs because it cannot bear separation, has become like a carrying-pole, with the sickness of love on one side and, on the other, the modesty that prevents me from revealing it.

**1164. There is indeed a sea of love,  
but there is no safe raft with which to cross it.**

Love surrounds and torments me like a sea; yet there is no safe boat with which to swim across it.

**1165. What will they be like in enmity, those who can bring us sorrow  
even while they are within friendship?**

If one can give us the suffering of separation even while remaining in friendship, what kind of person will that one become if enmity arises?
