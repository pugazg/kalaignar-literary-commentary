# 35. Renunciation

**341. From whatever one has detached oneself,  
from that one suffers no pain.**

Whatever attachment a person gives up among the many kinds of attachment, the suffering that comes from that particular attachment will no longer approach that person.

**342. If one desires, let one renounce while one still has;  
after renouncing, many joys may be gained here.**

If a person gives up possessions while everything is still available, so that suffering does not pierce and torment him, many are the joys he may gain in this world.

**343. The five senses must be conquered;  
all that they desire must be given up together.**

To subdue and conquer the five senses, and to give up everything those senses desire, are marks of renunciation.

**344. Having nothing is natural to ascetic discipline; possession  
creates delusion and leads on to further attachments.**

To remain without a single attachment is what suits renunciation. If one places attachment even upon one thing, it will multiply further attachments and leave one bewildered.

**345. For those who seek to cut off the suffering of birth, even the body is excess;  
what need then is there for any other bond?**

For renunciants who seek to remove the suffering that comes from birth, even their own body is something excessive; what need then is there for any further relationship or attachment?
