**586. Taking on the appearance of one who has renounced, probing deeply,  
and never weakening whatever is done to him—that is the spy.**

The finest spy is one who appears to have no connection with the event being investigated, examines it thoroughly, endures however much suffering may arise from it, and does not reveal who he is.

**587. A spy must be capable of hearing what is hidden  
and of knowing what is learned without doubt.**

The skill of intelligence-gathering lies in learning, through people who are present with them, what others secretly meet and do, and in clearly discovering the truth of those matters.

**588. Even the information brought by one spy  
must be verified through another spy.**

Even news that an intelligence agent discovers through his own skill and reports must be obtained again through another agent; only after comparing the two reports should a decision be made as to whether it is true or not.

**589. Let spies work without one knowing another;  
what three say in agreement may be accepted as certain.**

Three spies should be made to work in such a way that one cannot know another; if what all three say agrees, it may be taken as true.

**590. Do not publicly honour a spy for his excellence; if you do,  
you yourself make the secret public.**

If, in admiration of a spy's ability, one honours him in a way that others come to know, it amounts to exposing a matter that ought to remain concealed.
