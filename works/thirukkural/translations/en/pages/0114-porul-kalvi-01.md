# 40. Learning

**391. Learn without flaw what is to be learned;  
after learning, live in a manner worthy of it.**

One must learn what is free from error until one's own shortcomings are removed. After learning, one must conduct oneself accordingly.

**392. Number is one eye and letters the other;  
these two are called the eyes of living beings.**

Only those who possess the eyes of knowledge called number and letters are to be regarded as truly living.

**393. The learned are called those with eyes;  
the unlearned have two sores upon the face.**

Even without physical eyes, if one is learned, one will be regarded as having eyes. Even if an unlearned person has eyes, they will be regarded only as sores.

**394. To gather together in delight and part with hearts that ache—  
that is the way of the learned.**

Meeting and mingling with overflowing joy, and becoming troubled in mind when the time comes to part, are the ways of those distinguished in knowledge.

**395. Those who learn stand humbly before the learned as the poor before the wealthy;  
those who do not learn are the lowly ones.**

Those who stand humbly before the knowledgeable as though they themselves know nothing, and eagerly learn more, are the true learners; those who do not learn are regarded as people of the lowest standing.
