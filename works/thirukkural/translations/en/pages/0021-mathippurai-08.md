**1085. Is it Death, an eye, or a doe? The young woman's  
gaze possesses all three.**

Is it Death that takes life? Is it an intimate gaze? Is it a bewildering doe? The young woman's look raises all three questions.

## D. Commentary in Kural Form

The verse itself is a Kural; next, observe how the commentary Kalaignar has fashioned for it is itself formed as a Kural.

**1305. Sulking in the heart of the flower-eyed woman  
adds beauty to a worthy and good lover.**

The lovers' quarrel that arises in the heart of a flower-eyed woman adds beauty to a cultured and worthy lover.

## E. Grace of Interposed Words

The section below shows how inserting a few words in between can remove an obstacle that arises in understanding the meaning of a Kural.

**94. For those whose sweet words bring joy to everyone,  
there is no painful want that causes suffering.**

Those who speak sweetly and deal with everyone kindly suffer no misery of poverty in friendship.

**83. The life of one who welcomes and tends arriving guests every day  
will not, on that account, fall into misery and ruin.**

The life of one who welcomes guests every day with joy will not, for that reason, suffer distress and perish.
