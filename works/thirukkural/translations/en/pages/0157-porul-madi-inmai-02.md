**606. Even when they possess the affection of people of standing,  
the lazy find it hard to attain the benefit called honour.**

Even if lazy people are the recipients of the affection of people of standing, it is difficult for them to attain the benefit called greatness.

**607. Those who live in laziness without concern for making an effort  
will hear words of rebuke and contempt.**

People who live as idlers, without concern for making an effort, will become the objects of contempt.

**608. If laziness settles within a distinguished family,  
it will hand its people over as slaves to their enemies.**

Even one born into a family of great standing will, if laziness takes up residence in him, be made a slave to his enemies by that very laziness.

**609. When a person removes the rule of laziness over him,  
the faults that entered family standing and manly vigour will disappear.**

If a person drives out the laziness that has taken possession of him, distinction will come of itself to his family's greatness and to his manly vigour.

**610. A ruler without laziness will gain at once  
all the lands reached within the measure of his steps.**

The benefit attained by one without laziness is like that of an untiring ruler who brings every place he reaches within the bounds of his own footsteps.
