**36. Do aram without saying, “We shall see to it later”;  
when one dies, it remains an undying companion.**

If one takes up the path of aram without putting it off from day to day by saying it can be attended to later, it will endure even after death as undying fame and stand as a companion.

**37. Do not define the path of aram by the difference  
between the one who bears the palanquin and the one who rides it.**

Those who walk in the path of aram will travel through life joyfully, regarding both the pleasures and sufferings that come to them as light, like people seated and travelling in a palanquin. Those who have surrendered themselves to an evil path, however, will be like people carrying the palanquin: they will find no peace even in pleasure, lack the maturity of mind to bear suffering, and regard life itself as a great burden.

**38. If one performs good so that not even a single day passes uselessly,  
those good deeds become the stone that sets one's path of life in order.**

For a person who does not let even a single day pass without benefit and remains continually engaged in good deeds, those very good deeds become the stone that sets the path of life in order.

**39. The happiness that comes through aram alone is happiness;  
what comes by other ways is neither worthy of praise nor true happiness.**

Happiness arises from the praise that comes through a life lived on the path of aram with a pure heart. What comes by a contrary path is neither praise nor happiness.

**40. For a person, what is to be done is aram;  
what is to be avoided is blame.**

Avoiding deeds worthy of blame and taking an interest in praiseworthy actions on the path of aram is what brings a person fame.

> Editorial review note: Kural 38 deliberately follows Kalaignar's own explanation of `வாழ்நாள் வழியடைக்கும் கல்`: the good deeds themselves become **the stone that sets the path of life in order**. The first-pass word **“shapes”** remains excluded because Kalaignar's commentary specifically says the stone sets and orders the life-path. No conventional external interpretation is substituted.
