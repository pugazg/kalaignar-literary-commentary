# 97. Honour

**961. Even if an act is indispensable,  
abandon it if it would diminish one's distinction.**

Even if some acts seem absolutely necessary to be completed, if they would reduce one's greatness, those acts must be avoided.

**962. Those who desire heroic life together with glory  
will not do what is contrary to honour merely for fame.**

A person who desires a glorious life of valour will not engage in an act contrary to the sense of honour merely because he wants fame to come to him somehow.

**963. In prosperity, humility is needed; when that condition diminishes,  
one needs the honour that refuses servile submission.**

When one reaches an elevated position, a sense of restraint is needed; and when that position changes, one needs the sense of honour that refuses to become anyone's slave and submit.

**964. When people fall from their honoured position,  
they are like hair fallen from the head.**

When a person who once held an elevated place in people's hearts loses honour and falls low, he will be regarded as equal to hair that has fallen from the head.

**965. Even those who stand majestic as mountains will decline  
if they do an act as low as a single kunrimani seed.**

Even people who stand tall and majestic like a mountain will fall and decline if they engage in an act of degradation even as small as a kunrimani seed.
