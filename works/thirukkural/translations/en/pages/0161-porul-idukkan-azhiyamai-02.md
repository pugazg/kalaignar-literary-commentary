**626. Will those who did not think, “We have gained,” and guard what they had  
later suffer saying, “We have lost”?**

Those who did not rejoice that they had obtained so much wealth and did not think to protect it—will they become dispirited only when they happen to lose that wealth?

**627. Those who understand that the body is a target for suffering  
do not take distress itself as their helpless refuge.**

The great who understand that suffering is natural even to a body in which life exists will not regard suffering as suffering when it comes.

**628. One who does not wander in search of pleasure and regards suffering as natural  
will not be overcome by that suffering.**

A person who does not roam in pursuit of pleasure and who accepts suffering as natural when it comes will not become dispirited because of it.

**629. One who does not seek added pleasure within pleasure  
will not be overcome by suffering within suffering.**

Those who do not dance with excitement when pleasure comes will not be tormented when suffering comes. This is an example of the firmness that regards the two alike.

**630. If one has the firmness of mind to regard suffering as pleasure,  
one attains a greatness that even enemies will admire.**

Those who possess the firmness of mind to regard suffering itself as pleasure will attain a distinction praised even by their enemies.
