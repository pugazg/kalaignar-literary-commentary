# 29. Not Stealing

**281. One who wishes to live without being despised  
must guard the heart from stealing anything at all.**

Only one who keeps the thought of stealing any possession from approaching the heart can live without becoming an object of contempt.

**282. Even to think in the heart, “We shall steal another's property by deceit,”  
is itself evil.**

For a person even to think that another's property might be seized through trickery is itself a fault.

**283. Wealth gained through theft may seem to grow beyond measure,  
but it will perish.**

Accumulating property through robbery may at first appear to produce great wealth, but that act will also destroy the wealth that was already there.

**284. The unquenchable desire for theft  
brings unending suffering through its consequences.**

The thirst for stealing that arises in a person and cannot be satisfied will, through the consequences it creates, bring suffering that does not end.

**285. Those who watch for another's lapse in order to seize property  
cannot possess the loving nature that looks toward compassion.**

Those who wait for a moment when another person is forgetful so that they can steal that person's property will not possess the quality of behaving with love out of concern for compassion.
