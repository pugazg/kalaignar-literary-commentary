# 125. Speaking with the Heart

**1246. O my heart, when you see the beloved who joins me and ends our quarrel,  
you never truly sulk; this anger you now show toward him is false.**

O heart! Whenever you see the beloved who reunites with me and brings our sulking to an end, you have never once known how to remain estranged; so is not the anger you now bear toward him merely false anger?

**1247. Good heart, either give up love or give up modesty;  
I cannot bear these two together.**

Good heart! Either stop throbbing with love, or give up the modesty that prevents me from boldly speaking of it. I cannot bear these two at the same time.

**1248. My foolish heart longs, saying, “He has left without compassion,”  
yet goes after the very one who departed.**

At the very moment it yearns in grief that he has left us without compassion, my heart follows behind the one who has gone; it is like an ignorant child.

**1249. When the beloved already dwells within the heart, O my heart,  
why do you think of him and wander outside seeking someone else?**

When the beloved has made his home within the heart itself, O heart, why do you think of him and wander outside in search of him elsewhere?

**1250. Keeping within the heart the one who left without joining us,  
we shall lose still more of our beauty.**

Because we keep in our thoughts the beloved who left without staying united with us, we must lose still more of our bodily beauty, grow thin and waste away.
