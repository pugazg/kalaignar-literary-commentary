**346. One who cuts away the pride called “I” and “mine”  
will enter a world higher than that of the dwellers in heaven.**

One who completely casts aside the arrogance of “I” and “mine” becomes worthy of worldly fame that surpasses even fame reaching the skies.

**347. Sufferings cling without letting go  
to those who do not let go of attachments.**

Sufferings too continue to cling without leaving those who themselves cling to attachments and refuse to let them go.

**348. Those who renounce completely attain the highest state;  
the rest, deluded, are caught in the net.**

Only those who renounce everything fully, and not halfway, attain an elevated state. Those who do not renounce in that way are caught in the net called ignorance.

**349. Only when attachment is gone is birth cut off;  
otherwise impermanence will be seen.**

When attachments are given up, the pleasures and sufferings that come with birth do not arise. Otherwise, impermanence appears as those pleasures and sufferings come and go in turn.

**350. Hold fast to the attachment of one who has no attachment;  
hold to that attachment in order to let attachment go.**

One should attach oneself only to a person who is without attachment to anything. For those in renunciant life, attachment to such a person stands as support for giving up their own attachments.
