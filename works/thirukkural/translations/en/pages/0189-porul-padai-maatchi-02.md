**766. Valour, a sense of honour, following the established path, and confidence—  
these four are the protection of an army.**

Valour, a sense of honour, following the path taken by those who came before, and gaining the leader's confidence are the four qualities that protect an army.

**767. The army that knows how to withstand the battle that first comes upon it  
is the army that can advance bearing the garland of victory.**

If an army knows it has the power to withstand and break the first battle it encounters in the field, that army is the finest force, capable of moving forward bearing the garland of victory.

**768. Even if an army lacks fighting prowess and strength,  
it gains distinction through the excellence of its formation.**

More than the two qualities of valour in battle and the strength to stand against the enemy, the appearance of an army's formation must itself possess excellence.

**769. An army will conquer if it has no diminution, no resentment that drives it away,  
and no poverty.**

Only an army that does not shrink, does not turn to hatred of its leader, and does not remain in a backward condition can achieve victory.

**770. Even if an army possesses many steadfast soldiers,  
it cannot stand if it has no leaders to command it.**

Even when it possesses a large number of steadfast warriors, an army cannot remain standing if there are no leaders to provide leadership.
