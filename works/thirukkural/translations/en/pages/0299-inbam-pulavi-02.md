# 131. Lovers' Quarrel

**1306. If there are neither deep quarrels nor lighter sulks, love becomes  
like fruit overripe or fruit still unripe.**

If a life of love does not gain delight through both greater and lesser quarrels, it becomes useless—like fruit that has ripened until it rots, or like a tender fruit that has not yet matured.

**1307. Even in sulking there is a sorrow:  
will the time of reunion perhaps be shortened?**

Lovers experience a kind of suffering even in a quarrel because they wonder whether the period of delight spent united in an embrace might become shorter.

**1308. What use is there in grieving  
when there is no beloved to understand that one grieves for them?**

What benefit is there in suffering when there is no beloved to understand, “Is she not suffering because she thinks of me?”

**1309. Water beside shade is sweet; likewise, a lovers' quarrel  
is sweet only with one who loves.**

Water found beside shade is cool and pleasant; in the same way, only the sulking one has with a loving person is delightful.

**1310. My heart longs to unite with the one who lets my sulking wither away;  
its unrestrained desire is the cause.**

It is only my heart's ungovernable desire that makes it throb with the wish, “Let us unite with the one who watches while our lovers' quarrel withers without bringing it to an end.”
