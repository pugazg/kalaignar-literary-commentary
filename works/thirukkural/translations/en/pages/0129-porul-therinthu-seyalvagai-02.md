**466. Harm comes from doing what ought not to be done;  
harm also comes from failing to do what ought to be done.**

Doing what should not be done causes harm; leaving undone what should be done also causes harm.

**467. Think, and then venture upon the act;  
to say, ‘we shall think after beginning,’ is a fault.**

One should enter upon an action only after thinking well; to say that one can think after entering upon it is wrong.

**468. An effort not carried out in the proper way  
will collapse in the end even if many stand in support.**

However many people may stand as support, an effort that is not carried out properly will finally become blocked and fail.

**469. Even in doing good there can be error  
if one does not act after understanding each person's nature.**

Even good should be done only after understanding a person's nature. Otherwise, that very good may turn back and strike as harm.

**470. Act only after considering what will not bring contempt;  
the world will not accept what does not accord with one's proper standing.**

Since the worthy will not praise actions that are contrary to one's position, one should undertake only those actions that will not draw their blame.
