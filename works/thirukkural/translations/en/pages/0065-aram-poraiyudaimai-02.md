**156. Those who punish have one day's pleasure;  
those who bear wrong have fame as their companion until death.**

For those who punish the person who harmed them instead of forgiving, only that one day will be pleasurable. But for those who practise patience with the spirit of “let us forget, let us forgive,” their whole life will be filled with fame.

**157. Even if others do what is unworthy, it is good to suffer the pain  
and not do what is against aram in return.**

The finest quality is to feel hurt and grieve over the degrading things others do, yet refrain from doing the same things in return as revenge.

**158. Those who do excessive wrong through arrogance  
may be conquered by one's own worthy quality.**

Those who cause injustice through arrogance can be overcome by our own quality of patience.

**159. Those who endure the cruel words of people who cross all bounds  
possess the purity of renunciants.**

Those who bear the cruel words of people who behave beyond all limits are like pure renunciants.

**160. Those who fast without eating are great;  
yet they stand only after those who endure others' cruel words.**

Even those with the resolve to endure hunger and undertake a fast without food are honoured only after those who bear the cruel words spoken by others.
