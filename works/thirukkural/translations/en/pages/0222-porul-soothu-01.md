# 94. Gambling

**931. Do not seek gambling even if you win; that victory is like  
a fish swallowing the bait together with the hook.**

Even if victory itself is obtained, one should not seek the gambling place. That victory becomes like fish thinking they are swallowing only the bait fixed on the iron point of a fishing hook, but biting down on the iron point itself and carrying it away.

**932. For gamblers who gain one and lose a hundred,  
is there any path by which they may attain good and live?**

What path is there to well-being in the life of gamblers who, delighted by one victory, continue playing and embrace a hundred defeats?

**933. If one keeps the dice rolling and the stakes going without cease,  
wealth and the means of earning that wealth will go away.**

If a person makes it a habit to gamble continuously with stakes, both his wealth and the means by which that wealth is earned will leave him.

**934. There is nothing like gambling for causing many humiliations,  
destroying honour, and bringing poverty.**

There is no other evil act like gambling for subjecting a person to many sufferings, destroying his reputation, and plunging him into poverty.

**935. Those who refuse to let go of the gaming place, its implements, and the hand that plays  
become people who have nothing.**

People who have no mind to give up the place where gambling is played, the implements used for it, and the effort devoted to it will become people who have nothing at all.
