# 82. Harmful Friendship

**811. Even if those without good character seem to engulf us in affection,  
it is better that their friendship diminish than grow.**

Even if people without good qualities appear to drown us in a flood of affection, it is better not to let their friendship grow further, but to reduce it.

**812. Those who are friends when there is benefit and leave when there is none—  
what matters whether such friendship is gained or lost?**

What does it matter whether one has or loses the friendship of people who remain friends only while it is useful to them and depart when it is not?

**813. Those who weigh gain before forming friendship, women for hire, and thieves  
are all of the same kind.**

Those who calculate the benefit and form friendship only for that reason, women for hire, and thieves—these three are all of the same kind.

**814. Better solitude than the companionship of those  
like an untrained horse that throws one down and flees in battle.**

It is far better to remain alone than to have the friendship of people who resemble a horse that throws its rider down on the battlefield and escapes.

**815. The friendship of the base does not become a protection;  
better not to gain it than to gain it.**

The friendship of people of base character has an evil nature that does not provide protection. It is therefore better that such friendship never arise than that it arise at all.
