Today, many of us know that the world's manner of conduct—that is, the way the world operates—is becoming a debased and inverted order; we are moved to tears, wring our hands, tremble, and grow agitated at what we see. This condition has not arisen because aram does not exist in the world, because good conduct never came into being, or because it has vanished. One reason is also that the principles of good conduct, or the path of aram, have not been made accessible for many people to understand.

Many who tried to teach aram, whether because they believed people would not follow it if it were stated directly, or because they wished to spread certain other doctrines of their own through it, mixed aram—the precious remedy for human life—with religion, God, mythological stories, and many other things before giving it to the people. We see that, just as a medicine can lose its benefit when the oil and other substances mixed with it as aids become too strong, the harmful effects of those adulterating substances have instead prevailed and disordered human society. Thus we see that the methods adopted by many others to teach aram not only failed to produce benefit but also became surrounded by many harms.

Just as material resources must be made available to everyone, what is needed is to make aram understandable to all. Just as people must be prevented from exploiting the property of others, the next duty is to ensure that the common aram is not damaged by a few or by many. Those who, like Valluvar, carried out the first duty fully, impartially, directly, universally, and simply remain, even to this day, people who must be searched for and discovered.
