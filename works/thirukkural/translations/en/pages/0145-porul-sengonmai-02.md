**546. It is not the spear that gives a ruler victory;  
it is the straight sceptre that does not bend from justice.**

What gives a government victory is not the spear that brings down enemies, but the unbending just sceptre that enables its citizens to live.

**547. The ruler protects the world; if he does not fail in justice,  
that very justice will protect him.**

If a government is conducted without swerving from justice, that justice itself will protect the government.

**548. A ruler who does not examine and render justice, though surrounded by display,  
will fall into a low condition and perish by himself.**

A government that proceeds with pomp, without examining matters and dispensing justice, will sink to a low state and destroy itself.

**549. To protect and support the people, and punish wrongdoing without fearing personal blame,  
is the duty of government.**

It is the duty of government to protect and stand by the citizens, and to punish those who commit offences whoever they may be, without thinking that doing so will bring discredit upon itself.

**550. When a ruler punishes those cruel enough to commit murder,  
it is like removing weeds for the flourishing of a green crop.**

When a government subjects those who commit murder and other cruelties to punishment, it is like removing weeds so that a crop may flourish.
