**796. Even in misfortune there is one certainty: it is a measuring rod  
stretched out to measure one's friends.**

Even when harm comes, there is one benefit in it. That very harm becomes the instrument that measures and shows what kind of friends one has.

**797. A true gain for a person is this: to let go  
the friendship of fools.**

The good fortune gained by a person is to abandon the friendship he had with one who lacks understanding.

**798. Do not dwell on thoughts that make the heart shrink; do not take  
friends who abandon you when suffering comes.**

One should avoid even thinking about actions that destroy courage, and about friends who move away when suffering comes.

**799. The friendship of those who abandon one in a time of ruin will burn the heart  
even when remembered at the hour of death.**

Even when a person is facing death, if he remembers friends who abandoned him and ran away when harm came to him, that memory will scorch his heart.

**800. Associate with those whose minds are without stain; even at a price,  
sever the friendship of those whose minds are stained.**

Only those whose minds are without stain should be taken as friends. Friendship with those who carry stain in their minds should be cast off even if one has to pay a price to do so.
