# 41. Lack of Learning

**401. Speaking in an assembly without the fullness of learning  
is like playing a dice game without first laying out the board.**

Speaking in an assembly without full intellectual strength is like playing a game of dice without even setting out the board on which it is to be played.

**402. Desire to hear the words of an unlearned man  
is like desire for a woman who has no breasts.**

Wanting to hear the speech of an unlearned man is comparable to becoming infatuated with a woman who has no breasts.

**403. Even the unlearned are very good  
if they can remain silent before the learned.**

Even those without learning will be regarded as good people if they have learned to remain silent in the presence of the learned.

**404. However excellent the natural intelligence of an unlearned man may be,  
the knowledgeable will not accept him as learned.**

Even if a person without education has natural intelligence, people of knowledge will not accept him as one distinguished in learning.

**405. The pretended greatness of an unlearned person  
collapses when he begins to converse with the learned.**

The false guise by which those without learning display themselves as great intellectuals falls away when they enter into conversation with learned and accomplished scholars.
