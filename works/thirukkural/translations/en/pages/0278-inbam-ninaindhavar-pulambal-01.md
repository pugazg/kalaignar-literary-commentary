# 121. Lamenting the Absent Beloved

**1201. Because merely thinking of it gives an unending great delight,  
love is sweeter than liquor.**

Love, which sweetens the heart merely by being remembered, is more delightful than liquor, which gives pleasure only while it is being drunk.

**1202. Love is sweet in every way, for when one thinks of the beloved,  
none of the pain of separation comes.**

When one thinks of the beloved with whom one has lovingly united, the suffering that may arise in separation does not come. Thus, in every way, love is sweet.

**1203. A sneeze begins as though it will come and then dies away;  
does my beloved likewise begin to think of me and then cease?**

A sneeze seems about to come and then stops without coming; in the same way, does my beloved seem about to think of me and then cease to do so?

**1204. My beloved never leaves my heart;  
am I likewise present without leaving his heart?**

Just as my beloved remains without leaving my heart, do I too remain without leaving his heart?

**1205. He keeps me out of his own heart; is he not ashamed  
to enter mine without cease?**

He gives me no place in his heart; yet it seems he feels no shame in coming and entering my heart alone, without interruption.
