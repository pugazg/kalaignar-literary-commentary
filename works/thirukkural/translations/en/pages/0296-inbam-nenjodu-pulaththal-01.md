# 130. Sulking with the Heart

**1291. O heart, though you see that his own heart stands with him and not with us,  
why do you refuse to stand with me and instead melt thinking of him?**

O heart! When his own heart supports him in not thinking of us, why do you fail to support me and instead think of him and melt away?

**1292. Even after seeing that he shows us no love, O heart,  
you still go to him believing that he will not reject us.**

O heart! Even after seeing that he is one who shows us no affection, you go to him trusting that he will not hate us.

**1293. O heart, when you leave me and follow after the one you desire,  
is it because the ruined have no friends to stand by them?**

O heart! When you abandon me, desire him and follow behind him, is it as though the saying were true that those destroyed by suffering have no friends to support them?

**1294. O heart, who will ever plan anything with you again?  
You will not first sulk and then taste the delight that follows; I will not speak of it with you.**

O heart! You refuse even to think, “Let us first sulk and afterward enjoy its fruit in union.” So who is going to discuss such a plan with you? I am not.

**1295. My heart's suffering never ends: it fears not obtaining him,  
and once he comes, it fears separation.**

For my heart, suffering is an endless story. It fears because the beloved is not seen; when he comes, it fears again, thinking that he may leave.
