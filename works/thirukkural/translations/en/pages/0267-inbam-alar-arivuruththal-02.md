# 115. Awareness of Gossip

**1146. We met only for a single day; yet the gossip has spread  
like the tale of a serpent swallowing the moon.**

Though the lovers met only for a single day, that meeting spread throughout the town as gossip, like the event called an “eclipse,” imagined as a serpent swallowing the moon.

**1147. With the townspeople's gossip as manure and my mother's words as water,  
this malady of love will only grow.**

The love that blossomed as two people desired one another will grow—with the townspeople's words of blame as manure and the mother's harsh words as water—rather than dry up and perish.

**1148. To say that love can be extinguished by gossip  
is like saying that fire can be quenched with ghee.**

For the feeling of love to subside out of fear of the townspeople's reproach would be like trying to put out a blazing fire by pouring ghee upon it.

**1149. Can I be ashamed of gossip, when the one who said, “Do not fear; I will not leave you,”  
has left me in a way that makes many ashamed?**

When the one who firmly assured me, “I will not leave you; do not be afraid,” has gone away leaving me in a manner that would make many people ashamed, can I alone feel shame over the gossip spread by the town?

**1150. This town raises the gossip we desire;  
if my beloved desires it too, he will grant what we seek.**

The townspeople spread gossip in just the way we wish; if the beloved too wishes it, he will consent to it.
