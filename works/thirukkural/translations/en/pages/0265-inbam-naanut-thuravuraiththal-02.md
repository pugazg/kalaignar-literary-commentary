# 114. Declaring the Abandonment of Modesty

**1136. Even at midnight I will surely think of riding the madal,  
for my foolish eyes refuse to close.**

My eyes suffer without sleep because of my beloved; therefore, even at midnight, I keep firmly thinking of riding the madal.

**1137. There is nothing greater than the woman who, though tormented  
by love vast as the sea, does not mount the madal.**

Even when the sickness of love torments her like a surging sea, there is nothing to equal the greatness of the woman who endures it and does not mount the madal.

**1138. Love, caring not whether one is hard to restrain or deserving of pity,  
escapes concealment and comes before the public.**

Without pausing to think, “Poor thing! This person does not know how to conceal what is in the heart; this person deserves pity,” love can break out into the open for the whole town to know.

**1139. Thinking that no one knows it, my love wanders bewildered  
through the streets.**

Perhaps because it thinks that no one other than me knows of it, my love spreads through the street and wanders about in bewilderment!

**1140. Those without understanding laugh when they see us,  
for they have never suffered what we have suffered.**

Only those who have never experienced the suffering of people wasting away from the sickness of love will look at those who suffer from that sickness and laugh.
