# 31. Freedom from Anger

**301. One guards anger by guarding it where anger can prevail;  
where it cannot, what if one guards it or does not?**

The person who refrains from anger where that anger could have an effect is the one who truly guards against anger; where it cannot have any effect, what difference does it make whether anger is restrained or not?

**302. Anger where it cannot prevail is harmful;  
even where it can, nothing is more harmful.**

Anger toward those stronger than oneself will bring harm. Yet anger toward those weaker than oneself is no less harmful; there is nothing worse.

**303. Forget anger toward anyone;  
all evil consequences arise from it.**

Whoever one may become angry with, that anger should be forgotten. Otherwise, that very anger will become the cause of evil consequences.

**304. What enemy is there besides anger  
that kills both smiles and joy?**

For those who become angry, not only the brightness of the face but also the happiness of the mind disappears.

**305. If one would protect oneself, guard against anger;  
if not, anger itself will destroy oneself.**

If a person wishes to protect himself, he must give up anger. Otherwise, anger will destroy him.
