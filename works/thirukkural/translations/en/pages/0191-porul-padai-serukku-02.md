**776. A warrior counts among wasted days  
all the days on which no honourable wound was received.**

When a warrior counts the days he has lived, he will reject with contempt as wasted days all those on which his body did not receive an honourable battle wound.

**777. The warrior's anklet worn by those who desire fame that spreads all around  
and care nothing for life possesses a beauty of its own.**

The warrior's anklet fastened to the feet of those who desire only fame that spreads all around and do not concern themselves with life possesses a distinctive greatness.

**778. Warriors who do not fear for their lives when battle comes  
do not lose their honour even if their leader grows angry.**

Those who continue to perform their duty without losing distinction even when their leader is angry are the valiant warriors praised for not being troubled about their lives on the battlefield.

**779. Who can reproach a warrior who dies in the field  
without failing the vow he made?**

Can anyone speak in contempt of a warrior who, exactly as vowed, has the courage to die on the battlefield? No.

**780. If one can die so that the eyes of the leader who protected him fill with tears,  
such a death is worth obtaining even by begging for it.**

If a warrior dies a heroic death that makes tears well up in the eyes of the leader who protected him, there is greatness even in begging to obtain such a death.
