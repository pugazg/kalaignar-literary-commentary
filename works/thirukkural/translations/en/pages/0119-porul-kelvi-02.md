**416. Listen to good things, however little;  
for each measure of it will bring mature greatness.**

One must hold the confidence that the extent to which one listens to good things is the extent to which greatness will be gained.

**417. Even when they have understood something wrongly, those enriched by discerning listening  
will not speak foolishly.**

Those who examine matters closely and also possess knowledge through listening will not speak in an ignorant manner even when they have understood some things incorrectly.

**418. Though able to hear, ears not pierced by learning through listening  
are as though they cannot hear.**

Even if they are naturally capable of hearing, ears that refuse to listen to the words of good people will be called deaf ears.

**419. Those without subtle knowledge gained through listening  
will find it hard to possess a humble tongue.**

People without clear knowledge gained by listening cannot easily become people of calm character who speak with restraint.

**420. What does it matter whether those who know the taste of the mouth but not the taste of the ear  
live or die?**

For people who live only for the taste of the mouth without knowing the taste that comes through the ear, being alive and ceasing to live amount to the same thing.
