# 80. Examining Friendship

**791. No harm equals making friendship without examination; once friendship is made,  
there is no escape for those who live by friendship.**

A bad friendship entered into without examination can bring harm to such an extent that one cannot free oneself from that friendship.

**792. Friendship not chosen after repeated examination  
can finally bring sorrow severe enough to cause one's death.**

Friendship formed without examining it again and again can, in the end, produce sorrow so great that it becomes the cause of a person's death.

**793. Know a person's character, family background, faults, and lasting qualities;  
only then form friendship.**

What is the person's character? What is the nature of the family into which he was born? What are his faults? What qualities in him do not diminish? One should know all these before forming friendship with him.

**794. Friendship with one born in a worthy family who is ashamed of blame  
should be obtained even at a price.**

The friendship of one born in a cultured family and who lives with the fear that blame should not come upon him is a great distinction and should be obtained by any means possible.

**795. Examine and choose the friendship of one able to rebuke until tears come  
and to give counsel that sets one right.**

One should regard as true friendship the friendship of a person who has the ability to condemn a wrongdoer until tears come and to offer counsel that corrects him.
