# 129. Longing for Union

**1281. Rejoicing merely by thinking and delighting merely by seeing—  
these belong to love, not to liquor.**

Liquor gives pleasure only when it is drunk; love is not like that. Thinking of it is itself delight, and merely seeing one another gives lovers delight.

**1282. When love has grown as tall as a palmyra,  
there should not be even a millet-seed's measure of sulking.**

When love has grown to the measure of a palmyra, one should not indulge in even a millet-seed's measure of lovers' quarrel.

**1283. Even if he does only what he desires and does not cherish me,  
my eyes cannot find peace without seeing my beloved.**

Even if he does not embrace and care for me, and instead goes on doing only what pleases him, my eyes do not find peace without seeing him.

**1284. Friend, I went intending to quarrel with him;  
my heart forgot that purpose and went instead to unite with him.**

Even when I go for the purpose of sulking, the heart forgets it and agrees to union; that is the nature of love.

**1285. Just as the eye does not see the stick while kohl is being applied,  
when I see my beloved I do not see his fault.**

Just as the eye does not see the little stick that applies kohl while the kohl is being placed upon it, when I see my beloved I forget the fault of his having left me.
