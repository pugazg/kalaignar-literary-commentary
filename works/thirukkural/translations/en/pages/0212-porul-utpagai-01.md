# 89. Internal Enmity

**881. Even pleasant shade and water become harmful when they cause harm;  
so too do one's own people when their nature turns harmful.**

Even shade and water that appear pleasant must be regarded as harmful if they cause injury. In the same way, internal enmity among one's relatives and close people is harmful.

**882. Do not fear the enemy who comes openly like a sword; fear instead  
the enemy who stays close like kin.**

One must be more cautious of those who remain close and seek to destroy us through familiarity than of enemies who approach openly.

**883. Fear internal enmity and guard yourself; in a time of weakness  
it cuts like the tool that splits an unbaked clay pot.**

A person must protect himself in fear of internal enmity. Otherwise, at a time of trial, that internal enmity will destroy him like the tool that cuts an unbaked clay vessel.

**884. If internal enmity arises in a mind that will not be reconciled,  
it brings many harms that turn one's own people against one.**

If a feeling of internal enmity arises in a person to the point that his mind does not become right, it will create harm that turns even those connected with him into enemies.

**885. If internal enmity arises among close relatives,  
it brings many forms of suffering that lead to harm.**

Internal enmity arising among close relatives will produce many sufferings capable of bringing harm upon them.
