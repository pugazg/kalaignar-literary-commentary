# 68. The Method of Action

**671. Let deliberation reach its conclusion and then attain resolve;  
for that resolve to remain in delay is harmful.**

When deciding to engage in an action, the examination of the advantages and disadvantages that may result from it must also have been completed. After a decision has been made, delaying it can prove harmful.

**672. Let actions that should be done with deliberation wait and be done so;  
do not delay an action that must be done without delay.**

Matters that need to be carried out with deliberation may be done after taking time; but there must be no delay in matters that need to be done quickly.

**673. Wherever it is possible, completing the action is good; when it is not possible,  
act after looking for the way by which it can be done.**

Wherever possible, bringing an action to completion will yield good. Where that is not possible, one must know the way suited to it and complete the action through that way.

**674. When considered, the remnants of action and enmity, those two,  
will flare up like a remnant of fire.**

If either a fitting action or an enmity that has been confronted is left without being brought fully to an end, it will cause harm like a fire that has been only half extinguished.

**675. Consider, without obscurity, all five: resources, means, time,  
the action and the place.**

Before becoming involved in a matter, one must make sure without deficiency of all five: the resources required for it, the appropriate means, the time, the method of action to be undertaken, and the suitable place.
