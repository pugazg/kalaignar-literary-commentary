**1106. Each time I embrace her, life sprouts afresh through her touch;  
the maiden's shoulders must surely be made of nectar.**

Every time I embrace this young woman, I gain new life; perhaps the reason is that her beautiful shoulders are made of nectar.

**1107. The embrace of this lovely woman is like the joy of living from one's own means,  
sharing from what is one's own and then eating one's share.**

The pleasure that comes from embracing one's beautiful beloved wife is like the happiness of eating after sharing among many what one has earned by one's own labour.

**1108. Sweet to the two who love each other is an embrace  
so close that even air cannot pass between them.**

What gives lovers the greatest sweetness is embracing each other so tightly that even the air cannot enter between them.

**1109. Sulking, understanding that sulking, and then uniting—  
these are the fruits obtained by those joined in love.**

To sulk, then to feel that the pleasure born of sulking is enough, and then to unite and lose themselves in a still greater pleasure—these are the fruits received by those who live in love.

**1110. As each new act of knowing reveals what was not known before,  
so love grows new each time I draw close to the ornamented woman.**

Each time I enjoy love with the woman of mango-like complexion adorned with beautiful ornaments, the love that arises feels like discovering, again and again, things that had never before been known.
