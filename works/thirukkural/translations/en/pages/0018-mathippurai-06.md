## 4. Oozh

God and the soul are two ideas that, even to this day, have kept people from attaining clarity and have left them confused. There seem to be no other concepts that create confusion quite like these two. If we think a little more deeply, *oozh* may be placed next to them. In the case of some people, one may even say that this third concept creates greater confusion than the first two. Commentators have continued to write confusing explanations for the section of Kurals dealing with an imagined doctrine variously called *oozh*, fate, what is written on one's head, and karma. Kalaignar, as a rationalist, has not only given a clear explanation of the chapter *Oozh* but has also shown a way out of the confusion people have about this idea, as well as the fear, anxiety, and helplessness that arise from it.

Kalaignar gives *oozh* the meaning **natural condition**. By natural condition is meant the constitution of nature—that is, the way nature is arranged.

Nature is powerful. It is because of this power that nature also endures. Kalaignar shows in his commentary that Valluvar's thought in the chapter *Oozh* is that one must understand this and conduct oneself accordingly.

The following commentary offers evidence for the great power of nature: “Even though a person may have studied books capable of imparting keen intelligence, that person's natural intelligence will nevertheless rise above them.”
