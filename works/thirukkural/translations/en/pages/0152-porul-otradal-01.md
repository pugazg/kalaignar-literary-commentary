# 59. Espionage

**581. The spy and the authoritative book of right conduct—these two  
should be regarded as the ruler's eyes.**

A spy who possesses integrity and ability, and a book of aram that speaks of justice, must be regarded as the eyes of a government.

**582. To be able to learn swiftly, at all times, everything that happens  
to everyone is the ruler's duty.**

It is the duty of government, through spies, to learn quickly at all times every event occurring among friends, enemies and those who stand neutral.

**583. A ruler who does not learn the matter through spies  
has no way for victory to flourish.**

There is no way for the success of a government to flourish if it does not use spies to learn the condition of the country, examine the consequences and act accordingly.

**584. The spy must investigate all alike—those engaged in action,  
their circles, and those who are opposed.**

Those who work as intelligence-gathering spies for a government can be called upright spies only if they perform their work without discrimination between those who are wanted, those who are not wanted and their own associates.

**585. A spy must be able to assume an unsuspected appearance, fear no searching gaze,  
and never reveal what is within, whatever may happen.**

Only those who can carry an appearance that gives no ground for suspicion, remain unafraid of those who look at them suspiciously, and never disclose what is in their mind whatever happens can serve as spies.
