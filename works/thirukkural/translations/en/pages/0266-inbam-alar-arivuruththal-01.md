# 115. Awareness of Gossip

**1141. As gossip rises, my precious life endures;  
by good fortune, many do not know why.**

The townspeople will not know that our lives remain with us because we hope that their words of blame about our love will themselves become an opportunity for our love to come to fulfilment.

**1142. Not knowing the worth of the flower-eyed woman,  
this town, through its gossip, has given her to me.**

Without realizing the excellence of that flower-eyed woman, the townspeople spoke reproachfully of there being love between us—and thus their words themselves became an indirect help to us.

**1143. Will not the gossip known throughout the town arise?  
Though I have not gained her, it makes me feel as though I have.**

Will not the town begin talking about our love? That talk has come to give me the delight of feeling as though I have already attained the love that has not yet come to me!

**1144. Love grows richer through gossip; without it,  
it would lose its vigour and wither away.**

Our love grows because the townspeople spread scandal about it; otherwise this vine of love would lose its richness and wither away.

**1145. As liquor becomes sweeter with every intoxication,  
so love grows sweeter each time it becomes known.**

Love becomes sweeter and sweeter the more openly it comes to be known, just as one who drinks liquor comes to desire that very liquor more and more as intoxication deepens.
