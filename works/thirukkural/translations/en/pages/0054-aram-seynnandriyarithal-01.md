# 11. Gratitude for Help Received

**101. For help rendered without being sought,  
even earth and heaven can scarcely be its equal.**

Like the expression “a great gem that came unbidden,” if a rare help worthy of being praised as “help rendered without being sought” is given, even this sky and earth cannot equal it.

**102. Though help given at the needed time may be small,  
its worth is greater than the world.**

Even if help given at the time it is needed is small in amount, it will be valued as greater than the world.

**103. If the worth of help done without weighing what gain may return is weighed,  
its goodness is greater than the sea.**

The distinction of help that a person gives out of love, without considering what benefit may come from it, is greater than the sea.

**104. Those who know its benefit will regard even millet-sized help  
as great as a palmyra.**

A grateful person who benefits from even a millet-sized good done by another will regard it as being as great as a palmyra, which can be useful in many ways.

**105. The worth of help is not determined by the amount given;  
its measure depends on the character of the one who receives it.**

Help does not gain its distinction according to the amount in which it is given; its measure is valued according to the character of the person who receives that help.
