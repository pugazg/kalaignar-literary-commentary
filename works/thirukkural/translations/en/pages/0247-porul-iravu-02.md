**1056. On seeing those who have none of the misery of concealment,  
all the misery of the beggar's poverty disappears at once.**

At the very sight of people free from the degrading habit of hiding what they have because they lack the heart to give it, the suffering of poverty borne by those who beg begins to disappear.

**1057. On seeing those who give without scorn or contempt,  
the beggar's heart rejoices and delights within itself.**

When they see people of generous nature who give without speaking degradingly or mocking them, the hearts of those who beg are filled with happiness and delight.

**1058. If there were no beggars, this cool, wide world  
would be like a wooden puppet that merely comes and goes.**

To those who say that people who beg because of poverty should not come near them, there is no difference between such human beings and wooden figures that are made to move.

**1059. What distinction could there be for givers  
if there were none who sought to receive by begging?**

If there were no one to receive by begging, the opportunity to gain fame by giving away one's possessions would disappear.

**1060. The beggar must not grow angry; his own suffering from poverty  
is itself sufficient testimony.**

A beggar should not become angry with someone who says, “I have nothing.” His own poverty is evidence enough that another person's condition too may be the same as his.
