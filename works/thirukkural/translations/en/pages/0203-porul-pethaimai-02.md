**836. If a fool who does not know how to act undertakes a task,  
the task fails and he brings punishment upon himself.**

A fool who does not know the path of upright conduct cannot continue the task he has undertaken; as a result, the task itself is ruined and he brings punishment upon himself.

**837. When great wealth comes to a fool, strangers feast  
while loving relatives go hungry.**

Wealth accumulated in the hands of ignorant fools serves only for outsiders to seize and enjoy; it does not benefit their affectionate relatives who remain hungry.

**838. If even one possession comes into a fool's hand,  
it is like a madman who has also drunk liquor.**

A fool is one who cannot distinguish what is good from what is bad. If even one possession comes into such a fool's hands, it becomes like the story of a person already mad who has also drunk liquor.

**839. Friendship with fools is greatly pleasant, for on separation  
it brings no suffering at all.**

Friendship with ignorant fools is very pleasant, because when one separates from them no suffering arises.

**840. A fool entering an assembly of the learned  
is like placing on a bed an unwashed foot that has stepped in filth.**

A fool entering an assembly where learned people have gathered is like placing on a bed, without washing it, a foot that has stepped in filth.
