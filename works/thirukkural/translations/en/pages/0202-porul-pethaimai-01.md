# 84. Folly

**831. What is folly? Letting go what brings benefit  
and choosing what brings harm.**

Folly is the condition of failing to distinguish what causes harm from what brings good, abandoning the good, and seeking the harmful.

**832. The greatest folly among all follies is to desire  
and undertake what lies beyond one's capacity.**

To desire acts that one is incapable of doing and to interfere in them is the greatest folly among all follies.

**833. Not being ashamed when one should, not seeking what should be sought, not showing love where it is due,  
and not preserving what should be preserved—these are the ways of fools.**

It is the nature of fools not to feel shame where shame is due, not to seek and obtain what should be sought, not to show affection toward those to whom affection should be shown, and not to protect what ought to be protected.

**834. None is a greater fool than one who has read, understood, and taught others  
yet does not himself live accordingly.**

People may have studied, understood what they studied, and be able to make many others understand it; if they alone do not conduct themselves accordingly, there can be no greater fools than they.

**835. The self-willed fool, through one act, sinks himself  
into the mire of suffering for seven periods.**

A fool who acts entirely according to his own will will have to remain sunk in the mire called suffering through seven periods.
