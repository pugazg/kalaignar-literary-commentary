# 51. Knowing and Choosing

**501. Examine a person through virtue, wealth, pleasure, and fear for life;  
then choose him after knowing his nature through these four.**

A person should be examined and appointed to a responsibility only if he is firm in the path of virtue, honest in matters of wealth, not deluded in the pursuit of pleasure, and unafraid for his own life.

**502. Place confidence in one born to worth, free of fault,  
and possessed of a sense of shame that fears disgrace.**

One should recognize as truly high-born the person who is free from wrongdoing and who fears and feels ashamed to commit an act that brings blame.

**503. Even among those who have learned rare works and are said to be faultless,  
it is difficult, on examination, to find complete freedom from ignorance.**

Even when people are praised as having learned many rare books and as being without any defect, a deep examination cannot easily conclude that there is absolutely no ignorance in them.

**504. Examine a person's virtues and faults;  
weigh which are greater, and judge him by what predominates.**

One should study a person's qualities and shortcomings, determine which are present in greater measure, and only then come to a clear conclusion about that person.

**505. A person's own deeds are the touchstone  
for both greatness and baseness.**

By taking the actions a person performs as the touchstone, one can understand whether that person is of worth or of degraded character.
