**1016. Would the great cherish the vast world  
without taking modesty as their fence?**

In this vast world, the noble take the fence called modesty as their protection above every other form of protection.

**1017. Those ruled by modesty will give up life for modesty;  
for the sake of life, they will not give up modesty.**

Those who possess a sense of modesty will even give up their lives in order to protect their honour. They will not give up honour in order to protect life.

**1018. If one does not feel shame for what makes others ashamed,  
there is cause for virtue itself to feel shame.**

If people who have incurred blame to an extent that ought to make them ashamed feel no shame for it, we must regard moral conduct itself as having withdrawn from them in shame.

**1019. A failure of principle burns one's lineage; when shamelessness abides,  
it burns away every good.**

If one goes wrong in the principle one has adopted, disgrace falls upon one's lineage. If, without feeling shame for that, one commits acts for which others will blame one, all good will be destroyed.

**1020. The movement of those without modesty in their hearts is like a wooden puppet,  
made to seem alive by a string.**

There is no difference between a wooden doll made to appear alive by being moved with strings and a person who moves about in the world without any feeling called modesty in the mind.
