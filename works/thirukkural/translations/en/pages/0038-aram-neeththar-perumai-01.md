# 3. The Greatness of Renunciants

**21. The greatness of renunciants firm in conduct  
is what learned works resolve to place among the highest excellences.**

The greatness of renunciants who are steadfast in conduct will be recorded willingly and with distinction in the works of the worthy.

**22. To measure the greatness of those who have renounced  
is like trying to count all who have died in the world.**

Can anyone state how many people have died in the world? In the same way, the greatness of noble people who have truly renounced attachments cannot be measured.

**23. The world shines through the greatness of those  
who discern the two kinds, know good from evil, and embrace aram here.**

Those who examine and understand what is good and what is evil and take up what is good are the ones worthy of greatness in the world.

**24. One who guards all five with the goad called firmness  
is a seed fit for the field called renunciation.**

One who uses the goad called firmness to restrain and guard the five senses becomes a seed suited to the field called the life of renunciation.

**25. Indra, lord of those in the wide sky, is sufficient witness  
to the power of one who has subdued the five.**

Indra stands as an example of a person who goes astray because he cannot control the senses; through that example, he shows the power of those whose fame reaches the sky because they have controlled the desires caused by the five senses.

> Editorial review note: Kalaignar explicitly uses **Indra** as the example here. His commentary presents Indra as one who goes astray through failure to control the senses and, through that example, points to the power of those who control the desires produced by the five senses. That interpretive direction is retained without replacing it with another commentator's reading.
