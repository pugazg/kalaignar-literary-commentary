**196. One who keeps speaking useless words  
should be called chaff among people, not a human being.**

A person who speaks useless things hoping to gain from them is more fittingly called chaff than called a human being.

**197. The worthy may even speak words that are not pleasant;  
it is good that they do not speak useless words.**

People of good character may even utter words that are not sweet, but it is better that they refrain from uttering words that serve no purpose.

**198. Those with the wisdom to examine rare benefits  
will not speak words that bring no great benefit.**

Those who possess the capacity to inquire into and understand rare benefits will not use any word that produces no substantial benefit.

**199. Those of stainless understanding, freed from delusion,  
will not speak words emptied of meaning even through forgetfulness.**

People of stainless understanding, without even a trace of confusion, will not utter useless words even by mistake.

**200. Speak words that carry benefit;  
do not speak words among words that have no benefit.**

One should leave aside words that bring no benefit and speak only words capable of lodging in the mind and yielding benefit.
