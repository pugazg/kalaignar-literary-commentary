# 112. Praising Her Beauty

**1116. Unable to tell the moon from the maiden's face,  
the stars in their stations are bewildered.**

Unable to make out the difference between the maiden's face and the moon, the stars are bewildered and distressed.

**1117. Is there, as on the radiant moon that wanes and waxes,  
even a blemish upon the maiden's face?**

Even the small blemish found on the moon that wanes, waxes and pours forth light is nowhere to be found on this good maiden's face!

**1118. O moon, if you can shine like a woman's face,  
then live long and be worthy of love.**

Full moon! If you too wish to become one worthy of my love, may you live long, glowing like the face of my beloved.

**1119. O moon, if you would resemble the face of the flower-eyed woman,  
do not appear where many can see you.**

Moon! If you wish to take pride in being equal to the face of my beloved, whose eyes are like flowers, it would be better for you not to appear where all can see you—so that you do not suffer defeat in that contest.

**1120. The anicham flower and the swan's down are like nerunji fruit  
to a woman's feet.**

Whether it is the anicham flower or the feather of a swan, both are capable of causing pain as though a nerunji thorn had pierced them—so soft are the feet of my beloved.
