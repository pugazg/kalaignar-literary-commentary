**1052. Begging can be a joy to a person, if what is begged for  
is received without causing suffering.**

If something can be obtained without causing any distress to the minds of either the giver or the receiver, then even if that thing is obtained by asking for it, it will bring only joy.

## F. Clarity

As an example of clarity in commentary:

**1162. I cannot conceal this illness;  
to tell the one who caused it brings me shame.**

I am unable to hide this illness of love; yet because of modesty, I am also unable to tell the lover who caused it.

## G. Concise Explanation

As an example of concise explanation:

**1317. When I sneezed she blessed me; then she wept, asking,  
“Thinking of whom did you sneeze?”**

I sneezed; as is customary, she blessed me. At once, seized by some suspicion, she asked, “Because who was thinking of you did you sneeze?” and, contrary to the blessing she had first offered, began to cry—we may observe and enjoy this as well.

## H. New Meaning

The following passages bear witness to the innovation that does not depart from fidelity which Kalaignar found in assigning meanings to difficult words.

**98. Sweet speech free from pettiness  
brings joy both in this life and after it.**

Pleasant words free from pettiness can bring a person fame both while he lives and after he has lived and passed away.
