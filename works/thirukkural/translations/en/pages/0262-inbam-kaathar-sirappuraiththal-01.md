# 113. Declaring the Excellence of Love

**1121. The moisture that wells between the white teeth of her gentle speech  
is like milk mingled with honey.**

The saliva that wells between the pearly-white teeth of this woman who speaks sweet words gives a taste like milk mixed with honey.

**1122. What the bond between body and life is,  
that is the bond between this maiden and me.**

Life and body do not exist separately, apart from one another; such is our relationship.

**1123. O image within the dark pupil, leave! There is no room  
for the bright-browed woman with whom I have fallen in love.**

O little image in the dark pupil of my eye! Leave and make room for her, so that I may give the beauty I love a place within my very eye!

**1124. When the jewelled maiden is with me, she is life itself;  
when she departs, it is like death.**

When the jewelled maiden—adorned with rare and carefully chosen qualities—is with me, I feel as though life has joined the body; when she leaves me, I feel as though my life itself is leaving.

**1125. I would think of the qualities of the bright-eyed woman  
if I ever forgot them; but I know not how to forget.**

I do not even have to think of the qualities of my beloved, whose eyes are radiant; only if I forgot them would there be a need to remember them.
