Even if suffering comes like a flood, the moment people of wisdom think of the way by which it can be overcome, that suffering will withdraw and run away.

This commentary stands as evidence.

**When adversity comes, smile;  
nothing equals that for driving it away.**

If the thought of this Kural is considered together with the preceding commentary, we can see the richness of Kalaignar's scholarship shine forth.

> Editorial review note: this page supplies the complete quoted Kural containing `அடுத்தூர்வது அஃதொப்பதில்`, which Part 001 deliberately retained untranslated when only the compact phrase appeared there. The Part 002 rendering **“nothing equals that for driving it away”** is retained because it stays close to the complete verse and Nannan's immediately preceding explanation. ## 6. Gratitude

Before the Dravidian renaissance arose and Father Periyar, Perarignar Anna, Revolutionary Poet Bharathidasan, and Muthamizh Arignar Kalaignar created an awakening, the Thirukkural—which even today is praised as “there is no other universal work of aram that can be said to equal this”—lay pushed aside, unable to attain the influence it ought to have possessed. There are many reasons for this. One of them is that it did not become a work followed by people in general and by Tamils in particular. Even today, how many people live by taking it as a way of life rather than merely speaking and writing about it? There are also reasons why the Thirukkural has not thus been taken as a guide. One of them is the absence of a commentary that clearly points out and defines the path indicated by each Kural—showing today's Tamil reader, “this is precisely what it is,” in a way that can be understood and taken to heart. Kalaignar's commentary arose to remove that deficiency. Just as Kalaignar built Valluvar Kottam and created *Kuraloviyam*, his fashioning of this Kural commentary too has become a notable and valuable contribution to the work of spreading the Kural's path. Among the many commentators—from earlier commentators such as Parimelazhagar to modern commentators such as Thiru. Vi. Ka., the Revolutionary Poet, and Mu. Va.—Kalaignar stands apart through his work.
