# 18. Not Coveting

**171. If one covets another's precious property without fairness,  
one's family will perish and blame will come at once.**

If a person sets conscience aside and desires to seize a precious possession belonging to another, that person's family too will be ruined and blame will follow.

**172. Those who are ashamed to abandon impartiality  
will not do blameworthy deeds for the sake of gain.**

Those who believe that departing from impartiality is something for which one should bow one's head in shame will not engage in a blameworthy act merely because it brings them some benefit.

**173. Those who desire the other joy will not, for a little pleasure,  
do what is against aram.**

Those who desire lasting benefit on the path of aram will not depart from that path merely because an immediate benefit is available.

**174. Those of pure understanding who have conquered the senses  
will not covet another's property saying, “We have nothing.”**

Pure people who possess control over the senses will not desire to seize another person's property even when suffering in poverty.

**175. What use is subtle and far-reaching understanding  
if one covets anyone's possessions and acts madly?**

If a person desires to seize someone else's possessions in a way outside the path of aram, what use is it even if that person possesses subtle, discerning intelligence?
