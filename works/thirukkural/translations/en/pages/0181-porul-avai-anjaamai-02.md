**726. What use is a sword to those without fierce courage? What use are books  
to those who fear a subtle assembly?**

A sword in the hand is of no use to cowards; likewise, however many books they have learned, those who fear speaking in an assembly gain no benefit from them.

**727. The learning of one who fears in an assembly is like a bright sword  
in the hand of a coward on the battlefield.**

However many rare books a person may have studied, if that person is afraid to speak in the midst of an assembly, all those books become as useless as a sharp sword in the hand of a coward on the battlefield.

**728. Though they may have learned many things, those who cannot speak so their words take hold  
in a good assembly have gained no benefit.**

However many books people may have learned, there is no benefit if they are unable to express their ideas in an assembly filled with people of knowledge in a way that takes hold in their minds.

**729. Those who have learned and understood but fear an assembly of the good  
are said to be lower than the unlearned.**

People who fear speaking in an assembly filled with the worthy will, however many books they may have learned, be regarded as lower than those who are unlearned.

**730. Though alive, those who fear the assembly and cannot speak what they have learned so it reaches others  
are considered equal to those who are not alive.**

Those who fear an assembly and cannot express what they have learned in a manner that draws the listeners are regarded as equal to the dead even though they are alive.
