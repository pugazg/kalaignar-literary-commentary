# 88. Discerning Enmity

**871. Enmity is contrary to good character;  
one should not desire it even as a game.**

A hostile feeling is contrary to good character, and therefore a person should not take up enmity even for amusement or play.

**872. One may make enemies of warriors whose weapon is the bow, but never  
of learned people whose weapon is the word.**

One may even enter into enmity with warriors who possess weapons. But one should not make an enemy of learned and distinguished people who possess great power of speech.

**873. Poorer than the deluded is the solitary person  
who makes enemies of many.**

A person who stands alone and seeks the enmity of many is more appropriately called an ignorant fool than an arrogant man.

**874. The world rests within the greatness of one  
who turns enemies into friends and lives with them.**

The whole world will praise the generous quality of treating even enemies as friends and living with them in that spirit.

**875. If one stands alone without an ally while facing two enemies,  
make one of those enemies an ally.**

When one's enemies are divided into two groups and one stands alone without anyone to support him, he should take one of those enemies as an ally.
