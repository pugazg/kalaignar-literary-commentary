# 16. Forbearance

**151. As the earth bears those who dig into it,  
the highest quality is to bear those who insult us.**

Like the earth that bears even those who dig pits into it, the finest quality is to bear the conduct of those who speak insultingly of us.

**152. To bear even a wrong that exceeds all bounds is good;  
to forget it forever is better still.**

Better than bearing a wrong committed beyond all bounds is to forget that wrong completely.

**153. Among forms of want, the worst want is inability to receive a guest;  
among forms of strength, the greatest strength is forbearance toward fools.**

Even in poverty, the harshest poverty is being unable to welcome a guest who has come. Likewise, among forms of strength, the greatest strength is bearing the conduct of the ignorant.

**154. If one wishes completeness never to depart,  
one must cherish forbearance and live by it.**

The world praises as a complete human being the person who is an abode of patience.

**155. The world does not count those who punish as worthy;  
it treasures those who bear wrong like gold.**

The world will value and honour like gold those who bear the wrong done to them. It will not regard in the same way those who cannot bear it and instead punish the wrongdoer.
