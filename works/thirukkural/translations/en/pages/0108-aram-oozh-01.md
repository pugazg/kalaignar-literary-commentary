# 38. Oozh

**371. The natural condition for gain gives rise to tireless effort;  
the natural condition for loss gives rise to idleness.**

The natural condition that leads to advancement gives a drive in which weariness does not show itself. The natural condition that leads to the destruction of that drive produces laziness.

**372. The natural condition of loss makes one foolish;  
the natural condition of gain expands one's knowledge when it takes hold.**

The natural condition that brings destruction produces ignorance; the natural condition that brings advancement expands knowledge accordingly.

**373. Though one may study many subtle books,  
one's own natural understanding will prevail.**

Even when a person has studied books capable of giving keen understanding, that person's natural intelligence will stand above them.

**374. The natural condition of the world is of two kinds:  
wealth is one thing, and clear understanding another.**

The natural condition of the world is divided in two. One person being wealthy and another being knowledgeable is that difference.

**375. All good things may turn bad, and bad things good,  
when wealth is to be made.**

When one sets out to do good actions, they may end in harm; and when one sets out to do harmful actions, they may end as good. This is what is called the natural condition.
