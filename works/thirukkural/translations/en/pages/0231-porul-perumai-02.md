**976. The thought, “Let us cherish the great,”  
does not arise in the awareness of the small-minded.**

The intention to honour and accept the great does not become one with the feelings of people who are small in understanding.

**977. Even distinction itself becomes a deed of transgression  
when it falls to those without worth.**

Even a distinguished position, if it comes to people unfit for it, naturally makes them act beyond proper bounds.

**978. Greatness is always humble; smallness  
adorns itself by praising itself.**

People of great character will always move among everyone with humility; people of small character, lacking good qualities, will keep praising themselves and remain swollen with pride.

**979. Greatness is freedom from arrogance; smallness  
runs all the way to the limits of arrogance.**

To remain restrained without arrogance is called greatness. To go all the way to the limits of arrogance is called smallness.

**980. Greatness hides the shortcomings of others; smallness  
keeps speaking only of faults.**

Hiding the shortcomings of others is a quality of greatness. Continually speaking only of the faults of others is a quality of smallness.
