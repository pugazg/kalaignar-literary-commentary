# 75. Fortress

**741. A fortress is an asset to those who go forth to attack  
and an asset to those who, fearing attack, seek protection.**

A fortress is useful to those who march out against an enemy; it is also useful to those who fear the enemy and seek to protect themselves.

**742. Deep water, open land, mountain,  
and forest adorned with shade make a fortress.**

A fortress is one that possesses a moat of great depth and breadth, extensive open land, a lofty mountain range, and dense forest.

**743. Height, breadth, strength and difficulty of destruction—  
books say that a fortress is formed by these four.**

The defining qualities of a fortress are that all four should be present: height, breadth, strength, and a structure that the enemy cannot destroy.

**744. A fortress has a small area that must be guarded, a vast inner space,  
and the power to destroy the strength of a fierce enemy.**

What is called a fortress must have a spacious interior while the part that has to be defended is small, and it must be capable of destroying the strength of a fierce enemy.

**745. A fortress is hard to capture, possesses stored food,  
and makes it easy for those within to stand and fight.**

A fortress is one that cannot be seized by siege, contains the food required by the soldiers and people within, and is arranged so that fighting the enemy is made easier.
