**166. The family of one who envies what is given to another  
will perish without even food or clothing.**

If a person is envious on seeing help being given to someone else, that evil quality will leave not only that person but also those dependent on him without even the means for food and clothing.

**167. The lady of wealth, seeing the envious one,  
will show him to her elder sister and leave.**

Wealth is often personified as Lakshmi, and poverty as her elder sister Moodevi. When a man is possessed by envy, the younger sister Lakshmi points him out to her elder sister and departs from him.

**168. The evil called envy destroys one's wealth  
and drives one onto an evil path.**

The evil called envy will shatter a person's wealth and leave him upon an evil path.

**169. The prosperity of an envious heart  
and the suffering of one without envy are matters to ponder.**

That the life of an envious person may appear prosperous, while the life of one without envy may be painful, is something that causes wonder and deserves thought.

**170. None have risen to greatness through envy;  
and none without envy have fallen from prosperity.**

No one has gained fame and risen because of envy, and no one has lost fame and fallen because of being free from envy.
