# 108. Baseness

**1071. The base resemble ordinary people; nowhere have we seen  
a likeness equal to theirs.**

A person may be base in character, yet present himself as though he were good. Only among human beings can two such different states be seen within the same form.

**1072. Compared with those who understand what is good, the base are fortunate:  
they have no anguish in their hearts.**

Compared with those who are always thinking about what is good and worrying over it, the base, who worry about nothing, are in one sense fortunate indeed!

**1073. The base are like the gods, for they too  
conduct themselves by doing whatever they desire.**

Because the base, like the gods who appear in the Puranas, are able to do whatever their minds desire, the two may be regarded as alike.

**1074. When a low person sees someone still lower than himself,  
he grows proud and imagines himself superior.**

Uncultured base people, when they see those of still more degraded character than themselves, become arrogant in the belief that they are better than those people.

**1075. Fear is the code of conduct of the base;  
a little more appears when desire offers them gain.**

When a situation arises in which they may obtain what they desire, base people make themselves appear as though they possess proper conduct. At other times, it is only through fear that they behave with even a small measure of discipline.
