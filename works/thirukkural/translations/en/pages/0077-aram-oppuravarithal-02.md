**216. When wealth comes to one possessed of benevolence,  
it is like a fruit-bearing tree ripening in the midst of the town.**

If wealth comes to a person with a tender heart, it becomes useful to everyone, like a flourishing tree in the middle of a town bending under the weight of ripe fruit.

**217. When wealth comes to a person of great worth,  
it is like a tree whose every part serves as medicine.**

When wealth comes to one who possesses the great quality of helping others, it is like a good tree all of whose parts can be used as medicine.

**218. Even in a season of want, those who know their duty  
do not falter in helping others.**

Those who do not weaken in helping others even when their prosperity has left them and poverty has come upon them are people of worth who know their duty.

**219. The poverty of one possessed of benevolence is simply this:  
the state of being unable to do what ought to be done.**

For a great-hearted person who regards helping others as his duty, the sign that he has become poor is simply the state in which he is unable to help others and is left unable to act.

**220. If helping others should bring loss, that loss is of such worth  
that a person should buy it even by selling himself.**

If the noble worldly conduct of helping others should bring loss, that loss is valuable enough for a person to purchase even by selling himself.
