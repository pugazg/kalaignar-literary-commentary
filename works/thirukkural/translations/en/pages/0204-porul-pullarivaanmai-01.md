# 85. Possession of Little Understanding

**841. Want of understanding is the greatest want;  
the world does not regard other wants as want to the same degree.**

A famine of understanding is the most terrible famine. The world does not regard other kinds of scarcity with anything like the same seriousness.

**842. If an ignorant man gladly gives something like a generous giver, there is no other cause;  
it is the good fortune of the person who receives it.**

There is no other reason for an ignorant person to give something happily like a generous benefactor; it should be regarded as the good fortune earned by the person receiving it.

**843. The ignorant inflict upon themselves suffering  
that even enemies would find hard to inflict.**

People without understanding cause themselves pain that even enemies would find difficult to cause them.

**844. What is ignorance? It is the arrogance that says,  
“We possess wisdom.”**

The name for the arrogance by which a person regards himself as one who possesses understanding is ignorance.

**845. Pretending knowledge in what one has not learned  
makes others doubt even what one truly knows without fault.**

When a person falsely presents himself as knowledgeable about things he has not learned, others begin to doubt even the field in which he is already genuinely skilled.
