# 44. Avoiding Faults

**431. The influence of those without arrogance, anger or meanness  
possesses a greatness worthy of pride.**

Only the influence of those who are free from arrogance, rage, and degrading conduct is worthy of respect.

**432. Avarice, honour that has lost its worth,  
and delight in what is unworthy are faults in a leader.**

Greed in the mind, a defect in one's sense of honour, and taking pleasure in tainted actions are grave harms to the qualifications required for leadership.

**433. Those who are ashamed of blame regard even a millet-sized fault  
as though it were as large as a palmyra tree.**

People who are ashamed of blame will regard even a fault as small as a millet seed as though it were as large as a palmyra tree, and will protect themselves by not committing it.

**434. Guard against fault as the thing to be guarded against;  
fault itself is the enemy that brings destruction.**

Because committing a fault can become an enemy capable of bringing ruin, one must make not committing faults itself the aim.

**435. The life of one who does not guard beforehand against what is coming  
will perish like straw placed before fire.**

The life of one who does not remain alert beforehand and avoid a wrongful act will burn away like a haystack standing before a fire.
