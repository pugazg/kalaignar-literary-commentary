**946. As pleasure remains with one who eats knowing moderation,  
disease remains with the one who eats excessively.**

It is natural that one who eats in moderation lives in bodily health, while one who eats excessively becomes subject to disease.

**947. If one eats greatly without knowing or examining the measure of hunger,  
diseases will come without measure.**

If one eats excessively without knowing the measure of hunger and without examining it, diseases too will come without measure.

**948. Examine the disease, examine its cause, examine the way to relieve it,  
and act in the fitting manner.**

What is the disease? What is its cause? What is the way to cure it? These must be properly examined before treatment is given. This applies not only to bodily disease but also to social disease.

**949. The learned physician should act after considering  
the patient's measure, the measure of the disease, and the proper time.**

The physician must act only after considering the patient's age, the nature of the disease, and the proper time for treatment.

**950. Patient, healer, medicine, and the one who stays nearby to assist—  
medicine is constituted by these four.**

The medical method consists of four: the patient, the physician, the medicine, and the person who remains nearby and gives support.
