**696. Know the disposition, consider the time, leave out what causes dislike,  
and say what is wanted in a way that makes it desired.**

One must understand a person's state of mind, choose the proper time, set aside things that may cause dislike, and say only what is desirable in a manner that will make that person desire it.

**697. Say what is useful, and even if asked,  
always leave what is useless unsaid.**

Even when someone asks with interest, one should speak only of what is useful and leave useless matters unsaid.

**698. Do not despise those in authority as younger or as relatives;  
conduct yourself with respect for the standing they possess.**

Those holding responsibility in government must not be spoken of with contempt on the ground that they are younger than us or related to us in a particular way. One must conduct oneself with propriety suited to the eminence they have attained.

**699. Those of unwavering judgment do not, thinking, “We have been accepted,”  
do what cannot be accepted.**

People of clear understanding will not, emboldened by the thought that the government has accepted them, undertake actions that cannot be accepted.

**700. Taking long familiarity as a license to do what is improper  
will end in harm.**

Taking for oneself the right to do improper things merely because of a long period of close familiarity will only end in harm.
