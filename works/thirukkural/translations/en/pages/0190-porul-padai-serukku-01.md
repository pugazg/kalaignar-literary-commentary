# 78. Martial Pride

**771. Enemies, do not stand before my leader; many who stood before him  
have become memorial stones.**

A warrior on the battlefield cries out, “Enemies, do not stand against my leader; many who opposed him have become memorial stones.”

**772. Better to bear the spear that missed a mighty elephant  
than the arrow that struck a forest hare.**

Even when a warrior aims at a powerful elephant and misses the mark, that is finer than aiming at a weak hare and bringing it down.

**773. Fearless courage before an enemy is called great manliness; when that enemy suffers,  
going to his aid is the sharpest edge of that manliness.**

Courage that does not fear an enemy is praised as great manliness. When that enemy encounters suffering, helping to relieve it is praised as the highest point of manliness.

**774. Having thrown the spear in his hand at an elephant, the warrior who comes seeking another  
laughs as he pulls out the spear lodged in his own body.**

A warrior who has thrown the spear in his hand at an elephant and therefore seeks another spear to continue the battle rejoices on seeing a spear lodged in his own chest, pulls it out, and turns again to face the enemy.

**775. If the eye that stared at the enemy's thrown spear closes and blinks when the spear is cast,  
is that not retreat for a fierce warrior?**

On the battlefield, even blinking when the spear hurled by an enemy comes flying is comparable to turning one's back and running away.
