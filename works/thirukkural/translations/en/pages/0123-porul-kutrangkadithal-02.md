**436. If a leader first removes his own faults and then sees the faults of others,  
what fault can there be in him?**

What fault can come to a leader who first removes the shortcomings within himself and only afterwards sees and speaks of the shortcomings of others?

**437. The wealth of a miser who hoards without doing what ought to be done  
will perish instead of rising.**

The wealth of a miser that is saved up without being used for good works will go to waste without yielding any benefit.

**438. The clinging avarice called attachment  
is not a fault to be counted merely among other faults.**

Among all faults, the one regarded as a singularly great fault is attachment to accumulating wealth and living without giving anything to anyone.

**439. Never at any time admire yourself;  
do not desire an action that yields no good.**

At no time should one engage, out of self-importance that thinks highly of oneself, in actions that bring no good.

**440. If one can fulfil what one loves without letting enemies know that love,  
the designs of those enemies will fail.**

If one can fulfil one's desire without allowing enemies to know that desire, the intentions of those enemies will fail to succeed.
