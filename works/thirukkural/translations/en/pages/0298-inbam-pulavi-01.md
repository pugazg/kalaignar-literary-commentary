# 131. Lovers' Quarrel

**1301. Do not embrace him; sulk a little,  
so that we may see for a while the suffering that comes upon him.**

Hold back from embracing him and remain estranged for a little while, so that we may see him suffer from the sickness of distress caused by your sulking.

**1302. A lovers' quarrel should be measured like salt in food;  
if prolonged even a little too much, it becomes excess.**

The time between sulking and reunion should be kept to a proper measure, like the salt added to food. If that period is prolonged, it becomes like too much salt in the food.

**1303. To leave one who sulks without embracing and ending the quarrel  
is to add another sickness to one already exhausted by suffering.**

To leave the sulking of one who is already estranged without ending it through an embrace is to make someone already suffering undergo still more suffering and pain.

**1304. To fail to understand and comfort one who is sulking  
is like cutting a withered vine at its root.**

If one stays away without showing affection to the one who has become estranged in a lovers' quarrel, it is like cutting at its base a vine that has already withered.

**1305. The sulking that arises in the hearts of flower-eyed women  
is an added beauty to worthy and loving men.**

It is the lovers' quarrel that blossoms in the hearts of flower-eyed women that adds beauty to good lovers of worthy character.
