**146. Enmity, evil, fear and blame—these four  
will never leave one who transgresses another's home.**

Enmity, evil, fear and blame—these four never leave the man who thinks of behaving improperly toward another man's wife.

**147. He alone is called one who lives domestic life by aram  
who does not seek the womanhood of another man's wife.**

Only the man who does not go seeking sexual pleasure from another man's wife is said to conduct domestic life on the path of aram.

**148. The great manliness of not looking at another man's wife  
is not merely aram for the worthy; it is lofty conduct.**

The great quality of not looking at another man's wife with lustful intent is not merely a path of aram; it is also the summit of good conduct.

**149. Who are worthy of goodness in this sea-girt world?  
Those who do not touch the shoulders of the woman who belongs to another.**

Those who do not touch the shoulder of another man's wife are the ones worthy of attaining the greatness of this sea-surrounded world.

**150. Even if one does not act within the bounds of aram,  
it is good not to desire the womanhood of another man's wife.**

Acting out of desire for another man's wife is more evil than the act of one who does not walk on the path of aram.
