**1326. Sweeter than eating is the digestion of what has been eaten;  
in love, sweeter than union is lovers' quarrelling.**

There is a certain pleasure in the digestion of food that has been eaten, more than in eating the food itself. In the same way, for lovers there is a pleasure in lovers' quarrelling itself, more than in bodily union.

**1327. In lovers' quarrelling, the one who is defeated is the one who wins;  
that truth will be seen when they unite again.**

In the sweet battle called lovers' quarrelling, the one who is defeated is the one who has won. This truth is realized when the quarrel ends and the lovers come together in joy.

**1328. Shall we not, through another lovers' quarrel, obtain once again  
the delight born in union when the forehead breaks into sweat?**

Can we not obtain once again, through another lovers' quarrel, the pleasure that arises from coming together until sweat beads upon the forehead?

**1329. Let the radiant-faced beauty quarrel with me; while I plead with her,  
may the night itself stretch on still longer.**

Let the radiant-faced beauty engage in lovers' quarrelling; and so that I may enjoy the pleasure of standing before her and pleading with her to end that quarrel, may the hours of the night stretch on still longer.

**1330. Lovers' quarrelling is a delight of love; and greater still is its delight  
when, after it, the two come together and embrace.**

Lovers' quarrelling means remaining apart after becoming sweetly angry with one another. When the two come together again, it helps them drink more deeply of the pleasure of love. Therefore, lovers' quarrelling itself is an act of delight.
