# 123. Lamenting at Evening

**1226. I did not know, when my beloved had not left me,  
that evening could inflict such a sickness.**

When my beloved remained with me without leaving, I did not know that evening could bring so much suffering.

**1227. This sickness buds in the morning, ripens through the day,  
and blossoms in the evening.**

Love is a sickness that forms as a bud in the morning, matures throughout the day and blossoms in the evening.

**1228. The cowherd's flute comes as a messenger for the fire-like evening;  
its sound is like a weapon that kills me.**

The sound of the cowherd's flute seems to come as a messenger announcing the evening that burns me like embers because of separation from my beloved; does it not sound in my ears like the sound of a weapon meant to kill me?

**1229. When the mind-bewildering evening spreads,  
it seems to bewilder the whole town and plunge it into suffering.**

The evening that confuses my understanding seems to me to bewilder this entire town and immerse it in suffering.

**1230. Thinking of the beloved who has gone to earn wealth,  
my life, which would not perish, now perishes in this bewildering evening.**

Thinking of the beloved who has gone away to earn wealth, my life—which had not faded away—now fades in this bewildering evening.
