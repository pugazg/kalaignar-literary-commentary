The passages below stand as examples of the distinction with which Kalaignar takes the word *puththelir* (`புத்தேளிர்`) as having arisen from *puthumai*—newness—and assigns it a meaning that clears away perplexity.

**58. If women obtain the one they ought to obtain,  
they will gain the great distinction of a new world.**

If a woman obtains as her husband a man endowed with good character, domestic life will become for her a new world of great distinction.

**213. Even in the new world and in this one,  
it is hard to find anything better than helpfulness.**

It is difficult to find, either in today's world or in the new world to come, a quality better than `ஒப்புரவு`—the quality of helping others.

**1323. Can there be another new world beyond sulking  
in the heart of lovers joined in affection like water with earth?**

Can there be any other new world beyond the lovers' quarrel that arises between those united in love like water mingling with earth?

**234. Even the new world will not honour only the learned,  
leaving aside those whose fame extends across the earth.**

Even the new world to come will not ignore the great people who, giving up self-interest, earned fame in today's world and honour only those endowed with intellectual ability.

Among these, we can also see that the final passage shines as a commentary offering an entirely fresh and valuable idea.
