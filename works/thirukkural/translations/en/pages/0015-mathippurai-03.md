Although Valluvar taught aram by following an incomparable method, it would be more accurate to say not merely that people lacked the capacity to understand it because of differences in their levels of knowledge, but that they were made incapable of understanding it. That is why, beginning with the ten earliest commentaries on the Kural, so many commentaries have appeared down to the present day. Among them, the lucid commentary that has arisen with the ability to meet the needs of our time properly is Kalaignar's commentary.

## 2. Worship

The idea of God could not have arisen at the time human beings first appeared, or even in the period close to it. That idea could have occurred to them only when their knowledge developed and they began to think. Early human beings may have begun to think that there must be someone who created the world they saw and the many kinds of things found within it, and that it would be good if someone existed to hold all of them together, protect them, and administer them in an orderly way. The result must have been the emergence of many different ideas about God, which then multiplied and flourished. Later, with greater clarity of knowledge and with the aid of rational inquiry, the idea of God began to weaken. Further, because God's existence has not been demonstrated and established, contrary views about God have been gaining strength and spreading. Although the Thirukkural contains a chapter under the title *Invocation to God*, the name “God” itself does not occur in that chapter, nor is the chapter framed so as to accommodate many of the ideas about God expressed today. Kalaignar's commentary has not failed to play its part in this matter. This can be understood through the commentaries he has written from an impartial standpoint for the first ten Kurals.
