# 48. Knowing Strength

**471. Weigh the strength of the action, your own strength,  
the enemy's strength, and the strength of both sides' allies; then act.**

One should engage in an action only after examining the strength of the action itself, one's own strength, the enemy's strength, and the strength of those who stand in support on both sides.

**472. For those who know what can be done, know what must be known,  
and remain fixed in that understanding, nothing is unattainable.**

When undertaking an action, if one examines and understands everything concerning it and then makes the effort, there is nothing that cannot be achieved.

**473. Many, not knowing the measure of their own strength,  
have been driven by enthusiasm and broken down midway.**

There are many who, without knowing the measure of their own strength, have begun an action in a rush of emotion and come to ruin midway.

**474. One who neither conducts himself fittingly nor knows his measure,  
but magnifies himself, will quickly perish.**

Those who do not respect others, do not understand their own strength, and keep advertising themselves as great will quickly come to ruin.

**475. Even a cart loaded with peacock feathers  
will have its axle break if the load is piled too high.**

Even peacock feathers, if loaded in excess, acquire enough weight to break the axle of a cart.
