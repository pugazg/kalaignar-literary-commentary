**736. The foremost among countries is one that knows no ruin  
and whose wealth does not diminish even when ruin occurs.**

A country that knows no destruction of any kind and, even if some damage does occur, remains prosperous enough to set it right is the foremost among countries.

**737. Two waters, a well-formed mountain, incoming water,  
and a strong fortress are the limbs of a country.**

The two waters called river and sea, a lofty and extended mountain range, the incoming water that is rain, and a powerful fortress are excellent constituent parts of a country.

**738. Freedom from disease, wealth, abundant yield, happiness and security—  
these five are called the ornaments of a country.**

For the people, a life free from disease, abundance of produce, economic prosperity, a state of happiness, and proper security are the five things described as the beauty of a country.

**739. A country is one that possesses wealth without having to seek it;  
a land that must seek wealth is not a country.**

Countries that naturally possess every kind of resource are finer than countries that must undertake unceasing effort in order to obtain prosperity.

**740. Even when all these have been attained, there is no benefit  
in a country that lacks good government.**

Even if a country possesses every kind of resource, none of it will be of any use if a good government is not established there.
