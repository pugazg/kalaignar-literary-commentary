**686. Learned, unafraid before a hostile gaze, able to speak so the message reaches its mark,  
and able to know what the occasion requires—that is an envoy.**

A fine envoy is one who is learned, does not fear even the fiery gaze of the enemy, speaks in a way that takes hold in the mind, and understands at the proper time what must be understood.

**687. He is foremost who knows the duty, considers the time, knows the place,  
and speaks after thinking clearly.**

The finest envoy is one who knows the duty that must be performed, chooses the proper time and place for it, thinks clearly about what must be said, and then speaks.

**688. Purity, support and courage—these three—  
are the qualities of one who speaks the message along the path of truth.**

Courage, support, and purity of conduct are the three things required of an envoy.

**689. One who conveys one ruler's message to another must be firm,  
with a mouth that does not slip into blameworthy words.**

An envoy who carries the view of one government to another government must possess the firmness not to utter words tainted by fault even through a slip of the tongue.

**690. Even if it brings his own end, to hold nothing back and bring benefit to his leader  
is the work of a trustworthy envoy.**

Even if the task would bring destruction upon himself, the person who does not become afraid on that account and carries out his duty with firmness is a trustworthy envoy to his leader.
