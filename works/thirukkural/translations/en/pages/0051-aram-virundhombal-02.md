**86. One who tends the departing guest and waits for the one to come  
will be welcomed as a good guest by those in the heaven of fame.**

Even as one serves the guests who have come and sends them on their way, if one stands eagerly awaiting still more guests who may arrive, those who dwell in the heaven of fame will welcome and honour that person as a good guest.

**87. There is no fixed measure to the fruit of the sacrifice called hospitality;  
it takes its measure from the worth of the guest.**

By considering the distinction of the person who has come as a guest, hospitality itself may be regarded as a sacrifice.

**88. Those who do not undertake the sacrifice called hospitality will lament,  
“We guarded our wealth with care, only to lose it.”**

When people who have gathered and preserved wealth lose it, they will lament that it was never put to use for the sacrifice called hospitality.

**89. Even amid possessions, failure to cherish hospitality is poverty;  
such folly belongs to the ignorant.**

People without the understanding to welcome and honour guests will be regarded as poverty-stricken, however much money they may possess.

**90. The anicham flower wilts when smelled;  
a guest wilts when received with a face turned away.**

The flower called anicham can wither as soon as it is smelled. In the same way, a guest will wilt if welcomed with even a slight frown.
