# 132. Nuances of Lovers' Quarrel

**1311. All women feast upon you with their eyes as though you were common to them;  
I will not embrace your broad, faithless chest.**

Because every woman looks upon your broad chest as something common to all and devours it with her eyes, I, the maiden, will not embrace that chest of yours which has strayed from the path of fidelity.

**1312. While we were sulking, he sneezed, knowing that I would give up the quarrel  
and say, “May you live long.”**

While we were in a lovers' quarrel, he sneezed, thinking that I would abandon the sulking and bless him by saying, “May you live long.”

**1313. Even if I wear flowers gathered from a branch, she grows angry,  
saying, “You wore them to show some other woman.”**

Even if I merely gather flowers blooming on a branch and wear them, she becomes angry and says that I wore them only so that I could show them to another woman.

**1314. When I said, “I love you more than anyone,” she began to sulk, asking,  
“More than whom? More than whom?”**

Even when I naturally said, “I love you more than anyone,” my beloved took it wrongly and began to quarrel, asking me again and again, “More than whom? More than whom?”

**1315. When I said, “We shall not part in this birth,”  
her eyes filled with tears.**

The moment I said, “We shall not be separated in this birth,” my beloved's eyes filled with tears as she asked, “Then is there another birth? Are you saying that we will be separated then?”
