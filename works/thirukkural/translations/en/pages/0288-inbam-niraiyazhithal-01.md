# 126. Loss of Restraint

**1251. The axe of passion breaks down the door called restraint,  
whose bolt is modesty.**

The desire of love turns into an axe and smashes the very door of mental restraint that has been bolted with modesty.

**1252. Is there anything so merciless as love?  
Even at midnight it rules and torments my heart.**

The thing called the desire of love has no compassion at all; for even at midnight it exercises dominion over my heart and tosses it about.

**1253. I may try to hide my love, but without warning  
it appears like a sneeze that cannot be restrained.**

However much one tries to suppress it, a sneeze breaks out beyond one's control; the feeling of love is the same—however much I hide it, it reveals itself.

**1254. I believed myself a woman of restraint; yet my love  
escapes concealment and appears openly before the assembly.**

I believed that I possessed firmness of mind; but my love goes beyond even what I try to hide and becomes visible before everyone.

**1255. The greatness of not following one who has left in hostility  
is unknown to those afflicted by the sickness of love.**

Those suffering from the sickness of love do not possess the restraint to regard the beloved who has left them as an enemy and refuse to follow after him.
