# 116. Inability to Bear Separation

**1156. If he is hard-hearted enough to speak of separation,  
it is vain to hope that he will return and show love.**

It is futile to long for the return and affection of one hard-hearted enough to say, “I shall go and return,” and then leave.

**1157. Will not the bangles slipping from my forearm proclaim  
that my lord has left me?**

The bangles that loosen and fall from the joint of my forearm will announce to the whole town that my beloved has left me!

**1158. It is painful to live in a town without one who understands us;  
more painful still is separation from the beloved.**

To live in a town where there is no one who understands us and shows affection is painful; more painful than that is to live separated from the beloved who is dear to us.

**1159. Fire burns only when touched; can it, like the sickness of love,  
burn when one is apart?**

Fire has no state like the sickness of love, which burns both body and heart when two people are separated without seeing or touching one another. Fire burns when touched; this burns in separation!

**1160. Many may endure the hard thing, remove their suffering, bear separation,  
and continue to live afterward—but can I?**

There may be many who consent to their beloved's departure, overcome the suffering it causes, endure the separation and remain alive afterward; but I?
