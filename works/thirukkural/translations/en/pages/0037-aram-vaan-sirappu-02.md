**16. Unless drops fall from the sky,  
it is hard even for green grass to show its head upon the earth.**

Unless raindrops fall from the sky, it is difficult for green grass even to raise its head from the soil.

**17. Even the vast sea would lose its own nature  
if the cloud that draws from it should fail to give back.**

Only when seawater becomes vapour, turns into cloud, and falls as rain into that sea will even the sea remain without drying up. In the same way, those who rise to eminence and fame from human society must be of use to that very society if the society is to live.

**18. If the sky goes dry, even here no splendid festivals or worship  
will proceed for those said to dwell in heaven.**

When the sky itself fails, what festivals can there be for those who are said to live in that sky? What worship can there be?

**19. Charity and ascetic practice cannot endure in this vast world  
if the sky does not give rain.**

If rain fails in this great world, it becomes an obstacle both to charity performed for the sake of others and to fasting undertaken for oneself.

**20. If the world cannot exist without water,  
neither can conduct endure for anyone without rain.**

Since there is a condition in which conduct itself may deteriorate if there is no rain in the world, we must recognize the indispensability of water and act accordingly.

> Editorial review note: Kural 17 and Kalaignar's accompanying social analogy remain aligned with the already released Part 001 example. Kural 20 retains Kalaignar's explicit warning that **conduct itself may deteriorate** when there is no rain. Neither is normalized toward an outside interpretation.
