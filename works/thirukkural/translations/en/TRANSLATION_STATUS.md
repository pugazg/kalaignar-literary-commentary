## Source-check refinements in the final batch, scans 77–84

The final pass made source-supported verse/commentary separation corrections where first-pass wording had absorbed Kalaignar's explanatory prose too early:

1. **Kurals 216, 217 and 219** — the verses no longer import the commentary's specific “tender heart,” *oppuravu* / helping-others explanation; those details remain in Kalaignar's commentary.
2. **Kural 221** — the verse now says **the poor**, while Kalaignar's commentary separately explains them as those who have nothing.
3. **Kural 222** — the verse now preserves `கொளல்தீது` as **receiving is bad**, while the commentary separately uses the greatness/littleness contrast.
4. **Kural 224** — tightened to **being moved to pity** until the supplicant's smiling face is seen.
5. **Kural 226** — the verse uses **storehouse**; Kalaignar's commentary separately retains the stronger **treasury** image.
6. **Kural 229** — the verse now keeps the compact act of one who has amassed eating alone; Kalaignar's commentary separately explains hoarding from fear that giving will reduce one's stock.
7. **Kural 233** — corrected the first-pass modifier so **unequalled** belongs to fame rather than to the world.
8. **Kural 239** — removed the commentary-derived phrase **life called fame** from the verse; it remains explicitly in Kalaignar's commentary.
9. **Kural 246** — replaced the misleading first-pass **meaning and duty** with **bereft of substance and forgetful**; the commentary separately states forgotten duty.
10. **Kural 247** — restored the verse's **that world / this world** contrast while keeping Kalaignar's distinct renunciant-life interpretation in the commentary.
11. **Kurals 251–252** — clarified another being's flesh and the possession-of-wealth / possession-of-compassion parallel without changing Kalaignar's flesh-eating argument.

Earlier source-check corrections remain in force, including Kural 149's restored actor, Kural 188's less over-explicit verse, Kural 196's removal of commentary-derived “for gain,” Kural 210's **hard to ruin**, and Kural 214's separation of the verse from the helping-others commentary.
