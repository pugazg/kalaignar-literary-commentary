## Protected Oozh decision

Chapter 38 is deliberately **Oozh**, not Fate or Destiny. Kalaignar repeatedly explains its operative sense with `இயற்கை நிலை`, retained as **natural condition**. The editorial review preserves that language in Kurals 371–380 and does not normalize Kalaignar's explanation into a conventional fate/destiny doctrine.

This is the most important Part 006 glossary decision.

## Recurring terminology reconciliation

- `பொருள்` remains **Porul** as the structural book label.
- `அரசியல்` is **Statecraft** as the section-level metadata label for the supplied main-body pages.
- `இறை / மன்னன் / ஆட்சியாளர்` are translated contextually as **ruler / king / leader**, while Kalaignar's commentary-specific **government / administration** language is retained when he explicitly frames the explanation institutionally.
- `அறம்` remains **Aram** in structural/source-sensitive uses and contextual English in prose.
- `கல்வி` is **Learning** and `கல்லாமை` **Lack of Learning** at chapter level. The source's direct comparisons in chapter 41 are not softened.
- `கேள்வி` is **Listening** at chapter level; **knowledge gained through listening** is retained where Kalaignar's commentary makes the epistemic sense explicit.
- `அறிவு / அறிவுடைமை` use **wisdom / understanding / knowledge** according to syntax and context; chapter 43 is controlled as **Possession of Wisdom**.
- `குற்றம்` is **fault** by default in chapter 44; blame, wrongdoing and wrongful act remain contextual where Kalaignar's sentence requires them.
- `பெரியோர்` remains context-sensitive as **the great / great people / people greater than oneself**, without automatically converting it into an age category.
- `இனம்` in chapter 46 is handled through **company / group / association** according to context. The chapter title is **Avoiding Base Company**. This avoids importing a racial or biological sense not supported by Kalaignar's commentary, which repeatedly explains the concept through the group with which a person associates.

## Source-sensitive decisions retained

### Eradication of Desire / Oozh

- Kurals 366–370 retain desire/greed distinctions where Kalaignar's commentary uses stronger `பேராசை` language.
- Kurals 371–380 retain **natural condition** throughout Kalaignar's explanation.
- Kural 380 keeps the contrast between natural condition and an attempted artificial/alternative condition.

### Excellence of Kingship

- Kural 381 retains army, people, resources, ministers, allies and fortress, with Kalaignar's commentary separately expanding these into institutional qualities.
- Kural 382 commentary retains **qualifications proper to government**.
- Kural 385 retains Kalaignar's explicit revenue, treasury, safeguarding and planned-expenditure framing.
- Kurals 386–390 retain approachable government, justice, endurance of harsh criticism, compassion, impartiality and protection of the people.

### Learning / Lack of Learning

- Kural 393 retains the direct **eyes / sores** comparison.
- Kural 398 commentary retains Kalaignar's **seven times seven generations** formulation rather than silently reducing it to the compressed verse's seven generations.
- Kural 402 retains the source's breast comparison without sanitizing it.
- Kural 406 commentary retains **walking corpses**.
- Kural 407 retains the adorned clay-doll image.
- Kurals 409–410 retain the source's high/low and human/animal comparisons rather than replacing them with modernized social language.

### Listening / Possession of Wisdom

- Kurals 411–413 retain the repeated **food of the ear** image.
- Kurals 414–415 retain the walking-stick / slippery-ground image.
- Kural 423 commentary retains Kalaignar's insistence that one should not accept a statement merely because someone says it, but should examine and become clear about what is true.
- Kural 424 retains both clear expression and the ability to discern subtle meaning in others' words.
- Kural 425 retains Kalaignar's equal treatment of pleasure and suffering.

### Avoiding Faults

- Kural 433 retains the millet / palmyra scale comparison.
- Kural 435 retains the haystack-before-fire image.
- Kural 436 retains the leadership framing in Kalaignar's commentary.
- Kural 438 retains attachment to accumulating wealth and refusing to give as the singularly great fault described by Kalaignar.
- Kural 440 retains the strategic concealment of one's desire from enemies.

### Seeking the Support of the Great

- Kurals 441–445 retain friendship, support, counsellors-as-eyes and the ruler's need for learned advisers.
- Kurals 447–448 retain the importance of people who **rebuke and correct** a ruler rather than softening them into agreeable advisers.
- Kural 449 retains Kalaignar's pillar/building and capital/trade analogy.
- Kural 450 retains the claim that abandoning good people can be more harmful than acquiring many enemies.

### Avoiding Base Company

- Kural 451 retains Kalaignar's great/base and like-joins-like framing.
- Kural 452 retains the soil/water analogy and Kalaignar's explanation through the character of the group one joins.
- Kurals 453–459 retain mind versus company/group distinctions rather than importing a biological meaning for `இனம்`.
- Kural 460 retains the closing contrast between good company as support and bad company as suffering.

## Editorial changes made in this review

The source-check had already produced faithful, intelligible English. This editorial pass therefore made **no substantive wording changes** to the 21 page bodies. The deliberate decision was to avoid stylistic polishing that could weaken Kalaignar's directness or alter source-sensitive interpretation.

The review did make the following editorial decisions at the control layer:

1. confirmed chapter 38 as **Oozh** and `இயற்கை நிலை` as **natural condition**;
2. established the main-body controlled English headings for chapters 39–46 from the supplied context;
3. confirmed **Porul** and **Statecraft** as the structural metadata vocabulary used for this transition;
4. confirmed that chapter 46 `இனம்` should be handled contextually through **company / group / association** in this supplied commentary rather than mechanically translated as race or kind;
5. retained all source-supported direct social comparisons and institutional/governance vocabulary without euphemistic modernization.
