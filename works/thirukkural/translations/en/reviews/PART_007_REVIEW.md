## Protected source-sensitive decisions

### Kural 543 — `அந்தணர்` through Kalaignar's `அறவோர்`

This is the most important Part 007 interpretation-control decision.

The Kural contains `அந்தணர் நூற்கும்`, but Kalaignar's commentary explicitly explains the phrase through `அறவோர் நூல்களுக்கும்`. The English therefore retains **“the books of the virtuous”** in both verse-level interpretation and commentary context. The editorial review does **not** replace this with a caste-specific conventional gloss merely because another Thirukkural tradition might do so.

This treatment is source-specific to Kalaignar's supplied commentary and does not erase other source contexts in the repository where a distinct Tamil social term is explicitly used and translated differently.

### Kural 520 — working people

Kalaignar's commentary explicitly reframes the verse through the condition of **working people** and the daily responsibility of those who govern. The editorial review preserves:

- “the hearts of working people do not wither”;
- the continuing prosperity of the world while workers do not wither;
- the obligation of those who govern to examine their condition every day and act accordingly.

This framing is not reduced to generic administrative efficiency.

### Governance chapters 55–57

Kalaignar's commentary repeatedly speaks institutionally through **government, citizens, good governance, justice, tyranny, public resources, productive work, occupations, punishment and fear**. These terms are retained rather than mechanically reverting every occurrence to king/ruler language from the compressed Kural alone.

In particular:

- Kural 542 retains citizens' dependence on **good governance** alongside the rain analogy;
- Kural 543 retains **just rule of a government** as the foundation for the books of the virtuous and righteous action;
- Kural 547 retains justice itself protecting a government that does not deviate from it;
- Kural 548 retains the self-destruction of a government that proceeds with pomp but without examined justice;
- Kural 549 retains government's duty to protect citizens and punish wrongdoing;
- Kural 554 retains loss of financial foundations and public regard under tyrannical rule;
- Kural 555 retains citizens' tears as a weapon capable of destroying oppressive government;
- Kural 559 retains water-storage infrastructure / resource use as Kalaignar's explanation rather than replacing it with an unrelated conventional gloss;
- Kural 560 retains productive work and important occupations declining when government fails to protect the country properly;
- Kurals 561–564 retain punishment, moderation, fear and tyrannical government as Kalaignar frames them.

## Source imagery retained

The editorial review deliberately preserves the source-supported imagery rather than replacing it with abstract moral prose:

- the cart axle breaking even under too many peacock feathers;
- the ram drawing back before charging;
- the crane waiting patiently and striking when the time comes;
- the crocodile strong in water and weak once outside it;
- the chariot/sea and ship/land contrast;
- the elephant trapped in mire and killed even by foxes;
- a tank full of water but without banks;
- the crow calling its circle and sharing what it has found;
- timely rain and rich harvest;
- weeds removed so that a crop may flourish;
- citizens' tears becoming a weapon against oppressive rule;
- a frightening demon-like appearance for inaccessible, forbidding wealth.

## Recurring terminology reconciliation

- `அரசு` in Kalaignar's commentary remains **government** where he frames the issue institutionally.
- `மன்னன் / வேந்தன்` in compressed Kural lines remains contextual as **ruler / king** where the verse itself requires it.
- `செங்கோன்மை` is controlled as **Just Rule**; running prose may use **good governance / justice / just rule** according to Kalaignar's sentence.
- `கொடுங்கோன்மை` is controlled as **Tyrannical Rule**; prose may use **tyranny / cruel government / harsh rule** when directly supported.
- `சுற்றம் / சுற்றத்தார்` are handled through **kindred / relatives / one's own people / circle** according to context. The chapter title is **Embracing Kindred**.
- `பொச்சாப்பு / மறதி` are handled through **forgetfulness**; chapter 54 is **Freedom from Forgetfulness**.
- `தெரிந்து / தேர்ந்து / நாடி` across chapters 47, 51 and 52 remain related but are not mechanically forced into one English verb; **deliberate / examine / know / choose** follow the immediate source context.
- `இடம்` and `காலம்` remain **place** and **time** in the controlled chapter headings, preserving the repeated strategic framing.
- `வலி` in chapter 48 is **strength**, including one's own strength, an opponent's strength, the undertaking's strength and allies' strength.
