## Main-body editorial review — scan 303

The controlled structural identity remains:

**Inbam — Wedded Love — Joy of Lovers' Quarrel**

This carries forward the reviewed Part 014 treatment of `கற்பியல்` → **Wedded Love** and `ஊடலுவகை` → **Joy of Lovers' Quarrel**.

Kurals **1326–1330** and Kalaignar's commentary were reviewed for readability, rhetorical force, imagery, gender specificity, Kural/commentary separation, and consistency with the preceding chapter treatment.

The source-checked English is retained without a manufactured editorial rewrite.

### Kural 1326

The food/digestion comparison is retained concretely. Kalaignar's commentary explicitly contrasts the pleasure of digestion with eating and then bodily union with lovers' quarrelling. The English therefore keeps **digestion**, **bodily union**, and **lovers' quarrelling** rather than smoothing the image into a generic statement about anticipation or reconciliation.

### Kural 1327

Kalaignar calls `ஊடல்` an **இனிய போர்** — a sweet battle — in which the one who loses is the one who wins, with the truth understood when the lovers come together again in joy. The English retains this paradox and does not recast the quarrel as hostility or moral failure.

### Kural 1328

The forehead-sweat image remains bodily and explicit. The commentary identifies the pleasure arising from coming together until sweat beads on the forehead and rhetorically asks whether another quarrel may yield that pleasure again. The English keeps the rhetorical-question form and does not euphemize the intimacy.

### Kural 1329

Kalaignar's `ஒளி முகத்தழகி` is retained as the radiant-faced beauty. The speaker's pleading with her to end the quarrel and his wish that the night stretch longer are preserved as part of the pleasure of reconciliation, without inserting an external romantic interpretation.

### Kural 1330

Kalaignar explicitly explains `ஊடல்` as lovers remaining apart after becoming sweetly angry with one another and says that reunion allows them to drink more deeply of the pleasure of love. The English retains **sweetly angry**, **come together again**, and **drink more deeply of the pleasure of love**, preserving Kalaignar's rhetorical and sensual character rather than flattening the conclusion into a generic maxim.
