## Recurring terminology reconciliation

- `அறம்` remains **Aram** in structural labels and is translated contextually in prose. Existing `aram`, `path of aram`, and related source-close forms are retained where they preserve the Tamil sentence without forcing a single English equivalent.
- `தவம்` is controlled as **Ascetic Practice** at chapter level. In verse/commentary prose, **ascetic practice**, **ascetic discipline**, and **observance** remain context-sensitive rather than being mechanically flattened to one phrase.
- `அருள்` remains **compassion**, distinct from `அன்பு` / love. Kural 285 preserves both concepts rather than merging them.
- `வாய்மை` is **Truthfulness** as chapter title. Where the Tamil specifically uses `பொய்யாமை`, **freedom from falsehood / not lying** is retained rather than mechanically replacing it with the chapter title.
- `வெகுளாமை` is **Freedom from Anger**. `சினம்` remains anger in the verses/commentary; Kalaignar's stronger/weaker social framing in Kurals 301–302 is preserved in commentary.
- `இன்னா செய்யாமை` is **Not Causing Pain** as chapter title. Running prose may use **pain**, **suffering**, or **harm** according to the immediate Tamil wording and sentence function.
- `கொல்லாமை` is **Non-killing**. The hyphenated form is retained consistently as the chapter-level project term.
- `பற்று` and related forms are rendered through **attachment / attach / cling** according to syntax. The repeated attachment language in chapter 35 is deliberately preserved rather than stylistically diversified away.
- `துறவு` is **Renunciation** and `துறவி / துறவறம்` use **renunciant / renunciant life** where context requires. Do not replace Kalaignar's specific renunciant-life interpretations with a generalized spiritual vocabulary.
- `மெய்ப்பொருள்` is rendered through **true reality / true meaning** according to context. The chapter heading `மெய்யுணர்தல்` is controlled as **Realizing Truth**.
- `பிறப்பு` remains **birth** unless Kalaignar's commentary itself explicitly says `அடுத்த பிறப்பு` / another birth. The project does not silently import `rebirth` or a doctrinal reincarnation reading where the source does not state it.
- `அவா` remains **desire** in chapter 37; Kalaignar's commentary at Kural 364 specifically uses `பேராசை`, retained as **greed** there.

## Source-sensitive decisions retained

The review explicitly protects the following source-check decisions and Kalaignar-specific readings.

### Abstaining from Flesh / Ascetic Practice

- **Kural 257** retains flesh as **the wound of another living being**.
- **Kural 259** retains the thousand-sacrifice comparison; Kalaignar's commentary separately mentions substances such as ghee being placed into fire.
- **Kural 261 commentary** retains Kalaignar's explanation of ascetic practice through endurance and not harming any living being.
- **Kural 266 commentary** retains self-control, the path of love, and patience in enduring suffering.
- **Kural 268 commentary** retains rejection of attachment to **“one's own life”** and the pride of **“I.”**
- **Kural 270** retains the source-checked verse **“few practise ascetic discipline”** while Kalaignar's commentary separately explains the contrast through firmness of mind.

### Improper Conduct / Not Stealing

- **Kural 271** retains the five elements within the deceiver laughing among themselves.
- **Kural 273** retains the cow in a tiger's skin.
- **Kural 274** retains the hunter hiding in a thicket and trapping birds.
- **Kural 277** retains the kunrimani image.
- **Kural 279** retains the contrast between the straight arrow that kills and the curved *yaazh* that gives musical pleasure.
- **Kural 280 commentary** retains shaving the head / growing matted hair without abandoning blameworthy conduct as deception.
- **Kural 285** retains the source's distinct compassion-and-love framing.
- **Kural 288** retains aram in the honest heart versus deceit in the robber's heart.
- **Kural 290** remains deliberately layered: the compressed verse `புத்தே ளுலகு` is **the higher world**, while Kalaignar's commentary separately retains **life in the world of fame** (`புகழுலக வாழ்க்கை`). This review does not collapse the two.

### Truthfulness / Freedom from Anger

- **Kural 291** retains truthfulness as speech causing no harm.
- **Kural 292** retains the claim that faultless good can give falsehood a place as truth.
- **Kural 293** retains the conscience / one's own mind as the punisher of falsehood.
- **Kural 296** retains the source-checked verse **“There is no fame like freedom from falsehood; / it gives every aram without fail.”**
- **Kural 298 commentary** retains water cleansing the body while truthfulness in word and deed keeps the mind pure.
- **Kural 299 commentary** retains freedom from falsehood as the lamp that dispels inner darkness.
- **Kurals 301–302** retain the Kural-level can/cannot-prevail contrast and Kalaignar's commentary-specific stronger/weaker social framing.
- **Kural 306** retains anger as fire destroying both the angry person and the protecting raft-like circle of kin.
- **Kural 307** retains the hand striking the ground and hurting itself.
- **Kural 308 commentary** retains regret and the attempt to renew the relationship before Kalaignar urges freedom from anger.
- **Kural 310** retains the comparison of boundless anger with the dead and complete freedom from anger with renunciants.

### Not Causing Pain / Non-killing

- **Kural 311** retains refusal to harm others even when great prosperity could be gained.
- **Kural 314** retains doing good to one who caused harm so that the wrongdoer bows in shame.
- **Kural 315** retains treating another living being's suffering as one's own and moving to protect it.
- **Kural 319** remains deliberately layered: the verse keeps the **morning / afternoon** image, while Kalaignar's commentary separately makes the returning harm effectively immediate.
- **Kural 322 commentary** retains Kalaignar's equalizing principle: no division into haves and have-nots, sharing what is available, and allowing all living beings to live.
- **Kural 323** retains non-killing first and freedom from falsehood next in the order of aram.
- **Kural 325 commentary** retains observance of non-killing as greater than renunciation based merely on rejection of worldly life.
- **Kural 326 commentary** retains the striking claim that even death hesitates before one who follows non-killing as a path of aram.
- **Kural 329 commentary** retains the verified Tamil `பகுத்தறிவு இழந்து செயல்படும்`: killers act after **losing rational discernment**.

### Impermanence / Renunciation

- **Kural 332** retains wealth dispersing like an audience after a performance.
- **Kural 334** retains the day-as-sword image cutting life away.
- **Kural 336 commentary** retains the world's arrogant pride in making one alive yesterday absent today.
- **Kural 337** retains the source-checked **“do not know how to live even for a moment”**, distinct from Kalaignar's commentary about failure to reflect on the truth of life.
- **Kural 338** retains the body/life image alongside Kalaignar's egg-and-young-bird explanation.
- **Kural 339 commentary** retains birth as waking after sleep and death as irreversible sleep.
- **Kural 340 commentary** retains the statement that life separated from the body has no other refuge.
- **Kural 346** remains deliberately layered: the verse refers to a world higher than that of the heavenly dwellers, while Kalaignar's commentary separately gives **worldly fame surpassing even fame reaching the skies**.
- **Kural 350** retains attachment to one without attachment as support for giving up one's own attachments.

### Realizing Truth / Eradication of Desire

- **Kural 353 commentary** retains doubt being resolved through clear inquiry and the resulting confidence that heaven is nearer than earth.
- **Kural 356 commentary** retains Kalaignar's specific renunciant-life reading: learning what makes one fit for renunciant life, realizing truth, and no longer desiring domestic life.
- **Kural 357 commentary** retains the claim that those who firmly realize truth do not think there is another birth.
- **Kural 358 commentary** retains removal of the ignorance called another birth and establishment of clear truth.
- **Kural 362 commentary** retains the source-specific explanation that unremoved desires can produce suffering so severe that one may wish one had never been born.
- **Kural 364 commentary** retains purity as freedom from greed and its relation to seeking truthfulness.
- **Kural 365** retains the source-checked compressed verse **“the others have not truly given up anything”**, while Kalaignar's commentary separately explains that only those who have given up all desire are renunciants.

## Editorial changes made in this review

Only small readability / consistency refinements were made after source-check:

1. **Kural 276 commentary** — `There is no one more without compassion` → **`No one is more lacking in compassion`**.
2. **Kural 278 commentary** — `hiding themselves within the name of being people of distinction` → **`hiding themselves under the name of being people of distinction`**.
3. **Kural 285 commentary** — the awkward `quality of behaving with love out of concern for compassion` was smoothed to **`quality of acting with love out of regard for compassion`**, preserving both `அருள்` and `அன்பு` as distinct source concepts.
4. **Kural 297 commentary** — mechanically repetitive wording was tightened to **`Steadfastly observing freedom from falsehood yields the good of the path of aram more surely than merely refraining from deeds that should not be done.`**
5. **Kural 315 verse** — `another life's pain` → **`another being's pain`**, improving natural English without importing the commentary's fuller phrase `other living beings` into the compressed verse.
6. **Kural 317 commentary** — the word order was smoothed so `anyone` functions naturally as the object of the degrading act, with no change to scope or emphasis.
7. **Kural 329 commentary** — `act having lost rational discernment` → **`act after losing rational discernment`**, preserving the verified `பகுத்தறிவு இழந்து செயல்படும்` reading while improving English flow.
8. **Kural 334 verse** — `Showing itself as a day, it is a sword` → **`What appears as a day is a sword`**, preserving the day-as-sword image while removing an unclear pronoun.
9. **Kural 336 commentary** — `takes pride in the arrogance of saying` → **`takes arrogant pride in saying`**, removing redundancy while retaining Kalaignar's explicit arrogance/pride framing.
10. **Kural 361 commentary** — `appears and sprouts among all living beings at every time` → **`appears and sprouts in all living beings at all times`**.
11. **Kural 363 commentary** — `nowhere else too is there` → **`nowhere else is there ... either`**, a syntax-only refinement.

No source-sensitive metaphor, relationship, social category, doctrinal qualification, or Kalaignar interpretation was softened by these edits.
