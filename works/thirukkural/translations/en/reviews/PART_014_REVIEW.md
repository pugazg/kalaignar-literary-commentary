## Recurring terminology and imagery reconciliation

The Part 014 source-checked set is governed by the following editorial controls:

- `காதல் / காதலர்` remains **love / lover / beloved** according to Kalaignar's grammar and speaker context; source-supported gender specificity is retained rather than neutralized mechanically.
- `நெஞ்சு / உள்ளம்` retains the direct **heart** vocabulary and its personified conversational role where Kalaignar addresses or describes it.
- `பிரிவு` remains **separation**, retaining the bodily and emotional suffering Kalaignar attaches to it.
- evening (`மாலை`) continues the personified, hostile and life-draining force established in chapter 123; it is not flattened into a neutral time-of-day reference.
- bodily signs of longing — eyes, shoulders, bangles, pallor and wasting — remain concrete bodily imagery rather than generalized sadness.
- gestures and signs used by lovers remain tied to Kalaignar's explicit explanation rather than being expanded into a generalized theory of love-signs.
- `புணர்ச்சி` is handled in its lovers' union/reunion context without euphemistically erasing the intimacy present in the Tamil.
- `புலவி / ஊடல்` is rendered through **lovers' quarrel / quarrelling / sulking** according to sentence context. The translation retains Kalaignar's presentation of quarrelling as an intimate relational act that can intensify affection, not as generic hostility.
- rhetorical questions remain rhetorical where Kalaignar's explanation preserves that force.
- comparisons involving food, salt, water and earth, flowers, bodily beauty, embrace and separation remain source-specific images and are not replaced by conventional English proverbial equivalents.

## Chapter 133 control

The opening of chapter 133 on scan 302 establishes an important controlled treatment for subsequent work.

`ஊடலுவகை` is retained as **Joy of Lovers' Quarrel**. Kalaignar's explanations for Kurals 1321–1325 explicitly frame `ஊடல்` as something arising between lovers that can increase love, produce a small but meaningful suffering, create a distinctive delight, lead to tight embrace and inseparability, and make even temporary separation from the beloved's soft shoulders a form of pleasure.

Accordingly, later editorial work must not recast `ஊடல்` merely as anger, hostility, domestic conflict or moral fault. Its lovers'-quarrel character is protected by Kalaignar's own supplied explanations.
