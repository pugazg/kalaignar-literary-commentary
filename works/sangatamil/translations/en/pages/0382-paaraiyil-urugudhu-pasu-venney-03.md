Though he understood how hard it would be to set right the mind of the grieving, weeping warrior Vanangamudi, Thuyan continued with his counsel:

“She is no magical maiden who can simply vanish! Do not forget—there is no such thing as magic here! She is a jewel-lamp of humankind, the lamp of the home who will come seeking you, a lamp of womankind! Though she sent word to you that she had gone to her relatives' town, why wail as though she had disappeared like a coin fallen into the depths of the sea?

You are the valiant commander who, on the battlefield, knocks aside with his own hands the spears and arrows that fly like a swarm of locusts. Why stand useless now like a withered tree? This is not right! Cast off your worry! Do not let your heart toss like a boat in mid-sea amid a storm!”

The rain of consoling words from his affectionate friend did not wet and extinguish the mountain of love-fire in Vanangamudi, separated from his many-hued peacock! He began to melt like wax in the fire of love!

The woman poet Velliveethiyar explains, through a simile in the *Kuruntokai*, the torment suffered by his surging heart of love—and we marvel at it for the greatness of Tamil! His friend rebuked and consoled him, saying, “She who left today will return tomorrow.” Is there another simile that can surpass the one spoken by the broad-shouldered man to that friend?
