—what aptness there is in the poet-lord's comparison with the choosing of a village council! What rare skill in explaining, through this simile, an old piece of news from the Tamil land to the world!

The people of the village would gather together in the public assembly. Each would write on a palm leaf whom they wanted as members of the council and cast the vote into a pot bound with cord. The sealed pots would be kept very safely by the presiding officer. Then an official called the keeper of records would come and remove the seal; after that, palm leaf by palm leaf would be drawn from the pot, counted before the people of the village, and the names of those elected as members announced. That council, it seems, possessed many rights for safeguarding the welfare of the village!

It was this honoured custom that Marudhan Ilanaganar fashioned in Sangam literature so that later generations of humankind might recognize it as lines of history bearing witness!

The eagle and the hawk pulling out the intestines from the body of a warrior fallen on the battlefield—the poet makes it appear before our eyes like a hand reaching into the pot and drawing out the palm-leaf ballots cast for a village-welfare council carrying out its duty!

Though this is a single *akam* poem, how many, how many pieces of news are caught within it!

The story of love in which the husband, thinking of the tears of the fish-eyed woman, gives up travelling to another town!

Another piece of news, through the spoken words of the merchant youth pouring out his pain: “Her tears make my ribs ache like the spear-point of Pittan—the warrior who delights in drinking toddy, the commander of the Chera king who fells his enemies!”—and thereby showing that valour is an ornament of the Tamils!
