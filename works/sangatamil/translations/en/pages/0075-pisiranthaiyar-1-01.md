# Pisiranthaiyar 1

In the Pandya land, whose people made the fish  
swim upon their flag and piled up victories on the battlefield,

there was one sorrow in their hearts.

What was it?

It was their wish  
that the bent sceptre of Pandya king Arivudai Nambi—

the sword-moustached ruler  
who tried to increase revenue

by increasing taxes  
instead of increasing prosperity—

should be made straight again!

Many went to meet him carrying long petitions,  
describing the condition of the country,

hoping that the Pandya king would reform;  
their feet ached, and they returned!

Dance-hall, music-hall,  
the royal audience hall—

and in the flower-soft bed,  
quarrelling with the queen and making up again!

To spend one's time  
immersed in pleasures beyond counting

was said to be the way to guard a nation.

Four or five men in government office  
sat close beside Arivudai Nambi

and, one after another,  
began to ruin him!

Whatever the king said, they hurriedly nodded,  
“Yes!”

“Ah! Excellent! Excellent!”  
they praised and flattered him,

driving his chariot through the mire of pleasure  
and drawing the government under their own control.

Like birds from a dried-up pond,  
some relatives and close associates too

thought this was their opportunity.

They displayed every trick they had learned  
and multiplied without number  
their crimes against the people.

Many wise men feared  
that the garden-like Pandya land

would become a desert  
at the hands of those seeking only their own gain—
