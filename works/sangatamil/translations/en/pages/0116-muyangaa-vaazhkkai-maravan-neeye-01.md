# You Are the Warrior Whose Chest Does Not Falter!

He wielded the heroic sword  
that defeated and subdued

those who caused harm  
along the southern coast.

He crushed the armies  
of the northern Vaduga kings

and bore the garland of victory  
upon his shoulders.

He was the one  
who bore the name Ilanchet Senni.

A poet named Oonpothi Pasungudaiyar  
gave us the mighty strength

of that prosperous Chola king  
in Tamil flowing like a waterfall  
where fish leap.

Scholars say there is no room  
for two opinions:

the titles

“Chola of Neythalangaanal,”  
which marks the pride of his birthplace,

and “the Chola who conquered  
the famed Seruppaazhi and Paamoolur”

both refer to Ilanchet Senni.

It appears that even when Tamil poets  
composed *puram* songs,

stories of the epics  
were already in circulation.

And so, even then,  
the rare poet Pasungudaiyar

wrote a marvellous simile.

When the Chola sat in royal court,  
the poet came seeking a gift

and wrapped the king  
in a “garment of song.”

In return for that “song-garment,”  
the ruler wrapped the poet in silk

and gave many kinds  
of jewelled ornaments.

A pearl strand worn only by royalty,  
an emerald necklace,

a heap of ruby bracelets,  
and many other jewels—

Ilanchet Senni, smiling,  
showered them upon the poet.
