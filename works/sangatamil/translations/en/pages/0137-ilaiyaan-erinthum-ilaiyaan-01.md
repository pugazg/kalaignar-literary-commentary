# The Young One Is Young in Nothing!

He was a young calf  
who removed the anklets

that rang *kalakala* on his feet  
and put on the warrior's anklets!

He was a boy  
who cut away the long topknot

braided and knotted behind him  
and stood holding a flashing sword!

He was a youth  
who removed the lovely bangles  
from his soft hands

and carried a bow  
upon his shoulder!

He was still of an innocent age,  
wearing on his chest

the five-weapon talisman  
of childhood!

Yet that child  
born in the Pandya line

declared that his shoulders  
had strength enough  
to destroy the enemy,

took command of the army,

made the war-drum sound  
until the earth shook,

and set out to trample  
the head of fierce opposition.

“I shall enter the field  
and win,” he swore.

His name was Nedunchezhiyan—

his head held high,  
his fame unbending.

Idaikkunrur Kizhar, the poet,  
praised that lion-cub

who raised his sword  
and roared,

and called him  
a tender green shoot

from whom even  
the smell of milk  
had not yet faded.

Many fine Tamil poets  
rejoiced in singing

of that young Pandya prince.

Kunrur Kizhar,  
Kurungozhiyur Kizhar,

Kudapulaviyanar,  
Kalladanar,

Nakkiranar,  
son of Madurai Kanakkayanar,

and the great poet  
of his royal hall—

Mangudi Kizhar Marudhanar,  
who sang *Madurai Kanchi*—

having heard the Tamil verse  
of so many learned men—
