# The Poet-King of Parambu Hill

In the Pandya land
where the fish-emblem flag flew,

Kapilar was born
in Thiruvathavur,
a bowl of honey.

He formed
a friendship
with Pari,

giver like the sky,

firm as
a deer's antler.

If all the beauty
of a mountain
comes from rain,

then the beauty
of Parambu hill
came from King Pari.

<div align="right">There—</div>

even the sweetness
inside a jackfruit segment

<div align="right">could be called</div>

a gift
from his hand.

Pari,
the great giver,
did not value wealth

even as much
as one values weeds!

When a white mullai creeper
stood in bloom

but had no branch
to cling to,

he gave
his jewelled chariot
as its dwelling!

We may have read
in stories

that the quarrel-making Narada
went before Murugan
and said:

“There is a maiden
suited to your love.

Her name is Valli.

She is beautiful,
a round moon.

Once you see her,
you will want
to devour her!”

At once,
it is said,

Murugan mounted
his spotted peacock

and flew like a storm
to reach that peahen.

Now hear
another imagination!

After travelling far
without finding
the straight-bangled maiden,

the young-moustached Murugan
came toward Parambu hill

and asked
an old woman:

“Where is Valli?”

The old woman shouted,

“Climb the mountain;
you will see her!”

and went on
along the long path.

He climbed
the hill
with the speed
of a spear.

“Where is Valli?
Where is Valli?”

asked the Vel-bearing one.

“Come!
Look!”

said the people there.

All over the hill—

valli tubers!
