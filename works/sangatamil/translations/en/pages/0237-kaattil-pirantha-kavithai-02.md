Colourful parrots came,
circled around him,
and cried:

“Draw a song
about us!”

A flock of swans
became boats upon a stream
and floated by,
asking:

“Sing a little poem
about us!”

Nature,
stacking up
a thousand beauties,
asked him
for a special prefatory verse.

Still,
nothing moved him!

He entered the forest,
unfurled his imagination,
and searched
for the subject of a poem.

Before him stood elephants,
like dark-cheeked mountains,
displaying their majesty.

Hard-Shouldered Karaveeran
did not think them worth
even the expense
of a sidelong glance!

A roaring male lion
rose up,
its dense mane bristling,

as though asking:

“Is there any obstacle
to binding my pride
into a poem
and conquering it?”

A tiger sprang
from its cave
like a sword
from its sheath,

as though challenging:

“Is my fierce anger
not enough
to give splendour
to your poem?”

Yet the poet
did not take up
a palm leaf
to write
of any beauty
that fell at his feet
and begged:
“Write of us!”

At last,
he saw
a pair of monkeys.

“Certain!
Certain!
My poem will surely
live in this world
without disappearing!”

He beat his chest
and composed
a flower-like Tamil poem.

In this earth
where the maiden Beauty
sits enthroned everywhere,

beauty,
beauty,
beauty
in every direction,

how did two monkeys
with ungainly faces
capture his thought?

Our curiosity
surges like waves.

So let us too
see the scene he saw,

using as our eyes
the poem he composed,

and marvel
at the character
he chose
for painting affection
into song—
