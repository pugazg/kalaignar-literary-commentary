“—to heap up Tamil poetry of undying fame,
does married life say no?
Does pomegranate juice become bitter
because mountain honey is mixed into it?
My son, listen.
Do not refuse—accept my request!” she said.

“Do not think I am refusing, mother.
I do not hate Narkannai.
But I love the war-sword.
Not a flower-petal!
No! No! Not at all!” he said.

“The difference is plain, my son.
If, as the townspeople say,
I had truly been the mother who gave birth to you,
you would have shone as a son who never disobeyed his mother's word!
But Kavarpendu is only the nurse-mother who raised this child.
That is why you treat me lightly.
Dear prince, I am leaving,” she said, and moved away.

“Mother, you have hurled a spear into my heart!
I shall no longer cling stubbornly,
like a monitor lizard, to the hold I have taken!
You are the mother who gave birth to me.
As proof of that,
tomorrow morning I myself shall go to meet Narkannai
and tell her a good decision.”

Kavarpendu kissed the crown of Killi's head and blessed him.

“It is midnight now, so I shall come tomorrow.
Both of us will see Narkannai
and give her the sweet news,” she said.
Wiping away the tears of joy that filled her eyes,
she returned home.

*

Thinking of the victory of Narkannai's pure love,
Narkilli closed his eyes
and sank into delight.
After a little while,
the whole sea-girt world became her.
He journeyed into the world of imagination and dreams
and began to fly beside his parrot of love!

Because of fear, modesty, maidenly innocence and reserve,
she swayed away without coming near—
