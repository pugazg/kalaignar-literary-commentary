As though recalling that sight, the hero in love, on seeing his beloved, had come to clasp and embrace her, to give and steal countless kisses and float in delight. When that could not happen, he bowed his head like the millet ear; and that ear itself, Kapilar imagines, bent and drooped like the elephant's trunk. What delight there is in that imagination! A singular delight belonging to Tamil, which conquers time!

*

> “The elephant reaching upward to the tall, grain-bearing bamboo,
> its lowered trunk beside pearl-bright tusks;
> like that, the soft-headed, fully ripened great ear
> of fine-stalked little millet bends.
> Driving off the many birds,
> come when the sun sets ............”

*

(Pattuppattu  
Kurinjippattu, lines 35 to 39  
Poet: Kapilar)

## Explanation of meaning:

*Nerkol* = bearing grain.  
*Neduvedhir* = tall, high bamboo. *Anandha* = facing upward.  
*Maruppu* = tusk. *Irangu kai kadupa* = the trunk lowered and bent.  
*Thuy* = softness. *Punirudheer* = having lost its tender, unripe state. *Kural* = ear of grain. *Padu* = abundant. *Pul* = bird.  
*Oppi* = driving away. *El* = sun. *Pada* = to set.  
*Varudhiyar* = telling one to come.
