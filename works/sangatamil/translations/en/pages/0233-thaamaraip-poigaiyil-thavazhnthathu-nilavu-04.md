“—instead,
we shall fight
and see victory!

If, on the battlefront,
the garland of victory
should fall
upon the shoulders
of the enemy,

then let me be separated
from my golden-bodied wife,
my life's companion,
she of lightning waist!

Let me bear the blame
of a tyrant
who seats in his assembly
a wicked man
who knows no justice,

and rules a kingdom
amid injustice!

Let me bear the name
of a sinner
who lost all those
deep friends
whose companionship
surrounded me
with happiness!

If there is such a thing
as another birth,
let a great stain
cling to me:

let me cease to be
the king who guarded
the southern land,

and be born instead
a deceiver
who protects
the harsh lands
of the cruel!”

Thus,
raising his sword
and swearing his vow,
he entered battle.

The shoulders
of the Pandya army
rose high.

The enemy fled defeated.

Yet Bootha Pandiyan
had received
such a mass
of grievous wounds
on the field

that he lay
upon Perungoppendu's lap,

gave and received
one final kiss,

and then lived on
as an inscription
upon the hero-stone!

“There is no sky now
for the moon to glide through.

So I too
shall no longer remain
in a world
without him,”
said Bootha Pandiyan's queen,

setting out
to follow him.

“Do not go, mother!”
wailed the Pandya land.

Because the Pandyas
had no successor,
people stood begging her
to live
and rule the country.

But she cried:

“O learned ones!
O many learned ones!

Do not ask me,
with hearts of stone,
to go on living!

To end this sorrow
that will never end,

I have resolved today
to fall into blazing fire
and release my life.

This is sweet to me!

Once the life
I lived as one bird
of an inseparable pair
is gone,

I—”
