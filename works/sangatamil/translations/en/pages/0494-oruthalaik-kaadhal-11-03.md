Aiyai lifted Narkannai and held her close.

“Brother! What injustice is this?” she cried.

“Princess, do not say anything against him!
This is the sweet ending I sought for myself!
Did I not make my vow before you?
Do you not remember?
I have fulfilled it before your very eyes!

‘If I live, I shall live as your wife;
otherwise, if I fall,
I shall fall at the point of your sword!’

When I said that,
Narkilli spoke dismissively:
‘Neither will happen.’

Now look!
Look at whose sword has brought me down!

By changing the message
and throwing in a palm-leaf saying
that you, the princess, would be carried away,
I deceived your brother.
Thinking me an enemy,
he has somehow fulfilled my vow
without knowing it!

For that,
it is you whom I must thank again and again!
Only because I pretended that I was about to kill you
did my body gain the fortune
of kissing your brother's sword!

Though I did not finish building
the fortress I raised in my heart,
I have not strayed by even an atom
from my resolve
to give my life,
as I had intended,
to the strong son of Tittan.
I have won a wondrous victory!”

Not even the shadow of pain spread over her face.
Like a warrior accepting death on the field,
Narkannai went on speaking.

“Forgive me, Narkannai!” Killi wailed.

He knelt,
placed her head upon his lap,
and wept.
With her kanthal-flower fingers,
Narkannai wiped the tears
from Killi's cheeks.
