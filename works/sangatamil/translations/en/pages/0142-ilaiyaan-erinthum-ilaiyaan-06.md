Thinking of this with joy,

our heart becomes  
a flower-grove

blossoming  
with Tamil feeling!

<div align="center">*</div>

> * “Laughable are they,  
> those who boast loudly of their land!
>
> Saying with contempt,  
> ‘He is young,’
>
> they speak of their tall, fine elephants  
> with broad feet,
>
> their chariots, horses,  
> and battle-ready warriors,
>
> and, trusting in these,  
> utter small words
>
> without fearing  
> my great strength.
>
> If I do not strike  
> those anger-filled kings,
>
> break their hard battle-order,
>
> and capture them together  
> with their war-drums—
>
> then let the people  
> who live beneath my shade
>
> find no shelter  
> beneath their own king,
>
> shed tears and condemn me, saying,  
> ‘Our ruler is cruel.’
>
> Let me become  
> a king blamed by his people!
>
> Let Mangudi Marudhanar,  
> leader among poets
>
> of lofty learning  
> and distinguished greatness,
>
> and all the poets  
> whose praise endures with the world,
>
> refuse to sing  
> within the bounds of my land.
>
> Let those whom I should protect  
> suffer ever greater pain,
>
> and let me fall into poverty  
> in which I cannot give  
> to those who come begging.”

<div align="center">*</div>

<div align="right">

(*Purananuru* — Poem 72  
Poet: Pandiyan Nedunchezhiyan,  
victor of the battle of Thalaiyalanganam)

</div>

## Explanation of meaning:

**Naguthakkanar** = people fit to be laughed at.  
**Meekkooruthal** = praising excessively / boasting.  
**Ulaiyak koori** = speaking in a way that causes contempt and distress.  
**Paavadi panaiththaal nedunal yaanai** = a tall elephant with broad feet and large legs.  
**Uruthuppu anjaadhu** = without fearing my great strength.  
**Varaiga en nilavarai** = let them leave my country.  
**Purappor punkan koora** = let the sorrow of those who should be protected like one's own kin increase.
