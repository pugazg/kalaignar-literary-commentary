# Kuppaik Kozhiyaar!

Like a warrior
standing guard,

a rooster raises its neck
and displays its pride.

Village people
provoke it,
send it into the arena,

laugh in delight,
and watch a cockfight.

From small villages
to great towns and cities,
this custom of watching
cockfights
is not something
of today or yesterday.

It began even before
the late Sangam age
that gave birth
to the learned-celebrated
*Kalithogai*,
*Agam*,
and *Puram* works!

One piece of evidence
lies in beautiful *Kuruntokai*—

a sweet segment
of sugarcane
inside an *agam* poem.

See the suffering
of a maiden
whose seasonal beauty
blooms down
to her lotus feet,

struck by an invading army
called love-sickness!

“There,
between those mullai bushes,
is the handsome man—

shoulders like two hills,
walking with the stride
of a lion!

Unable to go there,
join him,
and embrace him,

my mother has killed me here
by keeping me imprisoned!

The instant
my eyes saw him,
he became the jewel
of those very eyes.

Could he not have come
with a compassionate heart,

called me
‘jewel of my eye,’

and gathered me
into his arms?

If his hand touched
the river of fire
that is my grief,

would it not become
a waterfall?

What shall I do?

When he himself
is the medicine
that can cure my illness,

what shall I think of
and why shall I weep?

Through the harsh work
of love,
I have become
a growth of severe suffering!

My heart,
once a soft breeze,
has become a storm!

No one brought
this sorrow to me.

And no one can come,
stop it,
and let me live!”
