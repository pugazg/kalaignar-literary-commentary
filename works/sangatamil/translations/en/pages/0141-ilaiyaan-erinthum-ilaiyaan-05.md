> —if such a state should arise,
>
> let the whole world condemn me
>
> as a cruel king  
> who did not protect  
> the welfare of his people!
>
> If defeat embraces me,
>
> let poverty come  
> and torment me
>
> until the world scorns me  
> as a sinner
>
> who gives nothing  
> to those who come,
>
> bow, praise,  
> and beg because they have nothing!
>
> If the garland of victory  
> refuses my shoulders,
>
> let there come upon me  
> a state in which
>
> I cannot help remove  
> the suffering of the poor,
>
> those who are like my own kin!
>
> If the enemy's hand rises  
> while our warrior-army falls,
>
> then let all the poets  
> who compose verse
>
> in Tamil sweet  
> as the mango-cuckoo's voice,
>
> under the leadership  
> of Mangudi Marudhanar,
>
> decide that it is no honour  
> to sing of the Pandya land,
>
> leave this Nedunchezhiyan,  
> and travel far away!”

Having sworn in this manner,  
the Pandya king

filled his eyes  
with a red glow,

took command of the army,  
and marched

toward the battlefront  
at Thalaiyalanganam!

<div align="center">*</div>

Though the Pandya shaped  
his oath-poem

in sweet, fruit-like Tamil,

what he firmly declares  
is surely the lofty culture

he held as dear as life itself:

protecting the poor  
as one protects one's own kin;

giving with generosity  
worthy of rulers;

holding attachment  
toward the people;

and living in a country  
where a gathering of learned poets

composes verse  
that the land itself praises.
