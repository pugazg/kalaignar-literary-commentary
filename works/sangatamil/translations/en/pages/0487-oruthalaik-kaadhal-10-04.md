“Mother! Have you come?” he cried in astonishment.

Yes—Kaavarp Penn stood before him!

“My son Narkilli!
Two poets, Mamulanar and Kalladanar,
met King Katti and his friend Paanan and told them:

‘Even on a battle-front that takes the lives of enemies,
there are rules by which culture is preserved!
You went to seize the princess of Uraiyur
and fled when you heard that Narkilli had returned.
Once you understood that the enemy's strength had grown,
your decision was wise.
But what justice is there
in arresting one of our Tamil poets?
Release Kaavarp Penn without a moment's delay!
If you do not issue such an order at once,
put a full stop here and now
and say that the bond of Kalladanar and Mamulanar with you
has ended.’

The moment the two poets became angry in this way,
they released me without hesitating even for an instant!
Dear son, I ran here to see you.
Narkannai came here to become my daughter-in-law—
where is she?” she asked.

“Mother, forgive me!
If I marry her,
Tamil may lose many literary jewels
it ought to receive.
She must compose nectar-like poems
so that Tamil's honour as a people
and its broad-shouldered valour rise still higher.
Instead,
if that singing bird is shut away
in the prison of the women's quarters,
what benefit at all will Tamil gain?” he asked.

stands in the way of literature being poured into Tamil?
