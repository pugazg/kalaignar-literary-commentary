# One Nāzhi to Eat; Two Garments to Wear!

Great ones who build mansions
and live there, lulled to sleep
by the gentle breeze!
Crying “money, money, money,”
you bathe day after day
in the worker's sweat
and heap up wealth.
Like vultures that eat corpses,
you keep enlarging your bellies.

Pushing the poor into lives wrapped in darkness,
you wander in search of possessions,
without a mind that ever says “enough.”
You who have made the increase of wealth
your one aim in life—
I ask you one question; answer it.
Do you place ruby fry and emerald curry on a leaf and eat?
Do you roast diamonds and serve them with cat's-eye aviyal?
Do you pound rice made of pearl and coral gems,
put it in the cooking pot,
and fill your stomachs with it?

This question, rich in meaning,
was asked from a public platform by Arignar Anna
to people who pile wealth up like mountains.
We too keep asking it, without forgetting.
Today we proclaim across the earth
that the fragrance of common ownership must spread.
This cry is no novelty.
The great poet Nakkeerar sounds
the drum of commonality in a *Puram* song. Listen:

For the king who longs
to bring the whole earth, ringed by the clear sea,
beneath his white royal umbrella
and establish exclusive rule,
and for the rough, unlettered man
who, like one throwing stones to bring down fruit,
wanders the wilds hunting animals—
