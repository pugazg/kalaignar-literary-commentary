—broke,
then the old mother
straightened herself
at once!

“When playing dice,
pieces are cut down.

The battlefield
is the same.

Was the wound
on his chest
or on his back?

Tell me!”
she demanded.

“On his back,”
he said.

The old woman trembled;
her heart burst.

She took up a sword
and hurried
in the direction
from which the war-drum
had sounded.

“I gave milk
to a coward!

And this fool
lying face-down
is called
a warrior!

Once,
when a spear
came flying,

my deathless husband
showed his chest
in answer
and fell there!

Was this one
born to him?

Alas!
Where is honour?

He has fallen
lower than
a broken little wall!

At the Himalayan boundary,
valour smiles.

Here,
music trembles
upon the strings
of the veena—

and even that
thunders only:
‘Honour! Honour!’

Are you descended
from a shameless line
that kneels to enemies
and lives?

Boy!

You have scattered stain
upon the martial Tamil clan!

I gave you my breast;
raised you upon my lap.

Where are those shoulders
that ate,
grew strong,
and swelled with pride?

Did they not itch
for battle?

Alas!
Coward,
who could find no way
to show the enemy spear
where to enter—

show me now
the way back
to the heroic milk
you drank from me!”

So wailed
the poor old woman,
nearing eighty.

She reached
the battlefront
and turned over
one by one

the Tamil bulls
lying scattered there.

There,
to protect
our Tamil land—
