—calling the warriors  
and hurrying them on!

He has fixed peacock feathers,  
fastened the bell,

and comes toward me  
shaking shoulders like mountains,

bearing his shield,

like a fighting bull  
among men.

Look at that young bull,

waist-cloth tied,  
armour fastened,

with the courage of one  
who has no desire left  
for his own life!

Look with those eyes of yours  
that did not sleep all night!

Tell me—

will these eyes of mine  
ever know sleep again?

Across land where spreading fire  
has destroyed forests

and turned them into desert,

thousands of soldiers  
march toward the battlefield,

resolved to protect this land  
even if they die there.

He too comes quickly  
seeking me  
to take his leave!

Do you understand now

the explanation  
for everything he did  
through the night?

You scolded me,  
burning with anger,

because I floated  
in a flood of pleasure.

Look at me now, friend!

A flood breaks its banks  
between my eyelids,

and the pupils  
of both my eyes  
disappear beneath it.

All the pleasure  
I saw in the night

has entered  
the account of expenses;

in the account of income,  
sorrow comes now

sum after sum!

Look at the scene  
that is about to unfold.

The sign I read  
has come true without fail!

With his own people—  
he staged the drama well  
until dawn;

after embracing  
my golden body

and feeding me nectar,

the radiant-faced man  
now causes pain

by leaving  
and squeezing my heart.

He will say:

“My dear,  
I am going to lead the army

against the enemy  
surrounding us.

I shall break  
their conspiracy into pieces,

wear the victory garland,  
and come back to see you.

Give me leave, deer-like one!”

He has left!

Whatever his words may be,  
they drip like honey—

but in my heart  
do they not sting  
like a scorpion?
