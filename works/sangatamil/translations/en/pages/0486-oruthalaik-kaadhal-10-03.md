When this good news reached the Uraiyur mansion,
the good Aiyai embraced Narkannai.

“The two venomous snakes that marched against us
could not stand before our Killi,
the mongoose springing upon them,
and have slithered away!
Turn that event into a poem!” she said.

“I would have been dead by now
at your brother's hand!
I have been waiting for the nurse-mother Kaavarp Penn to return.
She said, ‘I myself shall stand at the front and conduct the wedding.’
Trusting that,
I am still alive!
If my love is fulfilled sweetly as I hoped
and I gain the fortune of becoming your sister-in-law,
I shall heap up countless poems
like moonlight, breeze, sweet syrup and honey-rain!” she said.

Just then,
a friend came running, gasping for breath,
and told news that made
the very life-breath of Perungozhi Naaykan's daughter
seem to stop.

“Those two villains, Katti and Paanan,
have shut our Kaavarp Penn in a prison-cage
and dragged her away.
To rescue the nurse-mother,
Narkilli goes to the battle-front tomorrow!”

Because this painful news
followed so quickly upon the happy news,
both women refused the evening meal
and went to sleep in separate rooms.

*

Having vowed that at dawn
he would lay siege to Paanan and Katti's city
and recover mother Kaavarp Penn,
the Chola Narkilli tossed sleeplessly at midnight.
Then he heard a knock at the door.

“I cannot bear this trouble from Narkannai,”
he muttered,
rising and moving to the front entrance
of his chamber.
He opened the door and looked out!
