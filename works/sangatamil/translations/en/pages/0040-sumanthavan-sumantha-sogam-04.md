“Hear it all, great lady!  
You will find no fault in my state.

I do not deny that my eyes have swollen from weeping;  
nor is it false that I lament all day in anxiety.

You shall know why—  
I hide nothing!”

“Tell me,” said Avvai.  
And the woman spoke:

“You know well the victorious kings  
whose royal parasols do not droop,

the learned poets,  
the dutiful great warriors,  
and leaders above the rest.

Many warriors serve in such a king's army;  
they do not hesitate to march behind him  
in ordered ranks like a line of rams.

Though a thousand stand behind the king,  
he alone held boundless affection for my son.

On the battlefront, while resting,  
the valiant warriors gather to drink  
the invigorating liquor called *theral*.

At such a time the king would call my son near  
and delight in giving him more to drink than all the others.

Now hear the cause of my anguish, mother!

On the battlefield where foxes float  
upon bones made into rafts,

birds play ball with severed heads!

Only if my son's head too  
were one among those heads

would the *theral* granted by the king  
have true greatness!

He received the king's overflowing affection;  
he received an overflowing cup as well.

If he has died in battle and put on fame,  
would it be hard for me to behold that sight?

It was this that made me grieve, mother!

Was I wrong?” she asked.

Avvai Piratti's heart ached deeply  
that she herself was not such a mother!
