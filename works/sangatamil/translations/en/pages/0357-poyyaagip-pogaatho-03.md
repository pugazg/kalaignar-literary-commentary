—when the truth became known that the lion-like commander Aguthai had fought and perished,” all the ideas woven around the golden wheel-weapon called the “golden *thigiri*” proved false.

“Could this news too not become false, just as that falsehood did—the news that our beloved Evvi was wounded and died in battle?” lamented the towering poet when he heard it. He stood shaken like a calf that had lost its cow.

*

> “Would it become false? Would it become false?
> Like the gold-adorned *thigiri* seen with Aguthai,
> whose broad-footed elephants were never scarce for gift-seekers,
> the strong-footed man rich in distinction—would it become false?
> The lord of the great-bard circle, wearer of great ornaments,
> Evvi of the army that strikes in battle—
> the voice heard in the first light of dawn saying
> that many honourable wounds from steel lie upon his chest!”

*

(*Purananuru*, poem 233  
Poet: Vellerukkilaiyar)

*

## Explanation of meaning:

**irumpaan okkal thalaivan** = leader of the circle of the great bards.  
**ehguru** = pierced by a spear.  
**vaikuru** = dawn / daybreak.
