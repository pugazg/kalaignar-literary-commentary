# Does the Heart Wander, Unable to Recognize Me?

“He too entered the battlefield,
fire pouring from his eyes,
to guard the honour of Tamil soil and add to its glory.
Vowing fiercely, ‘I shall make the heads of Kanaka and Vijaya
bear the stone and carve a statue for Kannagi at Vanchi,’
he went north,
defeated the cruel-tongued men who stood against him,
and set out for war like a swelling lion,
to engrave great king Senguttuvan's fame of victory
like a bow upon the forehead of the Himalaya.
That my darling went so makes this woman here swell with pride—
and yet,
he bent my conch-like neck,
pressed a kiss upon my cheek
with lips like the tender flesh of a palmyra fruit,

and said:

‘Dear one, do not be troubled.
As soon as Kanaka and Vijaya are defeated,
I shall set out with the Chera king,
rush back, and fall into your lap.
My chest will return wearing battle-wounds as ornaments;
press your flower-petal lips upon them
and heal those wounds.
Give me leave—with your eyes and with your words.’

So he spoke when he journeyed away
as one among the Chera army's warriors.
And my heart, which had declared
that it would not remain apart from him even for an instant—
