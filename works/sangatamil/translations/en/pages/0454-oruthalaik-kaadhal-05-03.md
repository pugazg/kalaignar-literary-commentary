bearing the pain,
she thought a thousand thoughts about
what ornament might be placed upon that foot!

When her body was tossed like a straw in a gale
and you were born into the world, rolling out and crying,
she forgot all her suffering in a trance of joy!
Is it a sin, then, for her to teach you a lesson along your path today?
When she placed flowers and a forehead mark upon you and rained kisses,
she counted the years, wondering when you would grow into a young woman.
Do you think that mother was so innocent she did not know
a parrot might one day peck at your lips, red as kovai fruit?
Your mother is not forbidding you to step into the river and bathe!
She asks only that you know its depth before stepping in!
But if, before that, you wait watching the road until he comes,
pine day and night without sleep,
and ruin your body,
will the parents who hold you as their very life agree to it?
Your friend begs you—listen at once!
Sleep and rise! Be cheerful!
Uraiyur is not going to run away anywhere;
it is only going to come within your room!” said her friend, laughing.

“I have not been staying awake!
I slept and have only just awakened—
listen to my story without fussing!
At the moment I close my eyes and fall asleep,
it is his memory that presses upon me even in dreams!

* “On the great mango tree rising in the middle of the town,
a bat with thorn-like teeth
swings and sleeps upside down from a branch,
and dreams that it tastes the young sourness
of the gooseberry fruit in the dense forest
of the Chola king Azhisi of Aarkkadu—
just as that bat might see such a sweet dream,
a dream made this maiden too lose herself, friend!
