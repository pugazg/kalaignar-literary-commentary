When she rubbed his chest with her hand,  
he embraced her.

She slipped away!

Like anklets ringing in laughter,  
the elderly lovers burst out laughing  
in great delight.

The grandson and granddaughter too laughed,  
clapping their little hands,

spilling baby-speech  
sweet as the base of sugarcane!

Then, as though a sudden barrier  
had been thrown across a flood of joy,

a burning piece of news  
fell upon their ears.

They heard that the sons  
whom Uraiyur king Kopperuncholan cherished as his very life

had raised an army against him;

that the Chola king,  
who composed verse in parrot-sweet Tamil,

had sunk into deep grief  
at the conduct of his children;

and that the white-flower-hearted king,  
with no other path left,

had taken the heroic sword in his hand  
and risen to enter battle.

Hearing the astonishing news  
that war had broken out between father and sons,

Pisiranthaiyar's happiness became  
like property lost in a marketplace.

His heart deeply pained,  
he set out for the Chola country,

<div align="right">saying:</div>

“The Tamil people are rich in self-respect.  
If the day comes when they must bow before an enemy,

they will give up life without hesitation!

Their fame crosses the roaring sea;  
they trade in eaglewood, pepper and pearls.

They are fine masters of literature,  
sweet and good poets;

they praise nature  
and build beautiful mansions.

They inscribe the greatness of their people in writing  
and rule this land without failing in royal duty.

The Tamil people are an ancient race,  
arising before stone and soil, they say!

All that may be true.

But there is one thing—  
this same Tamil people can become caught  
in the evil storm of internal feud—
