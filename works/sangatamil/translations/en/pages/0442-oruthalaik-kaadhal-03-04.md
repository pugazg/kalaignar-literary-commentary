“Make a broth from goat-leg bones with the *appam*,
let it soak and deepen the taste,” said Narkannai!

“Only do not forget to add salt!
A dish without salt and one-sided love both belong in the rubbish!”
said her friend!

Narkannai started in shock!
Her state of floating like a raft in the sacred pool of delight vanished;
she sagged at once on hearing that inauspicious phrase!
“Wrong! Wrong, girl!” the friend said, striking her own chin,
but Narkannai's anger did not cool.

*

When all the invited guests had arrived for the feast,
Perungozhi Naaykan welcomed them warmly and seated them.
Smiling, he introduced his wife Perunthevi Naachiyar,
his daughter Narkannai and her close friend Porkodi
to the ruler of Aamur, the valiant Narkilli,
and the great poet Saathanthai who had come with them.
Then Perunarkilli pointed with swelling pride
to a distinguished woman:
“Do you know who this is? When I lost my mother in youth,
she made good that loss, guarded my precious life
and raised me to this state—she is Kaavarp Penn,
the virgin-Tamil poet-queen,” he said.
They folded their hands to one another in greeting,
exchanged ripened affection,
and finished the feast. Her eyelids fluttering,
Narkannai hurried toward Killi, who stood like a vengai tree!
“Your coming here has brought delight to our home and our hearts.
I have shaped my heartfelt thanks into a poem on a palm leaf;
read it whenever you have leisure,” she said.

that he might read it before her parents and the others,
