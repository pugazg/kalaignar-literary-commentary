—water is needed in the cooking pot;  
I do not deny that truth!

Nor is there any change in the fact  
that rice and water are needed  
for the body to grow and for life to continue!

But I place an argument before your thought.  
Judge it without letting the needle of balance tremble even a little!

Suppose there is a government ruling a country—  
and that government becomes enslaved

to harmful deeds and low qualities,  
bringing such cruelty through a sceptre stripped of justice

that town and world suffer  
for want of water and rice.

Then where is the body? Where is life?  
The two separate from one another  
and turn into a corpse!

Therefore, the excellence of the rule  
that governs the people—

that is life to those people,  
and life to the land in which they live!

Though there may be many kinds of armies—  
spear-forces, bow-forces and more—

must not the king act swiftly and vigilantly?

To drive away the enmity of foreign lands with valour  
and shape his own land into a radiant country,

is not clear and capable governance necessary?

<div align="right">If, through laziness,</div>

the course of rule bends and slackens,  
where will rice be? Where will water be?

If an intelligent government itself becomes the life of the country,  
then water-wealth, land-wealth and every other wealth

will fill the land and sway in abundance—know this!” he said.

Hearing Mosikeeranar's words, all the poets  
accepted their defeat,

admitting their own arguments  
to be like dust.

“Rice is not life! Water is not life!”—  
like letters carved in stone—
