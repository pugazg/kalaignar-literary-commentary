—stubbornly followed him then and there like his shadow, mother!
The husband who would play the veena
from the crown of my head to the soles of my feet,
loosen my bodice and make me dance in delight,
has not returned. And so,
the bed has no trouble—but my eyes have no sleep!
I have even given up the work
of counting the hours upon the time-disc.
I thought my heart would seize and drag back to me
the one who pulls me close and bathes me in honeyed pleasure.

Did my heart decide it should not return alone,
and stay behind so that it might come together with my lord?
Or did it discover that he is without love,
grow deeply distressed,
and now come slowly back toward me without strength?
Perhaps it came seeking me and looked closely,
but because pallor has spread over me,
because my body has wasted thin as a splinter,
it could not recognize my changed form.
Does it say, ‘She is some other woman—she is a stranger,’
turn away, and wander in search of me?
I do not know!”

When the gentle breeze scooped up
the fragrance stirred loose by swarming bees,
brought it here and blew it upon her with delight,
a woman in *Natrinai* voiced the sorrow of separation:
her bangles had loosened and fallen,
her husband—the warrior of honour—had gone away
and still had not returned;
and even her loving heart, which had followed him,
had not come to console her as grief spread over her.
