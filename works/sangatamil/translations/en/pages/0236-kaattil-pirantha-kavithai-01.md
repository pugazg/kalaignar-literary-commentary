# A Poem Born in the Forest!

There was a small village
in the Chola country
called Karaveeram.

There lived
a warrior
who displayed fierce valour
in battle.

Because countless men
had collided
with his hard shoulders
and been powdered
like sand of the sea,

Chola soldiers
who bore the tiger flag
praised and celebrated him
as
“Hard-Shouldered Karaveeran.”

He thought
engraving his name
only with the tip
of a battle-sword
was not enough.

He also wished
to pluck flower-like Tamil
and weave
a garland of poetry!

The warrior
who had been stroking
the sharpness of his spear

now sowed letters
and raised crops
called words.

He became ready
to harvest
the fine grains
of beautiful Tamil poetry
from a field of palm leaves.

Forms of nature
came before him
one after another,

longing:
“Create a song
about us!”

The round moon
of the heavens
embraced him
with wings of light
and nearly took his life,

saying:
“Count and write
of the star-eggs
I, the moon-hen,
have laid in the sky!”

The rising sun
came to file a complaint:

“Why hesitate
to write of me,
the flower of the east?”

Mountain ranges
where spotted peacocks roamed,

pouring waterfalls
like silver melted
and cooled,

leapt before him,
surrounded him,
and said:

“Drink deeply
of our beauty!”

A group of maidens,
throwing a ball
and playing,

while two other balls
swayed
and their slight smiles
could kill,

staged a protest:

“Write of the grace
of our deer-like eyes!”
