# The Warrior Who Guarded His Honour!

For the Kural that says,  
“Like the kavari deer that cannot live when its hair is lost,  
they give up life when honour is lost,”

the life of a warrior of the Tamil Sangam age  
stands as true witness, like butter rising to the top when curd is churned.

Even to kiss his crawling child  
he would not bend down;

he would ask that the child be lifted above his head  
and fondle it there—

that self-respecting Tamil lion!

Even his crowned queen,  
coming with two lotus buds in her hands,

he would embrace against his chest  
standing erect, without bending.

Never accustomed to looking at the ground,  
with his gaze straight ahead,

he would press his lips  
to the forehead of his moon-faced queen.

Even when lying on the soft bed,  
he would not curve like a question mark;

like an arrow fitted to a bow,  
he would sleep with his legs stretched out!

“Was he born with a backbone that would not bend?  
Was his heart a field in which the meaning of bowing  
could never grow?”

Many wondered, mouths open,  
finger pressed upon nose, and praised him!

The Chera king had legs like iron-hard arrow-wood;  
he was a peerless warrior descended from the Irumporai line.
