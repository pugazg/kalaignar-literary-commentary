The slender-waisted maiden followed,
grasped one of the chariot's posts,
and wept: “Is this young creeper,
leaping to spread over and embrace you,
to wither away?”

“I must go urgently to Uraiyur today!
I shall return here within a day or two!
There is no time to speak with you now;
then we can open our hearts and speak fully!” he said.

Realizing that he was brushing her aside,
Narkannai wept against the chariot:
“My lord! Hear the song surging and sounding
inside this heart that performs penance for you,” she said—and sang!

“Along the seashore are thazhai trees,
their leaves crowded with thorns like the long horns
on the face of a shark, and rough like the body of the ira-fish;
their buds ripen like the tusk of an old elephant,
then bend their heads like does among deer,
spreading fragrance across joyous festival grounds.
O chief of a warrior clan that plants the flag of victory even there!
Your charioteer drives swiftly
your long chariot with its swaying bells,
while you tell me that you will return after a few days!
Know this:
separated from you,
this woman will not live even a single day now!”

Through the sad music of Narkannai,
the chariot that had begun moving beside the house
heard Narkilli's command;
the horses broke into a four-legged gallop,
and in a moment it flew down the middle of the street and vanished!
Ignoring the dust raised by the horses,
she ran behind it.
Porkodi came and tried to stop her, but it was no use!
With anklets lamenting, she kept running!
