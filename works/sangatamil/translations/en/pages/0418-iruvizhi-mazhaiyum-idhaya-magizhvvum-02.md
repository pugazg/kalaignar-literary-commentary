I stood waiting for him to come back after hewing down the enemy.
My son, who trampled the bodies of cruel foes
as though trampling heaps of tubers,
fell as though a mountain had fallen;
with mountain-high fame he has taken up residence
in the history of this clan.

(Opening)

You see the rain from my two eyes;
will you also see the joy in my heart!

(Verse 2)

Honour was the lullaby my son heard;
death was the game he played!
Even when he was a milk-drinking child,
I taught him the art of holding the spear.
A son is to his father like the hanging root of a banyan;
so that the enemy, with a mind as stunted as a goat's tail,
should not seize our sceptre, he surged in fury,
stripped their hide and scattered it, and dies in battle—
he rules over great fame!

(Opening)

You see the rain from my two eyes;
will you also see the joy in my heart!

(Thogaiyara)

Stroking my hair, grey as a heron's wing, the handsome one asked,
“Open your mouth and bless my going, Mother!
Here is the broad battle-arm and the sword—where is the enemy
who came for war?”
He roared like a surging sea!
“Go, my son,” I said;
“He went and has not returned—what shall I do?
He planted a banner atop fresh glory,”
and a smile flashes upon my face!
With joy shining in my eyes,
the tears that well up, they say,
surpass the raindrops
that gather and spill
from tall-growing bamboo trees!
