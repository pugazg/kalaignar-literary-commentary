# Pisiranthaiyar 2

Because the language spoken was Tamil,  
and because the one who spoke was the great Pisiranthai,

the king not only gave his pledge  
that rule would henceforth proceed without stain,

he also kept the word he had given  
and made his fame blossom upon the earth.

Pisiranthai—  
a great light of clear, refined Tamil,

who became a lamp of wisdom  
for Arivudai Nambi—

held toward the Chola king Kopperuncholan  
an affection impossible to weave fully into words.

His gift for poetry,  
his way of honouring the learned,

his generosity praised across the world,  
his guarding of the country like an eyelid guards the eye,

his noble heart surpassing even the cool moon—

these qualities dwelling in Kopperuncholan  
deeply drew the poet whose heart was like cool water.

One day in Pisir, his birthplace,  
in a grove thick with shade,

the principled poet rested  
while turning over in thought  
many matters worthy of reflection.

At that moment he saw  
a swan-cock spreading its wings  
and flying across the open sky.

A desire arose in him  
to compose a poem about his dear friend, the Chola king—
