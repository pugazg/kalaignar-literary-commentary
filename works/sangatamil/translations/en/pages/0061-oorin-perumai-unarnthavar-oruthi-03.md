—a hunter lay embraced by sleep,  
even the hand that held his spear relaxed.

His companion, whose eyes were like sleek fish,  
wanted to lie beside him in place of the spear.

She stepped softly out of the house—  
that woman with lips sweet as jaggery—

and looked intently toward the vine-covered shade.

She wished to sit beside her weary husband  
like a flower, without a sound;

to feast her hungry eyes upon his handsome form;

if only, a little later, she could turn  
those two lotus buds toward his broad shoulders,  
that would be enough;

yet if, in haste, she placed a budding kiss  
upon the lips beneath his young moustache,

he might wake.

The man had hunted elephants  
and slept exhausted.

To disturb his rest, she thought,  
would not be good for his health.

Let his full sleep end first;  
after that, pleasure could continue

without ending until the morning sun rose.

Standing at a distance, that peahen-like woman  
smoothed her dark hair,

which rose wave after wave in the breeze,  
and stood absorbed in thought.

Her painted breasts rose and rose,  
taking away her breath,

then settled and rose again,  
revealing the desire that would not subside.

If she boldly went and fell upon him,  
the guilt of disturbing his good sleep  
would trouble her heart!

## Therefore

She decided to go near him,  
lie down without moving,

and when his weariness had passed  
and he awoke and turned,

to stroke his face with beautiful fingers,  
calling him “my lord,”

and give him a pearl-like kiss.

Planting upon dry fallen leaves  
her feet, worn and hardened by forest paths,

as softly as flowers,  
she began to walk toward the hunter

who slept with eyes closed,  
her navel's spiral uncovered,

moving like fragrant spring itself!
