Before he could reach a conclusion after thinking in this way, the poet reflected, tears budding in both eyes:

“My kinsfolk and household depend upon me. It would not be just to leave them alone and escape by myself! I cannot cut away, as one snips a thread, this circle of kin that stands binding my legs. ‘Living together is the fitting principle,’” he sang joyfully in Tamil. Long live, long indeed live, those who sought it out and compiled it!

> “Like a deer driven by one man
> across a long white barren tract like a hide flung down,
> one could run and save oneself;
> but life with kin binds the feet.”

*

(*Purananuru*: poem 193  
Poet: Oreruzhavar)

## Explanation of meaning:

*Athazh* = hide.  
*Erinthanna* = as though turned upside down and laid there.  
*Neduven kalar* = a long, open barren tract.  
*Aattum pulvay* = a deer being chased.  
*Okkal vazhkkai* = living together with one's kin.  
*Thatkum* = binds and restrains.
