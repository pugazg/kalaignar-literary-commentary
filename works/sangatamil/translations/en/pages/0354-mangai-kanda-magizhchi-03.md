—if the sky suddenly darkened and rain came; if that rain poured as though the sky itself had been torn open; what joy would arise in the farmer withered because his field had dried, in the people who had wept because prosperity had vanished, and in the women taking their pots to draw water, rejoicing that fish would now leap again in the ponds!

“My lady, that same measure of joy is about to come to you,” said the friend.

“Why, girl?” asked the slender-armed woman, stroking her friend's chin.

“Honey-sweet is the news I am about to tell you. Listen, my lady! Your lover is coming to marry you—there, the chariot he rides is hurrying toward us!” she said.

*

> “........
> when the country was gripped by famine and the plough slept,
> in the long summer morning stripped of green abundance,
> when every pond, with banks that looked like hills,
> could not give the birds a place to settle,
> in that blazing season, filling the vast pond and helping it,
> a great rain poured in the moist dawn;
> all the joy in which the many rejoiced
> has poured into me in just that way—
> for from the far land of lofty mountains,
> rising high and touching the sky,
> the lord of the hills has come!”

*

(*Akananuru*, poem 42, *Kalitriyanai Nirai*  
Poet: Kapilar)

## Explanation of meaning:

**varam** = famine.  
**naanjil** = plough.  
**paithu** = lush green abundance.  
**kottam** = pond.  
**sekkal** = staying / resting.  
**veesi** = giving help.  
**vantha maaru** = he is coming to marry her.
