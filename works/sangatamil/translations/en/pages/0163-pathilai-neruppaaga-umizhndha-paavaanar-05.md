(This piece was written
on the basis of the poems
composed in the *Purananuru*
by the poet
“Uraiyur Enicheri Mudamosiyar”

about the chieftain
“Vel Ay Andiran,”
who ruled Aykudi
near the Podhigai hills:

*Purananuru* poems
128, 130, 131, 132, and 134.)

<div align="center">*</div>

> * “A monkey upon
> the great branch of a jackfruit tree
>
> strikes the tightly bound drum
> hung there by supplicants,
>
> mistaking its clear face
> for a fruit.
>
> At the sound,
> the male swan answers and cries out.
>
> In rain-creeping Podhigai
> of ankleted Ay,
>
> dancing women may approach;
> for kings of rising might
> to approach is difficult indeed!”

<div align="right">

(*Purananuru* — Poem 128)

</div>

> * “Ay of shining gems
> and curved ornaments!
>
> Does each young female elephant
> in your land
> bear ten calves at once?
>
> For those who come
> singing of you and your mountain,
>
> you hide not your pleasant face
> but gladly give lordly elephants.
>
> Count them:
> they are more numerous
> than the spears
>
> the Kongars cast away
> when you drove them
> toward the western sea!”

<div align="right">

(*Purananuru* — Poem 130)

</div>

> * “Lord of the great mountain
> where rain-clouds gather,
>
> Andiran of the sharp sword,
> wearing a garland
> like a plantain flower—
>
> have these lovely forests,
> so full of elephants,
> perhaps sung of your hill?”

<div align="right">

(*Purananuru* — Poem 131)

</div>

> * “…
> …
> In the north stands
> sky-touching Himalaya.
>
> If in the south
> there were no Aykudi,
>
> would not this flower-faced world
> lose its balance?”

<div align="right">

(*Purananuru* — Poem 132)

</div>
