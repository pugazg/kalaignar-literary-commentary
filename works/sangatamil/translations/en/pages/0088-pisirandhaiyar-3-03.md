—and fall into the pit called division,  
which kills without killing.

That too is a trait  
found among the Tamil people!”

Thinking thus as he walked,  
Pisiranthaiyar tried on the way

to learn what had happened  
in the dispute in the Chola country.

He heard that the learned men of the town  
had gathered in a public hall

established by Kopperuncholan.

His heart beating with a commotion  
that words could not contain,

he went there to discover  
whether the smoke of war had cleared.

The poets Poththiyar, Boothanaganar, Naththathanar,  
and Pullatrur Eyitriyanar

leapt up and came running from the hall,  
embraced Pisiranthaiyar to their chests,

and rejoiced, crying,  
“Come! Come!”

“My limbs tremble and my heart trembles, sir.

What happened to the war  
between the lion and its cubs?”  
the poet asked, standing like a question mark.

“Nothing ended in disaster. Listen!

A danger arose,  
and this great man sprang like lightning  
and prevented it.

He asked, ‘Should the hen panic  
because its chicks tread upon it?’

With tender Tamil poetry  
he cooled even Kopperuncholan

and became the reason  
the war was stopped.

This is Pullatrur Eyitriyanar,”  
Poththiyar introduced him.

Pisiranthaiyar leapt with delight.

Then, in that gathering  
of fine poets rich in the power of speech,

many displayed poetic skill  
and let fall fruits of thought.

One poet looked at Pisiranthaiyar and asked:

“Poet! Your hair and beard  
have not lost their dark colour.

You are past sixty,  
yet there is not even the slightest greying.

How can this be?”
