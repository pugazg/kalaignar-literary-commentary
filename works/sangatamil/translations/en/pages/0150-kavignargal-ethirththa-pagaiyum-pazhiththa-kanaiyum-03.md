—the farming people labour on  
without losing heart.

Every victory  
your warriors shape at the battlefront

is impossible  
without the support

of the plough-front.

So let the clan of warriors live  
by honouring the clan of farmers!”

Thus Vellaikkudi Naganar  
offered the king

both counsel  
and blessing.

“When the whole land  
has grown thin from drought

like the waist  
of a dancing maiden,

Killivalavan of the rich land  
encircled by the Kaveri

protects us  
by pouring a rain of compassion.”

Thus the poet Nalliraiyanar  
praises him

with a graceful simile.

“Even if nature’s fury  
were to destroy this whole world,

why should we fear  
while King Killi remains?”

Erukkattur poet Thayang Kannanar  
strung together

countless pearl-like poems  
of praise.

Researchers say  
he was a great Tamil poet

who lived near Kulikkarai  
in Thanjavur.

Thus—

Avur Moolankizhar,  
Alathur Kizhar,

Aaduthurai Maasathanar,  
Aiyur Mudavanar,

Nalliraiyanar,  
Marokkathu Nappasalaiyar,

Erukkatturar,  
Idaikkadanar,

Vellaikkudi Naganar,  
and Kovur Kizhar—

all these tigers gathered around  
and praised the lion.

Yet that famous lion himself  
went seeking a spotted deer

and honoured him  
in a *puram* poem.

In Sirukudi,  
a small village of the Chola country,

Killi had a friend  
who was like cooling shade.

That great elder Pannan

gave away as charity  
to the poor

every grain of paddy  
harvested from his fields.

When Killi learned this,  
his heart rejoiced deeply  
and he went there.

He gave him the title  
“Physician of the Disease of Hunger”—
