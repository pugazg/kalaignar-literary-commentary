Another piece of news: parts of the lands called *kurinji* and *mullai* change into *paalai* when there is no rain!

Another: even if one must die, yielding one's body as prey to birds, the honour of confronting the enemy is the glory of the warrior clan!

And surpassing them all is the wondrous news that even under the rule of kings, every small village and great town shone as a cradle of the principle of people's rule—proclaiming the ancient greatness of the noble Tamils and the distinction inherited through our tradition!

With so much news, can there be any measure to the delight that wells up from the new light this poem gives? Is there yet another language in this world with the power to stand as an equal to our literary wealth?

*

> “..........................
> ..........................
> when the thunder-spewing sky has withdrawn, everywhere
> lies an old scorched waste from which settlements have moved away;
> from a cord-bound pot, those who take the palm leaves,
> the record-keepers who inspect the seal and break it—
> as life departs from the fearless warriors who have crossed good battle,
> red-eared vultures thrust in and pull from the belly,
> fearsome to behold;
> if one goes along the stony, difficult path toward the small village,
> beneath the mottled shade of the *itti* tree,
> in the darkening evening stirred by the evening wind,
> Pittan, commander of the Chera king, broad-armed with a curved bow,
> exulting in unfailing liquor,
> raised in hard battle against hostile kings
> a well-shaped, leaf-like spear—
> like that, her tear-streaming eyes
> bring unbearable pain!”

*

(*Akananuru*: poem 77  
Poet: Marudhan Ilanaganar)
