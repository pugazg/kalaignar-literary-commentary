If those words truly have the strength to protect me so, I shall call them good!

On a fierce rock scorched by the sun, imagine a man who has no hands and cannot speak, watching cow's butter melt. He cannot cry out, ‘Come, save the butter that is wasting away on the rock!’ Nor can he rush forward, take it away and keep it safe. He can only stand there, a helpless guard who watches with his eyes. Like that, you too keep loudly delivering lengthy counsel to me while I melt in the anguish of separation from love!”

Realising that no more counsel should be hurled like a spear into the heart of Vanangamudi—the ploughman whose plough is the bow, the crest-jewel of warriors—burning in the heat of separation, his friend Thuyan took leave and went away!

*

> “Dear friend who rebukes me! If your counsel
> can keep this body from wasting, that is well; if not—
> beside a hot rock scorched by the sun,
> like butter guarded only by the eyes of a handless mute,
> melting away,
> this illness has spread; it is hard to endure.”

*

(Kuruntokai: poem 58  
Poet: Velliveethiyar)

## Explanation of meaning:

*Nungkurai* = your counsel. *Aagam* = body.  
*Nirukkal* = stopping. *Vevvarai marungu* = beside a heat-filled rock.  
*Kaiyil ooman* = a handless mute.  
*Unangal* = melting away. *Nondrukolal* = enduring.
