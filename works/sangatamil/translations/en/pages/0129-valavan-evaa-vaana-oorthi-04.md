In this *puram* poem,  
shining like a diamond,

the phrase appears:

“a sky-vehicle  
not driven by a *valavan*.”

Does that not allow us to think  
that sky-vehicles driven by a *valavan*

may also have been in use  
in that age?

Since there was early concern  
for science,

perhaps people called *valavar*  
may even have guided vehicles

through the pathways  
of the sky!

Then, midway,  
old-fashioned and backward thinking

invaded like an army;

reason was left without work,

and this land  
fell into ruin!

In the counsel  
that poet Mudhukannan Sathanar

placed before the Chola king,

this one detail  
caught my eye.

So I have put it before you too:

read it, examine it,  
and tell me what you think.

If it is wrong, correct me.  
I shall accept it!

<div align="center">*</div>

> * “The lotus born in mud,  
> its hundred petals full and bright—
>
> many are those born  
> in noble lineages without distinction,
>
> but only a few possess  
> both spoken fame and song.
>
> Many vanish  
> like lotus leaves.
>
> Those whose fame poets sing,  
> after completing their deeds,
>
> are said to reach  
> a sky-vehicle
>
> not driven  
> by a charioteer.
>
> I have heard it said so,  
> my lord, Sechenni Nalangilli!
>
> Waning is true;  
> waxing is true;
>
> dying is true;  
> birth is true.
>
> Show these truths  
> even to those who do not know them,
>
> in this world  
> where the moon travels newly in the sky.
>
> Whether they are able  
> or skilled,
>
> look toward those  
> who come to you in distress
>
> and be able to show mercy.
>
> Let those without mercy,  
> who know only refusal,
>
> be your enemies  
> who oppose your unfailing strength.”
