The young bull said,  
“Tomorrow we leave.”

She wrapped herself  
around his shoulder,

clung to him,  
and departed.

When the parents learned the news,  
they were as though they had stepped  
on glowing fire.

The nurse-mother set out  
to search for that painted beauty.

The land where Mullai and Kurinji  
had changed

was the Paalai,  
a wasteland piled with hardship.

Thinking that the girl  
with bow-like brows

and the dark, stone-like young man  
must have crossed it,

the nurse walked quickly  
in anxiety.

There she saw  
pure-hearted elders

making their endless journey,

carrying staffs on their shoulders  
from which hanging slings swung.

These mendicants,  
whose minds never left God,

were stopped  
in the middle of the road  
by the nurse.

“O righteous men  
called Brahmins!

In this red-hot wasteland,

did you see  
our precious daughter

going like an *anril* bird  
with a handsome youth

who leaps like a calf?

They had rejoiced  
in a secret love-life.

Now they have dared  
to enter all the ways  
of worldly life.

Great men of rich hearts!  
Good men!

Did you see them  
on the road?

Tell me!”

Her eagerness overflowed.

They replied:

“Mother,  
we did see

the maiden  
who crossed beyond your protection.

And it is fitting indeed  
that that red-lotus beauty

joined the brave Tamil youth  
who is like a rooster.

Instead of blessing them  
from your whole heart  
and sending them on,

it is strange  
that you too have climbed  
hill and mountain  
to search for them!

Listen to one thing.

Though sandalwood  
is born in the mountain—

<div align="right">it</div>

gives its fragrance  
to the one who applies it  
and delights in it.
