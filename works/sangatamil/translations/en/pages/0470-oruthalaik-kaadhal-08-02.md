“Now it is only a lifeless body speaking with you, child!
This Saathanthai is only spent cane with no juice left!
A top without its string cannot spin;
your heart, learned in poetry, will understand my state, child!”

Streams poured from his eyes,
and the silver hairs of his beard held the white pearls of those tears.
Seeing the poet tremble as though blame itself had come upon him,
Narkannai mixed calm into her words and began to speak:

“No, poet; bid farewell to your sorrow.
Saathanthai shall be a deathless father to Tamil!
Heaps of gold and jewels, mountains of diamonds, vaidurya and pearls,
lofty mansions and towers, fortress-gates,
the rule of a victory-parasol before which people bow and praise,
and spectacles that seize the heart through hymns of acclaim—
these may be here today and gone tomorrow!
But is not service rendered to Tamil a work that lasts forever?
Therefore,
you shall live as long as Tamil lives—and
Tamil shall live as long as this earth endures!” said Narkannai!

“Good, child! All the rest of my life
I shall spend keeping Tamil from slowly dying!
Poet-maiden who fashions words that conquer—
but must you alone go away following Killi?”

Ashamed, not knowing how to answer the poet's question,
she immediately changed the subject:
“For what purpose has Killi gone to Uraiyur?”

“Between Tittan and Killi
a battle of principles took place.
A hurt arose between father and son,
and that gave an opening to their enemies, child!

1. For the white conch to resound,
for the victory drum to thunder,
for rows of lamplight raised by spear-eyed maidens
to be seen in the swiftly arriving evening—”
