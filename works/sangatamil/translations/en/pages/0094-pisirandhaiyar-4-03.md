—they become closely bound together.

So too, King Chola was in Uraiyur,  
and the great poet Pisiranthai  
in the Pandya land.

Though born separately, many leagues apart,  
like green gold and pearl,

once they became an ornament of friendship  
they were like jewels resting side by side.

They became hero-stones—  
a lamp of friendship that will never go out!

The worthy will always be found  
beside the worthy.

Those without worth  
will fall from history like dry leaves!”

Kannaganar's poem  
is not only a cup of honey

for the Chola king  
and poet Pisiranthaiyar;

for all who form friendships  
with pure hearts,

it is clear nectar  
that never cloys.

> * “In the good southern land  
> of the king who owns the Podhigai hills  
> there is one called Pisiron—  
> the guardian of my life.
>
> He may not stand here in the days of prosperity;  
> in the days of distress  
> he will not fail to come.”

<div align="right">

(*Purananuru* — Poem 215  
Poet: Kopperuncholan)

</div>

> * “Gold, coral, pearl,  
> and the lovely gem born of the enduring great mountain  
> may lie far apart from one another.
>
> Yet when strung together  
> to make an ornament of rare value,  
> they appear in one place.
>
> So too, the worthy belong with the worthy;  
> those who are not worthy  
> belong with those like themselves.”
