# The Chera Who Sang of a Warrior!

Adiyaman of Thagadoor planned
to bring under his rule Thirumudikkari,
the Malaiyaman of Thirukovalur
who ruled on the bank of the South Pennai,
and he succeeded in defeating him.
King Thirumudikkari escaped
and sought refuge with the Chera of Karur.
The Chera too came forward to support Kari.
On the battlefield the fourfold armies
sent by the Pandyas and Cholas
to aid Adiyaman Neduman
came rushing in—but it was of no use.
Even if the capital of Thagadoor could no longer be defended,
Adiyaman chose to remain a Tamil who guarded his self-respect,
and gave his life on the field.

The Chera of Karur entered Tamil history
as Peruncheral Irumporai, ‘he who destroyed Thagadoor.’
Not only did he smash enemies with his broad shoulders;
he also gave Tamil his shoulder,
lifting Sangam literature and raising it high.

Apart from the time he spent fighting with sword in hand,
in the remaining hours he took up the writing stylus
and shaped countless poems.
More than thirty pearl-and-gem poems
shine in the *Kalithogai* touched by his hand.
Because he sang the *Paalai Kali*,
that Peruncheral Irumporai was also praised
and called “Perungadungko who sang of the Paalai.”
In the *Pathitrupathu*,
the poet Arisil Kizhar placed him
as the ruler celebrated in the eighth decade.
Many tongue-fragrant poems he composed
are found in the *Natrinai*, the fine *Kuruntokai*,
and in love-fragrant *Akananuru*.
The fame-fragrant Chera king
ruled not only the precious Tamil land,
but also an imperishable wealth of Tamil.
