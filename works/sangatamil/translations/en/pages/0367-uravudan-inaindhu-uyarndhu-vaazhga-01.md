# Live High, United with Kin!

Countless poets whose personal names are unknown live on in Sangam literature in the form of verse! One among them bears the name “Orer Uzhavar”—the One-Plough Farmer! The way he uses a simile in a *Kuruntokai* poem to express the heartbeat of a hero who rushes in anguish to reach his sweet beloved is wonderful!

At a time when good rain has filled the wet and dry fields, a farmer who owns only a single plough, unable to plough and plant the crop with four or five ploughs, wrings his hands, his heart pounding, and wilts with distress. In just that way, unable to see his lovely beloved at once, that hero stood with his mind in turmoil! Because he made the one-plough farmer the object of his simile, this poet came to be known as “Oreruzhavar”!

The poet, learned in pure Tamil, had many who were his own—kinsfolk, relatives, parents. For a long time a burden had lain upon his heart, pressing him hard and causing him worry!
