With the fine Tamil name  
Cheraman Kanaikkal Irumporai,  
he took up the sceptre.

The port of Tondi was his capital—  
the Yavanas called it “Tindis”!

He defeated the crowned king called Moovan,  
smashed his jaw, tore out his teeth,

and, it is said, fixed them upon the great lock  
on the gate of his fort.

The poet Poigaiyar, who sang the *Kalavazhi Narpadu*,

<div align="right">—of his</div>

battle-glory, speaks in many ways and rejoices!

Unity among Tamils is like the aththi flower,  
like the crescent moon of Aadi—

<div align="right">and so</div>

by waging war among themselves  
and splitting the Tamil people,

they became lines in the pages of history

<div align="right">that tell</div>

that story;  
to display Tamil sword-skill

<div align="right">they divided in two!</div>

The Chola called Sengannan  
was a leopard on the battlefront!

Winning everywhere,  
he let the tiger flag fly in the sky of fame!

Hearing of it, the Chera king  
rose with a roar!

His arrayed chariots, horses, men and great army  
followed his command.

“Will my valour ever fade?  
Can the victory the Chola has won

stand before these mighty shoulders of mine?”  
roared Kanaikkal Irumporai,

and appeared like light  
tearing open the night!

Rough elephants with foreheads like war-drums  
charged against one another;

the linchpins of royal chariots broke loose  
and shattered into a thousand pieces!
