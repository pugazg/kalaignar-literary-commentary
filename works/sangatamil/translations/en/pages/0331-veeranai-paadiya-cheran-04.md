“I have obtained the benefit I sought, O king. Good! Good!
If I may hear the *Puram* poem you have shaped,
bathing my son in honey-dripping Tamil
so that his fame will shine as long as this world remains,
then I, still alive,
will bless you as though golden bracelets
had been fastened upon the hands that raised him,”
said the old man.

“My steel-hearted warrior,
to defeat the army that came against him,
without the slightest hesitation even to give his precious life,
received javelins, spears and swords upon himself.
Though countless arrows swarmed over his body
and split his chest in two,
though he fell,
the enemies who surrounded him front and rear
finally took the life of my sugarcane-sweet one.
They shattered even his shield that stopped weapons;
they tore his flesh to shreds like cotton
and scattered it across the directions of the battlefield.
You asked, sir, where he is.
Know this: because of the valour he displayed here,
he has left behind life upon this earth
and gone to live upon the tongues of poets,”
said the king.

Hearing the earth-ruling king's *Puram* song proclaim
that his son now flew on wings across the sky of fame,
the old man thanked him with a flashing smile
and walked away with a new stride—a tiger's stride.
