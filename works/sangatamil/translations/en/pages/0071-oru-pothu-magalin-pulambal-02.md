Ask Vaanan's wife,  
who mocks and blames me!

Who was it that begged like a supplicant,  
saying, “Let us sing songs of pleasure and rejoice”?

What did Vaanan say to me  
when he came with a wounded heart?

“The woman who became my companion in bed  
has become a full-time guard beside the child's cradle!

For many days I have suffered, starving.  
Today I have dared to seek you out and drink pleasure!

Embrace me and drive away this fever;  
loosen the cloth across your breast  
and cure this sickness of desire,” he said.

I agreed.

He shook out upon me  
a purse swollen with gold!

“I may be a courtesan,  
but once I have set my attachment upon you,

you will know my worth.  
First quench this thirst,” I said.

Like a hungry child rushing to the rice on a plate  
and overturning it in haste,

he sprang upon me in a frenzy.

I trembled in fear—  
yet, trusting the art familiar to me,

I stood his equal  
in the contest of the bed!

Jackfruit flesh, honeyed plantain, millet flour—  
we shared many kinds of delight  
and rejoiced for many days!

**Then suddenly, one day—**

perhaps because he had grown weary,  
or because he learned that Thirumagal, his wife,

was searching for him—

Vaanan mounted his chariot and left.  
He never returned, my friend!

He did not merely give me wealth;  
he stood beside me like a shaft of light

driving away the darkness  
of being called a public woman.

Look at the pattern of rolling time—

a tongue twists  
like a curling knife—
