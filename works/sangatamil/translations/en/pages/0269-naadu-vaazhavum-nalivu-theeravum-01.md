# So That the Country May Live and Distress May End…

Thondaiman Ilanthiraiyan,
king of Kanchi,
possessed both the power to compose poetry
and a strong sense of duty.

There is a story
that the ancestors of this guardian
came by sea.

The Chola Killi,
immersed in love,
and the Naga princess Peeli Valai
had a son—the sweet fruit Ilanthiraiyan.

*Manimekalai* says
that after surviving a shipwreck at sea,
that great warrior made Kanchi his capital
and wore the crown of Thondai Nadu.

It also says
that because a thondai creeper
was tied to him
as he reached the shore,
the epithet “Thondaiman” came to him.

And since *thirai* means wave,
and he floated upon those waves
and came to become a young king
who ruled the land,
he received the special name
“Ilanthiraiyan.”

Nakkeerar, in the commentary on *Iraiyanar Akapporul*,
is said to mention a work called *Ilanthiraiyam*
as one composed by him.

Avvai is also said
to have come as envoy
on behalf of Adiyaman Neduman Anji
and argued her case
before this same Thondaiman.

Mayilainathar,
who wrote a commentary on *Nannul*,
says that the place called Nallur—“Thiraiyanathur”—
was named after him.

Three poems in *Natrinai*
are examples
of Ilanthiraiyan's learned imagination.

Many kings who ruled countries
were his friends
and would come as guests
to his palace.
