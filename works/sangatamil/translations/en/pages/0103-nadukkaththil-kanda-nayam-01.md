# Grace Found in Trembling!

Savouring the courtesan's body  
as though it were jaggery,

he had no time even for the blink of an eye  
to remember that he had a home,

or that there a guileless woman  
was shedding tears for him,

worn thin by worry.

Like a bull caught in a snare,  
he kept discovering one after another

all the arts of union and pleasure,  
drinking from the section on desire  
in great draughts!

One might wish  
that Valluvar be born once more

to write a few more Kurals  
for the section on pleasure—

so sweetly, so freshly,  
the two kept digging out new treasures!

Two bodies became one;  
they rolled joined together,

wet with lips,  
entwined and turning!

Had the honey finally cloyed?  
Or had the sky of his heart cleared?

He mounted his chariot and left,  
to return and comfort his own lady.

Like some lost possession  
coming back by force

and landing in both one's hands,

the husband she thought had forgotten her  
returned like a parrot

to a cage whose door had been opened.

“My worry has flown away!”  
the chaste wife cried,

holding her friend's shoulder  
and leaning against her  
so she would not fall from joy.

The courtesan, meanwhile,  
longed to see again

the hero who had returned  
to the heroine's house.

So she devised a trick.

She called the heroine's friend  
to her own house

and began telling her  
about something that happened

when she had lived  
in intimate union with the hero—
