They approached a poet and pleaded:

“Let the morning sun rise;  
take up the duty of driving away this black darkness!”

That poet was named Aandhai,  
born in the little Pandya village called Pisir.

In the proud pages of history  
he came to be known as Pisiranthaiyar,

a man who walked with dignity  
and became a model for friendship.

Though he had never seen the king Kopperuncholan face to face,  
he had heard of his great fame

and had chosen in his own heart  
to become that king's friend.

In his native Pandya land  
the people had become weary,

suffering and staggering under hardship.

Declaring his confidence  
that he could help change their condition,

he courageously set out  
to meet the ruler Arivudai Nambi.

The king of the land, Nambi,  
heard that the poet Pisiranthai was coming.

Fearing that slighting the poet-king  
would bring great harm,

and because the culture of honouring Tamil  
had not diminished in him,

he received the Tamil poet with fitting respect  
and bowed in greeting.

“I rejoice at the honour you show refined Tamil,  
but is it right, king,

to turn the just sceptre  
into a cruel one?

Those beside you, speaking of bonds and kinship,  
burn the people and the good like torches!

You must learn to recognise  
the officials who make the king spend every day in pleasure

and then take politics into their own hands  
to play with it.

O king, I have come  
to offer a few counsels—

to prevent your destruction,  
and above all,

to guard forever from ruin  
the good name of our country.

Will you accept them?”

he asked.
