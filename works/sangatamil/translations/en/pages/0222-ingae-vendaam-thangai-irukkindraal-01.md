# Not Here… My Younger Sister Is Here!

With teeth
that smiled
like cucumber seeds,

and baby-speech
sweet
like the nectar
inside a flower,

a young treasure
of refined Tamil

played
upon a stretch
of white sand.

Closing
her flower-eyes,

using
her flower-hands,

she buried
punnai fruits
in the sand.

One of them
she forgot

and left behind.

<div align="right">That one—</div>

sprouted
and grew

into a flourishing
young tree.

The beautiful maiden
poured ghee
and milk

at the roots
of the punnai

and raised it.

The mother
had raised
her beautiful daughter;

the daughter
raised the punnai
and rejoiced.

One day,
the mother
looked lovingly
at her picture-like daughter
and said:

“My child,
every day
you tend this punnai

as though
it were a festival.

It has grown
alongside you,

risen,
and now gives shade.

It is like
your own younger sister!

I say this
with joy.”

The Tamil maiden
drank in those words
and tasted
a rare delight.

Every atom
of her mind
thrilled.

She leapt up,
embraced
the dear punnai,
and said:

“From now on,
you are
my younger sister.

<div align="right">My</div>

mother herself
said so!”

She stood there,
enchanted.

As the maiden's face
opened like a flower,

a hand suddenly
covered her eyes
from behind

and startled her.

At once,
the bright-eyed woman
understood:

it was the lord
of the rich neythal land,

where right-spiralled conches
raise music,

closing
the eyelids
of his clan's creeper

and embracing her!
