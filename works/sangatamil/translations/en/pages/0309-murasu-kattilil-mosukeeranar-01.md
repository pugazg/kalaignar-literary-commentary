# Mosukeeranar on the War-Drum Cot!

A waterfall beard,
strung like a garland of *thumbai* flowers
and left hanging!
A tall, fine body that had not lost
so much as a grain of its firmness,
thanks to the habit of eating *kambu* rice!
Beautiful sharp eyes
that could put even an arrowhead to shame!

There he comes—as though nectar-sweet Tamil itself
had taken the form of a rare poet and were approaching!
His name is Mosukeeranar;
even the breeze that brushes his body
seems to carry the fragrance of Tamil.
They say the three crowned kings—the Chera, Chola and Pandya—
held this king of poets in special regard.
Literature contains many histories
in which Tamil itself seems enthroned
upon the heads of the three crowned monarchs.

One of those three protectors of Tamil
was the Chera Irumporai who destroyed Thagadoor.
For Arisil Kizhar's eighth decade in the *Pathitrupathu*,
praising him and giving him delight,
the king announced as a gift to the poet
the whole land he ruled.
When Arisil Kizhar refused to accept it,
the king instead made him his minister,
to offer him moral counsel.

Because of the love he bore for classical Tamil,
the hand that held the sceptre
would at times take up the writing stylus as well
and create distinguished literature.

The elder poet Mosukeeranar,
like a bee coming to drink nectar from a flower-pot,
walked a very great distance
to meet the wise Chera king
and reached the royal palace.
