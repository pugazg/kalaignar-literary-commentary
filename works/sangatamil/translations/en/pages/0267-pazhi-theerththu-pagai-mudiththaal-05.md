“Until the lives
of those who blinded
my father, dear as life,
are gone,

I shall not eat
from a food-vessel.
I shall not change my clothing.

Until revenge is complete,
I shall know neither hunger nor sleep.

I shall end the feud!
I shall offer the enemies
as a sacrifice
for my father who lost his sight!”

Anni Minjili's vow
reached Thithiyan of Azhundur
the very next day.

Hearing of the sorrow
that had befallen the maiden,
he was distressed
and longed to see her.

Soon after the news entered his ear,
he saw Anni standing before him
like a tongue of red flame.

“Delight of the eye!
Woman suited to my thought!
I do not know
what medicine to give
the wound in your heart;
I do not know the way,”
he lamented.

“There is cavalry
inside your fort, king!
You have shoulders
like mountains, my dear!

If it is true
that you love me,
Anni Minjili,
then set out without hesitation
for war against the Kosars.

They have made my father blind.
Those unjust men
must be brought down.

I have come seeking
strong-shouldered Thithiyan
as the mongoose
that tears apart
snakes after blinding them,”
she said.

“Look!
My sword has risen from its sheath.
Know my vow too, beloved.

Only after taking the lives
of those who maimed
your father—my father-in-law—
will these hands touch you,”
roared the man of Azhundur.

The war-force set out.
The bodies of the wicked Kosars
were broken;
they fell dead
like crumbling walls.

Having fulfilled her vow
as she desired,
Anni Minjili leaned
upon the returning Thithiyan's shoulder.

The love that had budded
flowered that very day.

An *Akananuru* poem
is the basis for this story.
