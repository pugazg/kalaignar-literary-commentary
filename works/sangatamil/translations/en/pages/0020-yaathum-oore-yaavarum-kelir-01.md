# Every Place Is Our Town; Everyone Our Kin!

When I heard that the elder scholar Pandithamani  
Kathiresan Chettiar had been born in the great Mugavai district,  
before it was divided into three,

I went with delight to Mahibalanpatti.  
There, the great scholar Manickanar told me

that the name of the place recorded as Poongunram  
in the old literary work *Purananuru*  
had changed into Mahibalanpatti.

Hearing it, I was overwhelmed with joy.

The poet of that town, Kaniyan Poongundranar—  
I was invited to see a poem carved in a rock cave, and I went!

Was it carved by the hand of that ancient poet,  
or did later people carve the world-renowned poem?

Whoever may have done it, in that poem  
a Tamil of that distant age issued the call,

“Every place is our town; everyone our kin,”  
opening his full heart to declare

that the whole world encircled by the wide sea is our town,  
and that all people are our kin, like eye and light—

<div align="right">with a new</div>

light tearing apart evil darkness,  
uprooting the roots of difference and making a revolution!

Like a bee joining and mingling with blossoming Tamil,  
the poet who gave this earth a universal fruit of poetry

was Kaniyan, the poet born in Poongunram;  
when I say it, this eight-span body thrills and swells with pride!

In those days poets, in the courts of kings who ruled the world—
