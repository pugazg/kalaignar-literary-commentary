“—why would you stop me
from bathing in fire?

After my broad-shouldered king
has died,

for me
a lotus pond
and this pit full of flame
are one and the same!”

So spoke the queen—
and leapt into that fire!

<div align="center">*</div>

> “If I do not,
> raging like a lion,
> see their backs in flight,
>
> then let me be parted
> from this woman
> of splendid, battle-dark eyes.
>
> Let me place
> in an assembly
> that has turned from justice
> a man without worth,
>
> distort due order,
> and rule
> with a weakened, cruel sceptre.
>
> Let me lose
> the joyous laughter
> shared with my companions—
>
> Maavan,
> lord of unfailing wealth
> in the fertile settlements
> around famed Vaigai;
>
> Aandhai
> of the enduring fort;
>
> renowned Andhuvan Saathan;
> Aathan Azhisi;
> fierce Iyakkan;
>
> and all the others,
> dear to me
> like my own eyes
> in faithful friendship.
>
> And let me,
> leaving the ancient southern kingship
> that guards humankind,
>
> be born instead
> to guard
> another's harsh and barren land!”

<div align="center">*</div>

<div align="right">

(*Purananuru* — Poem 71  
Poet: Pandya king Bootha Pandiyan)

</div>

<div align="center">*</div>

> “O learned ones!
> O many learned ones!
>
> …………
> …………
>
> The dark-timbered funeral fire
> prepared in the great forest
> may seem hard to you;
> it is not hard for me.
>
> Since my broad-shouldered husband
> has died,
>
> the deep, dark lotus pond,
> its full petals opening
> after the bud has broken,
>
> and the fire—
> to me they are one.”

<div align="center">*</div>
