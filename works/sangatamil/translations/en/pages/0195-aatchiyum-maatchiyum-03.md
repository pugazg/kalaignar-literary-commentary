—like tender bamboo shoots
crushed beneath the feet
of towering elephants.

If he does not fall
before my broad-shouldered warriors

so that the earth itself
must bear him,

then let this fate
befall the garland
upon my own chest:

let it wither
in the embrace
of many courtesans,

women without love in the heart
who give pleasure
only through the body!”

Thus did the Chola
make his meaning clear
through his oath:

people of worth
do not seek
base courtesans.

Nalangilli Chola
left his fort.

Nedungkilli Chola
fell upon the battlefield
and perished.

<div align="right">Because of that—</div>

poets referred to him
as Nedungkilli
who died at Kariyaru.

There is also
a line of research
that says

Nedungkilli and Nalangilli
were brothers.

Even Kovur Kizhar
intervened
at the battlefront
and tried to stop them,

but it was of no use.

The words
of the cultured poet

<div align="right">did not enter their ears!</div>

Therefore,
Nalangilli also became
king of Uraiyur

and ruled
with an unblemished sceptre.

One day,
in the Aththani hall,

a debate grew
among learned men:

“Ruling a kingdom
is no easy thing.”

The king,
blessed with knowledge
and scholarship,

explained his view
of the system of rule
of that age.

Seeing his ability,
the learned men
of the assembly

praised him
and rejoiced.

> * “When crowned elders pass away
> and their descendant
> receives the chance
> to wear the crown
> and rule the land,
>
> I say the nature
> of that rule
> will match
> the nature of the ruler.
>
> For one
> who squeezes and wrings
> the people,
>
> heaps up taxes,
> and rules without vigour,
>
> the burden of government
> becomes too heavy
> even to lift—
