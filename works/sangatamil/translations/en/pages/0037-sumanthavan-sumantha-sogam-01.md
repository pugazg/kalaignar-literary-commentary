# The Sorrow Borne by the Woman Who Bore It!

Across a floor of green grass  
that guards eggs of dew,

a woman came walking  
as though a jewelled chariot were moving.

She was rich in learning,  
scattering words of fruitful Tamil;

in Tamil she was the elder sister  
of Kaakkaip Paadini and Ponmudiyar—

the radiant scholar, noble Avvai!

She sang the fame of  
Adiyaman Neduman Anji,

the generous Vel Pari of his land,  
Cheraman Maaven Kovan,

the Vazhuthi who gave Kaanapper,  
and the Chola Perunarkilli,

adorning Tamil  
with many ornaments.

Tamil Nadu was a land  
where protectors gave with reddened hands

like the morning sun!

If a man came carrying a palm-leaf message saying, “War!”  
without even waiting for the hour when garlands were worn,

the warrior would set aside the woman  
whose arms had embraced his shoulders,

sharpen his sword  
and raise it toward the sky—

that land of heroic warriors was

<div align="right">the mother-house.</div>

Among Tamils who divided their lands  
as paalai, kurinji, mullai, neydhal and marudham,

she made Tamil poetry bloom  
like flowers in a grove.

She sang of a homeland's unwavering chaste love;  
she sang of the valour of those who went to battle.

And of the hero who went to the field saying,  
“Let death come to me today,”

she wished that in literature  
he should remain fragrant like a block of sandalwood,

<div align="right">saying so,</div>

and again and again took pride  
in the community of mothers

whose bodies from foot to crown were cooled with feeling,  
while dewdrops vanished beneath the pressure of her flower-like feet upon the grass.
