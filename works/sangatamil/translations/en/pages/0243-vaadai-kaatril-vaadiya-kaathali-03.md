After the tips
of our two tongues
had joined
and drawn apart,

I said:
“My lord,
do not break
your word.”

With shining drops
at the corners
of both my eyes,

I sent him
on his way.

The marks
of his nails
and teeth,
fixed upon me
like crescents in the sky,
remain witnesses
to that scene!

What use
are so many witnesses?

What use
were all those oaths?

Though the cold-wind season
has arrived,

the prince has not returned
to unite in joy
with the woman
who is like tender coconut water
in summer.

And she,
her clothing slipping,
rolls in anguish.

“When he chose
the day of separation,

if I had stubbornly refused
and stopped him,

this body
would not now be burning
with the illness of love.

Should my eyes
not have known that
at the time?”

Thus,
the golden-statue woman
became angry
with her own eyes.

Let us see it here
as Sangam Tamil!

<div align="center">*</div>

> “Shameless indeed
> are these eyes of mine.
>
> They agreed
> to the appointed day.
>
> Now, as the sheath
> of a thick sugarcane bud
> loosens,
>
> like the swelling belly
> of a pregnant green snake,
>
> the cold north wind comes,
> bearing fine rain-drops
> dissolved from cloud;
>
> and for one
> whose lover has gone away,
> it is fire.”

Poet: Kazhark Keeran Eyitri)

“I say
these two eyes
have no shame!

When he asked leave,
promising to return
on the marked day,

they agreed,
‘Yes.’

What use is it now
to weep
like a river
flowing down
from the Himalaya?”
