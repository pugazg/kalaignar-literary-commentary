—today you become fiercely angry  
at the sight of morning!

Do not curse the dawn.

Night will come again  
to sing the music of pleasure,”  
said the friend.

“Shall we go  
to the waterfall to bathe?”  
asked the heroine.

“Why should I go there?

He will be there  
to keep you company,”  
said the friend.

> **(1)** “Morning, day,  
> the helpless evening,
>
> the night when the town sleeps,  
> and dawn—
>
> if lovers can recognise  
> these divisions of time,
>
> their love is false.
>
> If separation comes,
>
> I shall appear in the street  
> mounted on the palmyra *madal* like a horse;
>
> to be clearly reviled  
> is disgrace,
>
> and to go on living  
> is disgrace too!”

<div align="right">

(*Kuruntokai* — Poem 32  
Poet: Allur Nanmullaiyar)

</div>

> **(2)** “The rooster cried ‘Kukkoo!’
>
> At once my pure heart  
> started in fear:
>
> dawn had come  
> like a sword
>
> to separate me  
> from the lover
>
> who lay  
> embracing my shoulders!”

<div align="right">

(*Kuruntokai* — Poem 157  
Poet: Allur Nanmullaiyar)

</div>

## Explanation of meaning:

### (1)

**Oor-thunju yaamam** = the night-time when the town sleeps.  
**Pozhuthidai theriyin** = if the nature/divisions of time can be distinguished.  
**Maa** = horse.  
**Madal** = palmyra frond used for the *madal*.  
**Marugil** = in the street.

### (2)

**Thutkenru** = suddenly startled in shock.  
**Thol-thoy** = lying while embracing the shoulder.  
**Vaikarai** = dawn / early morning.
