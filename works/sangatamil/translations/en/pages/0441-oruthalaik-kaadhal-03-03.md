“tell me the truth,” she said!

“Mother, listen! I have written a rare poem.
I am planning to give it as a gift to Killi,
who comes to tomorrow's auspicious feast,
and so I am flying with wings across the sky of imagination!”
With that well-managed lie she sent her mother away,
then lay face-down, pressing her breast into the pillow!

Though Narkannai escaped by lying to her mother
that she had lost sleep because imagination would not conceive
and she could not compose the poem,
she did in fact inscribe upon a palm leaf
a poem like a golden gem,
to hint at her own condition.
She hid it inside the cloth across her breast
and waited ready to give it to Killi
the next day as soon as he came. Knowing none of this,
her mother believed her daughter's words
and nodded in the hall! Poor mother,
innocently unaware of her daughter's state!

*

Night opened its casket of eyelids,
and white waking day slowly peeped out.

At the appointed time, without fail, the town-rooster crowed!
In the eastern quarter, the rising sun too appeared!

*

In the kitchen of the sea-faring merchant's house,
five or six workers gathered and ran about in commotion!
“Let every leaf be a full plantain leaf,” said Mother!
“Let heaps upon heaps of fruit be piled up,” said Father!
“Fry the chopped meat until the ghee drips from it,” said the friend!
“Make the pepper gravy with chicken cuts fragrant enough
to perfume the tongue,” said Narkannai!
“Before putting the fragrant *seeraga samba* rice into the pot,
mix water and milk in equal halves,” said Mother!
“When the thick curd is lifted by hand, it should hold
like a ball of butter,” said Father!
