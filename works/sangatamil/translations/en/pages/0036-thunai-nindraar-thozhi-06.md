Just so,  
because you have delayed the day

on which you will wed this heroine,  
radiant like a lotus,

elderly men—like those cranes—  
are coming ahead of you to marry her!

If you wish to become like the parrot  
that waited for the ilavu fruit,

postpone the wedding for many more days!

If not, today itself  
fix the day on which you will marry this mullai flower!”

As soon as the friend said this,  
he started in alarm and cried:

“Is there any obstacle to wearing a pearl from the deep sea?  
Live long, friend! Live! Live indeed!

I have no thought now of waiting  
even for the passing of a naazhigai.

I will not let the vaalai fish  
become prey to an old bird circling above!

The wedding is tomorrow—  
let the arrangements begin!”

Ammuvanar, marvelling at the skill  
of the gracious friend

who spoke the truth without stating it directly  
and explained it through a simile,

gave us this fertile poem.  
Here it is:

<div align="center">*</div>

> * “Take her as yours within a very short time:  
> her beauty is like flourishing Tondi, where,  
> for food, an old crane unable to fly waits  
> for the fat fish brought by the fishermen of the great sea.”

<div align="center">*</div>

<div align="right">

(*Ainkurunuru* — Poem 180  
Poet: Ammuvanar)

</div>

## Explanation of meaning:

**Sirunani varaindhanai konmo** = make her yours within a very short time.

**Peruneer** = sea. **Parai-thabu mudhu-kurugu** = an old bird unable to fly.  
**Valaivar** = fishermen.

**Valsi** = food.
