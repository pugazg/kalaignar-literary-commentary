“He said
he would return
within the appointed day,

and that when
the cold became painful
we would be together.

But words written
upon water
do not remain.

In separation,
my heart grew heavy;
my desire burned!

Like the body
of a pregnant green snake,

the sheath of the bud
on thick-jointed sugarcane
opens.

The sky-maiden's upper garment,
cloud,
melts into rain-drops,

and the cold wind
called the north wind
makes me wither.

Even if I remove
my clothing,
heat will rise—

if only
he were near!

But it was
my own wicked eyes
that gave him permission
that day
to go away.

Why then
do they weep today
like an unceasing waterfall?

Unlearned eyes!
Bow down in shame
and look at the earth!”

The beauty of speech,
the beauty of eyes,
the great beauty
of pearl-like smiles,

the beauty of earth
that defeats
the beauty of groves—

because she had wept
until her face swelled,

she placed a hand
upon her cheeks
where tears streamed down
like hanging roots,

and sternly said:

“Eyes!
Be ashamed!”

The poet's heart
that shaped this event
into poetry—

is it not a spring
of imagination?

Is it not
a young southern breeze
spreading
the fragrance of Tamil?

## Explanation of meaning:

**Naan-ilam-anra** = they have become shameless.  
**Naal nerpu** = agreeing when he said he would return on the appointed day.  
**Nunnurai azhithuli** = fine drops of rain melting and scattering from cloud.  
**Pirindhisinorkku azal** = burning / weeping because the hero has gone away.  
**Kanaitha karumbu** = thick sugarcane.
