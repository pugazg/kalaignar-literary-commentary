—warriors bear on their shoulders  
quivers capped so the sharpness will not be blunted,  
packed with arrowheads.

Like those arrow-points,  
the iluppai flowers, with copper-plate-red new shoots,  
stand rich with buds.

Bees gather to drink their honey and stir them;  
their petals spread wide,

the wind beats upon them,  
and they break from their stalks.

Scattered everywhere upon the coral-red road  
like a violent shower of hail,

they make the scene appear  
like drops of fat floating here and there

upon the blood that pours  
where hands, legs and heads have been severed in battle!”

The ploughman, the goldsmith, the weaver, the painter,  
the young-hearted poet who sings of nature—

all of them have imagined flowers in their own ways.

But what is the unique distinction  
between all those imaginings

and the way this great warrior  
sees the scattered iluppai blossoms?

Do you understand?

Even when he is returning to seek his sweet beloved,  
blood and flesh from the battlefield

rise as a rare vision before his inner eyes,  
and he comes absorbed in that thought.

Kalladanar, who composed this *akam* poem,  
seems indirectly to praise the greatness of that town

while revealing the state of the warrior's mind.

I do not know how adequately to praise such skill!

How many such buried treasures there are  
in Sangam Tamil literature!
