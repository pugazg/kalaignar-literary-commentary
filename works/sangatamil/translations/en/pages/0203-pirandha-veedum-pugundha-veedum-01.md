# The House of Birth and the House Entered in Marriage!

The Chera
had taken a stone
from the Himalaya

and carved from it
a statue
for the exalted Kannagi.

His hands,
legs,
and shoulders
were formed
as though

the sculptor
had used
what remained
of that stone

to carve
this young man's body!

Her cheek,
meanwhile,

seemed made
from the most beautiful petals

of the finest flowers
showered
upon the statue
of that queen of chastity.

To one another,
the two of them
were cups
of honey
that never cloyed!

And so,
within their hearts,
they engraved
the sign of love:

from now on,
they would never
be separated.

Before that plan
could ripen,

their parents
decided
to break it apart.

They gathered
relatives
and kin.

“Our daughter
lives on the uppermost floor
of a seven-storeyed palace.

<div align="right">He—</div>

yes,
he is handsome.

No doubt.

But is he not poor?

Would anyone
take a pearl-studded
ceremonial lamp

meant for
the courtyard
of a palace

and place it
on the veranda
of a house
without even
a proper roof?

He is a fighting bull
with a body
like iron.

We do not deny it.

But—

is he not
a thoroughly poor man,

without even
a mustard-seed's worth
of wealth

to make
a thread of gold
the size of a splinter

and tie it
around the neck

of the sugarcane-like
divine daughter
we gave birth to?

In firmness of heart
he has no equal.

No objection there.

But for that reason,
would anyone place
a wax image

upon a pedestal
of fire?”
