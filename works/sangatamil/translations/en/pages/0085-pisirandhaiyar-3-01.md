# Pisiranthaiyar 3

Whether the swan-cock, after hearing Aandhai's words,  
went to the place where the Chola king lived or not,

the poet, with the satisfaction  
of having carved the excellence of his dear friend into verse,

sang it to his beloved wife  
and rejoiced.

“The swan-cock image is very beautiful,” she said,  
and served food to him  
and to their grandchildren.

“Look here—two rows of mullai blossoms are smiling,”  
he said.

Opening brows curved like a bow,  
his wife asked, “Where?”

“I called both your palmyra-spathe smile  
and the smile of this white rice  
mullai blossoms,” he said.

“Mind how you speak;  
the grandchildren are right beside us,” she warned.

“Our son is away in the fields with his wife;  
it will take time for them to return.

And these diamond statues we have fashioned—  
the children are too young to understand such words.  
Do not fear,” he said.

“They may come at any moment.  
Do not keep teasing,” she warned.

At once the poet closed his mouth—  
because of the rice she had prepared!

A morsel went down the wrong way  
and lodged in his throat,

and the poet struggled, choking.

The woman sprang up  
and gave her husband's head a firm pat—
