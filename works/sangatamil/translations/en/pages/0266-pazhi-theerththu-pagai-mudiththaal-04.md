By a terrible scheme,
some Kosars drove that cow
into their pulse-field
and let it graze.

If a hungry cow
is offered a field of crops,
would it run away in fear
without rushing in to eat?

Like the belly
of a cruel ruling class
that grows swollen
by jointly devouring
the labour of the working class,
that cow's belly swelled
until there seemed no room left.

Their degraded plan was this:

“If we destroy the eyes
of the old man
who refused to give us the fruit,
then the peahen will have no support.
We can all share
that incomparable beauty.”

With such thoughts,
they carried out their trickery.

They brought Anni Minjili's father
before the assembly
and told the story
of how his cow had grazed
the pulse-field.

The Kosars of the village council asked:
“Tell us the truth.”

The old man's face darkened
like a rain-filled sky.

“I shall not ask,
‘Who saw my cow grazing?’

I come from a lineage
that would not speak falsehood
even if the whole world opposed us.

It is true:
my cow grazed the pulse crop,”
he said.

“He has admitted the offence.
Therefore gouge out his eyes!”

Such was the judgment
given in the Kosar assembly.

The very next moment,
Anni Minjili's father
was dragged away.

Poor old man—
his two eyes were torn out.

“Father!
Have you lost your sight?

I will not leave those sinners
without taking revenge!”

The daughter wept,
raising the sound of a war-drum.

“They destroyed my eyes
so that they might steal you.
Who has the courage
to approach my daughter,
who bears the sword called chastity?”

Before her father could finish,
she, standing as though in fire,
gave her reply.
