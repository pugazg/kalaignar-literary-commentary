“—give her a purse of gold as a prize
and send her away yourself!”

As he turned to leave, she stepped in front of him.

“I am the daughter born to a wealthy merchant!
I did not come to live by writing Tamil
for prizes you might give!
O son of the crowned king,
I remind you once again
of the vow I declared before:
if I live, I shall live as your wife;
otherwise, if I fall,
I shall fall at the point of your sword.
Know that this is certain!”

“Neither of those will happen.
I tell you firmly once again!”

Pouring out those bitter words,
Killi went away.

Have you ever seen a flag that does not sway in a storm?
There is no lie here—
see, two flags have become motionless in a gale!

1. By Tittan's command,
the drum of the naalavai thundered,
and turmoil spread to all eight directions.
Paanan and Katti trembled in shock!
The enemies who had come,
while Killi was away,
to conquer Uraiyur and leap about in triumph,
now decided it would be enough
merely to lift away a cloth laid upon thorns.

“Who wants that dark-monkey woman Aiyai,
that kalli?” they said,
and without even coming to battle
they shut up shop,
called the fruit sour,
and ran away like a sneering fox!

“If we enter the field,
we shall have to show our backs—
and afterward our hearts will sink
and we shall have to shrink in shame!
Let Kaavarp Penn alone remain in our custody.
Let infantry, cavalry, elephants and chariots—
all four divisions—turn back!”

Hearing the order of Katti and Paanan,
the army thought,
“The pot has burned—the hand has let go!”
Like young crabs rushing toward their holes,
the fourfold army is said to have returned home safely!
