Then why should we lie quiet  
before the arrogance of Vengai Marban,

who has issued such a challenge?

O king, clear this doubt.

After that  
we may return to our play,” she sulked.

The Pandya caught her  
as she tried to move away,

embraced her, entwined their legs,  
and said:

“When morning blooms,  
I shall order the army—

infantry, elephants, horses and chariots—  
to march toward Kaanapper fortress.

Put away your worry!

Now remove the hand  
that is stopping me.”

She did!

In the time it takes to blink,  
their quarrel flew away;

their union continued  
until the rays of morning spread.

The next day,  
as the Pandya had promised,

the ranks of the army set out  
to fulfil the queen's wish.

They struck the guarded fortress  
called Kaanapper Eyil,

fought a fierce battle,  
and brought the siege to its end.

Under Ukkirap Peruvazhuthi's determined attack,  
Vengai Marban's fortress defence collapsed.

The Pandya king returned  
to tell the queen—

who had placed the victory-mark on his forehead  
and sent him off—

the news of triumph.

Upon the wounded king's chest  
she pressed without rest  
with the mound of her body;

every honourable wound  
from which blood still seeped

received her kisses  
and healed completely!

Some days later  
Aiyur Moolankizhar came to the Pandya court.

“O king! What greatness is this—

even enemies who did not respect you  
now respect and praise you!

O ruler who knows nothing but victory  
when you enter the field!

From today let epics sing you  
as ‘Ukkirap Peruvazhuthi who crossed Kaanapper Eyil’!

Let poetic assemblies praise you!

As long as the sun exists,  
let that name endure!” said the poet.

“Thank you, poet,”  
the king replied with a bow.

Then he looked at him and asked  
about this war—
