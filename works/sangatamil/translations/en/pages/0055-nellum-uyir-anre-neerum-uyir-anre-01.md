# Rice Is Not Life! Water Is Not Life!

One day, in the king's audience hall,  
an assembly of mature, self-controlled elder poets gathered.

Debates on many subjects  
would rise and end there again and again,  
and refined Tamil would fill the place with fragrance.

This time a rare question arose in the assembly:

What is equal to precious life?  
What is the true counterpart

<div align="right">for the body</div>

that is bone covered with skin?

The incomparable gathering of poets  
shot forth arguments like arrows,  
and their ideas clashed in that arena.

Some poets declared:

“Rice and water themselves are life.  
They nourish the bodies of many

and sustain the life  
within this impermanent long wall of a body.

They are life even to the king who rules the land.”

Thus they spoke of the excellence of rice and water.

Then Mosikeeranar—the poet who once slept upon the royal drum-bed  
and received the honour of a white yak-tail fan  
waved by the crowned king himself—

offered a stainless thought  
in the midst of the settled debate:

“My dear great poets!  
Great ones who shape Tamil sweet as sugarcane!

In fields where golden paddy grows,  
water flows, seed sprouts and wets the roots;

the farmer's sweat becomes the harvested heap,  
and for white rice to boil for us to eat—
