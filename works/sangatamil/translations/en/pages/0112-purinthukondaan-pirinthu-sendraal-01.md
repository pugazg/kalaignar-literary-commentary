# He Understood; They Parted!

Her form below the waist intoxicated the senses,  
like two veenas turned upside down.

She was a maiden in her prime,  
her tight bodice holding firm

the pair of tender coconuts  
that swayed together.

She tied a calf to its tether,  
sang as she always did,

and skipped along happily  
toward the garden near the hill.

Then a young bull with a little smile  
blocked her path.

With one hand he caught  
the tether-rope she held,

like an aambal creeper in her hand.

“Is the ash-coloured calf well?  
And you—my life,

who guards that calf like life itself—  
are you well too?” he asked.

The slender-waisted girl sprang up:

“Am I waterfall water  
for you to scoop up and drink?

Why pick a needless quarrel  
with me as I go on my way?

See the fire in my eyes and move aside;  
otherwise my words too will turn to flame,”

she warned.

Standing before the young woman, he replied:

“Jewel-bearing maiden,  
why should I move away?

Like a female buffalo-calf  
that kicks with its hind legs

and lashes with its tail  
at anyone who tries to catch it,

push me aside and run from here  
if you wish!

You too want to talk;  
you are only afraid  
that the village mouth cannot be shut.

Shall I first close your mouth  
with my lips?

Oh! No answer—  
is that a sign of consent?”

she said:

to block my path  
and cause me trouble?
