“Why still be shy, girl?
Before the young bull who bears the sword,
fish-eyed maiden, if you stand and sing a song of praise,
it will become a *puram* song that conquers time, dear!
Go without hesitation into the middle of the assembly!
After hearing your song, the man who stole your heart
will not remain unmoved,” her friend encouraged her.
The words peacock-like Narkannai then poured into her friend's ear
truly became, just as Porkodi had said,
a public heroic poem!

1. “Seeing the young bull with a short black beard like collyrium around the eye,
wearing warrior anklets upon his feet,
I fear for the mother who bore this maiden,
lest my bangles slip from my hands by themselves and betray me!
I pine like a parrot that has sighted a fruit tree
to embrace the shoulders of Killi who defeats fierce foes.
Yet, when I think of the assembly where the learned are gathered,
shame comes and restrains this Narkannai, friend!”

Her friend interrupted:
“All this wisdom should have been there at the beginning!
Now that you have climbed halfway up the palmyra, what use is fear?
Only by climbing the rest can you pluck and taste the nung fruit!
Do not climb down in terror—and later wander in madness!”
Hearing her friend's words, Narkannai
rose with courage, came forward,
and stood bowing to the assembly!

Taking up Tamil like a pearl from the deep sea,
she offered greetings to the lord of Aamur,
touched the feet of the eminent scholar Saathanthai and received his blessing,
and rejoiced to behold, with fish-swimming eyes,
the prince of Uraiyur who had defeated the enemy.
She sang an example of his fearless heart,
and a poem of the inner realm about her own heart pining for him,
