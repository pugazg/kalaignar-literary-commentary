—he also has
for giving gifts
and embracing poets!

In waters so deep
that even a boatman's pole
cannot reach the bottom,

women leap,
swim,
and play.

Even if their earrings
slip loose,
fall,
and escape their hands,

those standing above
can still see them clearly.

Such is the transparent water
of the Vaani river
of the Blue Mountain,

rushing with a *salasala* sound
while carrying sandalwood trees.

Yet sweeter and cooler
than that clear water,

I say,
is the beautiful Chera's
cool heart!”

So the poet sang
and raised him in praise.

The Chera then said,
“I shall go now
and return again
to the battlefield,”

took leave,
and departed.

Hearing the sound
of his army-drum,

his enemies
began trembling again!

<div align="center">*</div>

> * “The battle-ground,
> soaked until blood enters the earth,
> reeks of flesh.
>
> With fierce, broad hands
> that have crossed battle
> after killing,
>
> with the victorious spear,
> I had thought Poraiyan
> a harsh man of burning force,
>
> terrible to behold.
>
> But no—
> in this wide world
> where good fame endures,
>
> his heart is gracious,
> fit to seek out
> and relieve
> the sorrow of those who have nothing;
>
> he is patron
> of those who sing,
> lord of giving.
>
> Even where the depth
> cannot be reached
> by the pole,
>
> when women leap into the water
> to bathe
>
> and their fine gold earrings
> slip away,
>
> the ornaments remain visible
> from above.
>
> Sweeter and cooler
> even than the Vaani water
> that carries sandalwood—
>
> such indeed
> is his gentle nature!”

<div align="center">*</div>

<div align="right">

(*Pathitrupathu* — Ninth Ten  
Poet: Perungunrur Kizhar)

</div>
