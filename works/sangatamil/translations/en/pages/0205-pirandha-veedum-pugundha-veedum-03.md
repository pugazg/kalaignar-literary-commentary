“Our Vanji-born clan-daughter,
that form of beauty
filled with longing—

<div align="right">every day</div>

we will not allow her
to float in tears
inside a family

that struggles merely
to obtain gruel!

Friend,
come here!

The beautiful peahen
is in the grove.

<div align="right">Go to her!</div>

Tell her
this is the final decision
taken by parents
and relatives!”

Such was the order
of parents
swollen with pride in wealth.

The friend set out
to convey it
and change
the heroine's mind.

In the grove
where flowers joined
and danced happily
to the music of the breeze,

the heroine sat
without concern
for village gossip.

The friend approached
with a twisted face

and told her
the whole story.

<div align="right">To the heroine—</div>

her heart burned
like fire.

She swore an oath
and gave this reply:

<div align="right">she said:</div>

“Even if,
after a whole night
of heavy rain,

my parents see
the wet field,

take a strong yoke
from a palmyra trunk,

harness elephants
to the plough,

plough the earth deep,

sow silver seed
brought in pots,

and heap up gold
as the harvest—

once I have given myself
to one man,

what are silver,
gold,

diamond-studded mansions,
halls,

gardens
filled with leaping waterfalls,

or wells
to me?

I want nothing
from the house
where I was born.

With these two hands
I shall touch
none of it!

They did not offer
their shoulders

for this creeper
to spread upon,

thinking,
‘He will bring
crores of gold.’

If instead
I must reap
the green ears
of red kezhvaragu,

guard them,

dry them well
upon the red hill,

remove the chaff,

help those
who helped us,

gather fallen firewood
from the forest,

and eat
unsalted wild greens

with varagu rice
from a borrowed plate—
