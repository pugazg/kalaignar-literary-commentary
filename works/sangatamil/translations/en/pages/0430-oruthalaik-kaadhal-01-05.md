Let us watch from a little distance, through the window
of Saathanthaiyar's poem, the wrestling match
that whirled like a violent cyclone!

Narkilli sprang upon the wrestler,
folded one leg above the man's chest,
blocked the opponent's attack with the other,
and locked himself tightly across the brute's back. Like a hungry
elephant trying to eat bamboo,
bending, breaking and trampling it,
he struck and broke the wrestler's head and legs,
cast him to the earth and killed him.
The poet hailed the Chola youth with the title
“Poravaik Kopperu Narkilli,”
yet his heart grieved that Tittan, ruler of Uraiyur,
had not come to see his son's valour.

With rich drops of verbal sweetness,
the lofty Tamil poet Saathanthai
explains through a simile
the speed with which Narkilli fought. Here it is:-

*

> “As though a festival had begun, as though his wife had reached childbirth,
> on a day when the rain falls as the sun declines,
> the hand of the lowly cot-maker binding the cot—
> it was swifter than the needle that pierces and drives through the strap:
> such was the battle of the long-great one with the garland rich in splendour,
> against the warrior who came to seize the town!”

*

(Purananuru: poem 82  
Poet: Saathanthaiyar)
