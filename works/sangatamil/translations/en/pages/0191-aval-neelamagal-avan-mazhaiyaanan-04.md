—returned,
continued to sulk
and grew angry.

To win her over,
he showered upon her
a rain of descriptive flowers!

But she,
within herself,
was thinking:

“Why is he
still standing there talking?

Why does he not
pull me close,

lay me down
upon the green grass,

redden my cheeks,

make my fruit-like lips
swell softly,

and, as before,
give not merely a hundred,

but three hundred,
four hundred kisses?

Would I grab his neck
and push him away
saying it tastes bitter?

Why do his arms
not leap
to embrace me?”

She struggled,
holding within her chest

a love
boiling like fire!

“You laid me
upon the bed that day

and said,

‘My swan-footed one,
we two shall not be apart
even for half a moment!

I swear it, my love!’

What use
were all those explanations?

At dawn tomorrow,
one whole month
will have passed.

Did you think,
‘Let her die
if she must die’?

You forgot
the word you gave
that you would return
the very next day!

People will say
forgetfulness
is natural to men.

Then I too
shall show

that giving up life
is easy
for women!”

Thus she joined
the music of sorrow
to her words.

Before the lips
of that cast image of gold
could close,

he ran forward
and embraced her.

“My clear nectar
for whom I searched!”

he cried.

And she too
clung to him!

The moon-maiden
riding the sky
covered her face
with cloud
out of shyness!

A deer,
ashamed,
withdrew
behind a bush!

Parrots and doves,
pair after pair,

rose from the branches
and flew away,
as though saying:

“Have these children
no shame at all?

In the middle
of so many of us,

love-play
upon a white flower-bed?

Shame! Shame!”

Putting a full stop
to their sulking,

they completed
their joyous union.

When they rose,
the peahen,
having finished her dance
and folded her plume,
asked:

“Let ‘withering’
never again
enter our life!”
