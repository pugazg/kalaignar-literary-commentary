His son stood against him, saying,
“This does not accord with Tamil tradition,”
and continued to oppose it.

But the Chola king Tittan ignored every thought
spoken by his son Perunarkilli. Like an ignorant child
who, not knowing the worth of bitter medicine that cures disease,
refuses to drink it and knocks it away,
he rejected every good counsel Killi offered
and poured away the wealth of the land.

“It is the people's work to fill the royal treasury! Is it the ruler's work
to turn their lives into wasteland and drive pain into their hearts?
The beauty of a victorious ruler is to embrace his people and wield the sceptre;
the act of one without principle is to set fire to a good purpose!
Pouring milk, honey and fresh ghee by potfuls into the fire,
cutting down goats, cattle and horses as sacrifice,
feeding sacrificial pits in order to receive the ritual share,
and sitting in penance—is this not an act that shakes
the very foundation of the philosophy ‘Love itself is God’?”
Killi asked, trying to stop him.

“I am the king who rules the country—and you, my own son,
oppose me and wear your tongue sore speaking atheism?
Walk out of this country before I cut out your tongue
and throw it to dogs and jackals,” Tittan commanded.

“This very day I leave Uraiyur, the land that bore me. But, King,
I said that God is one and that God asks no living sacrifice;
you call that atheism. Yet you,
to reach a heaven you say hangs above the sky,
turn this world into hell and torment living beings!
Even a madman may be brought to clarity; but with the conclusion
that my father Tittan can no longer be corrected,
I leave,” said Perunarkilli, that golden image,
