# Would It Not Turn Out to Be False?

He was a Velir clan leader who produced victories and gave gifts to the poets who loved him. Bearing the name “Evvi,” he won a fame praised and raised in every direction.

As the harshness of night, which bites into the world and blackens it, began to lessen and the young morning opened, a piece of news came like a released arrow and entered the ear of the gifted poet Vellerukkilaiyar.

What news was it? What news? The wicked news that the ruler of Needur, Evvi, had died on the battlefield! Wicked news indeed! The great poet lamented, “Could it not turn out to be false?” Do you understand the kind of falsehood he wished it to become?

There had once been a fearless warrior of the field named Aguthai, who gave elephants as gifts to poets. People said that no one had been born in the world who could defeat him, and that he possessed a weapon of war called the “gold-adorned *thigiri*.” They said that whoever came to fight him would turn their backs before it. Because this talk spread everywhere, even a lion, they say, would become as subdued as a tiny mouse on hearing his name.
