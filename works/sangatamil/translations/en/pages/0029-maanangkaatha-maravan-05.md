One day thirst dried his tongue.  
He called the prison guard near

and ordered him to bring water quickly.  
The guard came after some delay

and gave him water in a cup;  
fire gathered and scorched the Chera king's heart!

He sank in anguish, feeling  
that all the ancient greatness of his lineage had been cut apart.

He wrote upon a palm leaf  
and left it to the Tamil land of the future:

“Sing this!”

“Even if a child is born dead,  
even if merely a lump of flesh is born and cast away,

even if one dies in old age,  
even if one is ended by some terminal illness,

to erase the disgrace of not having joined battle,  
won an honourable wound and died as a warrior of pride,

our tradition is to cut the body with a sword  
and only then lay it to rest,

adding sky-high fame to the life—  
that is the honour-bound custom

of the Tamil people,  
among whom battle-lions abounded!

And I, born in that line of custom—  
am I to suffer in spirit,

to be imprisoned by an enemy king like a dog,  
and wait even for water?

Even if they serve fragrant water, saying “Drink,”  
it is no longer fitting that I should live.

Therefore, Tamil Mother!  
To guard my honour,

I fall into your lap.  
Receive me!”

Thus, after writing a seven-line *puram* poem,  
the Chera king, like a male lion,  
planted his name in fame!
