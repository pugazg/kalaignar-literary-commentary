Aamur in Mukkaval Nadu! At that very moment,
the poet-lord Saathanthai, master of words,
refused to remain before the Chola king
and followed Killi.

When the young prince had renounced the throne
for the principle he held,
the court-poet's office seemed trivial; Saathanthai cast it aside,
vowed to guard the honour, valour, culture and tradition
of the Tamil people without letting them sink,
and stayed beside Killi.

One day in Aamur of Mukkaval Nadu,
a rough wrestler came running in, slapping his shoulders,
and thundered a challenge:
“Who is there in this town who can defeat me?”
He also declared that if the townspeople could not stand before him
and were defeated, all of Mukkaval Nadu would become his possession!

Hearing this, the ruler of the land strode forward in battle,
tried to defeat that buffalo-like man in wrestling—and lost!

Before the wrestler who shouted, “Aamur now belongs
to me who have won—aha! ho!”
a warrior of the martial clan leapt from the ground
with the speed of a star falling from the sky,
thumped his chest and cried, “Wrestler—stand!”
and sprang upon that human beast!

“Who is it? Who is it?” Aamur shook.
“Look there—it is Perunarkilli, son of Tittan of Uraiyur!”
cried one crowd, clapping their hands. Another crowd
stood with tears in their eyes, wondering what would happen.

“The victory denied even to the ruler of Aamur—
will it come to a youth so young in age?”
The people of Mukkaval Nadu stood astonished,
fingers pressed to their noses.

“One puff and you will fly like a speck of straw;
have you such arrogance?”
the brutal wrestler said, crashing into Killi!
