—‘We are people of Pisir,  
the town where the poet Aandhai lives.’

If you say that,

the king himself will come running,  
welcome you and honour you,

and delight your mate  
with many ornaments!

For such is the friendship  
he bears toward me.

Even honey may cloy;  
the friendship between the two of us  
never will.”

<div align="center">*</div>

> * “Swan-cock! Swan-cock!  
> In this bewildering evening, when the crescent moon shines  
> like the radiant face of the lord of battle  
> who wins victory and rules the land,  
> I am helpless with longing.
>
> Having eaten ayirai fish  
> at the great harbour of Kumari,  
> if you fly toward the northern mountain,
>
> and if on the way you enter  
> the good Chola country,
>
> go to Kozhi, to its high mansions.  
> Without lingering at the gate, enter the palace,
>
> and so that our great Killi may hear, say:  
> ‘I am the servant of Aandhai of Pisir.’
>
> Then he will give you fine ornaments  
> to adorn your noble and beloved mate.”

<div align="center">*</div>

<div align="right">

(*Purananuru* — Poem 67  
Poet: Pisiranthaiyar)

</div>

## Explanation of meaning:

**Aadukol vendri adupor** = winning victory in a battle where enemies are struck down.  
**Kaiyarubu inaiya** = to grieve helplessly, unable to act.  
**Ayirai** = a kind of fish.  
**Kurumpaṟai** = the female bird, the mate.  
**Maanda** = possessing greatness.  
**Nankalam** = ornament.
