for that man too,
who roams without sleep,
heedless of day and night,
only two garments are needed,
above and below,
to preserve bodily dignity.
And to end hunger without sinking him into stupor,
is not one nāzhi of grain enough for each person
in this ocean-girdled world,
whether eaten as gruel or pongal?

Even after knowing this truth—
that what one eats is a nāzhi,
and what one wears is two pieces of cloth—
is it justice that many should languish in poverty
while a few dance in the pride of abundant wealth?
When all who are born into the world
share the same needs of body and mind,
why, without thinking well about this,
should one person heap possessions like a flood,
while many die in hollows of life?
Break the private ownership that says “all is mine,”
find the proper way to drive poverty away,
and frame a common principle
that shares food and protects all living beings;
to find happiness in the world through that
is the purpose of having taken birth.

Nakkirar gathered and gave this thought;
all good people in this ancient world
have continued to hand it onward.

*

> “To the one who, without sharing,
> brings the clear-sea-bounded world
> beneath the shade of his white umbrella,
> and to the unlearned hunter
> who does not sleep at midnight or by day
> as he watches for fierce game—
> the food eaten is one nāzhi;
> the garments worn are two;
> all the rest is alike for everyone;
