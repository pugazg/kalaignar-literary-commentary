# She Became the Earth; He Became the Rain!

“Even if we cannot
see both our faces together

in that mirror called the moon,
hung in the sky

so the stars
may look at themselves
and sparkle,

my dear,
I see your face itself
as the moon!

Perhaps that is why,
to become truly moon-like,

you line your eyes
with kohl

and place
a dark beauty-mark
upon your cheek!

If I ask,
‘Show me the crescent
like a sliver of coconut,’

you press your face
close to mine,

green parrot,
and show me

the forehead
below the straight parting
of your hair!

Straight-bangled one,
you must explain
one thing to me.

There comes
a new-moon night

that turns the moon
completely dark.

Are you exempt
from that rule?

When I asked,
you buried
your whole face
in my lap

so that I could see
only your black hair.

Then,
as if pointing out
with your finger,

‘Take that
as my new-moon night,’

beautiful painting,

you made me understand.

I stroked
your dark tresses

and kissed
the crown of your head!

I said
I could not believe
that old myth

in which a serpent
swallows the moon,

you with a waist
like an aambal creeper.

But you argued
against me:

‘Then what do you call
what you are doing now—

swallowing this moon's cheek
into your mouth

without even cutting it off?’

you asked.

about the serpent
