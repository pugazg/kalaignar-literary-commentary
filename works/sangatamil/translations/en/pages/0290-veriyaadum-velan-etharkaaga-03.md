“Before the morning star appears,
my daughter once rose
like a leaping deer.

Now she lies upon the bare ground,
wilted like a plucked alli stem.

Her body bends and twists
as though it has been broken.

This delirium called possession
has ripened fully in her.

So do not reject my word.
Leap away to the Velan.
Tell him that I, her mother,
call him to perform the possession-rite
before the deity
and end the heroine's suffering.

Bring him here in a moment
to cure her illness,”
the mother said.

The friend explained to the heroine:

“What will the Velan come and say?
He will send his eyes wandering toward the sky,
raise explosive noises,
make a pillar out of a splinter,
and declare it all
the mischief of a deity!

Like the deep soil
turned upward by the plough,
the truth that the world-ending fire called love
is scorching you
must one day come to light.

So do not hide your secret love
like a chick beneath a hen's wing.

We do not need
a priestly Velan
who dances in possession
to cure this illness.

Let us tell your mother,
who laments without knowing the truth,
that you have fixed your heart
upon a warrior
who wishes to marry you rightly.

Let us explain
that the pallor spreading
across your dark-lined, flower-like eyes
is the illness caused
by the man of the nearby hill,
who captured your heart.

Before every bangle
slips from your arm and falls,
it is surely best
to tell your mother.”

The friend hesitated and hesitated,
unable to leave.

The heroine,
dazed and silent,
bent her head.

The mother-bird
understood them correctly
and flew away,
nodding that from then on
she would no longer stand as an obstacle.
