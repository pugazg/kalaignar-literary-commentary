The grace of poetry,
the beauty of elegant Tamil,
is rock candy.

And within it,
the beauty of meaning
that praises Ay Andiran,
ruler of the earth,

is a fresh bouquet
from a wedding!

Mudamosiyar sings:

when snow-wrapped Himalaya
stands in the north,

if fruit-rich Aykudi hill
did not stand in the south,

this great world
would tilt upside down,
lose its balance,
and fall to ruin.

If he could sing so,
was it not because,
thinking of Ay Andiran's greatness in giving,

the great poet became
like a bee drunk on honey?

Vel Ay Andiran,
giver among all givers,
like the rain of the sky—

some people
whose hearts held dirt

looked at that king of Aykudi,
seated high in fame,

and imagined
they could roll him
down into a pit.

They said
everything he gave away

was given only
in expectation of profit;

that all the help
he rendered in this birth

was based on self-interest,
believing it would benefit him
in another birth.

Merely because they possessed tongues,
four people, ten people
gathered together

and invented motives
for Ay Andiran's embracing generosity!

Hearing this,
Mudamosiyar grew fiercely angry.

He declared it evil
to invent such base motives,

and wrote a poem
with absolute clarity:

“Ay Andiran is no merchant in virtue
who says,
‘What I do in this world
will profit me in the next.’”

Do not imagine,
out of hatred,

that you can sting
like a scorpion

the Vel Ay Andiran,
whose thought does not contain
even an atom

of the degraded idea
that the wealth he showers forth

is a price paid now
to seize virtue back
in another world!

“Our king is no cheat
who gives green gold,
ornaments,
gems,
and elephants

to supplicants and gift-seekers
for calculated benefit!”

Thus the poet Mudamosiyar
spat his answer like fire.

Long live! Long live!
