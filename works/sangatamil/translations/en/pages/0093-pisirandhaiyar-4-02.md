“When a man sits enthroned with sceptre in hand,  
some say they see the light of a crore suns in his face,

and pray that his rule  
grow like the banyan tree  
and put down roots like arugu grass.

They pour out praise and blessings  
sweet as milk and honey, impossible to tire of.

But if a storm of pain, heavy with suffering,  
enters his life,

they stab a spear into his heart  
and run away!

Such men may be dismissed  
as base, degraded people  
with hearts of black darkness;

their friendship is lower  
than the dust beneath one's feet.

Even a clay pot made by a potter,  
once broken, may still have some small use!

But friendship with people  
who possess the nature of a little fox

only raises a flag  
over great harm.

Because their hearts are mines of self-interest,  
the friendship of many

becomes like water  
that runs only toward the low ground.

But the friendship of Aandhai—

gold dug out of Pisir,  
the little Tamil town of the Pandya country—

is pure friendship:

even if he does not come and show himself  
while one lives in prosperity,

when one sinks into distress  
he will run without hesitation  
and stand beside you.

Wise ones! Know this!”

Thus answered the king.

The next day he gave up his life  
and became a hero-stone.

Though Pisiranthaiyar came late,  
he too sat facing north beside his friend  
and gave up his life.

The poet Kannaganar turned tears into flowers  
and strung a beautiful garland of poetry  
for those very hero-stones:
