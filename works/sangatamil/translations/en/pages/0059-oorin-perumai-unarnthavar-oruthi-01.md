# A Woman Who Revealed the Greatness of Her Town!

Veerampattinam is said to be a village  
near Puduvai.

Scholars explain that it was the birthplace  
of the great poet Veeraiveliyanar.

The poet Thithanar, famed in *akam*  
for composing sweet poems,

was the son born to him.

Veliyanar, who fathered such a gifted son,  
turned a novelty into a scene in a *puram* poem

and, like an epic made of poetic light,  
showed the excellence of a woman  
in a hunter's household!

A commander took leave of his king  
and went to battle.

He returned home  
wearing victory as a garland,

having received many honours,  
his heroic flag touching the sky.

Hearing the news, a bard went  
to sing of him and receive a reward.

Veeraiveliyanar sings in fine Tamil  
of the generous nature of the commander  
whom the bard had come seeking.

He tells the bard that the warrior  
who has charged and felled enemies

has two hands reddened  
by giving away the wealth of the conquered land

and the gifts granted by his own king.

With a loving heart he will give rewards by the handful,  
says the deeply learned Veeraiveliyanar,

and then sings as well  
of the gracious qualities of the beautiful women  
who bud and bloom in that town.

Listen!

The musundai creeper and another musundai vine  
twine, spread and stretch wide;

beneath the dense shade of a jackfruit tree  
from which fruits hang,

there is no need to build a separate canopy against the sun.

There, rejoicing—
