—all a man's praise  
belongs only to the moments  
when he joins you upon the soft bed!

After that he will say,  
“I'll come Wednesday, Thursday, Friday,”

naming one day after another,  
then vanish without even showing you “Saturday.”

There is no use in my groaning  
and blaming him, friend!

There is that stone-hearted woman—his wife—  
who beats the drum, gathers the town, and blames me.

They say she twists her tears into a rope  
and ties him down with it!

When a person stands before a mirror  
and moves each limb,

does not the image in the mirror  
move in precisely the same way?

**It is exactly like that.**

She is the woman moving before the mirror;  
he is the doll reflecting every one of her movements!

What is the use of waiting any longer?

I am a mullai flower in bloom.  
Will no new bee ever come  
to drink the honey of my petals?

I dreamed of becoming a woman of art;  
but Vaanan blessed me instead:

“Live on as a woman for hire!”

May that useless man live long indeed!”

Thus a courtesan in love  
cried out the sorrow in her heart

and lamented to her dearest friend.

The poet Alangudi Vanganar  
gives that lament here as Sangam poetry,

using a golden Tamil simile!
