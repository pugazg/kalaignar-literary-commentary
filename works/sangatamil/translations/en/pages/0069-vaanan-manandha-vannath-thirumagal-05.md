—to that street of courtesans.  
Make him return there quickly!

Take as your fee the gold  
my husband, master of this house, gives into your hand  
and leap away delighted!”

As soon as the wife finished saying this in fury,  
the husband who had committed the wrong began to tremble.

“My life cannot go on without you, my dear!” he cried,  
and, like a tall tree, leaned upon her shoulder.

In half a moment  
he cast off the bard's disguise he had worn.

He had gone boldly, paid the price, and taken pleasure;  
now he had returned humbled, in the form of a bard, begging.

With no other way left to settle her anger against Vaanan,  
Thirumagal embraced him and said, “Live!”

Forgetting even that their child  
was watching with bright, curious eyes,

they embraced until their bodies pressed together—  
and then they put out the oil lamp too.

> * “Ghee and smoke have stained my garment;  
> my shoulder too is marked with black.  
> Sweet milk spurts from my soft, blemished breasts  
> after I embrace and feed my child, leaving the scent of nursing.  
> We are not like the bright-bangled women  
> appearing in the courtesans' street, fit for the charioteer.  
> Therefore, though you can raise sweet music  
> from the little yaazh with gold-like strings,  
> do not bow here. Take him away, bard!  
> Sing your song of the man from your cool-water town elsewhere.  
> Even the long-standing horse and its ornaments grow impatient;  
> speak no useless words. The path we desire is not here.”

<div align="right">

(*Natrinai* — Poem 380; Poet: Koodaloor Palkannanar)

</div>

## Explanation of meaning:

**Kuy** = smoke. **Kalingam** = garment. **Thithalai** = blemish/discolouration.  
**Pilittra** = to well up and spurt. **Pullip puniru naarum** = the smell of milk caused by embracing and feeding the child.  
**Vaalizhai** = shining ornament.
