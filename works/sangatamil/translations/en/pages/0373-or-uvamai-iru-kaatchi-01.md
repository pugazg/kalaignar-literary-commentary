# One Simile; Two Scenes!

Pandiyan Nedunchezhiyan of Thalaiyalanganam would not bow his head to enemies; he would give his head for Tamil! If he failed to win and wear the victory wreath in battle, he swore that the great gathering of poets assembled under the learned Mangudi Marudhan should cease singing of the land he ruled—this son of Tamil, this son of valour!

If Mangudi Marudhanar composed for him a work proclaiming Tamil greatness called *Maduraikkanchi*, is that not an example from history of how he beat the drum of victory in all eight directions and lived beneath the fish-banner? Believing that entering the battlefield and making enemies flee with their backs turned was the path that brought fame, he always raised the sword, confronted and killed enemies, and unfailingly heaped up garlands of victory. Though the Pandyan king stood exalted, saying that he had neither equal nor superior, poet-lord Marudhanar wished to turn the king from spending all his time in the martial sphere and have him carry out many deeds without slackening in the sphere of virtue. So he undertook to sing the *Maduraikkanchi*; in that honey-sweet *Maduraikkanchi*, he set one simile and two scenes dancing!
