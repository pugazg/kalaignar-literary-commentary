Uruthirankannanar explains to us
how a cowherd woman would rise before the sun,
carry out the household tasks beautifully and tirelessly,
then manage the family well,
welcoming and honouring with affection
the guests who sought her home.
He tells too of a custom in old Tamil country:
with the profit gained through hard work,
she would not roam with a desire
to buy ornaments and deck herself out;
nor would she wish to turn whatever remained in her hand into gold.
Instead she would buy and gather more buffaloes and cows,
and multiply her trade
in milk, buttermilk, curd, butter and ghee.
In setting this out as an idea,
Kadiyalur Uruthirankannanar stands before us
like a precise guide to plans for growth.

Here it is:

“O bards! O bards! Listen to what I tell you.
In this village there is a woman
who rises before even the dawn-bird wakes,
before the sun's hand of light touches the earth.
Her lips are red like coral;
to look upon her is to see great charm.
Her face spreads gracious goodness,
and her heart receives guests with loving hospitality.
She is a woman of such character,
one who upholds the honour of the land.
With a churn whose head swells and spreads
like the cap of a mushroom,
she works the rope until it raises a roar like a tiger's growl;
she turns curd into buttermilk and butter into ghee,
then fastens a soft head-pad
and lifts the vessel onto her head—
