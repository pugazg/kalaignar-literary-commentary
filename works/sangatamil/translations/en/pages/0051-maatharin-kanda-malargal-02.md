—he will say a queen of women has breasts  
like an inverted manoranjitham flower!

For the softness of her fingers  
he will offer the kaanthal flower as a comparison.

He will say the magizham flower is her navel;  
when she sulks upon the bed, she becomes an anicham blossom!

He will call her feet cotton-flowers,  
and the waist of that slender vine an aththi flower!

If a working weaver who lays the warp and works the loom  
tries to praise it,

he will say the flower's petal is like silk,  
and that women's shimmering beauty resembles

a front-cloth woven with shining zari flowers!

The ploughman, the goldsmith,  
the accomplished painter,

the poet who lives through music,  
the man who makes weaving his trade—

all of them, through sweet imagination,  
fondle nature's flower-children  
with the Tamil language!

Now behold a warrior.

He has faced his enemies on the battlefield,  
completed the task laid upon him,  
and is returning in victory.

To drink without interruption  
the sweet fragrant honey waiting for him at home,

he orders the charioteer to drive the chariot  
with the speed of lightning that flashes before thunder!

As that warrior races home,  
he rejoices in the splendour of his town,

where the woman dear as life—  
the one who dwells in his heart—lives.

His thought takes wing  
toward the many-coloured flowers

lying in bloom all along the roads  
as though scattered there by the handful!
