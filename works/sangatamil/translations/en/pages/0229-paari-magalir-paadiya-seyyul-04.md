“Under this month's white moon,
our father is no longer here.

The three victorious kings
have taken our hill.

Having lost our father
and lost our homeland,

we suffer today,
standing alone.”

Hearing the song
of Pari's daughters,
Kapilar was deeply pained.

With affection
he stroked their hair
and consoled them:

“Husbands will come
to be your protectors,”
he assured them.

“The hill
we had one month ago
is no longer ours;
our father too is gone—
what shall we do?”

Those two golden images
struggled
in the fire of grief,

and gave us
that very state
as a Sangam poem.

Though it is a song of sorrow,
we found within it
a taste—
we ate the rich fruit of Tamil,
did we not?

<div align="center">*</div>

> “On that month's
> white moon,
> we had our father,
> and no other had taken our hill.
>
> Under this month's
> white moon,
> kings whose victory-drums thundered
> have taken our hill;
>
> and we—
> we no longer have our father!”

<div align="center">*</div>

<div align="right">

(*Purananuru* — Poem 112  
Poets: Pari's daughters)

</div>

## Explanation of meaning:

**Attrai thingal** = last month.  
**Ittrai** = today's / this present one.
