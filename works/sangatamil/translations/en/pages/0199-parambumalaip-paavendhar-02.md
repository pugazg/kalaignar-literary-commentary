What mischief
from those tubers!

“The old woman tricked us
by calling the edible tuber
‘Valli’!”

Murugan,
deceived,
flew away.

Across the hill,
streams of honey
flowed like waterfalls.

Valli creepers and tubers
bathed and soaked in them.

Like piglets
pushing one another
to drink milk
from a sow,

jackfruits clustered
around the roots
at the base of trees.

On Pari's mountain,
where crops grew
without ploughmen,

countless springs
were like
the stars
of the rising sky!

I have told it
in familiar Tamil.

Let us hear
how Kapilar says it
in ancient Tamil:

> * “It has four benefits
> without farmers ploughing.

> First,
> rice grows
> from the small-leaved bamboo.
>
> Second,
> the sweet-pool jackfruit
> drops its fruit in season.

> Third,

> gives its tuber.
>
> Fourth,
> when the honeycomb
> ripens in colour,
>
> the dense tall hill
> pours honey.
>
> His mountain
> is like the sky;

> its springs
> are like the stars
> of the sky…”

King Pari,
rich in the wealth
of the mountain
and in the wealth
of the mind,

also possessed
an artistic heart.

That is why
he became life itself
to Kapilar.

As though
the moon
had split in two
and been born twice,

Pari the giver
had two daughters
of sculpted beauty.

Many kings
came seeking
to become their husbands.

When they were refused,
they attacked Parambu hill
with armies

and were defeated.

Then,
like bees bursting out
from a broken honeycomb,

the three crowned kings
broke past restraint

and surrounded Parambu hill
to destroy Pari's life.

Kapilar,
lord among poets,

walked calmly
before them
and stood there:

“Born in warrior lineages!
You who leap
into fierce battle
when anger rises!

Chera, Chola, Pandya kings!

Your drums
that thunder
with Muthamizh—
