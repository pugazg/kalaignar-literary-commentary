—the bedchamber story
in which you keep swallowing me
like a fruit
and letting me go again

is surely true,’
you said!

Dazed
by your poem-like speech,

I gave you
a hundred kisses
at once.

You returned them!

After all that,
is it fair, my life,

that you now sulk
and grieve

because I left you,
forgot all those moments,

and stayed
for many days
in another town?

Even in your sulking
I see a beauty.

Does not a song
need music
before it can be born?

<div align="right">Just so—</div>

before
the festival of union
can begin,

must not sulking
raise the flag first?

That is why
your sulking
fills me with delight.

Yet even nectar,
when taken beyond measure,
can become poison!

When I touch you
and you move farther away,

I drink in
your whole beauty
from a distance.

When I shift the cloth
covering your breasts,
gathered like buds,

you immediately
pull it back
and wrap yourself tightly,

saying sharply
with your mouth,
“Leave me!”

Yet I see
that only your eyes
secretly come
to some agreement

<div align="right">with</div>

my heart—

How shall I describe
the pleasure
of the rain of delight
that pours from it?

<div align="right">and I shall tell you!</div>

Come,
let us go
to the side
of the honey-waterfall
that flows.

Why let time
waste away?

Look at the flower-bed
waiting for our arrival!

Open the lid
of the box
that holds
your rows of pearl-like teeth

with the key
of a little smile.

Enough sulking.

Swing now
upon my shoulders!

Has the heart
of my colourful Tamil-parrot
withered?

Did it search?
Did it search and then
grow bewildered?

Did it perhaps tremble
like a worm
fallen into a blacksmith's furnace,

wondering whether
I would ever return?”

Thus,
a doe-like woman,

who had watched
for the return
of the lover
who had been away,

and had fallen
in sorrow,

when he—
