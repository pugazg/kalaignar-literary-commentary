Avvai thought
of Neduman Anji's gentle nature.

She soaked Tamil fruits in honey
and strung them into a poem.

“O fortress of protection,
like an eyelid guarding this land!
Leader who knows us
and moves among us with simplicity!

Whenever I think of you,
one elephant I once saw
comes strongly to mind.

Its behaviour itself
is the comparison
that suits you.

At a bathing-place,
a crowd of young people stands
and washes its long tusks.

There the elephant appears
as easy and harmless
as a plaything
in the hands of small children,
with nothing for them to fear.

Like that gentleness,
you too approach us
and move among us simply.

But when rut rises in that elephant,
it starts up in fury.

So too,
when you see the army of an enemy,
you become a storm-wind
that makes earth and sky tremble,
roaring to destroy hostility.

Though among us
you stand simple and gentle,
like the elephant at the bathing-place
among young children,

when you rise before enemies,
spitting fire,
are you not like a rutting elephant
placing each step with force?”

The beauty lies in how Avvai,
from her own experience,
matched both behaviours
of the elephant
to King Adiyaman.
