“He is like a young elephant of the kurinji hills,  
one who beats the resounding war-drum and crushes the enemy.

I ought to tear the mouth  
that compared him to a pig rooting in the earth!

You have ruined everything!  
Do not come near me—go away!” she cried.

<div align="right">At that shout,</div>

the friend grew dizzy and said:

“Do not become agitated! Put away your panic!  
There is no use trembling the moment you hear the word ‘pig.’

Hear the whole story! At the end  
you will bow to me and praise me!

There is no fault in the pig I spoke of.

I ploughed, guarded your crop, and brought it to harvest;  
it is not right for you to speak harshly of me  
as though I were a troublemaker who came to ruin the field!

I raised a bank around the seedling of your love,  
let water into it, and now rejoice  
to see the grain bending ripe!

## Therefore

“Listen first, with reason beside you,  
to the pig-story I told your cultured mother;

then weigh the worth of your friend,” she said,  
and began her tale.

“Do not think it a story. It is a sight I saw with my own eyes.

When our hunters hurried into the forested hills  
for a fierce hunt, sending their dogs running ahead,

a herd of pigs sprang and flew apart,  
scattering in many directions.

A mother pig with her piglets  
struggled in panic, not knowing where to turn.

Unable to bear what had befallen  
his mate and the young,

the boar blocked the hunters, faced them,  
raised his head and growled!

He leapt and charged.

Realizing that the hunters had hesitated for a moment,  
he hid the mother pig, with her hanging breasts,  
and the piglets beside a thick-grown bush,

and he alone—
