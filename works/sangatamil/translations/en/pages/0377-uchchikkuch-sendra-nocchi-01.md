# The Nocchi That Rose to the Heights!

Without kith, kin or parents knowing, the bow-browed maiden, with the spear-bearing warrior, amid the lovely grove beside the golden-lotus pond, shared in the delight of clandestine love. Later, because the warrior who had gone to the battlefield still had not returned to see his beloved, she dreamed many dreams, shed floods of tears, and stood with her thoughts scattered. Hearts melting at her condition, they would say that possession had seized and was tormenting the lute-voiced girl; they would conduct a *veriyattu* rite and worship the god Murugan himself!

Her parents, thinking her an innocent girl and not knowing her heart, conducted that *veriyattu*. The great poetess Kamakkanniyar wove it into a chain of verse, and so she is called Veripaadiya Kamakkanniyar!

There is also a *puram* poem sung by that Kamakkanniyar, praising a bull-like warrior who fell on the field! The warrior, equal to a young tiger, had gone to war and lay dead with a spear lodged in his chest. When he went, he had tied his girdle, put on armour, taken up his sword, and worn the emblem of a nocchi garland. As broad-beaked birds of prey circled around the battlefield to eat the corpses lying in heaps, the nocchi garland he had worn was drenched in blood.
