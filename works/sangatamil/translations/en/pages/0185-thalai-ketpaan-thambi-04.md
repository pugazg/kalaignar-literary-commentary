—and because the animals
showed such love
to the king,

he lived happily
in the forest.

There was only one
unending sorrow:

there was no poet there
to offer him
sugarcane-sweet Tamil!

As a result
of treachery
that had burst into flame,

the younger brother
seized the sceptre
into his own hand!

Was he satisfied
with that alone?

Cruel friends
stirred the hostility
that had sunk deep
into his mind.

“I have been patient
only until today.

My elder brother
has now decided
to wage war against me!

Do not stop me.

To whoever,
for me the younger brother,
cuts off my elder brother's head
and brings it here—

a reward
of ten thousand gold coins!”

he declared.

“Cut off his head?

What clan-destroying news
is this!

A heart broad enough
to defeat the pounding sea!

A spring of generosity
for every poet
who shapes the art of Tamil!

Mudiramalai itself
will melt away
if the crowned king's head is cut.

All our blood
will freeze!”

So they said,
spitting blackness
upon the purse of gold
the ruler displayed.

With the thought
of stringing Tamil flowers

and placing them
upon the victor's shoulders,

a poet came.

His name
was Sathanar.

Learning
that Kumanan
was in the forest,

he grieved.

Like finding
a grove in a desert,

like meeting
a breeze in the evening,

Kumanan saw the poet
in the morning.

From the palm-leaf,
he feasted greatly
upon Tamil!

“Rare poet!

You did not come
on that former day.

Today you have come,
and found me
in distress.

Take my head,
carry it
to my younger brother,

receive its price,
and cure
the disease of your poverty!”

said Kumanan,
drawing his sword!

The poet stopped him
and left the forest.

“Where is the younger brother
ruined
by the words of others?”

he asked—

and then
he let fly his arrows of speech!
