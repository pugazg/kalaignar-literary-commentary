He looked at her and said:

> * “My life,
> listen to one thing!
>
> There is no power
> in this world
> that can separate
> you and me!
>
> We never wished to know
> who gave birth to you,
>
> who gave birth to me,
>
> what kinship exists
> between your father
> and mine,
>
> or by what paths
> we two came here.
>
> We met
> without knowing any of that!
>
> I accepted you
> as one who came
> from somewhere;
>
> you accepted me
> as one who came
> from somewhere.
>
> We took each other
> into the heart—
>
> into the heart,
> my sweet one!
>
> When rain falls
> upon red earth,
>
> can the red colour
> be separated
> from the water
> that has mingled
> with the soil?
>
> In just that way,
> our two hearts
> have joined.
>
> So remove
> the very thought
> of separation
> this instant!”

When the young man
made this *Kuruntokai* poem
simple in speech,

the good woman
became the earth again—

and he became the rain!

<div align="center">*</div>

> “What relation are
> your mother and mine?
>
> What kin are
> your father and mine?
>
> By what way
> did you and I
> come to know each other?
>
> Like rainwater
> falling upon red earth,
>
> our loving hearts
> have mingled
> of themselves.”

<div align="center">*</div>

<div align="right">

(*Kuruntokai* — Poem 40  
Poet: Sembulap Peyaneerar)

</div>

## Explanation of meaning:

**Yaay** = mother.  
**Nyaay** = mother.  
**Endhaiyum nundhaiyum** = my father and your father.  
**Sembulam** = red-soil land.  
**Peyal-neer** = rainwater.
