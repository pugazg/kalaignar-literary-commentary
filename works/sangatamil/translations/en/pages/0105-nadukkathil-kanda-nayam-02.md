She told a story of what had happened  
and laughed to herself  
at the comic fragment of it.

“Friend, do you know something?

When that bull-like man  
was enjoying himself

with his arms around  
these peahen-like shoulders of mine,

the man from the town  
where pond-lilies with long crane-like beaks sway—

one day in anger  
he sprang at me,

seized and pulled my hair,

and, not stopping there,  
even tried to wrench off my bangles!

He came leaping toward me  
with a face hardened in fury.

‘Do not be angry, hero! Be patient!

If you do not calm yourself,  
this very moment

I shall tell this whole matter  
to your dear wife!’

The instant I said that—

what happened to the hero?

I cannot even tell you  
without laughing by myself without end!” she said.

“What happened?  
Tell me so I may know, woman,”

said the friend  
like a red thaazham flower.

The courtesan continued:

“Our king Malaiyaman lives  
with drums of honour sounding.

He takes up the bow,  
fights those who rise to seize cattle,

and proves his victory.

In his royal audience hall,  
musical performers

who have come from another land  
seeking gifts

beat their drums.

Where their fingers strike  
the side marked with *marchanai*,

that part of the drum trembles.

I saw his whole body tremble  
in exactly that way!” she said.

If the friend carried this story  
to the ears of his wife,

the courtesan knew  
his companion would despise him,

saying, “Shame! Is this even a man?”—
