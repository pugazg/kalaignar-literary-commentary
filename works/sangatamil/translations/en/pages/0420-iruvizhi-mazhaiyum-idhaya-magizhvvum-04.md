(Opening)

You see the rain from my two eyes;
will you also see the joy in my heart!

(Conclusion)

Believing that love should not be forbidden,
I waited to unite him in marriage with a treasure-chest of sculpted beauty—
a maiden whose honour suited his heart.
But he himself played ball with elephants, reduced horses to ruin,
bore the sword-points of villains upon his chest,
left his beloved to languish,
left this old woman, carried onward by time,
floating in tears,
and completed his duty!
Joy on one side—tears in my eyes on the other:
I praise the son who shows me both!
Am I not the mother who bore him in my womb? That is why I live!

(Opening)

You see the rain from my two eyes;
will you also see the joy in my heart!

*

> “The old woman, her white-grey hair
> like the plume of a fish-eating heron,
> heard that her young son had fallen after felling an elephant;
> her joy was greater than on the day she bore him. Her tears
> were more numerous than the drops of rain suspended
> upon strong bamboo swaying on the bamboo-covered hill.”

*

(Purananuru: poem 277  
Poet: Poongkan Uthiraiyar)

## Explanation of meaning:

*Vaalnarai* = whitened with grey.  
*Nonkazhai* = strong bamboo.  
*Thuyal* = movement or swaying. *Vedhiram* = a hill filled with bamboo trees.  
*Thoongiya sidhar* = accumulated raindrops.
