4.

> “Let fragrant smoke rise
> through her soft, sounding hair,
> gathered like the folded plume
> of a dancing peacock.
>
> Let her wear a cool, fragrant garland.
> Harness your swift horses
> to the long chariot.”

After Arisil Kizhar,
another rare poet born of Mother Tamil,
Perungunrur Kizhar,
said:

“King of the Avi people!
If you will step forward now,
end the suffering
of your life-like Kannagi,
bring bloom back to her heart and face,
and give her life again—
then that itself
will be the great gift
you give us.”

5.

> “That alone is our gift,
> O king of the Avi people.”

Standing before him,
he pleaded for compassion
on Kannagi's behalf.

Through the words of the poets,
Pegan understood his wrongdoing.

He harnessed the chariot
and flew like a storm.

New life blossomed again,
and the honey-flower maiden
became a breeze.

**Note:** Items 1, 2, 3, 4 and 5 are from *Purananuru* poems.
They were sung respectively by Paranar, Kapilar, Paranar, Arisil Kizhar and Perungunrur Kizhar.
The poems are 142, 143, 144, 146 and 147.
