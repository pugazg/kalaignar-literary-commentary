“That news reached my ears too.

They say the fortress was built  
in the city called Kaanapper.

Our court poet Aiyur Moolankizhar  
has given a rare description of it.

I shall tell it to you—listen!

Among all fortresses,  
it deserves first place.

Its deep moat goes beyond  
the depth of the earth;

its high wall rises  
as though touching the blue sky;

its arrow-turrets stand in that height  
like the stars filling the heavens,

<div align="right">there above;</div>

its guard-forest is so dense  
that even the sun's rays  
cannot enter;

and countless little subsidiary forts  
surround it.

Even if a thousand elephants rammed it,  
the Kaanapper fortress would not move!

I have rejoiced many times  
hearing our poet describe it  
in poetic Tamil,” said the king.

“The Pandya sees enemies  
that are far away,

and brings them in  
like tigers placed in cages;

<div align="right">yet</div>

nearby, in the city called Kaanapper,  
Vengai Marban has built a fort,

raises his sword,  
and shouts until the town shakes:

‘No army can come near it!’

After he has indirectly mocked our valour,  
is it not shameful  
to sit saying, ‘What is that to us?’

I have pulled out today  
the doubt that has long lain  
at the bottom of my heart.

For a king who can confront even a raging elephant  
and split its forehead,

what is the wall built by Vengai Marban—  
the Vindhya mountain range?

Even if the moat he dug  
were a deep ocean,

for my Pandya  
it is still a distance that can be crossed!
