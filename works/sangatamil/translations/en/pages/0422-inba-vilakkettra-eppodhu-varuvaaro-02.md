(Verse 3)

I told him love-pallor would spread over my whole body;
on the green-grove earth I held his hand and wept.
When I realize even that was of no use,
my heart becomes cotton thrown into fire, my friend!
If only he had a little of the nature that shows tenderness,
would this flower-bed be scorched by the sorrow of separation?

(Opening)

He parted from me to gather wealth—has he
lost all grace toward me, my friend?
Before darkness covers my life,
when will he come and light the lamp of joy, my friend?

(Conclusion)

Look, friend, at the beauty of a herd of stags
grazing the ears of millet that grow toward the cold wind;
nearby, clusters of *mullai* buds look like a wildcat's smile—
why does this flower deceive me into the pit of his continued absence!
See the lovely bee sit upon the blooming *mullai*,
scoop honey and circle in delight!
Will he run back today and end my grief,
thinking, “The pollen-laden flower-creeper waits for me”?
