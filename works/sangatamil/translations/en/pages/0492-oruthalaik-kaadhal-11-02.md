Could the figure simply seize her
and run away with her like a temple statue?
Was Aiyai mute?
Deaf?
A lifeless doll?

It seemed the dark-cloaked figure had decided
that only after making her unconscious
with a poisonous herb
could she be turned into a statue and taken captive.

The figure put down the dagger in hand
and tried to bring a bundle of herbs
near Aiyai's nose.

“Fool! Brute! Stop there!” cried Narkilli.

Like a hunter springing upon an animal,
the warrior-vengai Narkilli appeared
and raised his sword,
taking aim to hurl it!

Realizing that one more moment of delay
would prevent the plan from succeeding,
the dark figure seized the dagger again.
Aiyai's hand tried to stop it;
but the figure thrust her back upon the bed
and raised the dagger directly over her chest.

Iron-hearted Narkilli hurled his sword
at the black-cloaked figure.
The lamp beside the bed fell to the floor,
and black darkness spread everywhere—alas!

“Ah!”

The miserable cry rose and died away.
Realizing it was the voice of a maiden,
Killi screamed:

“Aiyai! Aiyai! My beloved sister!
Have I myself come as Yama
to take away your life?”

He quickly lit a lamp
and rushed near,
rain pouring from both eyes.

What a sight he saw there!

No harm had come to Aiyai.

But Narkannai,
bearing in her chest the sword
thrown by the prince of Uraiyur,
was taking leave of life.
The black cloak that had hidden her form
lay cast aside in a corner!
