> “Greater than the roaring, surging sea is his elephant;
> its bellow is the thunder of monsoon rain.
> Who indeed are those pitiable men—those who fall
> into the hands of the munificent warrior,
> wearer of a garland tightly strung with fibre?”

*

(Purananuru: poem 81  
Poet: Sathanthaiyar  
Subject: Chola Poravaikko Perunarkilli)

Hearing the great poet's verse of firm assurance,
that flower-creeper let a smile play upon her face!

Just then,

“What poem is this at the battle-front, poet?” asked young Killi,
a slight smile held upon his face as he approached.
“This mullai-creeper worries that trouble may come to you in battle,”
said the poet.

“Trouble may perhaps come. So is the advice that I should run away?”
he asked.

Seeing Narkilli's mockery and anger,
Narkannai fluttered with alarm, yet answered with grace:

“In Tamil land, where a garland is placed upon the man who embraces the bull and wins,
no woman will accept a man who runs trembling from battle!”

Killi hurried to answer sharply.
Before he could, the great poet stopped him:
“Enough of this play!”

“Do I not know Narkilli's valour? Here—
listen to the meaning of the poem I spoke to Narkannai!”

“The battle-host led in formation by the Chola prince,
his pulse beating with fury,
raises such a sound as it surges forth
that it surpasses the risen roar of the wave-filled sea!
And if the trumpet-call his elephants raise upon the field is heard—
