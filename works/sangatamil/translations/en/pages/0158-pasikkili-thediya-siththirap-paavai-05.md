> “Moving your shoulders in ordered step,
> with hearts fixed on no other path,
> your actions guided by disciplined purpose,
> O Brahmins of principled conduct!
>
> You who travel through
> these fierce stretches of wasteland—
> here, one daughter of mine
> and another woman's son,
> joined in a union
> known to themselves alone.
> Great ones, did you see
> those two?”
>
> “We cannot say we did not.
> We saw them.
> O mother of the jewel-wearing maiden
> who set out through the hard wilderness
> with that man of handsome strength,
> listen.
>
> Fragrant sandalwood may be born
> upon a mountain,
> but except for those who apply it,
> what use is it to the mountain itself?
> Think on this:
> your daughter is to you
> in the same way.
>
> Fine white pearls may be born
> within the sea,
> but except for those who wear them,
> what do they do for the sea?
> Consider this:
> your daughter is to you
> in the same way.
>
> Sweet seven-stringed music
> may be born in a yaazh,
> but except for those who make it sound,
> what use is it to the yaazh itself?
> Reflect on this:
> your daughter is to you
> in the same way.
>
> Therefore do not suffer
> over the woman of exalted chastity
> who has gone away.
> She has followed and honoured
> the worthy man;
> that too is a path
> that does not depart from virtue.”

<div align="center">*</div>

<div align="right">

(*Kalithogai* — *Paalai Kali*, Poem 8  
Poet: Perungadungko)

</div>

## Explanation of meaning:

**Suval** = shoulder.  
**Kolai** = principle / discipline.  
**Vevvidai** = severe stretches of arid wasteland.  
**Munniya** = having come intending to go onward.  
**Paduppavar** = those who apply it to themselves.  
**Muralpavar** = those who sing / make music.  
**Irandha** = exalted.  
**Evvam** = suffering.
