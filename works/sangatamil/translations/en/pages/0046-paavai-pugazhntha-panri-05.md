> * “Listen to something amusing, friend! Yesterday,  
> after driving off the powerful dogs and embracing his strong young,  
> the boar checked the young hunters' pursuit  
> so that the four-breasted sow might escape with her brood.  
> Leaving the forest, he stood at a narrow, difficult pass,  
> fixing his gaze upon the men.  
> Seeing that fearless pig, the hunter approached,  
> drew his sharp-pointed arrow,  
> but did not let it fly.  
> ‘Like our mighty lord who, when his army gives way,  
> stands in the burning path and blocks the foe—so is this creature,’  
> he thought, and withdrew without shooting.  
> The hill-chief returned with his garland loosened  
> as it caught in dense, tangled bushes,  
> its flowers drooping over his broad, raised shoulders  
> like the rope upon the hump of a bull.  
> Mother saw him standing at the house.  
> At once she looked at my face, laughed,  
> and said, ‘Good girl!’ before turning away.”

<div align="center">*</div>

<div align="right">

(*Akananuru* — Poem 248  
Poet: Kapilar)

</div>

## Explanation of meaning:

**Alkal** = yesterday. **Vayanaai** = powerful dog.

**Erindhu** = opposing and driving away. **Parazh** = young ones.

**Ilaiyar** = hunters. **Naanmulai** = hanging breast.

**Pinaval** = female pig. **Soliya** = to escape and run.

**Arumpuzhai mudukkar** = a rare, narrow pass.

**Tharukan** = fearlessness. **Madaiselal** = to oppose and fight.

**Seru aril** = thick-grown bush.

**Endhu kuvavu moimbu** = upright, gathered, powerful shoulder.
