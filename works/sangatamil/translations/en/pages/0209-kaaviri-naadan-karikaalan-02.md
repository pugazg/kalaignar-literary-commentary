—and, in a moment,
set fire
to the very house

to finish off
the young Valavan,

his loving mother,

and the old man
who had sheltered them
like giving shade.

The child,

who had drunk
the milk of valour

and slept
with closed eyes
in the cradle,

<div align="right">the child</div>

who would one day
hold the victorious spear
and bring enemies down—

the mother,
thinking of that future,

<div align="right">thinking again and again,</div>

had closed her eyes
and fallen
into a little sleep.

At that moment,

the fire
set by the frenzied men
rose to strike the sky!

“My dear!”

she cried.

Her fish-like eyes
bulged in terror.

Without hesitating
even for a moment,

she sprang
toward the cradle

where the heir
of Tamil land
slept.

A spear,
hurled with fierce speed,
came toward her.

She received it
in her chest
and fell.

The old great man,
who had given them
a place to live,

swore:

“I shall not allow
the Chola land
to be brought low!”

He lifted
Thirumavalavan,

placed the child
securely
upon his shoulder,

whirled his sword,

fought the enemy,

won his way through,

and escaped
with the child!

The treacherous kinsmen,
stunned,
wondered:

“Did that old tiger
go east?
West?”

Grieving
that what was
within their grasp
had escaped,

they sent men
through country and forest

to discover
where the old man
was hiding.

Though the child's mother
had become gold
inside the fire,

the old man,
despite his advanced age,

resolved
to make this boy
the gold of the Tamil people
in rulership.

With unshaken determination,
he shook
his broad shoulders!

He had swum
through fire,

carrying Thirumavalavan,

and killed
some of the hateful enemies

while fulfilling
his duty.

Sparks of fire
had touched
the child's leg,

slightly burning it!

Even the dark scar
on the burned leg

looked beautiful
to the old man's eyes.
