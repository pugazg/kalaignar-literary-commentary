—you are already lamenting  
that I shall become prey

inside the mouth  
of the tiger-cubs called my enemies.

Cast away fear, sir!

After death,  
the only remnant

a Tamil leaves behind  
is honour.

A man who scatters that honour  
like flowers beneath enemy feet

and makes his own life comfortable

is merely a rice-fed lump of flesh—

a kite  
whose string has snapped!

I am Nedunchezhiyan,  
who bows his head to Tamil.

I will not bow  
to the unworthy!

What are seven?

Even if seventy crore people  
beat war-drums

from all eight directions  
and come against me,

I shall create  
upon the battlefield

the literature called:

‘The young one  
is young in nothing!’

Does this great Pandya land  
hold only mendicants?

No! No!

Mother Pandya,  
mother of our clan,

has not given birth  
to cowards as her children!

Trust that young Nedunchezhiyan  
will add still more greatness

to the greatness  
of this daughter of the earth,

people of the land!  
Good people!”

So he roared.

Then the young king  
who ruled the land

began composing  
a *puram* poem

whose every nerve  
plucked the strings of valour:

“Those who mock are laughable,  
those who boast of their land…”
