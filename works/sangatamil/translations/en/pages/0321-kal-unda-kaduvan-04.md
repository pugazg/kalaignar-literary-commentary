—that nectar-like poem is indeed
the dense Tamil of the Sangam age.
But so that its sugarcane sweetness may be understood,
here I give it in a simpler form:

“Great lord of the mountain country,
where rich abundance sways,
providing enough for many kinds of creatures to satisfy hunger!
In a spring rising from the rock,
from flourishing banana clusters
that stand tall and gleaming,
their smooth, luxuriant leaves spread wide,
fruits fall, roll down and soften.
The sweet juice of jackfruit segments—
so delicious that those who eat them desire no other fruit—
mixes with them, soaks and floats in the water.
Warmed by the sun's rays,
it naturally becomes liquor,
unexpectedly producing a delightful intoxication,
so that one might joyfully leap up
and call it ‘naturally formed *theral*.’
A male monkey, thinking it mere water,
drinks it, grows dizzy and loses awareness.
Unable to climb the richly fragrant sandalwood tree
up which pepper-vines spread high on that mountain,
it lies down comfortably and sleeps
upon a mound thick with fragrant flowers—what a sight!”

Thus Kapilar shows
the richness of the mountain country
in lines of poetry.
I too have shaped it into a little story
and happily extend it into your hand—taste it!
