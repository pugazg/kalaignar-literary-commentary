—sang a prefatory blessing
for the poet!

“It was because of him
that you came.

So the blessing
belongs to him,”
she said.

Pressing her face
against her man's face,

plucking his shoulder
with her fingers,

she asked:

“My love,
I want one boon.”

“Except the boon
that I must never again
go to the battlefield

where flesh and skin
are torn open
as enemies are driven away,

and must remain
always beside you,
beautiful one—

ask any other boon,
my dear,
and I shall grant it!”

he said.

“I thought about
why you came today
and saved my life.

Would that have happened
without Perungunrur Kizhar?

So, for his joy,
give him gifts
without stinting—

pour them out
in abundance!”

she said.

“My delight,
here is a gift
for you first,”

he said,
joining his lips
to hers,

then releasing her.

He sat
in the Aththani hall

and ordered
that the poet Kizhar
be brought before him.

When the poet came,
the king stood respectfully
before him and said:

“Accept what I give
as your reward—

thirty-two thousand
gold coins!”

He spoke with a smile
and poured them out at once.

If that was the gift
he knowingly gave,

<div align="right">then what</div>

he gave
without the poet even knowing

was said to include
many towns
and houses!

Seeing this,
the poet said:

“I had thought
the Chera had learned only

to sit upon a bannered chariot
and rush forward

with the victorious spear
in the hand

that crushes
terrible enemies,

while severed bodies
fall and collapse

and the battlefield
reeks of flesh
from blood poured everywhere.

No! No!

The same eagerness
with which he marches
with an army
against enemy land—
