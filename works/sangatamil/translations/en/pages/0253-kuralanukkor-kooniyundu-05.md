**Short man:**

I shall not mock you
again.

I shall not scatter
words of insult.

I swear it,
my precious life!

Come inside
the palace.

I have found
a place fit
for the two of us
to embrace.

Golden peahen,
come without hesitation!

**Bent-backed woman:**

I too
shall no longer ridicule you.

Let us embrace
and delight,
like honey and milk,
my love!

But if we embrace
and take our pleasure
inside the king's palace,

those who see us
will say:

“Two ghosts
have rolled together,
entwined,
and joined in love!”

So let us go instead
to the nearby grove.

There,
the two of us
will cling together tightly

like a strip of palm leaf
written upon,
rolled up,

and firmly sealed
with lacquer.

Come!

Hearing her invitation,
what became
of the short man?

He leapt forward,
lifted her up,

and in the grove
of clustered flowers
they drank deeply
of immense delight!

“Short man”
and “bent-backed woman”—
those names belong
only to the body.

Within the two hearts
that mingle in joy,
there is no shortness—
there is fine height!

There is no bend—
only a gaze
raised toward the sky!

<div align="center">*</div>

> “What penance
> did I perform to deserve this?
>
> You of soft form,
> bending like a shadow
> in water…
>
> …
>
> like the assembly's faultless
> palm-leaf document,
> rolled,
> bound at the top,
> and sealed…”

<div align="center">*</div>

<div align="right">

(*Kalithogai* — Poem 94  
Poet: Marudhan Ilanaganar)

</div>
