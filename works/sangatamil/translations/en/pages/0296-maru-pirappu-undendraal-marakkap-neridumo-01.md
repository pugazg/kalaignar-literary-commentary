# If There Is Another Birth, Would I Have to Forget?

A woman who believed
that human beings have another birth
held fast to the memory of love.

The rain-Tamil poet Ammuvanar
once offered that memory
like the flesh of a ripe mango.

In one poem
of the four hundred *Natrinai* songs,
a beautiful woman
wearing chastity as her ornament
undertook a severe penance
waiting for her lover's return
and bathed in tears
from the heat of love.

“The day he said he would come
has ended.
The joy he said he would bring
has gone far away.

He kissed these shoulders,
calling them bamboo.
Where has he gone,
without seeing how they now ache with longing?

My eyelids have no work left to do.
Both eyes remain open,
watching the forest road,
bewildered and waiting.

Will he hear the news,
come,
embrace this red-lotus bud,
and give me delight?

Even reason has abandoned me
and gone away completely.

As evening approaches,
this illness called love only grows.

There is no use
in living even for an instant
without him.

Can a roof without walls
be called a house?

If the fingers are dismissed
and sent away,
what other way remains
for the veena to pour out music?

Of the two bellows of breath
that draw in and release air,
if one were lost,
I would not grieve!”
