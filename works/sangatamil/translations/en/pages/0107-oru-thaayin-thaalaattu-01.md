# A Mother's Lullaby!

Thinking of the bravery  
in the palm-hard heart

of my husband  
who had gone to defeat the enemy,

I would finish  
the household work of the whole day,

and before lying down  
for a little sleep,

call my son lovingly:

“My dear!  
Here is milk-nectar—come and drink!”

“I won't come—go away!”  
he would say,

and run beneath the silver moonlight  
to play in circles

with the child  
from the house across the way.

Anger would come over me.

I would lift a stick and chase him:

“Did you say you wouldn't come?  
Here I am—I have come to you!”

I would never strike him.

But my pure child, frightened,  
would tremble and hide.

“You are so afraid  
of a mere stick, child—

what answer will you give tomorrow  
when you face a spear  
on the battlefront?” I would ask.

Then I would pull him close,  
pinch his cheek in affection,

and slowly tip  
a bowl of honeyed milk

toward lips ripe  
like kovai fruit,

telling him to drink it all  
so he might grow strong.

The beautiful little child  
speaking baby Tamil

would refuse no more  
because of his fear,

and drink it all.

The child who had been  
a crescent moon

grew into a full moon,

and with a flawless form  
rose like a mountain.

My husband,  
who had given me a child to lull

and then gone  
to the field of battle—
