I flew toward the direction  
from which the voice had come.

“My strong-hearted son!  
Where are you?” I cried.

“Here,” he said.

I saw him.

“O right-spiralling conch!” I exclaimed,  
and embraced him.

The shield  
he had carried into battle

lay upon the ground.

There,  
my golden son had fallen,

an arrow lodged  
in his chest.

“Victory, mother—victory!” he said.

I kissed his forehead.

“My pearl!

You were born  
as the true successor

to your father,  
who gave his life at the battlefront

and guarded honour.

Live!” I said.

“The arrow has pierced  
deep into your chest.

The pain must be unbearable, my son!”  
I cried, and wept.

“Is that true, mother?

Until you saw it with your own eyes  
and told me,

I had not even felt it,”  
he said.

He gave up his life  
upon my lap.

He was gone—

leaving only me  
still alive.

Was this the son  
who used to run

when he saw the stick I raised  
because he stubbornly refused food

while playing with the children  
of the town?

Now, with an arrow lodged in his chest,  
he had stood firm

and dared to give his own life  
in the war to recover his homeland.

My life! My son!

Live on!  
I bless you!

>
> the thick-haired youth,  
> with a topknot like a horse's mane,
>
> lay upon his shield,  
> a young-bearded warrior.”
