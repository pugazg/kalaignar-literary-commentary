“Your brother loves only the war-sword!
Not a flower-petal!
No! No! Not at all!”

Even as she gave her life to the sharp-edged sword,
the straight-gazing maiden said this.
Narkilli swayed unsteadily
like a tree without roots.

“I was waiting for dawn,
to come running with mother
and install you as the lamp of Uraiyur palace.
Before that could happen,
your life has ended
in the darkness of my ignorance!” he cried.

“Has mother Kaavarp Penn returned?” she asked,
her face brightening with joy
as she inclined her head.

Bathing in the waterfall of blood
that poured out in gushes,
the queen of poetry said one thing.

“My beloved!
Today my love has been fulfilled!
Do you know
how the mother who gave birth to me
guarded me?”

1. Rain falls even in the blackest night,
giving life to all the people of the earth,
so that the open lotus-stalks and blooming petals
are shaken and shed.
Like the watchman guarding
a golden-lotus pond with little banks,
unable to stop the droplets
of waves tossed outward by the wind,
my mother who gave birth to me
guarded me without closing her eyes!

But to reach you,
I went beyond even her
and ran away
like a drop of water
that strikes the low bank of a pond
and splashes outside!

Now
you are shedding tears.
I rejoice by thinking of them
as drops of rosewater
at our wedding of love!

O my own beloved,
who loves only the war-sword—
will your gracious heart consent,
just once,
to bestow grace upon this flower-petal too?” she asked.

His budding moustache touched her lips.
He gave her a hundred kisses,
sweet as sugarcane!
