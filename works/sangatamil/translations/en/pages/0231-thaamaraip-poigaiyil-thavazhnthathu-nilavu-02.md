“Because two kings
oppose you,
I am afraid, my king.

They possess
large armies.
Think a little, my dear,”

said Perungoppendu,
her dark eyes
floating in tears,
trying to hold him back.

“What would you
have me do, queen?

To protect gold,
wealth,
this earth,
and you,

should I bend my knees
and bow my crowned head
before the enemy?

If so,
tell me—
why should I wear
a moustache upon my face?

Instead of taking up the sword,
dying,
and planting lasting fame
in the written record,

if we live
holding another's tail
and then disappear,

the world will spit soot
upon our grave!”

Fire flew from his words
as the lion-like warrior
bristled.

Leaning upon his shoulder,
she cooled that flame:

“My lord,
I did not ask you
to become a slave.

Even the meanest among men
would hesitate
to write the phrase
‘fall to one's knees’
upon the sand!

You are a great warrior.
Make the enemy
show their backs,

then return
and lay your head
upon my lap,
so I may tend
your noble wounds!

These gathered lips
and these two hills
are waiting
to soothe
this suffering lord of mine!

To the man
who called me
‘my honey,
my deer’

and left kisses
all over my body,

I myself now give
the battle-sword.

Go!
Cut down
the wicked enemy!”

Receiving the weapon
Perungoppendu gave him,

the Pandya of rare Tamil
embraced her
and drank deeply
of the wine of her lips—
again and again!

“See, warriors!
The sword has risen
from its sheath!

Hear also
my vow!

The enemy host
that comes beating drums
and marching upon us
shall turn its back
and fly—”
