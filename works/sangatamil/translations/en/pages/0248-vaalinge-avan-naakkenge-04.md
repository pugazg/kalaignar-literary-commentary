—a flood of blood
had run!

She walked,
gasping,
through heaps
of bodies.

Even at a wedding pavilion
there had been
no such joy.

Even when her son
was born,
her happiness
had a limit.

There he lay dead—
with his chest
shown to the spear!

She saw it,
and her heart cooled.

“Whatever else I see,
I have no more grief.

My son died
a warrior!”
she said.

“I was ready
to cut away
the breast he drank from—

alas!
Where is the wretch
who knowingly lied to me?

Here is the sword!
Where is his tongue?”

<div align="center">*</div>

> “The old woman,
> her soft shoulders shrunken,
> veins standing out,
> her belly drawn close
> like a lotus stalk,
>
> heard many say
> that her young son,
> seeing the army broken,
> had turned away.
>
> ‘If he broke
> from the fierce battle,
>
> I shall cut off
> the breast
> from which he drank,’
> she cried in anger.
>
> Sword in hand,
> she moved the fallen bodies
> and searched
> the red battlefield.
>
> There she saw
> her son lying,
> his body torn apart
> by noble wounds.
>
> Her joy then
> was greater even
> than on the day
> she gave birth to him.”

<div align="center">*</div>

<div align="right">

(*Purananuru* — Poem 278  
Poet: Kaakkaip Paadiniyar Nachellaiyar)

</div>

## Explanation of meaning:

**Narambu ezhundhu ulariya** = dried until the veins stood out.  
**Mulari marungin mudhiyol** = the old woman whose belly clung close like a lotus.  
**Padai azhindhu maarinan** = he feared the army, turned his back, and died.  
**Mandamar** = battle in the field.  
**Sinaii** = becoming angry.  
**Sidhaindhu veraagiya padumagan** = the son lying there bearing noble wounds.
