A warrior returning with sword held high
would bow at the feet of mother and father and embrace the mate of his life!
Of another warrior who had vanished upon the field,
they would place a garland upon his sword and hand it over,
telling the household the news indirectly.
Whether parents who had lost a son
or a wife who had lost her auspicious married state,
they would master the storm within their hearts
and say the land's fame had risen—
only their eyes would show grief;
Killi's heart would become wounded at the sight!

In one place,
children would leap to see the tusks of the elephant
their father had brought running for them!
In another,
toddlers would protest,
“Mother, why has father not come?”

Seated upon his horse, Killi would look upon these scenes
with a groaning heart and ask his conscience, pricked by guilt:
“Is a war that turns the land into a killing-field truly necessary?”

“As long as opponents call the principle of peace cowardice,
can the martial nature of honour-filled warriors be changed?
When the enemy raises the battle-sword—if we alone
stand plucking flowers,
is not the blazing fire called valour
nothing but charcoal soaked in water?”

Questions such as these would be born at once
in Killi's heart, melted by the torment.
The argument, “Let us stop the war,”
would fly away without remaining even a moment!
