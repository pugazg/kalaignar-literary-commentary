# The Poet Who Spat His Reply Like Fire!

With a moustache
like a sheath in which a sword sleeps,

the chieftain Vel Ay Andiran,
who ruled from Aykudi
in the Podhigai hill-region,

was a tiger
who turned those who came crying “war”
into mice
and chased them away!

Uraiyur Enicheri Mudamosiyar,
Kuttuvan Kiranar,
Thuraiyur Odaikizhar,
Paranar,
Karikannanar,
and Parangkotranar,
son of Umattur Kizhar—

a whole gathering of poets
sang and praised his fame.

<div align="right">Yet—</div>

the one who stayed close,
understood his heart,

and, overflowing with joy,
saw with his own eyes

the generosity of those firm hands
that gave in abundance every day—

the one who took
the feather of a spotted peacock

and, like placing it
in a child's fragrant hair,

turned that generosity
into a *puram* poem

and set it
upon the crown of Tamil—

was none other than
the great poet
Uraiyur Enicheri Mudamosiyar!

<div align="right">He alone!</div>

Let us see how Enicheri Mudamosiyar

<div align="right">sang</div>

with beauty
of a rare incident
that revealed the wealth
of Ay Andiran's mountain-land.

At a village common-ground
beside the hill,

mendicants had hung up
a tightly bound drum.

A monkey,
mistaking it for a jackfruit,
struck it to test it.

At that sound,
a male swan living
among the tree-branches—
