> —but a ruler
> who stands without fear,
>
> bows to no threat
> from an enemy,
>
> and protects the country
> without harm—
>
> remember that
> the written record
> will praise such rulers!
>
> Their rule
> will be light and easy,
>
> with no weight at all,
> like a swan's feather
> held in the hand,
>
> or like dried netti stalk
> from a parched pond.”

Thus he described
the two kinds of rule.

The learned men
marvelled
at his greatness.

They blessed
King Nalangilli!

He too
was a poet,

and therefore
he also ruled
and lived through

Mother Tamil,
the language of nectar!

> * “If he comes gently,
> bows at my good feet,
>
> and begs,
> saying ‘Give,’
>
> then the realm
> inherited beneath
> the fame-filled war-drum
> is his refuge;
>
> I would even give
> my precious life
> upon this earth.
>
> But if a foolish man
> who does not honour
> the strength
> of the strong
>
> despises me
> in his heart,
>
> escaping from me
> will be difficult,
>
> like a blind man
> who stumbles
> upon a sleeping tiger.
>
> If I do not go
> and make him suffer,
>
> like a hard young bamboo
> crushed under
> the foot of a mighty elephant,
>
> then let my garland
> wither
> in loveless embraces
>
> among women
> with abundant dark hair
> whose hearts hold no love!”

> * “If the old warriors,
> elder after elder,
> are taken by death,
>
> and the ancient royal inheritance
> comes by proper succession,
>
> and one receives it
> thinking,
> ‘We have attained greatness’—
>
> if that inheritance
> falls to a petty man
> whose manliness
> has little edge
>
> and who extracts tribute
> from his people,
>
> that is no greatness,
> O king—
