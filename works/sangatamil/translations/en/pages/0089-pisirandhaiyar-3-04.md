* “I shall explain the reason. Listen, sir!

I have a wife  
who has become the lamp of my household.

My son increases the yield in the field  
and is a bull who never takes crookedness as his principle.

Knowing measure, he has fathered two children;  
both are intelligent, beautiful little parrots of Tamil speech.

I also have servants  
who fill their fertile hearts with affection

and act in accordance  
with what lies in my mind.

Our people also possess the distinction  
of living under a government

that has sworn not to let even a tiny fault,  
finer than the thread of a spider's web,

spread across the just sceptre.

And in Pisir, the beautiful small town  
where this humble man was born,

<div align="right">in that place</div>

learned elders and worthy people

<div align="right">abound!</div>

Because all these blessings  
have come together in my life,

no wave of grey  
has been able to besiege my hair

and show its pearl-like colour.

Learned friend, this is the fruit  
of a life free from worry,”

said the poet.

At that very moment,  
a piece of news arrived  
that shattered his heart.

The Chola king who had ruled the land  
like the sun rising in the east,

broken in spirit  
by the opposition of his own children,

had gone northward  
to sit and give up his life.

The poet saw the message on a palm leaf  
sharp as a sword.

“This very moment I shall leave,  
go home, take leave of my family, and return.

We have lived as the grammar of friendship;  
we shall not be parted in the world of death!”
