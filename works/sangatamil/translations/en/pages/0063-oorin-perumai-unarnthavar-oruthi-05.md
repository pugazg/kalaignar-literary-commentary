“To avoid both these harms,  
the proper way is for me to hide and remain right here,”

she decided, and stood  
like an unmoving statue.

The breeze, blowing like music that never wearies,  
seemed to sing in praise of her character!

In a town where such a queen among women lives,  
respecting the feelings of deer

and pouring compassion like rain,  
could anyone refuse a gift  
to a human bard?

The proverb says  
that one grain of rice reveals the whole pot.

This maiden herself proves it true,  
Veeraiveliyanar tells the bard,

showing through her  
the culture of that town.

“Go and receive your reward  
from the chieftain who has ended the enemy,” he says.

The bard hurried away at speed,  
heaping thanks upon Veeraiveliyanar!

> * “In the courtyard, munjai and musundai vines spread thick,  
> beneath jackfruit shade needing no canopy.  
> A hunter of elephants had fallen into heavy sleep.  
> A shy young doe, embraced by a stag,  
> and another solitary male, released from pursuit, played in delight.  
> Seeing their joyful union, the woman of the house  
> feared that her husband might wake;  
> she also feared that the stag might leave the doe.  
> Therefore she moved nowhere at all…  
> O bard! In that town,  
> the great lord of famed generosity  
> gives without stint to those who seek rewards—  
> wealth won from enemy kings and gifts from his own king  
> never diminish in his giving.”
