# Give Help in Need; Give Substantial Wealth!

Like grains of paddy struck by the pestle in a mortar,
the husk separating from the rice,
he was a warrior who made the bodies and heads
of snarling enemies who desired no kinship
separate from one another.

With the sharp point of his spear
never missing the mark,
he tore open the entrails of cruel foes
and made them into festoons:
that ruler of immense valour
was the Pandya Nedunchezhiyan who crossed the Aryan army.
Because he gave classical Tamil a home in his mind,
he was also a king of the Chezhiyan line
possessed of noble learning.

There is also the story that, in the haste
of giving judgement without investigation,
Kannagi condemned him as an ‘undiscerning king.’
The moment he realized that justice had died,
he gave his own life to that justice
and made it walk alive again.
It is this Nedunchezhiyan Pandya, they say,
whom the Chera Ilango's *Silambu* praises.

While this king lived,
natural abundance flourished richly in the Pandya country.
He ruled by making procedures and plans
suited to making that prosperity grow still more
and remain secure.

The pearls of the southern Pandya sea,
the beauty of honeyed waterfalls on the hills,
golden-storeyed halls and radiant groves,
and the grain-heads grown heavy in fields
beside winding rivers, bending and dancing in the wind—
none of these gave Nedunchezhiyan's heart complete fulfilment.
He saw that, among all the forms of wealth
the vast world might obtain,
the finest wealth was one alone:
the wealth of education, a spring that never ceases to flow—
