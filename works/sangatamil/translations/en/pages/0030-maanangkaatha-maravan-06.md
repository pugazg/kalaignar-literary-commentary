In keeping with the saying  
that what kind of people lived and died  
is known from what they leave behind,

Cheraman Kanaikkal Irumporai  
made his heroic sword a palm leaf

and inscribed this *puram* poem upon it.  
I extend it now before you—

read it!  
You will tremble with feeling!

<div align="center">*</div>

> * “Even if an infant dies, even if a lump of flesh is born,  
> they do not spare the sword, saying, ‘It is no person.’  
> Made to suffer like a tethered dog,  
> without strength, to beg a small ration of aid from enemies  
> merely to cool the fire in the belly,  
> and eat what one has begged—  
> would mothers bear a child for such a fate in this world?”

<div align="right">

(*Purananuru* — Poem 74  
Poet: Cheraman Kanaikkal Irumporai)

</div>

## Explanation of meaning:

**Oonthadi** = a lump of flesh.  
**Aal-andru endru vaalin thappaar** = they do not refrain from cutting with a sword on the ground that it is not a person.  
**Nyamali** = dog. **Kelal kelir** = enemies.  
**Madhugai** = strength of mind.
