# Cow's Butter Melts on the Rock!

“Kind-hearted one! Dear friend! I am wasting away until my very bones melt,” I say; yet, with a smile blooming on your face, you scold and tease me without knowing the illness that torments me! Because of the green-bangled maiden whose row of teeth is like clustered *mullai* flowers bursting with fragrance, I tremble and wilt, friend,” Vanangamudi, the great warrior who stirs the world with his sword, told his friend Thuyan, who had put a hand upon his shoulder and teased him.

“In a flower-forest where bees kiss the blossoms; on a mountain peak where wild animals gather and dance; on a seashore where even crabs pair together—lovers shift four hands and four feet, scoop up and drink the honey of sweet lips in delight. They come together, quarrel, and then waste away in the pain of separation: is that not nature itself? After parting, there is also reunion, dancing and singing! Why then let your heart tremble like an ant beside a furnace where iron is hammered? Be calm!”

Taking the hand of the friend who spoke so, and pouring a waterfall from his eyes, he replied:

“Friend dear as life, listen! I will not say that she and I are nectar and honey; if taken beyond measure, even they become cloying! I would say that in any language one searches, no simile can be found for that pleasure which never cloys!...

I saw a little boy climb a tree, leap onto a branch and pluck a mango. Why did the very tree that gave the mango into my hand take it back again? I pine!”
