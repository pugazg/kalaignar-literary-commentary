# The Rattle That Gives a Dizzy Delight!

In *Pattuppattu*,
Nathathanar composed *Sirupanarruppadai*,
comparing Nalliyakodan,
a chieftain,
with the Chera, Chola and Pandya kings
who gave without restraint,
and with the last seven great patrons.

Scholars say
that the wide land once called Oymanadu
later became the Tindivanam region.

This poet was born
in Idaikkazhi Nadu;
some say the place is Nallur,
near present-day Chennai.

Maavilangai,
Kidangil,
Eyirpattinam,
Velur,
and Amur
spread fragrance
under the rule
of the great Tamil Nalliyakodan.

The poet Nathathanar's way
of describing them
is a distinction in itself.

In that *Sirupanarruppadai*,
born from the poet's imagination,
similes run forward and play,
and descriptions of towns
surpass one another.

Follow the carts
of salt-merchants.

If our eyes rest
upon the few lines
where the poet sets out a comparison,
we see the richness of our language,
understand the greatness of our ancestors,
and watch unfold the history
that the Tamils are an ancient people.

To buy salt for sale
from the coast of Korkai,
merchant folk came in groups
with their carts.
