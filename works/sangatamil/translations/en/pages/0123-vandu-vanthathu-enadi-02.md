The next morning,  
her mother was in the kitchen.

The daughter too went there,  
helping with the work

by taking things, giving things,  
and doing the tasks assigned.

Because of what had happened  
beside the waterfall,

the fragrance lingering  
on the young woman's shoulders

drew bees to circle around her!

Her mother understood.

“Tell me, girl—

when your foot slipped,  
upon whom did you fall?”

she asked.

The heroine realised  
that because the bees were swarming,

her mother had discovered  
the deed of the thief of love.

So she drew her cloth  
over the two flower-clusters,

hiding the marks of teeth and nails.

“Do not close your mouth  
and act as though nothing happened.

I am your mother,  
the one who bore you and protects you.

Tell me what happened!”

The mother blazed like fire.

Her dear friend leapt in quickly,  
knowing that only a suitable lie

could now save the heroine:

“Mother, listen!

You blame her without knowing.

Sandalwood is burning  
in the cooking fire,

and its fragrance has spread  
throughout the kitchen.

That is why the bees  
keep flying here in swarms!

And apart from that—

your precious daughter  
has never even raised her eyes  
to look at a man,

as you are now suspecting!

I could even swear an oath,  
though I think that is unnecessary.

Unless you grant permission  
through your own eyes

for someone to play this veena,

she would never consent  
to the young man who loves her

laying even a hand upon her—  
not even by forgetfulness.

That is the truth,” she said.

The mother believed her.

She accepted that the fragrance  
came from sandalwood

burning in the kitchen fire—
