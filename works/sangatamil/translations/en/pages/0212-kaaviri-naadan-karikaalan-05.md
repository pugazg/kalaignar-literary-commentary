“Son left incomplete!
What covers you now—

<div align="right">is a prison!</div>

Do not breathe too freely.

There is no crown
for you now.

<div align="right">Your end is here!”</div>

Thus roared
the gathering of kinsmen,

casting off
their sage-disguise!

<div align="right">At last!</div>

They shut
the prison door

and danced
with joy.

“Fools,
go on your way!

Do you not know
that the full moon
will tear through cloud
and emerge?”

Karikalan muttered
to himself.

How did
the imprisoned Karikalan,

like a flood
breaking a dam,

break prison
and escape?

We may drink
that story too

from the song
called *Pattinappaalai*,

as told by
the poet
Kadiyalur Uruthirankannanar.

A tiger
that roamed free
in the forest

had been trapped
inside an iron cage.

So too,
Karikalan
was trapped

inside
a fortress prison.

But he did not lie
subdued
like a goat!

The poet
takes another image:

an elephant
falls into
a deep pit,

pushes earth aside
with its tusks,

fills the pit,

climbs out
without fear,

and goes
in search
of its mate,

then embraces it
again and again.

<div align="right">That image</div>

is fitted exactly
to Karikalan's escape
from prison.

Like an elephant,
the young bull
came out
with skill.

He raised his sword
against the stone-hearted
kinsmen.

The *Pattinappaalai*
tells many stories

that the youthful bull,
Karikal Chola,

was the true owner
of the tiger flag

flying over
Puhar
and Uraiyur.

A man
leaving his beloved
to travel
through the forest road

is said to declare:

“That path
is harsher
than Karikalan's spear!”

And when he imagines
his beloved's shoulders,

he says:

“They are cooler
than Karikalan's
just sceptre!”
