# There Was a Kannagi Even Then!

Even before the Chera prince Ilango Adigal
wrote the refined-Tamil epic *Silappathikaram*,
the story of Kannagi had circulated
from ancient times as an oral tale.

Some flower-like verses
composed by Sangam poets
stand as fitting evidence
for the fame with which that story moved among the people.

There was a king
with mountain-like shoulders,
one who gave without exhaustion,
one who gave a golden shawl
to a peacock as a gift—

praised by great people of wisdom,
a king named “Pegan.”

He had taken the hand
of Kannagi,
a casket of sculpted beauty,
an embodiment of chastity,
a treasury of dignity,
and for a time lived joyfully in married life.

Then his mind became
like a monkey
that jumps from tree to tree.

The place later called “Palani”
was in those days called “Pothini.”

Among the many chieftains
born in the Avi clan there,
the one who gained the fortune
of becoming a hero of poetry
was Vaiyavik Kopperum Pegan—
a great giver,
and a warrior
who would not bend before enemies.

Like rain
that pours without discrimination,
falling wastefully even upon rich tracts
and swelling across barren land,
Pegan's mind gave gifts
without distinction.

About that king,

1.

> “He may fall into excess in giving,
> but he does not fall into folly in war.”
