2. “The creature called the yali hunts and conquers even striped tigers
that, when struck by its mighty foot, are said to fall only to the right and never to the left;
it kills mountain-like elephants and breaks their tusks.
Amid cool, grove-filled mountain ranges where such beasts roam,
my war-leader fears not to come alone,
with only one sharp spear as his companion!
But I, separated from him,
suffer from the illness called separation!
How am I now to soothe my eyes that shed dewdrops
and find relief, friend?”

Narkannai finished her song and trembled as she looked at her mother!
“Why fear when modesty is with you, maiden?” said the venerable poet,
turning to the assembly:
“This chaste lamp of her lineage stands before you.
Perungozhi Naaykan has come forward to offer her
as a gift for the victory won by Prince Narkilli!
I know that the maiden desires it!
Her father has agreed without objection!
Only her mother wonders whether this can happen!
So there is no one to prevent us from adorning Killi's broad shoulder
with this Tamil flower,” he declared!

“There is an obstacle!”
Narkilli, who had returned victorious with his army,
sprang up, pouring out words like flame!
“I love the sword of war,
not a flower-petal! No! No! Never!” he said,
and left the assembly!

Before the clamour they raised could pierce her ears,
the world darkened and spun around Narkannai;
her heart shattered, and she collapsed to the ground!
