—he trembled,
he stood bewildered.

Trying cleverly
to bring her around,

he poured out
syrupy words:

“My jewel,
you are floating in imagination;

that is why
you pour fire
from your eyes!

Just because I am a man,
you need not waste yourself
on a thousand suspicions.

All these days
I have only been watching

quail-birds
fight one another!”

He sprinkled powdered rock candy
over his tale,
hoping to win her over.

But she,
pouring fury
through a smile

as though scattering
red-hot embers,
mocked him:

“I have heard
all about the way
you went to watch
those quail-birds fight.

Those birds,
with their clothes removed,

embraced you tightly
and conducted battle.

And your true companion,
the minstrel,

must even have played his yaazh
like the soft breeze
blowing across the arena!

Alas, poor man!

Are these not
the unhealed wounds
all over your body

from those quail-fights?

I can see too,
peahen that I am,

how your rounded shoulders
carry wounds
that have not yet dried,

because new quails
kept colliding with you
and waging war!

Even though you wished
to win,

you spent so long
fighting those birds
upon the soft bed

that all the memorial marks
on your face

have become fixed
like the stains
spread across the face
of the moon!”

Hearing these words
burst forth,

he abandoned
the lie
he had so carefully shaped.

somehow you have learned
every bit of the truth—
