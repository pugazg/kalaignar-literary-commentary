# The Younger Brother Asked for a Head!

In the Kongu land,

where young women
squeeze Tamil
from sugarcane,

mix into it
the honey
brought by bees
from flowers,

and smile
like opening buds,

people stride proudly,
saying:

“Even the food
carried away by ants
was given
by our king,
the generous Kumanan!”

Every poet
who sings in Tamil
comes praising Kumanan.

Their words thunder
like waves
of the sounding sea.

The melody
of the Tamil language,
called nectar,
resounds.

It is a fragrant garden
of poetry-flowers,

a gathering
of hearts
over which love creeps.

There was a poet
named Perunchithiranar,

a great scholar
who composed poetry
in stamped, distinguished Tamil.

A wife
pure as gold
of the highest fineness;

the wealth
of children;

the womb
that gave birth
to the poet—

and yet,
the pleasure
he knew in this world
was poverty.

He grew terribly thin
from hunger
day after day!

His companion
withered like a flower.

His mother
curled up and lay
like a worm.

His child,
driven by hunger,

rolled the cooking pot
like a cat.

Standing helpless,
wringing his hands,

the poet remembered
the generous Kumanan,

whose hands
had reddened
from giving.

He set out!

“O king of Kongu!

You have shattered
the thought:

‘After Pari and Kari,
who remains
to give?’

‘Come,
take the wealth
I pour out!’

Let Kongu beat its chest
and proclaim:

‘Kumanan
is another name
for rain!’

I have one request.

For my family to live,

<div align="right">I need gold.”</div>
