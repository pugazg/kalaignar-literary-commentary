The little man,
longing to embrace her,
wanted to draw near
the bent-backed woman.

Do only graceful doves
have the right to love?

Does pleasure belong only
to parrots
that speak sweetly?

Is this not a world
where even owls
join in bodily union
and continue their kind?

Therefore,
that the woman lived
inside the short man's mind,

and the short man
inside hers,

was no wonder
for this earth!

In a world of pleasure
where monkeys
and mules too
fondle and delight,

is there any bar
that prevents
only these two
from bodily joy?

Because his body
was short,
must his heart too
be an empty hollow?

She may have had
a bent body—

but what wrong is there
if her heart
is full of love?

Perhaps thinking
all this,

the sweet Sangam poet
Ilanaganar
took up his palm leaf

and finished writing
the love-dialogue
of “the bent-backed woman
and the short man.”

Here it is:

**Short man:**

Like the shadow
of a tree standing
upon the bank

that falls into water
and bends
with the waves,

you whose bent body
leans toward the earth!

Do not brush aside
my words.
Stand a moment!

**Bent-backed woman:**

O wondrous chick
hatched by the strange bird
called *Aandalai-pul*,
with a human head!

You are a short man
barely a handful high
when one holds you.

Are you fit
to come near
beautiful me?
