—would welcome them all
and lovingly explain
many righteous principles
needed to conduct government well.

One such piece
of political counsel he once gave,
I would say,
still lives on, undying,
as a Sangam poem
fragrant like javvadu perfume.

“A cart has wheels,
and its axle and load are properly joined.
If the driver understands the road,
knows the right path,
and guides it carefully
along an orderly route,
then the sceptre of rule too
will gain distinction,
bring honour,
and yield prosperity.

But if the man driving the cart
takes it wherever he happens to see a way,
the spokes may sink
into deep hostile mud.

If those who rule the earth
follow every whim of the mind,
lose character,
and wander without discipline,
the country will become a ruined wall.

A distressed people,
a weakened government,
and industries losing brightness day by day—
everything will be buried.”

Does not the image
used by King Thondaiman Ilanthiraiyan
that day
still apply for all time?

To see a prosperous country
and bring renewal to the people's lives,
the mind must mature.

Here is the poem
that states that truth:

> “When the wheels and load are joined
> and the guardian's cart moves through the world,
> if the driver is skilled,
> it meets no obstruction
> and the road becomes pleasant.
>
> But if he does not know how to guide it,
> then every day
> it sinks into deep hostile mire,
> and many evils,
> one after another,
> arise.”
