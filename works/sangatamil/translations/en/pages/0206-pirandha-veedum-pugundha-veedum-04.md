—even if
I eat only
once every other day,

have no change of clothes,

and receive
only a torn sari to wear;

even if
I fall into
such a low state,

tell them, friend:

our love
will not perish!

Run and say:

where my man lives,
there is my palace.

There may be
an abundance of wealth
in the house of my birth,

but explain, friend,

that even gruel
in the house
I enter through marriage

is nectar to me!

To live
with the man
a woman has chosen
as husband—

that is the life
of chastity,

that is the life
of honour.

Do not stand here
even one more moment.

Go to my parents
and tell them firmly, friend!

Fly like the wind!”

At once
the friend said,

“What work
is left for me here?”

and flew away
without even
needing wings!

Finding no fault
in their daughter's reply,

the parents
offered no further refusal

and fixed
the wedding day.

> after night-long rain,
>
> even if,

>
> silver is sown

> the wealth
> of the house
> where I was born.
>

> I reap the green ears
> of red kezhvaragu,
>
> guard them,
> dry them on the hill,
>
> remove the chaff,
> help those
> who once helped us,
>
> gather fallen fuel
> from the forest,
>
> in distress,
> borrow a plate
>
> for unsalted
> wild greens,
>
> stop the tear
> in my sari
> with the rice I earn,
>
> and eat only
> once every other day—
>
> even so,
> friend,
> life with our husband
> is better.”
