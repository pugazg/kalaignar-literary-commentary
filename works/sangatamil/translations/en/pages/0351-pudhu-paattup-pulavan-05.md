> “In the time of Pakkudukkai Nankaniyar, the kings and other wealthy people living in the country developed a great attachment to worldly life. They held that ‘this world belongs only to those who possess physical strength; it is not fitting to say that it is common to all who are born in the world,’ and that ‘because wealth is indispensable for preserving that strength without loss, the pursuit of wealth is suited to the strong.’ Thinking thus, they came to spend every day concerned only with war and the acquisition of wealth. As a result, there was no place in the country for a life of happiness.
>
> “Our Pakkudukkai Nankaniyar saw this. ‘This world is not sweet: in one house a funeral drum sounds, while in another a wedding drum sounds. One woman shines in splendour without floral adornment, while another, separated from her husband, stands with tear-filled eyes. The one who created this world to be thus is surely one without the capacity to conduct himself with an understanding of gracious character. One who understands this nature of the world is not therefore required to remain caught within it and struggle. He must seek to see a joyful life in a better world of happiness.’ Through this poem he pressed this point.”
