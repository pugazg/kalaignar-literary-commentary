Yet even that poet did not forget his own town:  
he joined Poongunram to his own name

and walked the world as the poet  
Kaniyan Poongundran.

If we think carefully about it,  
is it not true that even that great human being  
was not free of attachment to his soil?

<div align="center">*</div>

> * “Every place is our town; everyone our kin.  
> Evil and good do not come to us from others.  
> Pain and its easing are of the same kind.  
> Death is nothing new. Nor do we rejoice, saying  
> life is sweet; nor, in anger, say it is bitter.  
> As a raft is carried along the watercourse  
> of a mighty river, swollen by cool drops from a lightning-filled sky  
> and roaring ceaselessly as it strikes against rocks,  
> so precious life moves along its ordained course.  
> Having understood this through the clear vision of the discerning,  
> we do not marvel at the great in their greatness;  
> still less do we despise the small.”

<div align="center">*</div>

<div align="right">

(*Purananuru* — Poem 192  
Poet: Kaniyan Poongundran)

</div>

## Explanation of meaning:

**Kelir** = kin. **Pudhuvathandru** = not new.  
**Munivin** = because of aversion.  
**Punai** = raft. **Thiravor** = the wise.  
**Ilam** = absence; here, “there is not.”
