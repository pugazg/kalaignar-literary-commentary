—in keeping with the culture  
of Tamil soil,

had taken a spear in his broad chest  
and stood as a hero-stone.

I too turned my heart to stone.

I resolved to raise my child  
as a citizen of a lofty warrior clan,

to preserve without blemish  
the honour

that makes the sky resound  
and the earth praise.

The enemy had split open  
the heart of the husband

I once embraced in delight.

So I told my son:

“Kick that enemy away!

Raise the flag of victory!

Let our war-drum thunder  
inside their fortress!”

I kissed the crown of his head,  
placed the victory-mark upon his brow,

smoothed his topknot,

put the sword into his hand,

and sent him away:

“Go, my son!”

Then came sweet news  
one after another:

he had killed elephants  
with his cruel sword,

rolling them like hills  
tossed about in play;

he was playing upon the battlefield  
and completing his task;

the enemy ranks  
were falling like tall trees.

Unable to restrain my longing  
to see it,

I entered the battlefield.

There was no room for doubt—  
victory was ours.

But one thing—

I could not find my son there!

On the battlefield  
fragrant with blood,

heads sprouted  
like kumuda flower-buds.

The bodies of fallen warriors  
were overturned boats.

Severed arms and legs  
floated like oars  
with no work left to do.

I walked among  
the heaps of corpses.

“My precious son!  
I am drowning in worry!

Are you living here  
as a corpse of the battlefield?

Or did you turn your back, run,  
and die somewhere in disgrace?”

I cried and trembled.

Then a voice said:

“I am alive, mother—  
alive as a warrior!”

That voice lifted  
my bowed head.
