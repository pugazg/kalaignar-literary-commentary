# The Lament of a Common Woman!

Along the edge of the field,  
on the branch of a mango tree growing above the bund,

grafted mangoes, impossible to tire of eating,  
hang and sway like the full breasts of colourful women.

At last they fall  
toward the cultivated field where kayal fish play—

into the mouth of a vaalai fish  
curving and leaping in the pond water.

Such is this fertile land!

When a ripe mango falls by force,  
is it not natural for the vaalai fish to swallow it with delight?

Just so—

Vaanan, the chieftain of this town,  
came to me saying the arrow of desire had pierced him.

His heart was scorched;  
he melted before me, pleading, “Show me mercy!”

If a mango falls with force  
into a vaalai fish's mouth,

will the fish simply let it go?

He was the bull; he fell into my lap.  
That is the whole story, friend!

After that—did I call him my lover? No.

Did I arrange a formal invitation and summon him?  
Did I cast the net of my eyes and deceitfully trap him?

Did I display my beauty and draw him in with tricks?  
Did I flash one smile and topple that “holy man”?
