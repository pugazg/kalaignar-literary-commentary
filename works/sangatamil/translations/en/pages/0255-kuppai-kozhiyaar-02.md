“This sorrow
came by itself
and leaves me suffering.

There seems no path
for it to go away
except by itself!

Those who prepare roosters
and release them to fight

eagerly set one
against another.

Then those very people
separate the fight
and cool the burning anger
of the leaping birds.

Just as people stand between them
both to provoke battle
and, when it crosses the limit,
to pull the two roosters apart,

who is there
to cure my sorrow
once its sluice-gate
has opened?

Among chickens
that feed upon a rubbish heap,
a fight breaks out.

They crow,
peck,
and attack one another.

No one there
provoked those birds,

and there is no one
to stop the fight
once it begins!

Likewise,
no one urged me:

‘Tremble in love!’

And when that fire of love
burns me,
no one comes
to cool it!

Like rubbish-heap chickens
that fight among themselves,
peck one another,
and destroy themselves,

an unrestrained illness of love
entered my heart
without permission

and breaks me down
atom by atom.

There is no one
to remove it
and prevent my destruction,”
she lamented.

Because the name
of the *Kurunthogai* poet
who made the rubbish-heap cockfight
a simile
for that maiden's anguished heart
was unknown,

he came to be called
“Kuppaik Kozhiyaar”!

Even if the names
of some who created
such sweet literature
in Tamil land
have somehow disappeared,

let us rejoice
that so much Tamil literature
itself escaped disappearance!

Let us dance
in that joy!
