# One-Sided Love

## 1

A woman poet called Narkannai,
who shaped poems in Tamil sweet as sugar-candy,
did not spend herself composing songs of this world and the next;
in the Sangam collections of *Akam*, *Puram* and *Natrinai*,
she set six poems like golden gems—
the juice of poetry pressed from the fruits of love!

Along with those six poems,
there is the battle-song in the *Puram* anthology sung by a mother
of a warrior clan called Kaavarp Penn: when a maiden asked
about the son who had gone to battle, the mother, swelling with pride,
said, “Here is the cave in which the tiger dwelt!”
That mother, too, was the foster-mother who raised
the Chola nobleman Perunarkilli as though she were his own mother.
And Narkannai, enchanted by Killi's beauty and prowess,
floated in a dream-world as though drinking him in;
while the great poet Saathanthaiyar shaped Perunarkilli's
mighty manliness into a proud-striding *Puram* song
and crowned him with a garland of praise—
because I studied all this with some depth,
a fancy rose in me like a hanging root from a banyan!

The love that leapt up in Narkannai for Narkilli—
I now shape that story,
like milk brimming from a golden bowl and spilling to the ground,
into a little sequence of pictures called “One-Sided Love,”
a brief epic of love. Read it—and savour it!
