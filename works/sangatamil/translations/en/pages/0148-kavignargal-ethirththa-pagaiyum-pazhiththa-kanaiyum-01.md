# The Rosewater Poets Sprinkled, and the Tears They Shed!

“Even if the whole earth  
turns upside down,

changes direction,  
and collapses,

the life of one  
who kills gratitude—

that sinful ingrate—  
will surely be ruined  
and destroyed.”

Thus the poet Alathur Kizhar,  
who had received gifts from him,

thanked him with affection  
and offered good wishes.

When King Killi  
laid siege to Karur,

that same Alathur Kizhar  
went before Killivalavan and asked:

“When the sound  
of the guard-trees falling

can be heard,

what greatness is there  
in defeating

a coward without manliness  
who hides inside the palace  
and trembles in fear?”

Hearing the counsel-poem  
Alathur Kizhar poured out,

the king turned back  
with the army  
that had surged forward.

When his sword sprang  
from the sheath,

one could say:

“See—  
an enemy land has fallen!”

He was the king  
who ruled from Uraiyur,

the Chola king  
praised by the Tamil world.

The great ruler  
of the rich Ponni land—

<div align="right">in his eyes</div>

if someone of Kulamutram came,  
his heart would melt,

he would welcome him  
and grant heaps of gold  
without measure.

Because the king  
met his natural death

in the town called Kulamutram,

he became fixed  
in living historical literature

as:

“Killivalavan,  
the Chola who died at Kulamutram.”

In the treasury of *Purananuru*  
there are many poems

singing his greatness,  
and many poets  
who sang them.

who cut the udder of a cow,

and destroy the unborn child
