The companion who went off with a woman for hire  
will return again!

You—clothe yourself in patience like Mother Earth  
and stretch out your bangled arms to welcome him,”

he sang in curling Tamil verse upon the yaazh.

“Forgive him for running after a public woman!  
He returns as a new man.

If only you say, ‘I forgive him,’  
he will not hesitate to gather you close  
and drink your honey.”

As the supposed bard played and pleaded this way,  
she understood that it was her own husband Vaanan  
performing the daring act beneath a bard's disguise.

Once she knew he was no musician,  
what fear was there in speaking before her husband?

So the queen among women poured out  
all the anger in her heart:

“O bard who plays the little yaazh!  
Look at the ruined state I am in!

Because hot ghee and smoke have touched me,  
and because I have drawn black kohl  
around the eyes of my sugar-candy child,

my clothes have become soiled  
and lost all their beauty, sir!

Even after feeding my sweet child until his hunger is gone,  
milk keeps swelling in these lovely breasts

and flows upon my shoulders and chest.

So there is an unpleasant milk-smell  
your friend dislikes, sir!

**Therefore, leave me aside,**

and send him instead  
to the street of your women for hire—

where ornaments cast their light,  
women dance and sing and give delight,

thumbai-flower garments fly in the breeze,  
and eyes that bewitch men flash—
