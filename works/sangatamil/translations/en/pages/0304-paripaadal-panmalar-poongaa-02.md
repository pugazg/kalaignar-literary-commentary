When it sings of a forest stream
swelling and running without banks,
it compares the river to
“a maiden who knows no dance”
and “a joyful woman who knows no sulking.”

Thus, in the lofty gold-mine called *Paripadal*,
particles of the soil called Purana fall too,
while the flavour of golden Tamil melody also drips.

Hearing the roar of the Vaigai flood
striking the bank and rushing with fierce speed,
people wake startled, rubbing their eyes;
forgetting even that they are on this earth,
they harness to carts the horses meant for chariots,
and, without realizing it,
harness oxen to chariots in turn.

*Paripadal* paints those bewildered,
stumbling people in sweet Tamil:

> “They yoke the strong chariot-horse to the wagon,
> and drive the strong chariot with the wagon-ox.”

If you have grown weary of watching
the splendour of warriors marching to battle,
shall we watch the graceful gait
of women going to bathe?
Through the window that is *Paripadal*,
shall we drink in all their beauty?

Swan-like walk—
a very slender waist—
sugar-cane-sweet speech—
a body bright as lightning.
On a silken body
whose form has not slackened,
two plantain buds have sprouted,
reaching out,
drawing men's hearts,
and piercing them through.
