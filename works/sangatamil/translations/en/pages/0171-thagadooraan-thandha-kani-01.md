# The Fruit Given by Thagaron!

The southern king
with a moustache
like a squirrel's tail—

Adiyaman Neduman Anji—

belonged to the Atiyar line.

His ancestors, it is said,
were the first
to bring rare sugarcane
into Tamil land
in ancient times

and cultivate it,
finding its sweetness
in Tamil soil.

Born in that Atiyar clan,
he became Adiyaman.

With Thagadoor
as his capital,

he ruled that Tamil town
with his head held high.

He stored strength
in his firm shoulders,

swept enemy lands away
with a movement of his eyes,

and preferred
to fall bearing wounds
upon his chest

so that his fame
would endure on earth.

Though his very name
was Anji,

he lived with honour
like a striped tiger
that knew no fear.

Avvai,
queen among poets,

made ornaments for him
out of Tamil words

and joyfully adorned him
from the fullness of her heart.

She marvelled
at his virtue

and praised
his prowess.

“Enemies who think
of entering the battlefield,
know this!

Among us stands
one warrior
whose heart is full of valour.

A carpenter
may make eight chariots
in a single day;

but a wheel
fashioned over one whole month
has such strength.

Like that wheel
in bodily power and firmness

is the fighting bull
Adiyaman.

Know it!”

The verse Avvai sang
is this:

> “Enemies,
> guard yourselves
> before entering the field
> and facing battle!
>
> Among us
> there is one warrior—
>
> like the wheel
> a carpenter,
> who makes eight chariots a day,
> labours a month to fashion.”

One morning,
that warrior Adiyaman
went into the forest,
wandering there—
