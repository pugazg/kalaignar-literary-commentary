# There Is a Hunchbacked Woman for the Short Man!

A maiden
whose hair touches her heels,

and a handsome young man
radiant as the morning sun—

poet-kings tie
a perfect knot of love
between such two

and shower poems
upon the earth!

A swan-gaited,
lightning-waisted maiden;

a young bull
with budding moustache
and iron shoulders;

sugarcane-sweet lips
pressed together
upon a bed,
learning love's art—

“Come, see the scene!”
poets say,

breaking the dam
of imagination
and letting love-stories
pour like a flood.

A woman shining
as though cast
from melted gold,

and a man
who walks with
the proud stride
of a lion,

become one life
through surging love—

Tamil poets
have said so
from Sangam times onward.

But in contrast
to all this,

a poet named Ilanaganar
draws two other forms
before us

and describes
the love rising
inside the hearts
of two people
who might ask:

“What does beauty cost?
Where is it sold?
Do they measure it out,
or weigh it?”

Know the Tamil meanings:

**Kuralan** means
a short man;

**Kooni** means
a woman whose body is bent;

**Koyil** means
the king's palace.

In a palace,
a bent-backed woman
and a short man
finished the duties
given to them

and walked across
the palace entrance,
each returning home.

The moment
the short man
saw her,

love flared up
inside his heart.

That love—
