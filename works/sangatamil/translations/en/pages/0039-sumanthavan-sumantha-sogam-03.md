On the battlefield, where enemy heads fall  
like grains of paddy beneath the threshing floor!

Thinking of this,  
Mother Avvai walks on—  
mother of fragrant Tamil, mother of wisdom, mother of beauty!

Before the mother who comes planting each foot upon the dew,  
another mother came and stood leaning on a staff.

“Ah, you whose head once bore the thumbai flower!  
When both your tired legs have grown cool  
like plantain stems, why have you come here?

The days when your man called your brows bows are gone;  
young woman whose back has now taken the shape of a bow!

Once they called your two eyes spears;  
today, saying the road has darkened, you carry a staff!

Woman who, declaring all the pearls of Tamil Nadu exhausted,  
has shed your teeth and turned to trade with foreign lands!

From where have you risen? Why this agitation?  
What afflicts you?” asked Avvai, teasing her.

“Even after knowing that youth cannot be fenced in,  
I do not understand your habit of mocking,”  
the mother answered, objecting:

“O mother of learning, in my wounded heart  
I feel your teasing pouring in  
like the medicine of cultivated Tamil.

I have not forgotten the bull-like son  
who touched the hand of this blind old woman,  
bowed, and went on his way.

His body was like the tender shoot  
of a mango sapling rising from the soil.

Even if gold were poured out before him,  
his strength of mind would not bend;

a tiger cub seemed to play upon his lap!

That son—my cherished son—joined the ranks;  
he took up his duty to guard the welfare of his native soil!”

The old woman had not yet finished.  
Avvai could not bear it and interrupted:

“To praise the valour of a son who has gone to battle,  
and show a mother's courage without tears—  
that is Tamil custom!

Old woman! In the warrior clan's way  
you seem a newcomer. I marvel at you,” she said.
