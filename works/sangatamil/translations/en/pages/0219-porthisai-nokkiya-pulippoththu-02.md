—if I do not die,
we shall meet again.

If not—

<div align="right">without grief,</div>

give kisses
to my corpse

and bless
my valour,”
he said.

Before she could open
her eyes
hidden behind
a curtain of tears,

he had already
leapt toward
the direction of war

like a tiger-cub!

Flags moved
in the wind
like waves.

Armies roared
like a thousand
thunders in the sky.

The hair-knots
of warriors

floated
soaked
in battlefield blood.

Into that battlefront
of warrior clans
he entered!

Cutting off
the heads
of enemies
who opposed him,

he moved forward.

Then,
after striking
an elephant
with his sword
and bringing it down,

he drew the blade
and looked at it.

It had bent
and twisted.

He stood confused:

how could he
straighten it
and continue the war?

A strategy
arose
in his heart.

To carry it out,
he quickly approached
the fallen elephant.

It lay dead,
like a swollen mountain.

Between
its two tall tusks,

he inserted
the sword
he held in his hand,

pressed down,

and straightened it

as one might
straighten
a green bamboo.

He came again,
sword in hand.

His enemies,
thinking:

“There is no army strength
left for us
to die yet again
under that hand,”

fled in terror
and survived.

When he returned,
she clung
to his shoulders,

giving
sweet kisses
without tiring:

“Those who wanted
to separate us—

have they been defeated,
my lord?

Have they perished,
my lord?”

The two
held one another.
