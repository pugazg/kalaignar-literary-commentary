—you cannot conquer
Pari's land
of three hundred villages
merely by siege!

Take my word!

All those
who came singing,
dancing,
and seeking gifts
from Pari—

the king
has already divided
everything among them.

Know this:

only Parambu hill,
King Pari,
and I, Kapilar,
remain!

If you continue war,
thinking your shoulder-strength
will seize Pari's land,

the parrot of defeat
will perch
upon your shoulders!

Know the strength
of spear-warrior Vel Pari.

Even after a hundred days,
Parambu hill
will not bow.

I shall tell you
how you may conquer
our king,

born of the Evvi line.

Relax the siege.
Stop this decision.

Cease battering
the fort.

All three of you
go there.

Leave your armies behind.

Stand before Pari
like gift-seekers,

sing,
dance,

and receive
the king himself
as a gift!”

said Kapilar.

Thus,
standing on one side,

Kapilar's Sangam Tamil
rang out
at the battlefront:

<div align="center">*</div>

> * “Even if the three of you
> gather your armies,
> cross over,
> attack,
> and fight,
>
> Parambu
> is difficult to take.
>
> The cool good land
> of Parambu
> had three hundred villages.
>
> All three hundred villages
> have been given
> to gift-seekers.
>
> Only we
> and Pari remain.
>
> The hill remains too.
>
> Come as singers,
> and you may depart
> with what you seek.”

<div align="right">

(*Purananuru* — Poem 110  
Poet: Kapilar)

</div>

The three kings
rejected
the good-hearted poet's words.

They thundered:

“We shall not
put back
the foot
we have already placed forward.”

Through a net
of schemes
woven by hard hearts,

they destroyed
the life
of generous Pari.
