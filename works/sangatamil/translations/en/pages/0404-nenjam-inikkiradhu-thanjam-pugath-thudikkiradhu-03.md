on one branch I saw a great honeycomb.
Thinking perhaps a few drops of honey might reach him,
a man completely crippled
sat upon a root at the foot of the tree,
holding out his cupped palm in the hope
that honey would drip into it;
alas, he kept licking his empty hand!
Is his state my state, dear one?
If you answer “Yes,” then I, a woman,
must act like that crippled man; I must pine,
unable to come near you!
Though the fruit may be beyond reach,
I must surely perform penance believing
that you will come within my reach; one day
I must nestle in your hands!
At the very thought of it—if in a dream you come
and embrace me—
my heart grows sweet—it longs
to seek refuge in you!

If the penance I undertake does not bear fruit—
you, the lion who stole my heart—I will float
in the imagination of your body embracing mine;
and when I rejoice in that thought,
my heart grows sweet—it longs
to seek refuge in you!

*

> “On the long mountain where the short-stemmed *koothali* sways,
> a seated cripple sees a great honeycomb;
> cupping his palm like a little bowl and sitting below,
> he points toward it and licks his empty hand.
> So too, though the beloved does not desire her and shows no affection,
> to see him many times is sweet to the heart!”

*

(Kuruntokai: poem 60  
Poet: Paranar)
