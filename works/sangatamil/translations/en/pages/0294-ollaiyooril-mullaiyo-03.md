Struck by the shock
that the man who stood like a fort had fallen,
the poet surged
like a sea whose waves rise high.

Travelling toward Kudavayil,
he entered the land
of generous warrior Perunchathan.

The moment his feet touched
the border of the Ollaiyur region,
his eyes fell upon mullai creepers
spreading everywhere in fragrance.

“After Sathan,
who conquered enemies
with thunderous force,
has died,
why is the mullai creeper
in such haste
to burst into flower?”

With a heart like a flower-garden
ruined by a storm,
the great poet lamented
before the blooming mullai:

“Young warriors
will not pluck and wear you.

Women wearing lovely bangles
will not come to your side.

The bard who sings for gifts,
and the woman singer too,
will not pluck you
and adorn the yaazh with you.

Having lost the warrior Sathan,
the whole town overflows with grief,
its eyes streaming tears.

Mullai,
why did you bloom
in the land of Ollaiyur
without pausing even a moment to think,
without compassion?”

Because the poet Kudavayil Keerathanar
composed such a lament,
the truth became known to all:

Sathan, though dead,
had filled the hearts of the people.

So let us praise
this culture of our Tamil people.
