His heart overflowing with joy,  
the great poet hurried home

and poured the gifts  
into the hands of his guileless relatives.

They had never seen  
such ornaments before.

So they picked them up  
however they pleased:

jewels meant for the neck  
went onto their hands;

those meant for the hands  
were hurriedly placed around their necks.

Not knowing that a finger-ring  
belonged on a finger,

someone fastened it  
as though it were an earring!

Seeing this,  
Oonpothi Pasungudaiyar laughed aloud

and at once found  
a simile for what his relatives had done.

When Ravana, king of Lanka,  
carried away Rama's wife Sita,

she is imagined as throwing off  
her ornaments along the road.

The monkeys who found them,  
not knowing how they should be worn,

put waist-ornaments around their necks,  
neck-jewels on legs and thighs,

and wandered about  
wearing them all wrongly.

Taking that scene as his simile,  
the great poet portrayed

the conduct of his own relatives  
in a way that delights  
those learned in Tamil.

That poet, rich in words,  
praised Ilanchet Senni

in another poem too.

His manner of praise  
is nectar from a flowering grove;

its grace  
is the breeze of Podhigai hill!

Here it is:

“You are like the eyelid  
that guards the eye

to all who come and bow before you.

Those who come to you  
to bring down others by slander—

you know them for what they are,  
cast them aside at once,

and drive them away, saying,  
‘There is no place for you here!’

Wherever wrongdoing is found,  
whoever committed it,

you punish it severely  
so that royal justice does not fall—
