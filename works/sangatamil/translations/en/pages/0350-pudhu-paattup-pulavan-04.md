On one side, lovers adorned with flowers unite in joy; on another, women pour waterfall tears and die. To decree that all the pleasures of life belong forever to one group while suffering follows another section like an unbroken shadow—what a great cruelty in this earthly world, a rule made by those without human feeling!

“Change this. Make this world common to all. Let good people rise today and begin the sweet tasks that will attain the goal of everything for everyone,” said the poet. If we are good people, let us stand before Nankaniyar, say “Yes,” and put his words into action. For the country and this fourfold land to flourish, let us remove the evil called private ownership and make an earth without mound or hollow.

*

> “In one house the funeral drum resounds; in another house
> the rhythm of the sweet, cool drum overflows.
> Those united wear their flower ornaments; those parted,
> with sorrow-darkened eyes, stand shedding tears.
> Surely the creator is that man without gracious nature!
> Alas, this world is bitter;
> let those who know its nature seek what is sweet.”

*

(*Purananuru*, poem 194  
Poet: Pakkudukkai Nankaniyar)

## Explanation of meaning:

**pakku** = bag.  
**neithal** = funeral drum.  
**karanga** = to sound.  
**eerndhan muzhavu** = a drum with a sweet sound.  
**paani** = sound / rhythm.  
**paithal unkan** = kohl-darkened eye revealing sorrow.  
**panivaarpu uraippa** = to shine as water pours down.
