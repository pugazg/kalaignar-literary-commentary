“Because of the enemy invasion, our family has become like a tree that has shed its leaves, little brother! No matter—they went to the field; they died! Yet as long as this land endures, they will bear the name ‘those who guarded honour.’

“My son! As long as there is strength in your shoulders, strike the enemy! Sing a *parani*! This is your sacred motherland—run at once!”

She sprang forward and embraced her tender son, combed and dressed him, placed in his hand a sword stained blood-red, and blessed him: “Go, my son, toward the battlefront!” Who is there, O Lady of Fortune, in this flower-world who would not praise that sacred scene?

*

> “Let the mind perish—how fierce is this woman's resolve!
> It befits her to be of the women of old.
> In the battle fought the day before,
> her father struck an elephant and fell upon the field.
> In yesterday's battle, her husband,
> opposing the great herd, fell there.
> And today, hearing the battle drum, delighting and stirred,
> she puts the spear into his hand, clothes him in white,
> smooths oil upon the tuft of his rough hair—
> she who has no child other than this one—
> and sends him, saying, ‘Go toward the battlefront.’”

(*Purananuru*, poem 279  
Poet: Okkur Masathiyar)

## Explanation of meaning:

**than-ai** = father.  
**nel utra seru** = the battle fought yesterday.  
**velithuvirithu** = dressing in white clothing.  
**paaru-mayir** = rough / dishevelled hair.
