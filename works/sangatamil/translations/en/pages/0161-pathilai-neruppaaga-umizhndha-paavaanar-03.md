—startled,
beat its wings,
cried out,
and flew away.

Toward Podhigai hill,
where cloud like smoke creeps,

smiling celestial maidens
may go there to dance;

but enemy kings
would not set out with armies

or even dare lie down
with their heads pointing
toward the mountain he ruled!

He was the warrior
who opposed the Kongars,
destroyed their fierce hostility,

and drove them
as far as the western mountain-sea coast!

In the poem composed by Enicheri,
we can see both

the beauty of the land he ruled
and the suffering his enemies endured.

The defeated Kongars
showed their backs
and fled.

When they hid
among the western coastal hills,

the number of spears
they had thrown away and abandoned,

it is said,
was still fewer

than the elephants
Ay Andiran lovingly gave
to those who came seeking gifts!

Astonished,
the Tamil scholar Enicheri asked:

“King!
Do the elephants of your land
give birth to ten calves
at a single time?”

So many elephants
did he have

to give as gifts
to minstrels, poets,
and supplicants
who came singing!

Overwhelmed by wonder,
Mudamosiyar,

the king of poets
who praised Ay Andiran
in verse,

was famed as a master
at singing the wealth of a country.

<div align="right">Yet—</div>

one day, it is said,
he went to see
the wealth of the forest.

What he saw all along the way
made him unable
to believe his own eyes!

Elephants,
large and small,
in group after group,

stood in their hundreds
beneath the shade

of trees flourishing
without distress.

There,
he composed
a flood of fine Tamil verse!

“Only singers
go to Ay Andiran

and receive hundreds of elephants
as gifts.

Have these forests too, perhaps,
learned poetry,

gone seeking
the long-famed Ay Andiran

to receive rewards,

and brought back
these elephants as gifts

to gather them
beneath the shade
of these cool trees?”
