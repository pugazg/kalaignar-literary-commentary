She:

“Is it not a sin to ignore the spreading mullai creeper,
showing it no kindness and bringing it sorrow?”

He:

“King Pari's chariot served only
for a mullai creeper to spread over it;
did that wooden chariot ever breathe in and delight
in the fragrance of the flowers that spread upon it?
Maiden, if you mean to spread yourself over me,
what use is it if I remain merely Pari's wooden chariot?”

She:

“You are the son of a king who rules a country—
not some great sage who rules a forest!”

He:

“Even great sages sometimes fall into women's laps,
and secretly learn the arts of Manmatha!
I am a king's son; that is true!
My desire is to bend the land to my sway;
I have never desired to bend a woman's waist!”

She:

“O lord who says you will sleep only with your sword in its sheath!
Hear a vow I make!
If I live, I shall live after marrying you!
If I fall, I shall fall upon the point of your sword!”

He:

“Poet-maiden who thunders like monsoon thunder,
neither of the two things you said will happen!
I love only the sword of war,
not a flower-petal! No! This too is certain!
And just as certainly,
I shall not take your life with that sword!
I am leaving; forgive me,
and send me on my way!”

Saying this,
without waiting for beautiful-eyed Narkannai to answer,
he hurried out, climbed into the chariot waiting beside the house,
and sat down.
