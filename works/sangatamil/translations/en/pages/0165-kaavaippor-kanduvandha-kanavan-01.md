# The Husband Who Returned from the Quail-Fight!

Like a stone
striking the mark
upon his forehead,

the straight-bangled woman
stood before him
and struck with one word:

“Stop!”

Startled,
he asked:

“O woman
whose eyes are bow and arrow,
why this hatred?”

The mullai in the garden
had longed to spread herself

across the chariot-like breadth
of his chest.

But he turned her
into the very shape of suffering

and went away

<div align="right">to</div>

the houses of courtesans,

learning upon their soft beds
every art taught

by those cuckoo-voiced,
beautiful-eyed women
who speak in coaxing sweetness.

If one lifts
a lotus-creeper from water

and lays it
upon a rock
burning in summer heat,

will it leap and dance in joy?

Or will it droop,
collapse,
and wither?

He took a flower of fire
and fixed it
to the wax-candle of his household.

While she melted
and died a little
day after day,

with no room even
for refusal,

he became thoroughly trapped
by the beauty
of the smiling flowers

of courtesans
who offered him
a feast upon their beds!

Here,
from morning until evening,

she renews the home,
cooks the food,
washes clothes,

sends their jewel-like child
to study,

finishes every duty,

then waits
until her legs ache
and her eyes bloom with strain

for her husband to return.

But he—

would be elsewhere,
tasting again and again

the courtesans' lips,
red like coral
from the touch of many lips,

as though each one
were itself a flower-petal!
