“How then  
did this happen?

Listen—  
I shall tell you  
what struck my mind!”

she said.

<div align="center">*</div>

Wearing a garland  
that gleams like gold,

flashing his sword  
like lightning,

the firm-charioted Valavan  
destroys enemy armies  
upon the earth.

No one could easily defeat him!

So I do not think  
the Death that took Valavan’s life

did so through hidden malice,

nor by standing before him  
and displaying its strength  
in open battle,

nor by binding him  
and touching his body  
to bend him down.

Death, which came  
to kill him,

must instead have disguised itself  
as a poet

with a palm-leaf manuscript,

bowed with folded hands,

and begged from him  
his life

as a new gift.

Otherwise,  
there was no strength  
in any body

capable of taking  
his life!

Thus—

is it not true  
that Lady Nappasalai

sang from the overflow  
of spotless love?

Killi, the Sembiyan  
of inexpressible skill,

filled the Tamil land  
with incomparable fragrant fame.

Without coming forward  
to protect

all the greatness  
of such a Tamil people,

can the pride of a people  
become like a tree

snapped and fallen  
in a ghostly gale?

Think—and answer!

<div align="center">*</div>

> * “Not by anger,  
> not by open hostility,
>
> not by closing upon him  
> and grappling with him—
>
> there would have been  
> no escape for Death.
>
> It must have come  
> like a singer,
>
> bowed with folded hands,  
> praised him,
>
> and begged—
>
> for the Death that took  
> the firm-charioted Valavan,
>
> whose army crossed  
> fierce battle
>
> beneath shining  
> golden garlands.”

<div align="center">*</div>

<div align="right">

(*Purananuru* — Poem 226  
Poet: Marokkathu Nappasalaiyar)

</div>
