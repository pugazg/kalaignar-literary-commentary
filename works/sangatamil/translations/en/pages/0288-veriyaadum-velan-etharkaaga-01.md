# Why Does the Velan Perform the Possession-Rite?

“She looks as though madness has seized her—
this golden creeper I gave birth to.

Her two eyes stare without movement,
like seeds driven into the ground.

Friend, hurry!
Bring the Velan
and have him perform the rite
to drive away this possession!”

The aged mother
who had given birth to the heroine
held the friend's chin
and pleaded for mercy.

This is evidence
that even in ancient Tamil land
there existed a bewilderment called *veri*,
held as a belief among mothers.

Those called Velars
were people who performed possession-rites
and gave oracles.

Mothers would say
that they could drive away
such delusion or illness.

Time has moved rapidly
and gained much light,
yet this blind custom
that darkens thought
has still not vanished
from our homeland.

The swan-gaited friend
told the heroine
of her mother's request
and asked:

“Shall I bring that Velan?”

The heroine replied:

“Oh no!
Do not, friend.
If he comes,
perhaps he will make my mother learn
what my inner illness truly is.”

But the mother of the beauty
would not let the matter go.
Her heart broke,
unable to understand
what strange thing had happened
to her daughter.

“She does not lie upon her mat.
She does not drink milk.
I cannot understand
what sorrow has come upon my maiden.

Even when I wake at midnight,
that spotted peahen
is tossing without sleep—”
