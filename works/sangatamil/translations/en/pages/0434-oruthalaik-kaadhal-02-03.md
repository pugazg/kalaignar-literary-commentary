she wrote into song the valour of handsome Narkilli,
who had defeated and brought down the wrestler of Aamur.

She picked up what she had written
again and again and read it.
Then, overcome with shyness, she lay down without taking it up.
Sleep would not come, so she sat up and pressed the palm leaf
once more against her breast!

“Is there sugarcane that is sweet without being bitten?
Yet there is love one tastes without seeing!
As proof that comes without seeking,
here I am today, rolling about with eyes that will not close,”
she lamented to herself.
As the tender creeper suffered,
her life-friend Porkodi came near and asked,
“Why do you tremble like a poor person
who has lost gold?
I am asking you, peahen.”

“Do you not understand why I tremble? Listen
to what I have written,” she said, taking up the leaf.
Kissing each letter with her two eyes,
she explained to her friend the echo of her heart.

> “Though this is not my lord's town,
> though this is not my lord's land,
> one group cries, ‘He will win, he will win!’
> another group says, ‘He will not win!’
> Good indeed are the two words spoken by the many!
> My anklets ringing, I ran and, at our house,
> stood hidden beside the broad-trunked palmyra;
> I myself saw that it was his victory.”
