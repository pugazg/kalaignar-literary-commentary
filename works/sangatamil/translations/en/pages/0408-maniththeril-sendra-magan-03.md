Not even the thought came to him of racing back
to see his loving mother!
The others had returned; in their homes
their kin had already found joy!

Her son who went in the bell-adorned chariot has still not returned;
he did not wish to return without driving away the enemy.

(Thogaiyara)

That valiant woman of Tamil Nadu's line of mothers,
untroubled that her son was late in returning,
said, “Only after he has felled the enemy with his broad shoulders
will my golden son come to see his mother's face!”
That is why, though a thousand warriors of that town had returned
and every one of their homes rang with celebration,
her son who went in the bell-adorned chariot had still not returned;
he did not wish to return without driving away the enemy.

*

> “Though neem branches are broken and set up, though *kanchi* is sung,
> though hands bearing ghee smoke mustard seed,
> every house resounds with a clattering din.
> Is he still raging hot and striking down the foe?
> Long indeed is the coming of the noble one's chariot!”

*

(Purananuru: poem 296  
Poet: Vellaimaranar)

## Explanation of meaning:

*Vembusinai* = a branch thick with neem leaves. *Aiyavi* = mustard.  
*Vendhu udandru erivaan* = one who, boiling with anger, fells the enemy kings. *Nedidhu* = with delay.
