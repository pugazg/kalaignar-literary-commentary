# The Flowers Seen by a Great Warrior

If a goldsmith came forward  
to praise the splendour of a flower-garden,

he might first say in general  
that it was a grove filled with golden-hued blossoms,

then call a row of mullai buds  
a necklace of pearls!

Seeing hibiscus flowers,  
he would call them a pendant of red stones!

He would say that many jewel-like flowers  
raise the glory of the garden—see for yourself!

If a farmer praised the same scene,  
he would point to the pond and say

that the buds on the stems of water lilies  
look like the tips of the tails  
of bullocks pulling a plough—

a rare and lovely sight!

If he were a painter, he would say:

“Thumbai flowers, magizham blossoms, blue lilies—  
this garden looks as though

many colours had been scooped up on a brush  
and splashed again and again upon a canvas!”

But if a cluster of flowers falls into a poet's hands—ah!

Only after taking each flower one by one  
and comparing it with every part of a woman's body  
will sleep come to him!

“The colour of a tender body  
is the colour of a thaazhai flower,” he will say.

“The lotus itself is her face.”

of jasmine, champak and chrysanthemum—
