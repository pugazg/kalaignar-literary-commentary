—the Tamil Kaniyan Poongundran,  
born of an ancient people who arose before them,

plants those truths firmly  
with the staff of poetic speech!

Looking at those who slump and sit helpless,  
hand on cheek, saying “Everything is the fruit of fate,”

that fine Tamil poet speaks with grace:  
“The causes of good and evil  
are our own actions, not other people.”

And is that all?

He judges well and says  
that the swift succession of pains,

and their departing and disappearing too,  
come from the actions we undertake.

“Living beings born upon this earth  
dying and vanishing is nothing new.

Nor is it worthy of resolute minds  
to say that life exists only for pleasure,

or to hate life  
when suffering comes!

Like a raft travelling with a river,  
rocking and swaying and finding calm,

the journey of precious life  
moves across this world.

Therefore follow the rare deeds  
shown by the wise!

There is no bowing of the head  
before those immersed in wealth;

nor is there contemptuous speech  
toward those called small.”

Thus,

world unity, the doctrine of one human clan,  
the nature of life, noble actions,

regarding both suffering and joyful pleasure  
that come in life with an even mind,

not bowing before the wealthy who stand high,  
not despising the humble who labour in vain—

with many such many-coloured flowers,  
we see in this poem a great heart

stringing together lofty thoughts.  
May these ideas spread across the earth like a new flood!

Yet one thing:

The poet proclaimed as a noble ideal  
that every town should join into one world

and that humankind should stand as one through kinship—
