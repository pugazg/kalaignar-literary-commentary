“O beauty among humankind!  
Let it happen as your heart wishes,” she said,

and stood there placing all her senses  
at his feet.

Stroking her curling, winding hair, he too said:

“O pleasure! O sweetness! Slice of ripe fruit! Milk-nectar!  
There—the darkened pavilion calls us!

My heart, come;  
let us drink bodily delight!”

The deer's eyes startled for a moment;  
the next moment,

<div align="right">she clung</div>

to his rounded, powerful shoulder as one who belonged there!

Before they could enter the pavilion  
where even moonlight could not enter,

a voice cried, “Stop!”  
Poor thing—it stopped them without knowing their moment!

As a hen protects its chick  
from a swooping hawk,

her friend arrived!

“Do not touch that peahen!”  
she said, standing in their way.

With his eyes he asked,  
“Why needlessly stop a bee  
that is about to drink honey?”

“This blame is too grave,” he said.

“O man with long shoulders capable of lifting a hill!  
O man with the power to conquer an enemy!

I see no defect in your love.  
Accept the blessing of a full heart!

Yet do Tamils not have a custom?  
I remind you of it lest you forget.

Without marriage and without entering household virtue,  
to sing the song of love secretly

on the seashore sand and inside the pavilion—  
the parents of this maiden will not consent to it for long!

Are you not the leader of this town?  
With a burning heart I tell you something—listen!

Here in the port of Tondi,  
fishermen sweat in labour and stake their lives,

go out upon the sea and catch a fat fish.  
Have you not seen an old crane,

whose wings no longer have strength enough to fly,  
waiting to seize it?

Just so—
