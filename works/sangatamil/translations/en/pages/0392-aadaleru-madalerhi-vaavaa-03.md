(Verse 4)

Was it false that the maiden rested upon my lap?
Was it false that we were immersed in pleasure on the flower-bed?—that I
drew *thoyyil* upon her breasts?
Was everything that happened without others knowing false?

(Why did she say she would not marry me?)

(Conclusion)

I, the fierce bull, will mount the *madal* and come; I will tell
all that passed between her body and mine.
Even if they offer the whole world in exchange,
I will not dare lose the woman who dwells within my heart!
Let righteous sceptred justice give its judgement—let that
woman of refined Tamil become mine alone!

(Why did she say she would not marry me?)

*

> “I fashioned a doll of the bamboo-shouldered young woman,
> gathering *panchai* from the hollows; yet even her guards do not know
> of the *thoyyil* I drew to make her swelling, lovely breasts shine.
> If I make my claim for her in the assembly
> of the just king's righteous sceptre, what will happen?
> Very foolish indeed—
> pitiable is this clamorous town!”

*

(Kuruntokai: poem 276  
Poet: Kozhikkotran)

## Explanation of meaning:

*Madal* riding = a love custom in which a male lover, seeking to attain the heroine, rides through the town on a horse made of palmyra frond.  
*Panchai* reed = a kind of sedge grass.  
*Thoyyil* = a design drawn with sandal paste on women's breasts. *Panai* = bamboo. *Thai iyum* = making and giving.  
*Yaandhar kadavin* = if I ask for her.  
*Paavai* = image, woman.
