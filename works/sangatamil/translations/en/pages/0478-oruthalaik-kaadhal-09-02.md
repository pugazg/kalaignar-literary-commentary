Even after Tittan had answered in words like leaping flame,
the chief minister in the athani hall
did not think the matter could simply be left there.
He told the king:
“Do not cast aside my counsel as merely one among many.
It is best to weigh carefully the fact that Paanan's support
has fallen to Palverkatti before taking a decision.”

“The poet Kalladanar speaks in a poem
of Paanan's strength. King of the earth, listen!

1. He has the power to destroy the forts of enemies
far beyond the frontier,
and to seize and drive away cattle together with their bulls.
With a long spear in hand, its dark fitting anointed with ghee,
he stands his ground and defeats the enemy—
a tiger-cub that never shows its back.
Thus Kalladanar marvels at Paanan's valour!

2. Mamulanar praises Katti of the Ganga country
as a great warrior who meets the enemy
with many kinds of spear-bearing forces!
When two warriors celebrated in song
are marching with armies upon Uraiyur,
it would be wrong for my lord the king to forget that truth!”

The king did not push aside in anger
the counsel of a minister who had thought deeply.
He nodded hesitantly and asked:
“Do not leave me lost in confusion.
What other way is there to protect honour?”

“When you already hold in your hand the butter called the great warrior Narkilli,
who would cry for the ghee called victory
on the battle-front where honour is defended?
My king, say no word of refusal.
Send a palm-leaf to your son Killi at once!” said the minister.
