“Vengai Marban has been humbled.

Will he, spitting sparks of anger,  
raise another battlefield

and try once more  
to recover Kaanapper Eyil?”

Aiyur Moolankizhar's answer  
gave Mother Tamil  
a rare simile.

What was it?

If a drop of water falls  
upon iron heated by a smith in the forge,

can that drop be recovered?

So too—

the Kaanapper fortress  
already won by Ukkirap Peruvazhuthi

has become like that drop of water  
swallowed by forge-hot iron.

‘He speaks of your greatness  
and stands grieving in his heart,’  
said the poet.

I say the Pandya king  
must have struggled to know

what reward could ever match  
the beauty of the golden poet's simile.

Am I wrong?

Those who know the taste of Tamil—  
you tell me!

<div align="center">*</div>

> * “O victorious king,  
> whose fame passes beyond the reach of poets:
>
> Kaanapper Eyil had a moat  
> deeper than the earth's measure,
>
> walls rising as though to touch the sky,
>
> arrow-turrets like fish-stars flowering in the heavens,
>
> a guard-forest so dense  
> that sunlight could not enter,
>
> and rare smaller forts encircling it.
>
> Yet Vengai Marban grieves, saying  
> it is harder to recover
>
> than water swallowed  
> by iron placed in the red fire  
> of a black-handed smith.
>
> O conquering king,  
> praised by poets of the thumbai flower  
> who rejoice day after day in victory—
>
> may those who scorn you perish with their song,  
> and may your spear bloom in glory!”

<div align="center">*</div>

<div align="right">

(*Purananuru* — Poem 21  
Poet: Aiyur Moolankizhar)
