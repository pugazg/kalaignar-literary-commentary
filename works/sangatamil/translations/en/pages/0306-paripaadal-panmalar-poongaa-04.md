> Her hands are lotuses, her breasts water-lily buds;
> her red mouth an *ambal* bloom, her feet lotuses afloat.”

So praises *Paripadal*—
it sings that every limb
of those golden-creeper women is a flower.
The maiden's moon-face is a lotus in bloom.
Her breasts are unopened lotus buds.
Not only her hands—even the feet
of that graceful woman are lotuses.

Come, let us see here,
as though in a story,
the flood of imagination
in the poet's writing.

Near the waterside pavilion,
anklets chiming at their feet,
a group of slender women approached
to bathe and rejoice.
One wore white like moonlight;
another, the colour of the blue sky.
One maiden's green garment loosened;
another flower-like woman wore a coral-coloured sari.
Singing and singing they played in the rushing water;
dancing and dancing, their hearts leapt with joy.

Then—

*

A mahout came with a young elephant
that he regularly bathed in the Vaigai's waters.
A female elephant caught the young elephant's eye.
Delighted by the chance, the tusker
opened its mouth and gazed at her,
forgetting its way and standing still.
Though the mahout prodded hard to move it,
the elephant refused to stir.
Seeing the tusker in love,
the maiden elephant too flapped her ears—again and again.
Once eye had met eye,
as though thinking, “What need has love for spoken words?”
the tusker moved slowly,
and its beloved female followed gently.
Lost to herself in the intoxication of desire,
the female elephant saw—
