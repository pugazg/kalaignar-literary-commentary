“—like her two eyes?” she asked.

“Even though two are born from one mother's womb, the one whom the mother regards as the better and takes delight in is the wise son who stands like a newly blossomed flower in the stream of education that teaches virtue and ability!”

Saying this, the king leaned upon his lovely queen, let his thoughts run on, and wrote further upon the palm leaf.

“This condition applies not only to a mother; it applies to the state that rules the earth as well. Merely to honour seniority, the government does not call the eldest of a household to ask counsel, saying it does not matter even if he is a fool. On matters concerning actions that bring good to the country, even if one is younger, the state summons and speaks with the person who is second to none in learning and understanding.

“In our country, where four kinds of people live, not all who live in a higher station are truly higher, nor are those who live in a lower station truly lower. Even if this evil condition has arisen through circumstance, when one below excels in learning and knowledge, the one above becomes a person beneath him.”

The king wrote this and read it to the queen. She pressed her lips to his and moistened them, then asked, “Where is the finger that held the stylus?” She drew it to herself and touched it to her eyes.

The king breathed the fragrant breeze of the pleasures of *akam*.
That southern ruler sang, as though uncovering a treasure for *puram*, the greatness of education.
