“Justice, right conduct, and uprightness  
that never bend—

these are your rule,  
and the drum in the street  
proclaims them by law!

If those who committed a fault  
realise it, reform, and bow before you,

you grant them mercy  
strung upon a thread of love.

You honour the guests who come,  
and the splendid queen

whose grace wins their blessings—

her mango-like body  
alone can make your chest yield.

Even if a thousand enemy arrows come,  
you are the warrior  
whose chest does not falter!”

Thus, in the flood of poetry  
poured out by Oonpothi Pasungudaiyar,

the simile steals the heart!

The greatness of the Chola king's strong chest,  
fearless before a powerful enemy,

seizes our own hearts too!

> * “He broke the strength  
> of the southern Parathavars,
>
> and drove back with the sword  
> the northern Vadugas…
>
> …He could not contain  
> the rare wealth and splendid ornaments
>
> of his superior greatness,  
> but poured them out.
>
> Seeing them, my great clan,  
> worn down by poverty,
>
> put on the finger-rings as earrings,  
> the earrings upon their fingers,
>
> waist-ornaments upon their necks,  
> and neck-ornaments about their waists.
>
> It was like the great red-faced monkey clan  
> that found the ornaments
>
> dropped upon the earth  
> by Sita, companion of fierce Rama,
>
> when the powerful rakshasa  
> carried her away.
>
> Our unending rare laughter  
> became sweet indeed,
>
> seeing my great clan  
> released from hard suffering.”

(*Purananuru* — Poem 378)
