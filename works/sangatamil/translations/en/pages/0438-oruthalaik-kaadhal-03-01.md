# One-Sided Love

## 3

Narkannai had thought she might send her friend Porkodi as messenger
and continue the serial story of her love.
Though good news now arrived as though the very wealth
she had gone seeking had itself come in search of her,
another piece of news her father gave
stirred great confusion in her mind!

A king from an enemy land, intending to seize Aamur,
was first coming with an army to capture its herds;
the ruler of Aamur had therefore planned
to send the powerful Killi as commander
to drive that force away and had asked him to go;
Killi would beat the war-drum and depart the next evening
for the battle called “Karandhai,” fought against an enemy
who raids the cattle;
and in the meantime he would come to Narkannai's house for the feast.
Only after Perungozhi Naaykan had finished the whole account
did Narkannai release a great sigh and regain her breath;
without moving her tongue, the maiden
blessed her father within her heart!

*

Though night and day simply carried on their own work,
ceaselessly performing the duty nature had given them upon earth,
is it not the nature of lovers, according to their condition,
to curse both night and day and then exalt and praise them?
When two bodies have become one and forgotten the world,
there are those who leap up in fierce anger and ask,
“Why is this night already racing like a hare?”
