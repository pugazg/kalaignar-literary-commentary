Men who saw her refused to close their eyelids again.

Mouths opened to praise her remained open.

Her eyes were a silver stage upon which black bees circled and danced.

One day the mother who had given birth to that lotus called her golden daughter close and seated her beside her.

She began a selection that would shape her daughter's future life.

“My dear daughter! Your father and I have decided that it is time to arrange your marriage.

“So many sons of wealthy households, enslaved by your beauty, have sent messengers.

“Several princes of royal families have sent representatives too.

“How can we agree without knowing your wish?

“Look—here are their portraits.”

The mother placed before her, one by one, the portraits of the men who wished to marry that jewel of a woman.

“Mother! Who is this man wearing a diamond crown upon his head and a pearl garland around his neck?”

“Oh, my daughter! From the very first you think only of greatness!

“This is Prince Ilanagar. He is lord of seven towns and eight hills.”

“Is that so? And this man standing with a sharp gaze and a fierce appearance—who is he?”

“Him? He is great in strength and vast in wealth. Though he lacks beauty, his riches can never be exhausted however much is taken from them.”

“Mother! I do not wish to marry any of these men.

“One has wealth. One has strength. One has beauty. One has learning. One possesses the distinction of authority.

“You are waiting to show me picture after picture of men like these, are you not?”

“Yes, daughter. Whichever of them you consider the highest, we will arrange for you to marry him.”

“Mother, must I choose only the one who is highest?”

“Yes, my dear. Even Valluvar has sung that whatever we think, we must think of what is high. Surely you have studied that Kural?”

“I have, Mother.

“But do not define ‘the high’ as merely the grandeur of walking in upper storeys or the distinction of living in mansions.

“I do not include such things at all in my list of what counts as greatness.”
