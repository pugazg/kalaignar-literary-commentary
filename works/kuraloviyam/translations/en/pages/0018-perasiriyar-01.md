# Professor

After conducting Kalaignar's *Mani Vizha*, this celebration feels almost like an opportunity to hold another such event: that was the *Mani Vizha*; this, I feel, has taken shape as a *Mani-mudi Vizha*—a crowning-jewel celebration.

Even if we say that politics is not entirely a battlefield scene, no one can forget that politics is, for the most part, a scene at the forge. Kalaignar is one who has dedicated himself there as an untiring worker—as a commander—as a leader.

One who stands ceaselessly at the forge must work amid such intense heat that sweat pours from him, his work is hindered, and fatigue can set in because of it.

A politician who has the duty of standing at that forge and shaping the implements of battle—if he goes to stroll in a grove, the gentle breeze that blows there would bring his mind delight, fulfilment, enthusiasm and a smile. In the same way, Kalaignar stands at the forge of politics. When the need arises, he stands on the battlefield. Yet, from about his fifteenth year onward, he made it natural for himself to wander also in this literary grove; he breathes in all its fragrant flowers; he distinguishes each fragrance separately; and, as though filling a manuscript once again with all those scents, he fills his writing with them and turns them into a feast for everyone else. To that extent he shines as an artist with artistic sensibility who moves through the literary grove, and through it he has made this literary flower-garden a counterweight to all the weariness that politics brings him.
