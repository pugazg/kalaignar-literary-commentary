“She too may have been absorbed in the stream of my music in the same way.

“Yes—she was.

“Many days later, when we finally met, surrendered ourselves to one another and rolled in embrace beneath the shade of a punnai tree, that is exactly what she told me.

“What did she say?

“‘Whether on that day in the dance hall, or on the one or two occasions when I met you afterward, not even the shadow of a feeling called love crossed my heart.

“‘But the people of this town misunderstood me.

“‘Without understanding my artistic heart, they spread public gossip and began saying openly that love had budded and ripened between you and me.

“‘That talk itself created such a feeling within me.’”

Do you know what answer I gave Malarvizhi when she said this?

“‘My story is the same, my honey, my fragrant Tamil!

“‘I became a slave only to your art of dance.

“‘But once the stories this town tied around the two of us had spread in every direction like horses whipped into a gallop, I too decided there was nothing left to fear.

“‘Let us bless these townspeople who, without even knowing it themselves, helped us and brought us together.’”

We blessed them together.

The birds in the branches of the punnai tree blessed us too.

> Not knowing the worth of the flower-eyed woman,  
> this town's public gossip gave her to me.
