He says that darkness waits for the moment when the lamp will lose its light.

Only when we see what event he compares this imagination with do we gain the satisfaction of fully experiencing its literary flavour. Just as darkness waits for the lamp to stop shining so that it may enter, Valluvar shows us another figure waiting in the same way.

Do you know who it is? Its name is pallor. Darkness waits for the lamp's light to disappear. What does this pallor wait for? It waits to spread over a woman's body. What is the way for it to do so?

The hero is embracing the heroine. If two figures, male and female, were made of wax, pressed together in an embrace, and a glowing fire were lit between them, the two figures would melt and merge into one. So too the hero and heroine embraced in delight. Pallor longs to spread over that green-parrot maiden.

Only when the lamp goes out can darkness enter. In the same way, if even the slightest interval arises in the hero's embrace of the heroine, pallor immediately flies in and seizes the maiden. In that loving event where they embrace deeply and rejoice, it is enough for the heroine to be left alone for a moment; pallor will spread over her.

Thus, like darkness waiting for the lamp to disappear, pallor waits for the pause taken by the hero who embraces the heroine. If even a moment's slackening occurs in that delightful embrace, pallor's invasion strikes her body.

What a splendid imagination! Darkness waiting for the lamp to vanish; pallor waiting for a moment's lapse in the embrace—this sweet Kural compares the two:

> As darkness waits for the lamp's light to cease,  
> pallor waits for my beloved's embrace to end.

Chapter 119 — Suffering from Pallor; Kural 1186

`முயக்கம்` — embracing; `அற்றம்` — ceasing; `பசப்பு` — pallor.
