“Bring me the spear,” he says—she gives him her gaze. “Where is the blood-stained sword?” he asks—she offers her red lips. “This is no time for that, my dear, this very moment! O companion from whom I cannot be parted!” His commander stands ready, looking toward him. “Give me the spear; give me leave! O beauty without a waist!” Pouring out a flood of love as though a sluice had opened, the warrior sets off toward the ranks of the great heroes.

On the way his mother sees him and blesses him: “Return, tiger, with victory!” she says. “I know that your commander bears a little anger toward you. Do you think I would not know merely because you hid it, my son? The ranks that go to give their heads for the greatness of the motherland are waiting—go and do your duty, my dear.” She kisses the wound already borne upon his chest.

The warrior goes to battle, smashes the enemy and casts them into the rising dust. Amid the mire of blood, warriors on both sides prepare for their final journey. Then a message flies in that final victory is going to the enemy. Crying, “Are we blind though we have eyes?”, our bull of a warrior sweeps across the field like a whirlwind. Alone, before his commander's very eyes, he destroys much of the enemy force, drives away the cloud of defeat and sows the seed of victory. Alas! In the end he becomes the victim of an arrow that had come unseen.

The victorious commander sees the warrior fallen and cries out in grief.

The commander, who had held some difference of mind toward him because of a small bitterness caused by the mischief of lesser men, now bathes the fallen bull of a warrior in tears.

The dying warrior says:

“Commander! Deaths on the battlefield that make the eyes of the leader who protects his people overflow with tears—warriors should obtain such deaths even if they have to beg for them.”

That his commander sheds tears at his death gives the warrior such great pride. The heroic death that brought tears to the commander's eyes is worthy of praise. Here is the Kural for it:

> If death can make the eyes of one's protecting lord brim with tears,  
> such a death is worth begging for.

Chapter 78 — Martial Pride; Kural 780
