That is, he marvels that the strength which makes enemies tremble with fear on the battlefield has been brought to a halt before the shining forehead of this great beauty. Even when sunlight struck the fronts of chariots on the field and each chariot appeared as a blaze of light, he had stood against them without his eyes shrinking, fought and won. Yet he laments that though he conquered that chariot force, he cannot even stand before the radiance of her forehead.

After the chariot force, he sees an elephant force in her as well. The beauty with which Valluvar paints it is beauty indeed!

> The cloth upon a woman's firm, unyielding breasts  
> is like the eye-cover laid upon a rutting elephant.

The beautiful upper cloth worn upon a woman's firm, unsagging breasts is like the face-cover laid upon a rutting male elephant.

Because the covering placed upon an elephant's face is arranged so as to conceal its eyes, Valluvar calls it an “eye-cover.” By comparing the elephant's face-cover with a woman's upper cloth, he seems to make the elephant's very head a comparison for the woman's swelling, full breasts! After the chariot force, the young warrior now sees this elephant force before him as well.

How long can two opposing armies stand facing one another? Is a “Gita sermon” taking place here? No! She began to shoot her arrow. Yes—she looked at him. What is that look like to him? He tells himself:

“She looked at me. I looked at her. In answer to my gaze, she looks back at me with an opposing gaze. Alas! She alone is enough to strike me down! And not stopping with that, the way she looks at me with her eyes makes it seem as though she has brought an entire army to defeat me!”

That great warrior who made enemies turn their backs on the battlefield sees before him a peahen-like woman appearing as both chariot force and elephant force. Is not the explanation these Kurals give of how completely he is overcome by a sense of defeat as sweet as a segment of jackfruit?

> She looked at me; when I looked back, her answering glance  
> was like a death-dealing goddess attacking with an army.
