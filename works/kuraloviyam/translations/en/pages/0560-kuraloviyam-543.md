"Brother! Is this right? Do you see how many boys have been left yearning?" he asked with concern.

"My skill won it for me! Am I responsible for their helplessness?"

That was the youth's reply. Just then, at the entrance of a large house nearby, a servant woman threw out four or five used banana leaves and went inside. A little food remained on each leaf. A hungry crow saw the leaves fall in the street. It flew down and settled beside them. The very next moment it began crying, "Caw! Caw!" A flock of ten or fifteen crows gathered there. Without fighting among themselves, calmly and with restraint, they ate the food left on the leaves.

The devotee looked at the young man and said:

"Brother! Did you see that? This crow is only an ordinary bird. Yet even it does not think that food it has found must be consumed by itself alone. It calls its own kind and has the quality of sharing its meal. Only those with such a nature rise in this world. One who says 'everything is for me' gains no prosperity—only ruin. Learn this, brother!"

After saying this, the devotee went away. Ashamed of what he had done, the young man lowered his head in regret.

The Kural says:

"The crow does not hide what it finds; it calls its circle and eats. In this world, prosperity belongs only to those with that nature."

> The crow conceals not, but calls its kind and eats;  
> prosperity belongs only to those of such a nature.
