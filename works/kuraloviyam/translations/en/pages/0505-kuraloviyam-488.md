## Visual material

Large upper illustration of soldiers carrying a stone with a mounted figure nearby.

It was the time when Cheran Senguttuvan, elder brother of Ilango Adigal who gave the Tamil world the treasure of Silappathikaram, ruled his homeland with overflowing valour.

Among the famous kings of the northern lands were two named Kanakan and Vijayan.

Their tongues knew no restraint. They were men who could easily mock anyone.

One day they attended a feast and staggered about drunk with liquor.

Even then their arrogance did not fail to reveal itself.

Some kings at the feast spoke of the old accounts that the three crowned Tamil kings had once marched their armies as far as the Himalayas and engraved upon the mountains their emblems—the tiger, the bow and the fish.

Kanakan and Vijayan flared up.

They mocked the valour of the Tamil kings.

“When the Chera, Chola or Pandya marched proudly all the way to the Himalayas and returned, there must not have been kings like us living here. Had we been here, they would have turned their backs and fled!”

they jeered.
