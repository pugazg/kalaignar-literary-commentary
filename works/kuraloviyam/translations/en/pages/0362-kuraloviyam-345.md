## Visual material

Large upper illustration of a Roman ruler seated between two women.

“A fortress of warriors—a camp of warriors”—thus fame spread in every direction about Rome! Those who wore the crown to rule that land gathered armies, crossed frontier after frontier, raised their flags upon the forts of other countries and rejoiced to beat the drums of victory. At the sound of their horses' hooves, the enemy camp trembled and cried out. Those great warriors, who regarded the battlefield as a playground and counted the glorious wounds upon their bodies as the very numbers of victory, raised their swords and charged, never knowing how to bow at an enemy's feet. Those diamond-hearted men who would not bend delighted in drawing the borders of their empire with bubbling blood. Rome, once the land of warriors who learned courage in the cradle and cut down enemies in ranks upon ranks, later began to say that the pleasure found upon a bed was heaven itself—and that even heaven could not equal that pleasure—and began to prove it in practice!

The tiger-like warrior kings who had raised weapons of war now longed to lift and embrace pleasure-queens as though they too were weapons of war, biting soft lips, tasting tender bodies and drowning in delight. Through the night—
