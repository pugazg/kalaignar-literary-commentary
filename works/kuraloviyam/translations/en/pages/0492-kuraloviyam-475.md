—thousands upon thousands of times. I have never tired of it, so even now I keep looking at those honeyed jackfruit-sweet lips.

“But leave that aside. Wherever I am, you keep staring as though your eyes would swallow mine. Why?”

She loosed the arrow of her question and rested upon his lap.

“Even if your eyes look like bees sitting in the middle of white flowers and drinking nectar, they aim at me like arrows released from a bowstring. I keep my gaze fixed, waiting to see when they will strike. Do you not understand even that?”

he said.

He lifted the woman from his lap and embraced her against his chest.

The clothes, unwilling to be crushed and wrinkled in the commotion, took temporary leave of them.

The stars winked mockingly at the round moon.

The moon pulled a cloud across its face.

On the riverbank their two bodies joined so tightly that not even air could enter between them.

“Sea! Sea! A sea of delight!”

their lips danced softly.

“Yes. When we are united, pleasure truly is an ocean. And just so, when love torments us, sorrow rises in a flood greater than the sea itself.”

Even in that journey through the city of delight, they worried that the city of sorrow called separation might cross their path.

> Pleasure is an ocean; but when love causes suffering,  
> its sorrow grows greater than that sea.

Chapter 117 — Lamenting in Wasting Longing; Kural 1166

அடுங்கால் = when it causes suffering
