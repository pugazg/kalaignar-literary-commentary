## Visual material

Large upper illustration of a turbaned patron and another man speaking before an attendant.

The villagers had named him “Vidiyan,” and not as a joke. No place his feet touched ever seemed to see a dawn of prosperity. Nothing he touched shone—and there was nothing he would not touch.

With his own welfare as his sole aim, he would attach himself to anyone and cling on. Once attached, he would not allow anyone else to come near that place.

He found friends in mansions with upper stories. Through Vidiyan's association those friends would be reduced to paupers and end up living on raised roadside verandas. That was all. Vidiyan would simply look elsewhere for the next hunting ground of self-interest.

Though he had arranged such a life for himself, he would point at others and call them “self-seekers” and “mad for office.” Yet every beat of his own heart throbbed with selfish desire:

“Position! Position!”

At a moment of trial he cut the throat, figuratively, of a friend who had loved him as life itself, and instead sought out and secured the friendship of a prosperous man of that town named Peraalan.
