## Visual material

Large upper illustration of an embracing couple in an intimate indoor setting beside an open moonlit window.

This was a meeting between Thingal and Sevvai!

Not the names of days, but the names of two jewel-like women cast in moulds of beauty.

Her parents named the maiden with a cool, tender face Thingal. The parents of the woman whose red lips looked like a split-open mango named her Sevvai.

What problem could there be between Thingal and Sevvai?

“What is it, Thingal? Your face looks as though you are cursing someone,” Sevvai asked as she came near.

“I am not cursing anyone. I am cursing the illness that has fastened itself upon me. Look, Sevvai! This love-sickness that my lover has caused to rule all over my body has clung to me so stubbornly that it torments me and drives me to follow the lover who has left me!”

Saying this, Thingal shed pearl-like tears.

Without Sevvai even realizing it, her lips murmured the Kural:

> Was this suffering that has come upon me granted  
> only to make me follow the one who has cast me off?
