Because of their stubborn attachment to office, some people wander about having lost both honour and judgement, feeling no shame at the reproach of the town or the contempt of the world.

Valluvar condemns those who lose honour for selfish gain.

Yet if he also permits even the priceless possession called honour to be sacrificed for the advancement of one's people, is he not making it clear that the people must be placed first and the self afterward?

Those who “work for the kudi”—that is, those engaged in raising their people—need not wait for a convenient time before beginning that work.

“There is no season for those who labour for their people,” he says.

Rather than taking “season” to mean only the proper time, may we not also understand that for those who work for their people's advancement there is no question of whether it is youth or old age?

The Kural declares that if those who labour for the uplift of their people grow weary through laziness—or if they worry that the insults and accusations hurled by enemies have injured their honour—they will fail to achieve their goal of raising their people.

The same Valluvar who said honour is higher than life says that when one works for the advancement of one's people, one must not even pause to worry about honour; otherwise the progress of the people will be ruined.

> For those who work to raise their people there is no season;  
> if they grow idle and think of honour, the work will fail.

Chapter 103 — The Work of Sustaining One's House and Line; Kural 1028

மடி = laziness
