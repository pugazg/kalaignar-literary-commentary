## Visual material

Large upper illustration of physician Maragatham reclining, with the remembered figure of a man above.

Maragatham held a degree in medicine. At the hospital where she worked, all the patients believed that if they received medicine from her hand and drank it, their illness would disappear.

Sweet-spoken and flawless in beauty, the moment she entered the hospital a new bustle would arise as a matter of course. She would go near every patient lying there and examine them. One patient would be tormented by a severe headache; she would order a medicine to stop the pain immediately.

Another would be suffering from a high fever. She would examine him with an instrument that measured how high the fever was, then write instructions to her assistants on how to reduce it little by little, relieve his weakness and administer the necessary medicines.

A woman writhing from a lightning-swift pain that had struck her lower abdomen would cry out, “Amma! I cannot bear it. If there is any medicine that will let me die, give it to me.” Even then Maragatham, smiling, would pat and console her, perform surgery on her lower abdomen at once and cure her.
