—and what a fitting place he found for it!

A river comes roaring in flood.

If its banks break, many villages and fields will be destroyed.

The alarming news reaches the king.

An order is issued at once to strengthen the banks.

Thousands are put to work, and the embankments are reinforced and raised.

Villages and property are saved from the destruction the flood might have caused.

Then another thought arises in the minds of those governing the land:

“Why allow this overflowing water simply to run wastefully into the sea? Why not build a permanent dam here, store the floodwater, and release it into fields and forests when needed?”

So, following the work of raising the banks, they successfully complete the construction of a dam as well.

The region prospers.

Valluvar's idea is that while performing one action, another action too should be accomplished through it.

Use one action to complete another.

He compares the first action to the trained elephant, and the second action to the wild elephant that is to be captured and trained through it.

What thought! What a comparison! And how many centuries ago!

> Make one action produce another—  
> as one elephant is used to capture another.

Chapter 68 — The Method of Action; Kural 678
