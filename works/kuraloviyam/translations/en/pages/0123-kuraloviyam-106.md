The younger man was somewhat startled. Even so, he went into the next room as instructed.

While both the elder and the younger man were fast asleep in their separate rooms, the owner of the rest-house quietly came around to the back of the elder man's room, lifted the chest that lay beside him and ran. By then the elder man had awakened and caught hold of him. The captured man stabbed him in the chest with the short sword he carried. In the commotion, the younger man came running, stopped the owner of the rest-house and tied him to a pillar there. Then he saw his friend pleading for life before him, and tears poured from his eyes.

“My dear friend! My younger friend! I had examined your friendship and become certain of it before placing my trust in you. Yet, needlessly, I suddenly began to suspect you. This wicked man has corrupted my mind. And not only that: without examining him at all, I trusted him immediately. Now I am suffering the result. Forgive me.

> Trusting one without examining him, and doubting one  
> whom one has examined and trusted, bring unending misery.

That Kural has proved true in my own case.”

Even as he said this, the elder man died.

The younger man's eyes looked like waterfalls.

> To trust someone without examining and understanding him, and to doubt one whom one has examined, understood and trusted, both bring great suffering.

Chapter 51 — Knowing and Trusting; Kural 510
