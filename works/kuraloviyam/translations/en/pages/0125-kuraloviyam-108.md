“I have been noticing it too. Every day you are the one who runs to his house like a dog and plays with him. Has he ever come to our house even once to play with you? Why should you go to the house of someone who does not value you? Just try going again! I won't be gentle after that!”

“All right, Amma! I won't go anymore!”

Why does she pay such deep attention to this conversation between her mother and younger brother? Ah! How many changes pass across her face! She runs from the window and throws herself upon the bed. Tears pour down. What her mother said to her brother resounds again and again like scripture:

“You are the one who runs to his house. Has he ever come to our house even once?”

Why should she cry over these words? Her heart looks at her and asks, “Why are you crying, girl?” She gives no answer. Thinking, she sobs.

“My heart! I tell you exactly what my mother told my younger brother. My brother Marudhan runs to Thenappan's house in the next street to play. But Thenappan stays in his own house without coming here. My heart! When my lover's heart does not come seeking me, why do you alone leave me and keep running in search of him, just as Marudhan runs seeking Thenappan?”

If her heart had answered her, what might it have said?

“Girl! My dear! I will not submit as your brother Marudhan submitted to his mother. Even if your lover's heart does not come seeking me, I will not stop going in search of him!” it might have said.

To make us understand that the heart of the jewel-adorned maiden is just like this, the great Valluvar delights in turning the heart of the peacock-like woman into a picture.

> Though you see that his heart is his own,  
> why, my heart, do you not belong to me?

Chapter 130 — Chiding the Heart; Kural 1291
