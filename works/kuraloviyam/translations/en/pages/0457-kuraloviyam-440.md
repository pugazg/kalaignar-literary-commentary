## Visual material

Large upper illustration of a young man holding a rescued woman beside the river.

The overflowing Kaveri ran through that grove beneath the silver moonlight, winding its body as it flowed. The poet who regularly sat beneath a fruit-laden tree on the bank, weaving poems of imagination, had not failed to come that night either.

His heart was captivated by the sight of the moon's fingers of light teasing the river-maiden's wave-hands.

Then came a small boat. In it was a woman of extraordinary beauty—like a bronze statue, like a tiny decorated chariot. She was rowing the boat toward the opposite bank.

“Nighttime! A woman alone! Where could she be going?”

The question struck the young poet. He abandoned his imagination and kept watching the young doe's boat.

A cry came from the boat as it neared the far bank:

“Alas!”

No second cry followed.

The poet sprang up in alarm. It was clearly visible in the moonlight: the boat had overturned.

The young poet jumped into the river and swam swiftly toward the capsized boat. He reached the beauty, lifted her, carried her to the opposite bank and laid her down. Without hesitation he gave her the treatment she needed and stood there rejoicing: “She has survived.”

She lay there exhausted—
