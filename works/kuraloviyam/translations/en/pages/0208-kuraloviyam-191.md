The enemy army besieging the capital grew restless to enter the fort and bring down the flagstaff.

Understanding that the future well-being of their country depended upon smashing the surrounding enemy to pieces, the commander raised a battle-cry among the assembled warriors:

“O warrior-gems, strong as bulls!
Do you know what gives beauty to a warrior?
In his lifetime, among the days that have passed,
not every day is truly a day!
What other day belongs to him?
Which is the golden day he desires?

Here! Valluvar tells us—listen:

> Taking stock of his days, he counts as wasted  
> all those on which he received no wound of honour.

Are these not Valluvar's lines of valour, declaring that every day on which a warrior went to the battlefield yet received no honourable wound upon his body is a useless day!

My beloved warriors, who know this truth!

Death is death at six and death is death at a hundred—let what must happen, happen! Raise the war-cry! There is our enemy! Come, let us pay the price in wounds of honour and win the fruit of victory! The enemy's bannered chariot is circling the wall of our fort! Break it apart and throw it into the moat—set forth! There! There! Those are not flashes of lightning at the floor of the heavens; they are the gleam of the enemy army's swords!

Let your eyes open upon the enemy, overflowing with anger. But one thing—do not forget! Do not forget the rule Valluvar laid down for eyes that look upon the enemy in wrath. If eyes opened fiercely at the foe close and blink when they see the spear hurled by the enemy, that reveals defeat for the warrior.

> If the eye opened fierce against the foe closes and blinks  
> when a spear is hurled, is that not defeat to the valiant?

Kuralar says that even blinking at the thrust of the spear is cowardice!

Let that Kural ring out! Let the war-drum thunder! Grind to dust the enemy massing thick as honey!”

The battle begins—

Can we ever forget the two Kurals suited to this valour-filled scene?
