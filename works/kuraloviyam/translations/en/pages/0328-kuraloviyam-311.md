## Visual material

Large illustration of a richly adorned woman at sunset.

A painting of beauty.  
A printed goddess.  
A tender young parrot.  
A graceful dove.  
Pearls of a pomegranate.  
A ruby creeper.

One could go on piling up descriptions of her like this. Words themselves would become scarce before they could adequately praise that body of pure gold. Youth flourished in her from crown to sole. Hers was a maidenhood capable of drawing even an ascetic who had renounced the world. Brows like a bow; two eyes like arrows. One might call her row of teeth a pearl garland—but a pearl garland gives no taste when kissed! Were not her teeth pearls soaked in a waterfall of honeyed nectar? Two proud peaks rose without concern that her slender waist might snap beneath them. She was a treasure-house of unrestrained beauty that composed poems of delight, to such an extent that Tamil poets could escape only by concluding, “In short, let us call her a flowering creeper of beauty.”
