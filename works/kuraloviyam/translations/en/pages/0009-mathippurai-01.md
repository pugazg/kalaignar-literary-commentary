**By Dr. V. Sub. Manickam, eminent Tamil scholar  
and former Vice-Chancellor of Madurai University**

# Critical Appreciation

It is my duty to write a critical appreciation of *Kuraloviyam*, which our Kalaignar reflected upon day after day and painted week after week as a Tamil word-picture; it is also an added distinction for my writing.

Who is the author who wrote *Kuraloviyam*? What manner of person is he? What is the nobility of his heart? What breath animates his thought? Even a reader who encounters this *Kuraloviyam* centuries from now will think as follows: “The author of this book is rich in love of Tamil, knowledge of Tamil and Tamil feeling. He is steeped in Sangam literature. He holds that Tamil civilization is human civilization. He is an imaginative poet who creates new literature. In both letter and word he is a prose-poet rich in alliterative and linked expression. He has a dramatist’s eye. He has studied many commentaries. He has held high office in the government of the land. He possesses a world-political outlook. By moving closely among people of many fields he has acquired a firm grasp of the life of his age. In the training-ground of life he has seen both mountain and sea. His tongue is undefeated; his heart does not falter. He is a rationalist who does not enter the whirlpool of caste and religion. He is a man of thought in action, a mind shaped by the Thirukkural, a leader of the Tamil people. In the arts of writing, speech, drama, poetry, politics and beauty alike, he is truly a Kalaignar whose heart, intellect and action are immersed in them.” There can be no doubt that the Tamils of the future, just as we do today, will esteem the many capacities of this author. Why? Could this *Kuraloviyam* have been written as an artistic epic by anyone other than a great artist in whom so many arts dwell together? Fertility reveals the excellence of the soil; Tamil reveals the excellence of melody; the colours of thought reveal the excellence of Kalaignar.

Dr. Kalaignar’s *Kuraloviyam* contains many new methods. Its first distinction is the way an appropriate situation or background is set before each Kural. The Thirukkural did not spring from a single day. Rather, Valluvar examined the movement of human life and distilled it into Kural form; many events he witnessed became the essence of Kurals. Knowing this truth, our Kalaignar Karunanidhi guides the learner by placing around each Kural a fitting setting, even when that setting is imaginative. In view of this distinctive method, *Kuraloviyam* may also be called a “situational commentary.” If the traditional word “commentary” is to retain its special value, displaying the field of words is easy; displaying the background is not so easy. We see the generosity with which, for some Kurals, even two or three pages of background have been laid out.
