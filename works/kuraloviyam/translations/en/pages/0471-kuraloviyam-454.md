“They spin all sorts of fantasies—that their feet never touch the ground, that their eyelids never move. Some call those women celestial maidens.

“I thought of writing about those celestial women. But when one chaste, sculpted beauty—one fish-eyed beauty, the jewel of my eye—melts inwardly thinking only of me, would I write about celestial maidens instead?”

“Who is that sculpted beauty, that lovely-eyed woman, that jewel?”

Her coral lips trembled. Her swelling breast revealed how her heart yearned to know the answer.

“In a grove humming with bees, I studied many brightly coloured peacocks and selected one. I thought I might turn it into a word-picture.

“But she shines like a chariot of beauty that has pushed even the grace and fascination of the peacock I chose into second place. So should I praise her as a celestial maiden? Should I praise her as a peerless peacock? Both garlands of praise fall short.

“The reason is that she stands as a woman of this earth wearing heavy, radiant earrings. In beauty and grace she defeats both the celestial maiden and the peacock.

“So I wondered whether to celebrate the one who captured my heart simply as an earthly woman—neither a woman of the heavens nor a peacock with its plume spread wide.

“No. None of those will do. I finally wrote of her only as ‘the woman who bewildered my heart.’”

Even after hearing his answer, the young woman was not satisfied.

“Who is this woman who bewildered your heart?”

she demanded angrily, trying to rise and leave.

“You are!”

He caught her by the waist, drew her back and laid her across his lap.

Then he sang this Kural melodiously:

> Is she a celestial maiden? A peerless peacock?  
> A woman with heavy earrings? My heart is bewildered.

She sulked. Then they were united.
