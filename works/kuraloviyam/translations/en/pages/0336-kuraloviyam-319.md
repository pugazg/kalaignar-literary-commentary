## Visual material

Large upper illustration with a large turbaned male portrait at left and the crowned small ruler Arimaanenjan speaking with the bearded poet at right.

“No matter how much wealth it costs, I must obtain the enmity of that Narithalaivan. Gaining it is the only thing that can give me boundless joy.”

Arimaanenjan, who had been praised in song by the poet seated beside him, said this to him. Arimaanenjan was the ruler of the little kingdom called Kayal Nadu. Narithalaivan was the man who had won the chance to be counted as his close friend. True to his name, he possessed a crafty mind and, weaving webs of intrigue, had driven away Arimaanenjan's beloved relatives and friends. Whenever the poet had explained this truth, Arimaanenjan had refused to believe it. Now, when he told the same poet that he wished to obtain Narithalaivan's enmity even at the price of wealth, the poet was astonished.

“Is this the guardian of Kayal Nadu speaking in such a way? Why has anger suddenly flared up against Narithalaivan? Whenever I told you he was evil—more cruel than fire—you refused to accept my words! Even now he has shown you no enmity. Is he not still your friend? What good will come from suddenly making him your enemy?”

The poet asked Arimaanenjan this with a teasing smile.
