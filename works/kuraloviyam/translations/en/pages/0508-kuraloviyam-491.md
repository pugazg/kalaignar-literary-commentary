Did that anger last?

No.

The man who had been separated tried to remain firm: “Let her come to me herself.”

He could not.

Nor could he patiently wait and think, “Let that young woman who has been separated from me fly back to me herself.”

A canal branches from a great river and nourishes the fields. It is always full of water.

A bridge made from a palm trunk crosses it.

From this bank he thought she would come.

From the other bank she believed he would come.

But the sickness of love that had seized them burned away the firmness of their hearts like fire.

He began walking onto the bridge.

She too began walking.

They stopped in the middle, staring at one another without moving their eyes away.

From a little distance came the sound of laughter.

They looked closely.

It was her friend.

“The proud resolve not to follow someone who has rejected and left us—people suffering from love-sickness know nothing of that.”

the friend said.

“What are you saying?”

the red-faced young woman demanded angrily.

“Kural, girl—Kural!”

said the friend, and bounded away.

> The proud resolve not to follow one who has turned away  
> is unknown to those afflicted by love-sickness.

Chapter 126 — Loss of Restraint; Kural 1255

செற்றார் = one who has rejected or turned away
