As early as two thousand years ago, Valluvar had to declare with force and clarity: "Birth is equal." Why?

Just as society in this twentieth century lies fragmented into hundreds and thousands of caste divisions, in Valluvar's own time too people must have cherished notions of high and low by birth. Unable to bear that pain, Valluvar composed the Kural saying that birth is equal to all living beings.

The moment Valluvar said "all living beings," the animals, the birds, and the crawling families of worms and insects came before him and piled question upon question.

"Among us there have never been caste quarrels. We have never raised caste feelings and created disturbances because of them. If that is so, is it proper for you to demean us too by including us in the statement 'birth is equal to all living beings'?"

Thus the animals asked. The birds asked. The crawling creatures asked.
