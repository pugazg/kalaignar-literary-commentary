—to the entire village. Some declared that an end must somehow be put to this disgrace. “Why not drive him out of the village altogether?” one man asked boldly, then looked all around to make sure the drunkard was not arriving somewhere nearby.

“I can reform him,” another said, thumping his chest as he stood up. Then came a ringing burst of laughter. Everyone turned in surprise. “Impossible, my friend, impossible,” said Valluvar as he entered the assembly.

“Why is it impossible? I can do it,” said the man who had boasted earlier.

Valluvar said calmly, “If anyone has a gold coin, throw it into the pond opposite us.”

Hearing Valluvar, one man threw a gold coin he had into the pond.

Looking at those gathered there, Valluvar asked, “Can any one of you dive into the water and search for that coin and bring it back?” A young man saying, “I can,” plunged into the water and began searching.

Valluvar called the man who had boasted that he could reform the drunkard. “Brother, do you know in which part of the pond the young man who dived for the gold coin is now searching?”

“I do not,” came the answer. “Then take this lamp, dive into the water yourself, and you can find where he is,” Valluvar said, placing a clay lamp in his hand.

The man laughed mockingly. “What kind of joke is this? Take a lamp underwater to search for someone? Won't the lamp go out?”

“That is exactly what I too am saying, brother. Trying to reform a man lying stupefied by liquor by giving him reasons is like trying to find someone submerged in water by carrying a flame,” Valluvar said, and then uttered a Kural for it:

> Reasoning with a drunken man is like carrying a flame  
> to search for one submerged beneath the water.

Chapter 93 — Abstaining from Liquor; Kural 929

`களித்தான்` means a drunkard.
