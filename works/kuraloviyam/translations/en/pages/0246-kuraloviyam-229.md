—Poonguzhali rejoiced again and again at the good fortune that had come to her. She wished that Maangani too would marry and gain a good life as she had.

After the wedding, unable to meet Poonguzhali often and rejoice with her in song and play, Maangani continued to suffer from longing. Five or six months passed. Preparations began for Maangani's marriage as well.

Poonguzhali and her husband took the lead and conducted Maangani's auspicious wedding with order and splendour. Maangani's husband then took her with him and left for his village.

Since Maangani had not found a bridegroom in her own village as Poonguzhali had, she had to go to a village far beyond easy reach.

The life-long friends were separated. For Poonguzhali, the pain of that separation gradually lessened under the affection poured upon her by her husband.

The man Maangani had married was also strikingly handsome enough to enchant her. To that handsome man, she herself became his Maangani. Yet somehow, though her body received pleasure, her heart did not.

After some days Maangani and her husband came back to her village. Her husband did not refuse the invitation to stay for a day or two at his father-in-law's house. On the evening of the day they arrived, Maangani ran to see Poonguzhali. The two gathered each other into their arms.

“How is married life?” Poonguzhali asked Maangani. The two spoke about how each night seemed to fly past.

With a deep sigh Maangani said, “Poonguzhali! What is there to hide from you? In bodily union my husband carries me into the very realm of pleasure. Yet for some reason my heart does not become fulfilled along with that fullness of feeling. I expect something more from him than bodily pleasure alone, and in that I am disappointed. You must show me a way for my life and the pleasure I receive to become complete.”

Hearing this, Poonguzhali smiled. Laughing, she offered a way.
