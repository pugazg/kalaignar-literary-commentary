In Moscow's Red Square, Lenin's body and, nearby, Stalin's body had been preserved and displayed. Because of Khrushchev's hostility toward Stalin, he ordered that Stalin's preserved body be removed. The order was carried out immediately.

Stalin's names, symbols and statues were for the most part erased or removed.

Khrushchev, who had done all this, was himself removed from office by members of his own party within a year or two.

For a time people did not even know where he had gone or what had become of him.

Much later a report appeared saying that, during an election in the Soviet Union, Khrushchev had come to vote at some polling station and no one had taken any notice of him.

Later even the news of his death appeared in the papers only as a very small item in a dark corner.

At one time, news about Khrushchev had occupied the front page—indeed whole pages—of newspapers around the world. That was all the honour his death received.

A crocodile is powerful only while it remains in water. Once it leaves the water, can even an ordinary creature not defeat it? Many people in authority are like that crocodile. Should they not remember what will become of them if they leave the water that gives them strength?

Does not Khrushchev's history stand as an example of this?

> The crocodile conquers in deep water; once it leaves  
> that conquering water, others can overcome it.

Chapter 50 — Knowing the Place; Kural 495
