# Maha Vidwan Dandapani Desikar

The arrangement of this book is a beautiful one: two Kurals concern Aram and Porul, and the third concerns Inbam. I have wondered why he handled it in this way.

An artist who creates an epic or a painting must always be able to carry it through to the end without allowing its flavour to diminish. This strength has soaked deeply into Kalaignar's mind. That is why he has placed the Book of Inbam at intervals and completed the painting without allowing weariness to arise in us.

I see Kalaignar carrying this painting forward in a very beautiful manner, without letting its flavour diminish. This book has produced wonder even in me—why do I say even in me? Because I am one who knows how to savour—and I have also received the opportunity to praise it.

If Kalaignar were to paint pictures for all the other Kurals as well, I believe it would benefit everyone. All our efforts are efforts on the page; all his efforts are efforts of thought. This effort of thought will triumph; the Tamil people will rise.

For a long time, the Thirukkural remained as though it were a possession of scholars. In my twelfth year itself I memorized and recited it together with Parimelazhagar's commentary.

At that time there was no one to explain how something memorized and recited in this way could later be put to use. Now our esteemed Kalaignar, through the beauty of his art, has impressed it upon the mind as something to be seen.

I have read and enjoyed it from time to time. Today *Kuraloviyam* has taken shape in a manner useful to everyone, from the young to an old man like me.

By savouring it again and again, I have transformed my old age.

This *Kuraloviyam* should not stop with 354 Kurals; it should continue to grow and become a painting for the whole Thirukkural. Until then, Kalaignar's life and prosperity will continue to grow; Tamil will rise; Tamil will grow; Tamil will live. It is with that hope for the future that I offer him my wishes.
