“We will grant that boon later to the sugarcane doors at the entrance of our mouths.

“For now, wrap me tightly against your chest. Let my cheeks add their taste!”

He laid her face upon the soft mattress of his chest and forgot himself.

“Woman who wounded me with your breasts! Golden jewel who stole me with the light of your eyes! Why are you so intoxicated merely by resting upon my chest, my doe?”

he asked in a trembling voice, staggering in delight.

She answered:

“Thief who stole me! You have seen people who drink liquor, have you not?

“In that intoxication they may even perform degrading acts. They become slaves to liquor, accept suffering, and even become objects of public ridicule.

“Yet after drinking and rejoicing in that state, they want to drink still more of the same liquor.

“Thief, your chest is like liquor that makes those intoxicated by it desire it again and again.

“That is why I keep wanting the pleasure of burying my face in your chest.”

After that explanation there was no room left for speech.

> Even if it causes shameful suffering, the drunkard desires liquor again;  
> so, thief, is your chest to me.
