—beauty upon beauty! Her waist curves like two crescents joined left and right. Her walk is a dance.

She is the one now lying on the ground like the bare string of a garland from which every flower has fallen. What other pain could it be?

Selavan, who had given Seyizhai pleasure like Kovalan gave Madhavi—embracing her, holding her, wetting her lips, tasting her, giving every delight and rejoicing—left to earn wealth, and two months have waned and vanished.

The colour of pallor that had waited restlessly for the moment he would leave so that it could fly to her and seize her body now embraced Seyizhai.

Can that colour be cast off like a garment on fire? Only when her companion returns to loosen the suffering called pallor that has seized her can he loosen her garment too, upon the bed scented with akil.

Her sighs are blazing fire; the flow of blood is a burning pipe; her gaze is empty and without light. Her grandmother is an old woman ripened by age. She knows that her granddaughter is now enduring some of the very torments she herself endured in her youth. She approached Seyizhai to offer comfort.

“What is this, my dear? Why such suffering? Will the one who left never return? Tch, tch! What a foolish girl you are! Is this how you let pallor spread over you merely by thinking of separation?”

Seyizhai knew that her grandmother had not spoken after forgetting her own youth. She understood too that the old woman was only scolding her affectionately in order to console her. Even so, forgetting her wasted body and exhausted state, she sat up angrily and replied.

“Grandmother! For people like you it is easy to blame me and say I have grown pale because of separation. Instead of blaming me, none of you thinks of finding fault with the lover who left me. Grandmother, why? Why do you make true the saying that blame falls on one person and sin on another? Is his leaving the fault? Or is the pallor that came to me because of his leaving the fault?”

Seyizhai burst out, anger boiling over.

The grandmother felt that what she said was just. Yes—the grandmother too had old memories.

> People say only, “She has grown pale”;  
> there is no one to say, “He is the one who left her.”
