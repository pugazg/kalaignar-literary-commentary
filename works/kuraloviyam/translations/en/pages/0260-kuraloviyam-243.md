## Visual material

Large upper illustration anticipating the later reunion: the adult Alagan embraces his ill uncle while the aunt stands beside them.

He was a five-year-old child. His name was Alagan. At that tender age itself he had lost both mother and father. He grew up in his maternal uncle's house. Though his uncle showered affection upon Alagan, to his aunt the sight of him was as bitter as neem fruit.

Her heart had no room to raise Alagan as an equal to the three children she had borne. Only after serving food to her own children would she call Alagan into the kitchen.

That young stomach, tormented by hunger, would fly there and swallow with delight the food his aunt tossed to him.

Whenever that little bud longed, “Would I have come to this if my mother and father were alive?” his uncle's arms would gather him into an embrace and console him. Alagan's pain would ease.

One day his uncle had to travel to another town for work. All four children were playing in the street. They held a running race among themselves to see who would win. Alagan won.

One of his aunt's children, running fast to overtake Alagan, stumbled and fell. It was only a very small injury. Even so, he ran inside crying. When the aunt learned what had happened—
