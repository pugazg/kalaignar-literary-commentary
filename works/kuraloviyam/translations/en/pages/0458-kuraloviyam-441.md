Her clothes were soaked with water. Clinging to her body, they heightened her beauty in the white moonlight.

After a little while she opened her eyes and stared at him.

“Were you the one who saved me?”

“Yes. But leave that aside. In this darkness, alone in a boat—how much courage you have!”

“My lover lives in the small village beyond this bank. I am kept under strict guard so that I cannot meet him. I heard that he is unwell, so I set out to see him.”

When the poet learned that the beautiful woman was on her way to see her lover, he spoke to her with affection:

“Dear sister! May you and your lover live long! May the love in your hearts prevail!”

She was deeply moved by that blessing.

Instead of beginning some new danger by saying, “I saved you from danger, beautiful one—come near,” he had stood as an elder brother and accepted her as his sister. She touched his feet and bowed.

“If we measure the goodness in the love of one who helps without first calculating what benefit he may receive, it is greater than the sea.”

The meaning of that Kural continued to sound sweetly in both their hearts.

> If one weighs the goodness of help given without weighing its gain,  
> its worth is greater than the sea.
