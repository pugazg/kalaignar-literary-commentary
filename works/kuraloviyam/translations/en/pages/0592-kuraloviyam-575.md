"...remove its poisonous fangs, remove its poison sac, and only then raise it—shut inside that box! And that too only to make a living with the snake! But I have formed a friendship with mine. I live with it affectionately. We lie down and sleep together."

It was his habit to boast like this.

As usual, after finishing his work he returned to the hut, fondled his snake-companion, played with it, then laid it beside him and went to sleep. The snake too slept soundly.

Midnight.

The man, deep in sleep, rolled over. As he turned, his hand came down hard upon his companion's head.

With lightning speed the "cobra friend" hissed and raised its hood. In that same flash of fury, its poisonous fangs sank deeply into the man's neck.

The venom surged through its channels toward the nearby brain and heart.

Friendship hoisted the flag of poison. The friend's life too flew away.

From the bank of the pond came the call of a frog. Answering it, the snake quietly slithered away.

Valluvar makes clear that a life lived together with people with whom there is no harmony of mind creates a condition like living together with a snake in a hut—one in which, one day, the consequence must be faced.

> Life with those who are not of one mind  
> is like dwelling together with a snake inside a hut.

Chapter 89 — Internal Enmity; Kural 890

உடம்பாடு = harmony of mind  
குடங்கர் = hut
