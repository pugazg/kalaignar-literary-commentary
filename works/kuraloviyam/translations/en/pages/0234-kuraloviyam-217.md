—my mother understood where my gaze had fallen. She bargained for it, bought it and placed it in my hands.

Dances performed by people dressed like tigers and demons; songs of young boys selling sugarcane while crying out “Pongalo Pongal!”; the beating of drums—all these were immersing everyone who had come to the festival in delight. A bangle stall looked like a rainbow made small, with a thousand rainbows stacked upon one another! “Would you like to wear some bangles?” my mother asked eagerly. “No, amma,” I replied. Until then my eyes had swarmed over the bangles; suddenly they turned toward the bangle-seller.

Though I had told my mother I did not want bangles, I did not move away from the front of that stall. Yes, his beauty would not let me move. That handsome young bangle-seller was one of the boys of our village who had played with me in the streets when we were children. Later his family had moved to the town. And now, like Kovalan of Silappathikaram, he had appeared before me as a statue of beauty! What was I to do? Perhaps he already had a Kannagi of his own; without investigating any of that, I stood there enchanted like Madhavi.

He too embraced me with his eyes. His gaze measured every part of my form. My own eyes refused to leave him and look at the festival. My mother pressed my shoulder, pulled me away from there and took me elsewhere. So many kinds of festival events! My heart took interest in none of them. It kept circling only that bangle stall. The festival ended and we returned home. I lie turning on my bed, having said I need not even a mouthful of food.

The handsome bangle-seller has become a picture within my mind. Without my knowing it, my eyes are weeping.

“What a strange thing! It was because these wicked eyes showed him to me that I was seized by an incurable love-sickness. Why are the very eyes that brought such an illness upon me now weeping?”

The longing sigh of a young woman who shoots this question at her own eyes is this Kuraloviyam overflowing with love.

> Why do these eyes now weep? It was they who showed him,  
> and through them I saw and gained this unending illness.
