...and at once that maiden blesses him, saying, “Long life!” But suddenly a thought occurs to the woman who has just blessed him.

“Yes—what peahen of a woman was thinking of you? Why did you sneeze?” she asks, beginning a lovers' quarrel and crying playfully.

> I blessed him when he sneezed; then I wept and asked,  
> “Thinking of whom did you sneeze?”

When someone sneezes, those nearby bless them. This is an ancient custom. Using that custom, Valluvar not only has lovers begin a quarrel; he also uses the same custom to end one.

When the red-lipped woman was with her husband upon the pure soft bed, between them arose the sacred play of love called lovers' quarrel. Had she promised nine kisses but stopped after giving eight? Or had she herself drawn away and stood at a distance? Had he called her “sugarcane bound in sweetness” and then hesitated without tasting, provoking her quarrel? Did she herself rise from his lap wondering, “Why did he ask for nine by counting? Why was she afraid to receive them without counting?” However it happened, the moon of lovers' quarrel had risen over the city of delight. A few seconds of silence!

Suddenly the lover sneezed. Forgetting the quarrel, she too blessed him: “Long life!”

“You've lost! You've lost!” he said, making the maiden's red cheeks grow redder. She too became entangled in affection. The sneeze had ended their quarrel.

The lover may simply have sneezed naturally. But the next morning she tells her friend, as an example of her lover's cleverness,

“I was sulking—he deliberately sneezed. I forgot my quarrel and blessed him!” she says, stroking her cheek and pouring out sweet words. Here is that Kural:

> While we were in lovers' quarrel he sneezed, knowing  
> that I would say to him, “May you live long.”

Chapter 132 — Subtleties of Lovers' Quarrel; Kurals 1317, 1312
