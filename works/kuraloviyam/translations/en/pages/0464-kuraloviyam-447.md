## Visual material

Large upper illustration of the ruler Athanar reclining with a woman while reading a document and drinking.

A man named Koothan took charge as minister of Athanar's small kingdom. He possessed his own distinctive administrative ability.

The king trusted him completely and, without interfering in ministerial work, remained in the inner apartments enjoying himself.

Koothan had a great desire to make himself appear before the people as a mighty genius and as a man pure as the white thumbai flower.

To make the people believe this, he announced many schemes in the name of the government. But he had no continuing intention of carrying them out. Thus the schemes took shape in speech and withered in practice.

Minister Koothan suddenly issued a proclamation declaring that he would free the people from the evil habit of drinking liquor. The proclamation was issued as a severe order.

The king's seal of approval had to be placed upon that order. But the king lay drunk and senseless, using the lap of a woman as his bed. It was therefore very easy for Koothan to obtain the king's seal of consent.
