Only when a flag flies at the top of a fortress does the fortress gain distinction! And only when the flag flies atop the fortress does the flag itself gain greater honour!

Within the fortress of a man's heart, a woman-creeper must keep flying, shining like silk!

By urging domestic life upon humanity, and by giving us the Book of Love, Valluvar has explained this truth both clearly and more sweetly than clear honey. Ah! How many kinds of Kurals of love he has created! Every one of them offers delight in a manner suited to Tamil culture!

That lovers' union after lovers' quarrel has a special excellence of its own is Valluvar's thought—and a truth known to those with experience as well!

Look—he now lets us meet two such young doves!

Thinking that his beloved might be angry only if he wore a flower from a creeper, the young man comes wearing flowers blossoming on tree branches. Even then she looks at him and becomes angry: “You wore these so that you could display your beauty to some other woman.”

> Even when I wear a flower from a branch, she grows angry:  
> “You wore it to show yourself to another woman.”

When he leaps close and embraces his tender beloved, he cannot properly see her face. For that is the moment when their two faces are pressed together! All four eyes are without sight then; so he has not seen her face “at that time.” But now she has drawn away and stands talking. So he looks closely at her face. Even in anger, her face pours forth a new beauty. Upon the red lips trembling with fury, a flash of smile appears without her knowing it! He savours that radiance; the young bull discovers a new delight. Through his eyes he drinks intoxicating liquor—unable to tear away the gaze he has fixed upon her. She, meanwhile, journeys toward the summit of lovers' quarrel.

“Why are you staring at me like this? With whose beauty are you comparing me?” she asks angrily. What can the poor man do? If he looks, it is a fault; if he does not look, an accusation will be read out: “Do you hate me that much?” This Kural is about a lover caught in just such a state.

> Even when I look at her, absorbed in thought, she grows angry:  
> “Thinking of whom are you looking at me like this?”
