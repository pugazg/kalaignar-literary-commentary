As a paddy crop turns yellow when a wasting disease strikes, his child too had grown thin and pale from anaemia. When he showed the child to a physician, the doctor said there was no alarming emergency and that, if he came to the physician's house the next day, he would give him medicine. That was why the warrior's feet hurried toward the physician's home.

When he arrived, the physician was asleep on the raised veranda, having spread out a towel beneath himself. Nearby, a few dried herbs lay scattered and neglected. The stone used for grinding medicine too lay idle.

A long time passed, yet the physician's deep sleep did not leave him. Losing patience, the warrior tapped him and woke him. Opening his eyes, the physician showed not the slightest concern or urgency and said:

“I still have to find one more herb. Come tomorrow; I will give you the medicine.”

It was not just one day, nor two days. For many days, disappointment alone was the gift the warrior received.

Once, as he returned empty-handed from the physician's house, an elderly man came toward him.

“What is it, son? Why do you look so troubled?” he asked.

The warrior spoke wearily about the physician who promised to help but remained buried in sleep.

Stroking the beard that hung like a honeycomb, the elder let a smile spread across his face.

“Of all people, was it to this man that you brought your sick child? Is he not like damp firewood drenched with dew, unable to catch fire at once? He will say, ‘I will give medicine and help,’ but he will never enter into any effort. He will simply sleep. So his house sleeps; the stone that grinds the medicine sleeps. Trust him, and the lives of his patients too will sleep—in the cremation ground. Do you remember that coward who once waved a sword about and made a spectacle of himself? One who says he will help without making any effort is just like that coward who does not know how to wield a sword. Leave this physician and find another who does not sleep in the lap of idleness! Only then will your child survive. The sword in a coward's hand and the help promised by a man without effort are one and the same!”

The elder's thought takes beautiful form as a Kural:

> The helpfulness of one who has no persevering effort will fail,  
> like swordsmanship in the hand of a coward.

Chapter 62 — Diligent Effort; Kural 614
