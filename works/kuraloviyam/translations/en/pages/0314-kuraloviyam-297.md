—the elder said with concern, “Alas, poor fellow! This is the youthful season for entering family life, the springtime for enjoying its pleasures, yet poverty has prevented you from receiving that opportunity!”

Then someone was heard laughing brightly. The elder and the young man turned. They looked all around. No one could be seen, yet the laughter did not stop. It was certainly a woman's laughter. But who was she? Both were bewildered. Gathering courage, the elder called out loudly, “Who is it? Who is laughing?” An answer came.

“It is I, the maiden Earth, laughing. I am laughing to myself. Do you know why? Elder, shall I tell you? Even at this advanced age you rise through labour. Your drops of sweat sway in the wind as grains of paddy. Under the touch of your hands the bunds of your field have risen. Through tireless labour you see a rich yield. Your family lives without want. Look at this young man. He has a body like iron. Yet idleness has enslaved him. That is why his field before you lies dry, while beside it the channel runs in full flood. He is not prepared to direct that water into his field, rejoice at seeing grain there, or use the wealth it yields to set his life in order. Do you understand, old man? I, the maiden Earth, stand ready to give every kind of abundance. He has no wish to make use of it and wanders as an idler. Then he says, ‘I have no money in hand, and so my marriage has not taken place.’ It is while thinking of this strange young man that I laugh to myself.”

After that explanation the voice of the maiden Earth was no longer heard. The laughter too ceased. The elder stared at Karmegam. Karmegam shook his shoulders and rose. He picked up a spade lying in a corner.

“Elder! After this very earth has mocked me to my face, I will no longer curl up like a worm of idleness. Look—I have set out to work,” he said, cutting into the channel and turning the water toward his field.

Smiling, the elder patted him and said, “Younger brother, I will give you my own daughter as your life-partner.”

Here is the Kuraloviyam that forms the basis of this imaginative picture:

> When the good lady called Earth sees one sitting idle and saying, “I have nothing,”  
> she laughs.
