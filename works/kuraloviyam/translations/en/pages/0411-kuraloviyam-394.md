It was a house standing by itself. She was alone inside. He opened the door and entered. The accountant—yes, that handsome young man—came behind her with the Kavadi Sindhu still upon his tongue:

> “Is your withering only deceit?  
> Is your heart cruel black stone?  
> Is the medicine for this love-sickness so little?  
> In Manmatha's ancient epic of desire,  
> why is the path of sweet speech poison to you?  
> Is there such a famine even for one word?”

He strung together a garland of delicious words and shot arrows of love in sweet Tamil.

She was extremely careful not to let him win over the womanly strength by which she governed her own heart—not to lose her restraint.

“My sweetness! My nectar-fruit! Why still this hesitation? That day beneath the temple car, your honeyed lips trembled. I have come now with folded hands only to ask what command they gave me before they left. One day in the festival crowd your eyes were stealing me. I turned back—and those same eyes became flames. I have run here now with folded hands only to ask what crime I committed. I am always ready to obey as a command the dance shown by your moving waist. Why then this fire in your eyes? Why does the lotus face hesitate to turn toward me? Must I fall completely to my knees and perform penance before I may embrace you? No! I have already made my heart into a flower for the worship of love and placed it at your flower-feet, which ache even when cotton touches them. Do you not see even that? If you still have no mercy on me, bend the bow of your brows and destroy me beyond recognition!”

He kept speaking. Before that speech full of humility and affection, the fortress called her womanly restraint collapsed.

She clung to him. He embraced her. The street door closed. In that little house there was utter silence.

Only her heart spoke to her after he took leave saying, “I will come tomorrow.”

“The humble words of my lover, that master thief of many deceits, are they not the army that breaks the fortress of my womanhood?”

Thus her heart marvelled, and her breast swelled with emotion.

> Are not the humble words of the many-wiled thief  
> the army that breaks our womanly restraint?
