She flew into a rage. She put an iron rod into the stove, heated it red-hot and pressed it against Alagan's forearm. Poor child! He writhed and trembled in pain.

“We have to waste our lives raising whatever some prostitute has given birth to and thrown away!” his aunt said. Those degrading words made Alagan suffer more than the burn from the iron rod.

The whole village spoke of his mother as pure gold. “My aunt has flung the word ‘prostitute’ at her!” he grieved. He ran outside.

The boy who ran away never returned. No one knew where he had gone or what had happened to him. His uncle shed tears again and again thinking of him. The years rolled by.

The aunt's three children married, established households of their own and moved away to different towns. Alagan's uncle fell ill and became bedridden. Not one of those children came even to see him.

Alagan's aunt sat beside her husband, shedding tears as she thought of the conduct of her own children. Then they heard someone call, “Aunt!” They turned.

Alagan stood before them in a soldier's uniform. He said that he had rushed there after hearing that his uncle's health had failed.

At the sight of Alagan, half his uncle's illness seemed to disappear. The aunt asked whether he had been serving in the army all these years, treated him affectionately, seated him beside his uncle and brought milk and fruit for him. As Alagan received them, the scar from the burn she had inflicted with the iron rod was still visible on his forearm.

“Forgive me, Alaga! In some fit of anger I did that to you that day. When did the wound caused by this sinner finally heal?” the aunt asked.

“The wound you burned into me with fire healed quickly, Aunt. But you insulted my mother, a goddess of chastity, by calling her a prostitute and burned me with your tongue. That wound in my heart has still not healed.”

Alagan answered. With tears in his eyes, his uncle embraced him and pleaded, “Forgive her, son. Forgive your aunt.” The aunt too wept, took Alagan's hand and pressed it against her face.

> A wound burned by fire will heal within;  
> the scar burned by the tongue will never heal.
