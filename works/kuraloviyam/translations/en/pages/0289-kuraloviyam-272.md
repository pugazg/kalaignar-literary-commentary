“That is why I came in search of you. Tell me what is fitting so that my mind may mature.”

Valluvar looked at him with a gentle smile and said:

“Do not keep clinging to attachments! If you cling to them, sufferings too will cling to you without letting go.”

Having said this, he recited a Kural rich with that thought:

> Afflictions cling without letting go  
> to those who do not let go of attachment.

On hearing the Kural, the visitor bowed to the great Valluvar.

“Great one! This Kural alone is enough to guard my path of renunciation. Grant me leave!” he said. Vowing, “I shall cast away all attachments completely,” the new ascetic started to leave. Then Valluvar's voice fell upon his ears. What did it say?

“Cling!”

That was the word. Startled, he turned back. “He only just told me to let go of attachment, and already he says ‘cling.’ What contradiction is this?” he wondered.

“To cast away attachments, one must cling to one attachment. That is the discipline of renunciation.”

Valluvar explained. The visitor did not understand. If no attachment at all was permitted, where had this one attachment come from? He himself became a question mark.

“One must cling only to one who is unattached to everything. The attachment you form to such a person will help you cast away your own attachments.”

The great Valluvar then recited another Kural:

> Cling to the attachment of the one who is free of attachment;  
> cling to that attachment in order to let attachment go.

Illuminated by the explanation given both for letting go of attachment and for taking hold of attachment, the man who had come to see Valluvar went on to excel in the ascetic life.
