He handed them the purses of gold while speaking this way. The young men accepted the prize and left, cursing the doctor inwardly.

At a wedding there was a performance in the house afterward. The doctor was asked to congratulate the woman who had danced. She had been properly trained in dance. The doctor, who knew nothing at all of its grammar, said that her performance had faults. Before he could finish his remarks, the woman angrily left the stage.

The students of Mankadu arranged a function to thank the great teacher who had educated and shaped them. The doctor was invited to preside.

Instead of praising the teacher, he advised him about all the things he still ought to learn. Disgusted, the students mocked the doctor and walked out.

When the government learned that crimes such as murder and theft were increasing in the village, soldiers were sent there for protection.

“Can theft and murder occur in Mankadu beyond the power of the doctor? Is this guard force stronger than I am?”

the doctor sneered.

The guards moved from Mankadu to the next village, wishing to teach the doctor a lesson.

One night robbers entered the doctor's house. They swung curved blades and frightened him. His family scattered and fled. The doctor too ran into the midnight darkness without knowing where he was going.

An oil lamp was burning at the entrance of a house. He entered and cried:

“Alas! Save me!”

The woman of that house was the dancer whom he had insulted. She refused him refuge and bolted the door.

The doctor ran from there. He ran and ran and kept running. Dawn came.

A group of students approached. He stopped before them and wailed:

“Robbers are chasing me!”

The students, who already despised him, surrounded him and began mockingly dancing a kummi around him. Unable to bear it, the doctor ran again.
