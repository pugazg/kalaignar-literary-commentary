## Visual material

Large upper illustration of a young couple reclining together amid harvested straw.

Vanchikkodi lay withered. Shadows along the field's threshing ground rolled one after another like moving pictures. Unable to bear the weight of the ripened grain-heavy ears, the golden crops stood with heads bowed like a bride. They were harvested, tied into bundles, carried to the threshing ground and piled there.

One of the young doves engaged in that work was Kaali. That was merely her name! She was a casket of sculpted beauty, a treasure-house of pleasure to Kadamban, who found delight in touching and embracing her.

A row of teeth shone between her lips as though the moon had been cut into pieces and strung together.

Her beautiful eyes had the power to swallow up courage itself, as though saying, “No heroism has any force before me!”

Though her complexion was somewhat dark, she was a queen who, like sugarcane, only grew sweeter to Kadamban. Even while the harvest went on, their eyes became iron and magnet.

As though the heat given by the sun were not enough, the bodies of both burned within at the thought of the feast they would taste that night.
