The news reached Senguttuvan's ears.

“I will not leave these arrogant men who insulted the Tamil kings until I make them bow at my feet!”

he vowed, and had the war drums sounded.

His army set out toward the north.

With Minister Azhumbil Vel beside him and Villavan Kothai leading the army, Senguttuvan took leave of his companion Venmaal, raised his warrior's sword and departed.

Along the way many kings welcomed him and bowed before him. Some even sent troops to support him.

In the north, at a place called Kuyilaluvam, Senguttuvan clashed with Kanakan and Vijayan.

Final victory belonged to Cheran Senguttuvan.

Kanakan and Vijayan disappeared.

At last they were found disguised as ascetics and brought before Senguttuvan.

“Take a stone from the Himalayas to fashion a statue for Kannagi, queen of chastity. Make these oxen carry it!”

Senguttuvan ordered.

And so the stone was placed upon the heads of Kanakan and Vijayan.

Those creatures with no restraint on their tongues trudged on with aching legs under the stone.

At that moment Minister Azhumbil Vel said:

“Because good and evil both arise from the words a person speaks, one must guard against fault in speech.”

That is what this Kural explains:

> Since gain and loss arise from speech,  
> guard against failure in one's words.

Chapter 65 — Power of Speech; Kural 642
