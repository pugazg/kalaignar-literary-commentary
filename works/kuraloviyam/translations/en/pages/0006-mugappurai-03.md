After that, Kuraloviyam continued in the *Kungumam* weekly published by Murasoli Maran.

As of now, three hundred Kuraloviyam pieces have appeared. The number of Kurals used is 354.

Of these, 76 Kurals are from the Book of Aram, 137 from the Book of Porul, and 141 from the Book of Inbam.

Those who enter the pages of this book will understand that, in composing word-pictures for these 354 Kurals, the abundance of classical Tamil has stood by me as my support.

I introduced a new style as early as 1945, when I was at the *Kudiyarasu* office in Erode under the care of Thanthai Periyar: a style that mingled poetic movement with prose without turning it into rough, forbiddingly difficult Tamil, but instead added beauty and presented it in simple language.

It was then that I began writing free verse under the title “Not Poetry.”

> “Your son has died on the battlefield,” he said.  
> Her heart broken, the old mother straightened herself once!  
> In a game of dice, pieces are captured;  
> the battlefield too is just that!  
> “Was the wound on his chest? Or on his back?  
> Tell me,” she said — “On his back,” he said!  
> The old woman trembled; her heart burst;  
> she took up the sword;  
> toward the direction where the war-drum sounded  
> she drove herself with speed!  
> “Did I give milk to a coward?  
> Is the fool who lies fallen face-down  
> to be called a warrior?  
> Once, on an earlier day,  
> to answer the spear that came flying,  
> my deathless beloved showed his chest  
> and fell.  
> Was this one born to him?  
> Hey! Where is honour?”

These are only a few lines from a long free-verse poem I wrote under the title “The Purananuru Mother”!

In 1953, when I was serving six months of rigorous imprisonment in Tiruchirappalli prison because of the Kallakudi protest, many word-pictures took shape in the style of “free verse.” They have appeared in book form as *Vairamanigal*.
