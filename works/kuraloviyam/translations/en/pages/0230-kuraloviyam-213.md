“There is no ghost and no demon! Some shock has disturbed her mind. Let her stay tomorrow in the government hospital under my supervision. She needs rest, peace and sleep. She must also be given medicines,” she would explain, and cure the woman's illness of delirium.

Thus, from morning until night, patients of every kind! Whoever came, she never tired of them. Because she worked with courage and confidence that the illness could be cured, her skill became famous throughout the town. Yet Maragatham, who knew methods and medicines capable of curing everyone else's illnesses, did not know a medicine to cure the illness that had seized her. That illness could not be cured by tablets discovered by the greatest medical experts!

Beautiful Maragatham had a handsome lover. He too was skilled in medicine. To deepen his medical knowledge he had gone abroad, and six weeks had already passed. Only after another three months would he return to his homeland.

Maragatham, who cured the bodily ailments of hundreds from morning onward, had no medicine at night for the illness of the mind that tormented her.

Was not the medicine thousands of miles away? Separated from him, turning alone upon her bed, Maragatham clasped the long pillow beside her and spoke to herself.

“Heart! Will you not at least think and tell me of some medicine that can cure this painful illness born of love and tormenting me?”

With longing she asks her own heart to discover a medicine for her.

She knows that for the love-sickness that has afflicted one who gives medicine for every other disease, there is no medicine except the return of her lover. Yet she pleads with her heart. This Kural gives that plea its picture:

> Heart, will you not think of something and tell me  
> a medicine that can cure this painful illness, whatever it may be?
