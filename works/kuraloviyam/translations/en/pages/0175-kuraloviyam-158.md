The leader who had ridden that fiercely fighting elephant was no longer upon it. Perhaps he had fallen in battle. That was why the elephant charged like an enraged tiger. Chariot forces surrounded it on all four sides in order to encircle and destroy it. Eight chariots stood around it. Commanders with hearts of steel took up their bows, set arrows to the string and shot them at the elephant. One, two, a hundred—many hundreds of arrows pierced the body of that fighting elephant. Valluvar says that the elephant's very body disappeared beneath the arrows that struck it.

Yes, if enough arrows clung to that enormous body to hide it, how many arrows must have flown into it! The elephant may have looked like a porcupine. Yet though wounded by hundreds of arrows, its resolve did not weaken. It did not cry, “Alas! So many arrows!” and flee from the place in shock. What, then, did it do? Even in that condition, it upheld the greatness of refusing to lose heart. There were so many arrows that no space remained on its body for even one more.

The elephant lifted both its forelegs. It raised its trunk majestically. It looked at the chariots surrounding it and at the commanders standing in them. In the time it takes to blink, it smashed one chariot before it to powder with a tremendous roar. Another chariot shattered. For a moment the commanders in the remaining chariots stood stunned. Everyone marvelled at the fierce way the elephant wheeled about, determined to end the lives of many enemies on that field before its own life ended. Valluvar too gives it a beautiful Kural and uses it to show the greatness of possessing resolve.

> Those of firm resolve do not shrink when ruin comes;  
> like an elephant pierced and buried beneath arrows, they stand in their pride.

‘Even when wounded by arrows until its body is hidden, the elephant maintains its greatness to the very end. In the same way, people of resolve will not lose heart even when destruction itself arrives’—that is the meaning.

Chapter 60 — Possession of Resolve; Kural 597
