## Visual material

Large upper illustration of a ruler speaking with a seated woman.

There was once a king with powerful armies.

Rather than think and act to give the people under his rule a prosperous life, he spent all his time and thought conquering new lands and extending the boundary of his domination.

Each time he invaded and conquered another country, thousands of his own people had to lose their lives before he returned victorious.

For many days they had to go hungry.

Because he had created enemies all around him, the entire treasury had to be spent on enlarging and strengthening the army.

Unable to obtain either prosperity or a peaceful life, the people suffered.

Hunger and disease began to dance.

Many regions of the country withered into fields of poverty.

The king, without even the slightest concern for this condition, wanted poets to sing of the greatness of his rule.

A public proclamation was made.

It declared that whoever composed a poem praising the excellence of the ruler's just sceptre would receive ten thousand gold coins as first prize.

There was a young man who hated that government.

He dressed a middle-aged friend who was wasting away in poverty as a poet, wrote a poem for him, and sent him away saying:

“Go recite this before the king and collect the ten thousand gold coins.”

The poor man, not understanding the prank, was tempted by the gold.

Taking the lines the youth had written, he entered the royal hall dressed as a poet.
