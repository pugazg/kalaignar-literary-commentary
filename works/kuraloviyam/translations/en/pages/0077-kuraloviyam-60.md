A lion-hearted young man who had been imprisoned to await beheading escaped from prison one day and fled the country. He went to the place where Valluvar lived.

“Great one! In our land, blazing fire is called water! Rule of the jungle is called government! Tell me a way to change this and save the people!” he cried. The young man's voice echoed through Valluvar's hall. By then the prison guards who had pursued him caught him and put him in chains. Valluvar stood quietly watching the scene. The chained youth was tied to the wheel of a chariot, and the chariot was driven swiftly back towards their country. Valluvar followed them.

He searched for the young man who had been dragged back into that land where cruelty danced. Preparations were under way in the middle of the street to impale him. Thousands upon thousands of people had been summoned to witness the sight—for only then, it was thought, would they shrink in fear of the government and lie subdued like tortoises. They had been brought there to be terrorised.

Before the youth was raised on the stake, ignoring the guards who tried to stop him, he fired a volley of questions at the people.

Those questions painted the sufferings taking place in the country: the anguish of women who had lost their marriage tali; the cries of children who had lost their mothers; the lament of mothers who had lost their sons. His parched lips sang this song of sorrow. The brave young man saw tears streaming from the eyes of the people gathered like a flood.

“Ah! Are not these tears themselves the weapon that can break tyranny? Why delay any longer? Is not the water pouring from your two eyes the red blood that has risen from your hearts? Go forth and show the strength of these tears!”

Thus he sang a song of struggle. The sea of people surged. It became like a flood breaking through a dam. The impaling post was shattered. The youth's handcuffs were broken. The ranks of the government's soldiers fled. Those drops of tears entered even the fortress. Before that, the ruling dynasty itself had fled somewhere to escape.

There! There! The royal family is running away! Valluvar sees them fleeing after losing country, city, prosperity and inexhaustible wealth. Had he not also seen the people near the impaling post rise to the young man's voice and beat the drum of revolt? He marvels at the power contained in their tears. He reflects on how those who lived in a palace with elephants, armies and a thousand privileges lost all their wealth in half a moment.

The young man's voice becomes a voice of joyous Tamil, a voice that arouses revolt, a speech that gives hope. It is this:

> The tears shed by those who suffer and cannot endure—  
> are themselves the army that wears away a ruler's wealth.

Chapter 56 — Tyrannical Rule; Kural 555
