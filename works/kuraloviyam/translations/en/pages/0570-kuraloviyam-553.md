The flower-like woman who once watched the hour and blessed it now watches that same hour and curses it. There were many days when she said, "Live long, O hour!" and blessed it.

When she rose from dawn's sleep, still delighted by the sweetness she had savoured through the night, she would bathe, dress well, place a dot upon her forehead, and tirelessly take up the tasks of that great university called a good household. After that she would yearn for evening to arrive.

For is not evening the bell that announces the coming of the elephant called night? Finishing his work in the merchants' street, was it not at that very evening hour that the man who filled her eyes returned home each day?

This is Tamil Nadu, which created the image of a woman who, as dusk approaches, finishes the kitchen work, bathes with delight, marks her forehead with kumkum, fills her hair with flowers, and stands beside the pillar on the veranda without blinking, waiting for her husband's return.

That golden statue of Tamil womanhood blesses the whole span of time that gives her the hour called evening. Even in that colourful Tamil painting's blessing, "Live long, O hour!" there are two opposing emotions.
