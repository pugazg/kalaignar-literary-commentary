"...faith! Likewise, in your ability and the way you command an army, I have not even the slightest doubt. May garlands of fame pile higher upon you! Live! Win! Go! Return victorious!" the king said, and sent him forth.

The commander marched with a great army and laid siege to the enemy king's fortress.

The enemy ruler was stunned by the unexpected siege.

Though he had been preparing for war, he had never imagined that his fort would suddenly be surrounded like this.

The king learned of his commander's skill in war and of how the enemy, shocked by the surprise siege, had been forced into helpless confinement.

His eyes shone.

"Victory! Victory!" he cried in delight.

He shouted that the arrogance of the enemy trapped inside the siege had been shattered.

A feast was held in the palace.

Then sudden news struck the king in the midst of his celebration.

The commander who had gone to besiege the enemy fort had made an agreement with the enemy and stopped the war.

The enemy ruler had agreed to give the commander several regions of his own country and make him a minor king over them.

Delighted by the new distinction of being called a "king," the commander had shaken hands with the enemy.

One soldier from the army that had marched under him ran back carrying the news.

For a while the king's face reddened and sank in grief.

Then he broke his silence and rose like a lion.

"No matter.

"The commander who took up a sword to defeat the enemy now lies holding the enemy's feet.

"He has received his reward for it too.

"The enemy will now join him and come against us.

"We shall face them.

"We shall meet them.

"But what pains me is this: our commander, who stood tall with fame like a mountain, has lowered himself through his own degrading act."

Wiping his troubled eyes, the king entered the battlefield.

Even those who stand mountain-high in greatness will be regarded as low before the people if they commit even an act as small as a kunrimani seed that causes their fall.

> Even those who are like a mountain will fall  
> if they do a deed of abasement as small as a kunrimani seed.
