A waterfall plunges down the mountain.

Are silver threads swaying in a swift wind?

No, no.

At intervals it looks as though hundreds of palm-fibre strands are being poured down at once.

Jackfruit trees stand all around.

Fruit grows from their roots.

The tastes hidden inside those fruits cannot be seen from outside. Yet the very sight of a jackfruit awakens the happy feeling that the flavours within must taste like honey.

Suppose that jackfruit suddenly split open and its sweetness showed itself.

Suppose a fruit from the pomegranate tree beside the falls dropped, burst, and scattered its ruby seeds.

Would not the sight of them immerse us in a joy whose source we cannot quite name—not only because of their beauty but because of the flavour we know lies within?

A golden maiden is bathing in that waterfall.

The hands of the falling water embrace and caress her whole body.

Her face is like a coloured lotus; her neck like a right-turning conch; the two forms rising from her chest like banana blossoms; the two ivory forms that seem to support a waist so slender one fears it may fade away—
