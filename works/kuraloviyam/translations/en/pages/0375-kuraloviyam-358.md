Having rejoiced in seeing all the places worth visiting from northern India to southern India, could the eyes of those foreign travellers fail to fall upon Chennai's long seashore?

There, between coconut groves on the shore, their eyes turned toward a tomb made of black stone.

Removing their footwear at a distance, they walked into the grove where silence had made its home.

The ever-burning lamp at the tomb welcomed them.

A tall pillar stood as though informing the holy person sleeping within the tomb of their arrival.

The tourists worshipped the tomb with their eyes.

“Here sleeps a heart that could bear anything.”

The foreigners wished to understand the meaning of that inscription. Their interpreter explained:

“He gave the Tamil people three words like sacred watchwords: Duty, Dignity, Discipline. He urged those who followed him that they must possess a heart able to bear anything. The owner of that heart—his name was Anna. He was born as Annadurai. Because he gave such imperishable watchwords, he passed from this world as Anna.”

Hearing this explanation, the foreign tourists touched the tomb and pressed their hands to their eyes.

Duty, Dignity, Discipline—they savoured the depth of those words, praised them and honoured them.

They were also shown the greatness of the Kural idea:

“The greatness of wise men whose words are full and fruitful  
is shown on earth by the enduring words they leave behind.”

> The greatness of men whose speech is full and complete  
> is revealed on earth by their enduring utterances.
