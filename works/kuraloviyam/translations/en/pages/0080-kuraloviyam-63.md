That is the condition! Everything must be done from the mat and the bed. Illnesses that had hidden themselves while strength remained seem to gather together and say, “Let him weaken; then we shall see,” and finally lay siege and plant their flag upon the fort.

Even after all this, that life has no courage to call out, “Death, come!”

Some lives, it seems, continue to suffer with an undying love for worldly existence: somehow they must survive through the skill of doctors, through the power of medicine and herbs; regain fresh strength and live again; all the pleasures of the world already drunk in are still not enough—they wish to drink more and revel in them.

Valluvar searches for a comparison for those lives that yearn and struggle in this way.

At last he finds one. Do you know where? At a gambling place!

Some gamblers stake what they possess and lose it. Defeat after defeat. Yet they hold on to a hope: if they continue gambling, they may recover what they have lost.

The more they lose their possessions, the more their attachment to gambling grows. They are one kind.

The more a body is weakened and distressed by disease, losing strength again and again, the more uncontrollable desire arises in the person who is left with life alone to cling to that very body. They are another kind.

How can one adequately praise the intelligence of the great man who made these two kinds of people into a comparison and composed a Kural?

> As gambling is loved the more one loses, so life  
> grows dearer the more suffering it undergoes.

Chapter 94 — Gambling; Kural 940

`உழத்தொறூஉம்` = whenever suffering is experienced  
`காதற்று` = possessed of love / attachment
