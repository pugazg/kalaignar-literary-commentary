His body looked as though carved from a rounded block of black stone.

A rough moustache. Shoulders like rocks. Eyes that scattered sparks and frightened those who saw them.

He travelled seated upon a horse whose majesty surpassed even his own.

His name was Anbukkadal—"Ocean of Love."

We are about to see how well that name suited him.

A bag hung from his shoulder, its belly filled with diamonds.

Another bag held food for his own stomach during the journey. He had tied that one near the horse's neck.

Along the road he came upon a cool stream and shade upon its bank.

He stopped the horse and dismounted.

Taking the diamond-filled bag with him, he untied the food bag and sat beneath a tree.

The horse rolled upon the green grass and relieved its weariness.

Anbukkadal satisfied his hunger.

He was putting the remaining food back into the bag and tying it up when a voice came:

"Sir! My stomach is burning. A morsel of food!

May you live like a great king."

Anbukkadal turned at the sound.

A poor man stood there—skin and bone, his face already carrying the remains of death's summons.

"Chi! You beggar donkey! Get away! Go!"

Anbukkadal's anger flared.
