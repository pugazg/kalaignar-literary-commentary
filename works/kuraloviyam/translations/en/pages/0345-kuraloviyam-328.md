Aththi could do nothing but weep. He could say no more. Valluvar understood. Embracing him consolingly, he asked, “The ship capsized at sea in a storm—is that what happened?”

Aththi nodded to show that it was so. Valluvar calmed him, fed him and put him to sleep. The whole of the next day he devoted himself to restoring Aththi's spirit. “There is a way to begin your trade again and rise in life! Do not remain bent under the weight of what has happened. Wipe away your sorrow and walk with dignity,” he advised. Aththi too rose with some measure of renewed courage. Valluvar walked with him for a short distance.

“Great one! I laboured every day to place each gold coin, one by one, in the chest in my house. Did you see how the gold that came to me one piece at a time left me now all at once, in a single day?”

Aththi said this to Thiruvalluvar in anguish. Valluvar stopped.

“Look there, Aththi! Do you know what that is? A theatre! When people come to enjoy a performance in that theatre, they enter the hall one by one, do they not?”

“Yes! Yes!”

“But when the performance ends and the people leave, they disperse as a crowd, do they not?”

“Yes! Yes!”

“That is how wealth comes and goes. Like the gathering of a crowd in a theatre, wealth accumulates little by little. And its leaving us is like the crowd dispersing when the performance is over.”

“Ah! What an image! What an image! Hearing such a comparison, I no longer even feel the grief of my ship sinking. It seems as though all the wealth I had gathered left me only so that you might give me such a wondrous comparison!”

Praising him in amazement, Aththi took Valluvar's flower-like hands and pressed them to his eyes.

> Great wealth is like the crowd gathered for a performance;  
> its departure is like that crowd dispersing when the play is done.
