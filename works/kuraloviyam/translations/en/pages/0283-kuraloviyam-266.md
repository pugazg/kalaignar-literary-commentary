“Ask for any boon; I will grant it,” they would not even open their eyes to look at him!

They united and rejoiced—and because they rejoiced, they united again. The jasmine and mullai flowers spread across the bed had no power to cry aloud. Poor things: crushed and crumpled, they ended their lives. Tasting the flavour of life without satiety, the lovers blessed the night. Resting her breasts upon her husband's broad chest, she ran her hand over his face as though to make sure it was still there. He used his lips to pluck the fruits of hers and struggled as though unable to release them. The strings of veenas were plucked. The beating of hearts sounded as rhythm. Their mingled breath became music.

A very brief rest! Even during that interval his hands remained around her waist.

She looked at him and asked in a flute-like voice:

“Atthaan! What is this delight like? Can you give any comparison for it?”

Holding her tightly, he answered:

“My dear! Valluvar has pointed to another delight as equal to this.”

“What a wonder! Is there a delight equal even to this? Has Valluvar spoken of such a thing?”

“Yes, my gold! Listen. A man builds a house through his own labour, works and gathers wealth there, shares that gathered wealth with others, and then eats his own share. Valluvar compares the delight that arises then with this delight.”

“Atthaan! Working with self-confidence, producing benefit, and sharing the benefit one has produced with others—how beautifully Valluvar has pictured what a great joy that is.”

“Yes, my life! It is hard to find anywhere else in the world a poet who has compared such a fresh idea with the joy of union!”

Praising the Kural in this way, the two joined again in delight. This is that Kural:

> The embrace of this wondrous maiden is like living by one's own means,  
> sharing what is one's own with others and then partaking oneself.

Chapter 111 — The Joy of Union; Kural 1107
