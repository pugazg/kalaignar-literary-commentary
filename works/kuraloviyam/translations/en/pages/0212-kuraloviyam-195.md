The enemy king banished Thennavan alone from the country and issued an order that “no one must follow him.” Those who rejoiced most on hearing that command were Thennavan's former commander and his spies. Another order flew out: seize Thennavan's only son and have his head crushed beneath an elephant's foot! Looking at those who had betrayed him, Thennavan said, “Friends! I am leaving. May you live well!” and departed the country alone.

Even after Thennavan gave up his land and lived in the forest, the enemy king would not allow him to live there in peace. Those who had already betrayed him continued to poison the enemy king's mind and themselves took on the work of causing Thennavan further trouble.

They drove fierce wild animals into the region where Thennavan lived. The king overcame them all through his strength of mind.

The traitors grieved that their efforts had failed. Days and months rolled by. The enemy king began to think. He asked himself: if those who betrayed their own beloved king through treachery could do that, how long would it take them to defeat him too by intrigue? Realizing that the enemy king had begun to suspect them, the traitors rapidly wove their web of conspiracy. Through their cunning they captured that very fortress. The foolish enemy king, trapped by them, fled the fort. Forced to run alone, he met Thennavan in the forest.

“Those who betrayed you have betrayed me too,” he said.

At that moment a warrior rushed forward with sword raised. Who was he? One of Thennavan's soldiers, a man who held Thennavan dearer than life itself. He had fled his country precisely to cut down the enemy king. But Thennavan stopped him and saved the enemy king's life.

“Thennava! I inflicted suffering upon suffering on you. As though that were not enough, I also allowed the traitors to torment you. Yet in return you have saved my life. Why?” the enemy king asked in astonishment.

Thennavan replied:

“Valluvar has said that the highest of all wisdom is not to return evil deeds even to those who cause one suffering.”

Then he recited that Kural to the enemy king. Here it is:

> Of all wisdom, this is called the highest:  
> do no evil even to those who inflict evil upon you.

Chapter 21 — Fear of Evil Deeds; Kural 203
