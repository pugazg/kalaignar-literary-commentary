“Commanders! Throw this arrogant man into prison! Warriors! Let the cavalry ride out. Let the mob gathered outside the fort be crushed beneath its hooves, smashed and destroyed! Hmm! Carry out my order at once.”

So the king roared.

The commanders seized the man who had accused him and imprisoned him. Warriors mounted their horses. Outside the fort, blood flowed like a flood. Thousands of people died.

The king looked at those in the assembly and asked:

“That scoundrel cast a stain upon me, who am without blemish! Do you believe him?”

“O ruler of the land! We do not believe him,” said those in the hall.

The assembly dispersed. The king entered the inner apartments. An iron chest filled with bars of gold seemed to face him and beckon. He went near it. This was the wealth he had amassed by starving the people of his own land. He stood looking at it.

A heavy blow struck his heart. He cried, “Alas!” Before him stood a figure exactly like the king himself. It laughed aloud. The laughter was terrifying. The figure spoke:

“Do you not know me? I am your conscience! In outward appearance you may call yourself just, honest and stainless and deceive the world. But can you hide your stained deeds from me?”

The question fell upon the king's head like a hammer blow. Unable to bear it, he leaned against the iron chest and collapsed unconscious. His conscience laughed loudly once more and vanished from the place.

> To be without stain in the mind—that is all virtue;  
> all else is merely clamorous show.

Chapter 4 — The Insistence on Virtue; Kural 34

ஆகுல நீர = clamorous nature
