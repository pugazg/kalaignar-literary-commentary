## Visual material

Large upper illustration pairing a uniformed man with a field worker carrying a bundle.

Among the duties of a ruler, Valluvar says, is the destruction through the death penalty of cruel men driven by murderous frenzy. Yet he stresses that even such punishment must be an act comparable to removing weeds in order to protect the crop.

In a paddy field, the number of crop plants is great and the number of weeds is small. Therefore, Valluvar says, in order to protect the citizens who flourish like the crop in a field, it becomes necessary to destroy those cruel men who do not shrink from murder—few in number though they may be—just as weeds that can ruin the crop must be removed.

This political Kural appears in the Chapter called “Just Rule”:

> A king's punishing men cruel in murder  
> is like removing weeds from the tender green crop.

Valluvar also did not fail to state what becomes of a government that, saying it is destroying weeds, destroys the crop itself.

The excellence of the comparison he gives for such governments is remarkable!

He says that a government that repeatedly commits unlawful acts and makes the tormenting of its citizens its occupation is crueller than one whose occupation itself is murder.
