“Then listen!

“To give humankind a work of ethical guidance, Valluvar travelled through many towns and saw many lands. He observed the governments functioning there. He learned the differences that arose among people.

“To teach the principles that should be honoured by householders, renunciates, rulers, lovers and warriors, he first studied the whole world as a book. Then he turned that world itself into Kurals and gave it to us.

“Among the people he met in that process was one man who became the target of his unrestrained anger.”

“My friend, you are still speaking in riddles. Who was he? Why did Valluvar become angry with him? What harsh words did he pour upon him?”

I fired question after question.

My friend calmly explained.

“Among the many people Valluvar met in the world, he met that man too.

“The man presented himself as one of immense intellectual ability. Though he knew nothing, he boasted as though he knew everything. His lips were always twitching to say something, anything. He had none of the maturity to think deeply and explain calmly.

“Yet he considered himself an intellectual and came to debate Valluvar himself. He expected the world to praise him as a man capable of opposing even Valluvar.

“Valluvar understood him. He recognized that he was empty of knowledge. Still, the ignorant man kept babbling in an attempt to introduce himself as a great scholar, and this angered Valluvar deeply.

“Do you know what Valluvar said to him?”

“Good heavens! No. Tell me!”

“To that man Valluvar said: ‘When ignorant people like you strut about as though you know everything, it is like a woman naturally lacking both breasts trying to present herself as embodying womanhood.’”

“Really? Did he become that angry?”

“Yes. Listen—here is the Kural.”

> The unlearned man's desire to speak  
> is like a breastless woman's desire for womanhood.

Chapter 41 — Lack of Learning; Kural 402
