## Visual material

Large upper illustration of wild and trained elephants in a forest.

Those who go to capture an elephant roaming freely through the forest without restraint take along a trained elephant already in their possession.

A pit is dug deep enough to bring down the wild elephant.

Its opening is hidden with leaves, tender branches and thin sticks so that the pit cannot be seen.

At one end the trained elephant is stationed.

The wild elephant, seeing another elephant at the far side of the pit, assumes it is one that has been living in the forest like itself and approaches.

But the pit lies on its path.

The moment its feet fall upon the green covering over the pit, the elephant plunges inside.

Afterward it is bound with huge ropes and iron chains, lifted out, and taken from the forest into the settled land.

Could Valluvar have failed to know this method—using one trained elephant to capture and train another untrained elephant?

Having known it, would he not have thought of placing it somewhere in his Kural as a comparison—
