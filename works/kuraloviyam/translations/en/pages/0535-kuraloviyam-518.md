## Visual material

Large upper illustration of a stern man holding a knife with a second figure or background scene.

The twentieth century is now in progress.

For many years the world has debated two opposing views:

Should those who commit murder be subjected to the death penalty?

Or should a lesser punishment be given so that the murderer may be reformed?

Even in this age some countries carry out the death penalty with extreme cruelty.

Criminals are shot in public places where thousands gather.

In some countries punishment is carried out by beheading.

There are also countries that impose the death penalty even for some grave crimes that are not murder.

Just as weeds are removed to protect a crop, Valluvar says in the Book of Wealth—under statecraft—that it is the ruler's duty to remove cruel wrongdoers through capital punishment in order to protect society.

But Valluvar, who presents capital punishment as one of the state's duties in the Book of Wealth, also strongly insists in the Book of Virtue, under renunciation, that no one should engage in killing.

How does he frighten people away from killing?

That is worth looking at closely.
