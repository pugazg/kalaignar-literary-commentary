“Does not the *Purananuru* speak of a mother who bore a heroic son and, when asked where he had gone, pointed to her belly and said, ‘I do not know where my son has gone, but here is the cave in which that war-tiger lived’? You are a mother descended from that line. Explain, mother. Why has your warrior-son not risen in fury and set out for fierce battle?”

Parithi asked Arima's mother these questions again and again. At first she gave no answer. At last, yielding to his insistence, she smiled and replied:

“Parithi! You are amazed that shameless men speak words of arrogance and yet my son remains restrained. More than that, after enemies insult even the courage and heroic spirit in his heart that cry ‘War! War!’, you are eager to know why he remains a sword still in its sheath, an arrow not yet placed upon the string.

Do you not know that, though my son has seen many battlefields, he has faultlessly studied the Kurals in Valluvar's chapter ‘Knowing the Proper Time’?”

“Mother! Is Arima not the warrior who once threw the spear in his hand at an elephant, then, when he searched for another spear to attack the enemy, rejoiced on seeing one embedded in his own body, pulled it out and fought with it? Is that man now waiting because he has read the chapter on knowing the proper time?”

“Yes! Even if he forgets, I am the mother who bore him and can remind him. The restraint of an energetic warrior is like a fighting ram drawing its legs backward before attacking the enemy. The farther it draws back, the more fiercely it springs forward. My son is like that ram. Do not mistake his seeming restraint for cowardice.”

Hearing the mother's explanation, Parithi swelled with joy.

“My doubt is gone, mother!”

He took leave and walked away with a proud stride.

> The restraint of one filled with energy is like the fighting ram  
> drawing back in order to charge and strike.
