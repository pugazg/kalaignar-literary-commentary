Both hearts yearned for the freedom of India. Both hearts laboured for victory in that freedom struggle.

Prabhavati Ammaiyar guarded herself in steadfast chastity, stood in the field to redeem the motherland, remained a firm support to her husband and protected him as well, never swerved in the least from the promise she had made, earned worthy honour, and brought distinction to Jayaprakash.

With the companionship of such a resolute woman, Jayaprakash was able to bear so many hardships and cruelties with a smile.

They restrained natural desire—no, no. Rather, so deeply were that hero and heroine absorbed in love of country that such desire itself could not approach them. Is it not certain that whenever the younger generation thinks of them, they will be transformed into flames of sacrifice?

"Who is a woman? How should she be?"

Was not Prabhavati Devi a woman who stood as an example of the definition Valluvar laid down?

> She guards herself, cares for the one who has taken her as his own,  
> preserves worthy honour in word, and never falters—that is a woman.

Chapter 6 — The Worth of a Life-Partner; Kural 56
