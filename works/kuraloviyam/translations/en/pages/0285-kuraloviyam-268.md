—and, taking the skin of each fruit and its inner taste or nature as examples, he distinguished the categories. After the festival, a feast was given to the poets. Their conversation there went as follows:

“How is the prosperity of your country?”

“Rain falls in season. Poverty itself is poor in our land! The country stands high in language and art. Agriculture and trade flourish. What is the condition of your country?”

“Exactly the opposite of what you have described. The people of our land wander like walking corpses. Only today, after many long days, have I seen your country and found a little solace in the joy it gives me.”

“What is your king like?”

“Our king? No one can shake him. He is immensely powerful. No one has the courage to stand in his way.”

“Is that so! Our king listens even to the opinion of an ordinary citizen. Your king seems entirely different!”

“Yes. No one in our country comes forward to rebuke him or offer advice. A right that even the smallest person possesses in your country is unavailable even to the greatest person in ours.”

“Alas! I grieve to hear that such a government exists in a neighbouring country. But one thing, poet: however strong your king may be, no one need come forward to destroy him. He will seek out his own destruction.”

“How can you say that with such certainty?”

“Have you forgotten what Valluvar said? He wrote with unmistakable force that a king who has no great men to rebuke and correct him will perish by himself even when there is no one to destroy him!”

The poets' conversation ended. In both their hearts that Kural shone as an indelible picture.

> The unguarded king who has no one to rebuke him  
> will perish even though there is no one to destroy him.
