We delight in watching colourful fish swim in a tank.

We rejoice in seeing tiny rabbits run round and round in the garden like balls of cotton rolling in the wind.

We stroke the wings of pigeons, scatter handfuls of grain for them, and lose our hearts to the sight of them pecking and eating.

If someone at home asks, "Shall we slaughter and cook one of the fish we raise, or one of the pigeons, rabbits, or chickens, and serve it today?" we answer with affection overflowing:

"No, no!"

Because they have grown under our own hands, killing and eating them has become something our hearts refuse to accept.

But when we think deeply, we know that not only creatures raised by us but also creatures that were never raised by us are killed—and killed for food.

Then a question inevitably arises:

Should we not show the same love and compassion that we show to the lives we ourselves have raised to the unraised creatures that fly and wander freely...
