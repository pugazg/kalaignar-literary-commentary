—chief. Bhutto, whose heart burned with a fierce desire to devote his life to the country and labour for his people, failed to judge correctly when choosing the commander he trusted.

What was the result? Using the unrest that followed the election in that country as his reason, General Zia arrested Bhutto, locked him in prison and seized power. Did it end there? Bhutto was implicated in a murder case and, without allowing a proper inquiry, a judgment sentencing him to death was issued.

Appeals poured in to Zia on behalf of most governments around the world, asking that Bhutto not be hanged. Zia's heart did not soften. Bhutto did not beg Zia for mercy to save his life. He declared that he would die as a brave man.

The cruelty inflicted on Bhutto in prison, and the indignities committed whenever he was brought to court, were unbearable even to hard-hearted people. Yet Zia presented all those cruelties as gifts to Bhutto, the friend who had lifted him into a position of great responsibility.

One morning the news came that made people throughout the world shed tears: Bhutto had been put to death in the prison. Zia had given Bhutto the final gift he had prepared for the man who had trusted him.

Bhutto's heart must surely have wept: “Because I failed to examine a man many times before choosing him as a friend, I am now going to die through that deceiver called my friend.” Without displaying it outwardly, that brave man accepted death with courage.

“Friendship with one accepted without repeated examination will in the end create the very sorrow that brings about one's death.”

Is not Bhutto's friendship with Zia, entered without such examination, an example of how true the Kural written by Valluvar two thousand years ago has proved?

> The friendship of one chosen without repeated examination  
> will in the end bring the grief that causes one's own death.

Chapter 80 — Examining Friendship; Kural 792
