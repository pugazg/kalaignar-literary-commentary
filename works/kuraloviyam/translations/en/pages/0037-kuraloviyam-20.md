## Visual material

Colour illustration of a pensive woman among flowers with a flute-playing man behind her against a warm sunset backdrop.

In the evening—at the edge of the grove—eyes that have ceased their labour learn love. In the same way, women whose beloved is not near writhe in anguish like sugarcane crushed in a mill. They look upon the beauty of the evening sky—and grow weary because the handsome one who would compare it with her lips has still not come. The breeze stirs their garments. They sigh that the lord of the world of delight, who would bless the breeze and teasingly mock them, is still nowhere to be seen. They watch the honeybee fly in even before the flower on the blossoming branch has opened its petals. “See the flower the bee embraces, my sugar-candy sweet! Yet I still do not see my lord, who comes and gives me overflowing delight,” they yearn. Their hearts swell. The evening hour, in their hearts, becomes an army—
