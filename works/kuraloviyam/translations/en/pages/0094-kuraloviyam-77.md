—sat down. She imagined the act of beating a public drum. The crier announces, through the sounding of the drum, news meant for everyone in the town to know. Then she thought of her own eyes.

“My eyes are just like this drum. Through the beating of a drum, at least public news is announced to the townspeople. But my eyes announce even the sorrowful secret that ought to be concealed—my suffering as I think of my lover. There is no difference between my eyes and the beating of a public drum.”

> If those with eyes like beating drums are like me,  
> hidden matters are not hard for the townspeople to learn.

“For one such as me, whose eyes are like a sounded drum and reveal sorrow, it is no difficult thing for the people of the town to discover what should have remained secret!”

While that screw-pine-hued maiden was speaking thus to herself, her friend opened the garden gate and entered. The green-parrot maiden welcomed her friend.

“My friend, why do your eyes look so tired? Perhaps your beloved did not come last night? Did your two eyes therefore stay open, watching for his arrival?”

Thus asked the green-parrot maiden. Her friend replied:

“You silly girl, Paingili! Do you not know even this? If my lover does not come, I lie awake without closing my eyes, thinking, ‘He has not come, he has not come.’ Even if he does come, I do not close my eyes. If he comes, there is no sleep. If he does not come, there is no sleep. How could my eyes not grow weary?”

Hearing this answer, Paingili grew shy; she was younger than her friend, after all.

> When he comes not, they sleep not; when he comes, they sleep not—  
> between the two, my eyes have suffered grievous pain.

Chapter 118 — Reproaching the Eyes; Kurals 1180, 1179
