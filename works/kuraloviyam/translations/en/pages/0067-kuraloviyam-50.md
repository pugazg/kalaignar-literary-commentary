Not only Poovazhagi—all the women working with her stood bewildered. At that moment Karmegam's son-in-law, Kannayiram, came there. On seeing him, the women moved aside.

“What are these little sparrows saying? My father-in-law has luck if anyone does! Always surrounded by young doves—ah! This threshing floor is one great revel!”

When Kannayiram spoke like this, Karmegam grew angry.

“Hey! Do not speak like that! Every one of them is like a daughter born to me!”

Kannayiram laughed loudly on hearing this.

“What, father-in-law? You speak without knowing the world! Touch any one of these women and she will wilt! Well, my little parrots, am I not right?”

Until then the women had stood patiently. Poovazhagi burst from among them like a storm and stood before Karmegam.

“Sir! You asked how one could identify the chaff among people. There is no need to go searching—here is the very form of human chaff!”

she shouted, anger surging in her voice.

“Yes, Poovazhagi! Valluvar himself has said that one who keeps speaking only useless words should not even be called a human being; he should be called the chaff among people!”

Karmegam explained.

“Ah! The little sparrow's words taste to the old man like sweet millet-flour *pittu*!”

Kannayiram said as he walked away, proving himself to be “human chaff.”

> Do not call the one who delights in useless speech a man;  
> call him the chaff among humankind.

Chapter 20 — Refraining from Useless Speech; Kural 196

*Pathadi* = chaff  
*Makan* = human being
