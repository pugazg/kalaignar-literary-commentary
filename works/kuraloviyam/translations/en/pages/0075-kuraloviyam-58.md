How were they now to continue the journey? How were they to satisfy their hunger? It had become a problem for which they could find no answer. A flood of anguish engulfed and choked them.

The children cried out, “We are hungry! We are hungry!” and begged for some way to get food.

The head of the family had never expected such a storm to strike. Just then a beautiful fish-eyed woman came that way carrying a tray of fruit and calling out her prices.

As soon as she saw the family she exclaimed, “Madam, is it you? How have you come so far? Why are the children crying?”

The woman of the family explained the terrible ordeal that had befallen them. On hearing it, the fruit-seller melted with compassion. She gave all the fruit on her tray to the children. Nor did she stop there.

“Please do not misunderstand me. You must come to my house. We can share the gruel in the pot, and then you may continue your journey. I have a little money too; please take that for your travelling expenses.”

The head of the family could hardly believe her affectionate words. Seeing his astonishment, she continued:

“Are you wondering why I should hurry to help you? What I do now is not even a millet-seed's worth of repayment for the help your wife once gave me without even knowing who I was. Once I too found myself in a situation like yours. Escaping from wicked men, I reached your town and sought refuge there. I suffered terribly from hunger. After wandering street after street without finding even a single mouthful of rice, it was this lady who relieved my hunger. Could I forget that gratitude while I am alive?”

With tears filling her eyes, the fruit-seller said this and insistently led them to her home.

As they walked along the road, the woman said to her husband:

“Do you see how Valluvar's words come true? Relieving the hunger of those who come saying they are starving is never wasted. He says it becomes a treasury in which one stores what one has gained so that it may help in a later time. Do you see the wondrous greatness contained in the quality of giving?”

> To relieve the destroying hunger of the destitute—  
> that is the storehouse where one lays away what one has gained.

Chapter 23 — Giving; Kural 226
