In the morning this disease of love appears as a bud; through the whole day it swells into a great bud; in the evening it becomes a flower. Here is the Kural:

> Budding in the morning, swelling through the whole day,  
> In the evening this disease blossoms.

Do you know what that evening in which the disease blossoms feels like to the maiden? The army that comes to kill her arrives as the messenger of the fire-like evening.

He says, “A killing army, becoming the messenger of the evening that is like fire.” Is that all?

At evening the cowherd is playing his flute. Sweetness pours from the flute like a waterfall; that music adds delight even to the breeze! The swaying clusters of flowers appear to perform gestures to the flute-music. They say the sound of the flute can enchant even a serpent. Do you know what the beautiful woman who has not seen her lover says about that flute-music? “The army that torments me seems to come not merely as the messenger of evening—it comes as the cowherd’s flute as well!” she laments.

> Becoming the messenger of the fire-like evening, the cowherd’s  
> Flute comes like a killing army.

Chapter 123 — Lamenting at the Sight of Evening; Kurals 1227, 1228
