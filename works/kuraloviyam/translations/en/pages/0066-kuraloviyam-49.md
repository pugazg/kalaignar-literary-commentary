## Visual material

Large colour illustration above the prose showing women working with grain in an agricultural field, with a seated man and another worker nearby.

On the threshing floor beside the fields, women of the farming community, like springtime birds, followed the saying that one must winnow while there is wind. Holding winnowing trays in their hands, they tossed the paddy grain. Good grains rose in heap after heap. The chaff gathered separately in another place. The owner of the fields sat upon a mound of black stone, watching the work on the threshing floor proceed briskly. His name was Karmegam. He regarded the women who worked on his farm as his own daughters. When their work ended, he began measuring out their wages in paddy grain with the wooden measure.

“Today's winnowing produced very little chaff,” said one woman, smiling as she showed Karmegam the chaff she held in her hands. Her name was Poovazhagi. He too expressed his pleasure and then asked her a question.

“My child, Poovazhagi! We can easily identify chaff among grains and set it aside. But there is chaff among people too. How are we to identify them?”
