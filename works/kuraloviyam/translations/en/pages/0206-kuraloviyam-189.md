Suppose a young man falls in love with a young woman and lies with her beneath a moonlit pearl-pavilion. Some villagers who have no connection at all with the matter (`ethiraar`) then speak `alar` about it. In the Book of Inbam, Valluvar depicts such gossip from many different angles.

After delighting with her lover, she returned home with nail-marks and tooth-marks on her cheeks and breasts, and sat smiling softly as she thought of what had happened. Her friend came in teasing her. “My dear! Couldn't you keep your love a little more secret? It has come to the point where the whole village is talking `alar` about it! What are you going to do now? You will have to tie up and put away your memories of love,” her friend warned.

But she, reclining with her clothes disordered while her inward joy remained undisturbed, answered her friend's warning only with a dismissive smile. Not understanding anything, the friend stood bewildered with her hand on her cheek. Even the friend who had come to warn her felt strangely affected when she saw the deep coral-red lines upon her companion's body.

At that moment the fire in the stove began to burn more strongly than necessary. “Put it out,” the friend told the woman of the house. At once she rose, took down the pot hanging from the ceiling rack and poured the ghee in it upon the fire. “My dear, has madness gone to your head? Can you put out a fire with ghee?” the friend asked mockingly.

She replied, “You cannot extinguish fire by pouring ghee upon it, can you? In just the same way, the `alar` spoken by the people of the village cannot extinguish the love—desire—that I bear for him! Just as pouring ghee makes the fire blaze larger instead of putting it out, know that their `alar` will not cool my love but make it grow still more.” Here is the Kural for it:

> To say that gossip will quench desire is like saying  
> that fire can be quenched with ghee.
