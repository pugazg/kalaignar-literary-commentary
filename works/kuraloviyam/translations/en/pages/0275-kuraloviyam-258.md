“—he is on his way. I came running only to bring you this good news. Now you can prepare to welcome him. Bathe in rose-scented water and put on new clothes! I know that your arms, which grew so thin from thinking of his absence that the bangles slipped off, will receive new bangles. The prince is coming as the supporting branch for that vine-like waist of yours which can be seen only when you walk swaying. Poor thing, I feel pity for this bed inlaid with coral and pearls. A voiceless object! What ordeal will it have to endure tonight?”

Saying this with a mischievous laugh, her friend ran downstairs.

The man who had gone to drive away the enemy—the man who had left her the pain of separation, who had departed while pallor spread over her and her heart trembled—would soon return. The joy she felt on hearing this could conquer the world. She removed her clothes and bathed. Thinking of the feast she was about to enjoy, every part of her body swelled with expectation. Standing before the mirror, she adorned herself with jewels. She placed a large kumkum mark on her forehead as though only so that it might later cling to his cheek.

In the mirror she did not see herself. She saw only him. Her feet would not remain still. Drums roared, announcing that the victorious army had entered the capital.

Now she faced a great problem. Three questions began piercing her heart.

“My dear husband will be here in a little while. When he comes, shall I quarrel with him for leaving me to suffer? Or shall I embrace him? Or shall I unite with him in delight?”

She struggles in indecision. If she spends time quarrelling, even a little of the time that could be spent in his embrace will be wasted. If she only keeps embracing him, the delight of union will be delayed. The battle within her mind torments her.

Shall I quarrel?

Shall I embrace?

Shall I unite?

This Kural explains the state of the honey-voiced woman who cannot find an answer to those three questions:

> When my dear one, precious as my eyes, returns,  
> shall I sulk, shall I embrace him, or shall I unite with him?
