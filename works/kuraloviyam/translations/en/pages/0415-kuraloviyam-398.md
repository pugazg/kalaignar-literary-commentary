“None of that superstition has ever come near me! I can say even to you now that you will soon come to ruin.”

“How? Why?”

“That is Valluvar's word.”

“That is what I am asking you to explain.”

“A young woman trusted your younger brother—yes, a young bird of a girl. He abandoned her. In the end she was buried without anyone even knowing whether she died or was killed. Is that not so?”

“Killed?”

“Do not roll your eyes in surprise. You are the village headman responsible for investigating it, telling the village the truth, and punishing even your own brother if needed. Yet you yourself ask, ‘Was she killed?’ A little later you will probably ask whether your brother and she were even in love! Not only was that young peahen ruined and sent to the grave, you are also trying to frighten her parents and push the truth into darkness. That is why I say there is a deep meaning in the story of the Chola king who was willing to sacrifice his own son for the sake of a cow's calf.”

Hearing Maraimudhalvan's words, Nalangilli stood with head bowed. Maraimudhalvan continued:

“As your friend I still tell you this: if a man wishes to know beforehand that ruin is approaching him, it is enough for him to recognize that his own heart has abandoned impartiality and is thinking of doing wrong. That is a wise saying that defeats the words of fortune-tellers—a still higher saying—the word granted by Valluvar long ago.”

After giving this warning, his friend went away in anger. Nalangilli's heart trembled, knowing ruin was coming.

> Know that I shall be ruined when my own heart  
> abandons impartiality and chooses what is wrong.

Chapter 12 — Impartiality; Kural 116

நடுஒரீஇ = departing from impartiality  
அல்ல செயின் = if one does wrong
