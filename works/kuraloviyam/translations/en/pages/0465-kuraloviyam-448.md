The details of the order were announced throughout Athanar.

Many elders of the town approached Minister Koothan with an appeal:

“The habit of drinking liquor can indeed cause harm. We do not deny it. Yet it is not right to frame laws this harshly and punish the people so severely.”

Hearing their appeal, Koothan spoke angrily, all the more eager to make himself appear holy:

“Elders! Do you know or do you not know? Is there not even a law to banish from the country those who defy a government order? Here—read what this law book says!”

he thundered.

The elders stood silent.

“Because you have come to me and called this law cruel, I am going to make it still harsher. From now on, whoever drinks liquor in defiance of my order will be banished from the country.”

Koothan shouted this and issued the order accordingly.

Those who drank in violation of the order were banished. The force of the decree did not subside. Very soon three-fourths of Athanar's population had disappeared. Only one-fourth remained.

Most people left for neighbouring countries, saying that the banishment order was actually an order that benefited them. At Athanar's own government expense they were transported across the border. In those countries there were efforts to reform people who drank liquor, but there were no laws exiling them.

In Athanar there were no longer enough people to carry out farming, improve the towns, or complete any work at all. Athanar lost its prosperity and sank into poverty.

Koothan finally understood the cause and announced that he was withdrawing his own order. Not only that—he had the drums beaten to proclaim that a liquor shop would be opened inside the palace itself for all the people of Athanar.

There was no one who did not laugh at his ignorance.

“Even if one has learned what books say about how things should be done, when putting them into practice one must understand the nature of the world and act in harmony with it.”

The elders of the town asked mockingly in the royal hall whether Minister Koothan had at least now understood the idea of this Kural.

Koothan stopped coming to the royal hall altogether.

> Even after learning the methods of action,  
> act by understanding the nature of the world.

Chapter 64 — The Minister; Kural 637

செயற்கை = the methods of doing things learned through books
