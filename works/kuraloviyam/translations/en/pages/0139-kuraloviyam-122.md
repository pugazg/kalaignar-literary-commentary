celebration. On a lower branch beneath the high branch where the owl sat, two crows whispered to one another:

“What's this? Can't this owl's swagger be brought under control at all?”

“Be patient. The right time will surely come.”

“When will that time come? When will our worry end?”

“You foolish girl! Shouldn't one wait for the right time and moment in everything?”

“Oh, go on, my dear—you have no courage! Which official are you going to ask about the proper time and moment? You simply do not have the daring to subdue this wretched owl!”

As the crows spoke like this, the owl growled from the top branch. “Hey! Who's there? What's that noise?” Hearing that commanding voice, the crows shut their beaks. But fire flared in their eyes. Then came complete silence. After a long while the male crow whispered into the female crow's ear, “Look—the time I told you about is coming!”

The morning star appeared. The rising sun came up, stretching out hands of light. Everywhere there was brilliance, a great flood of brightness. “The time I spoke of has come,” said the male crow as it flew toward the top branch. It pounced on the owl. Struck by the attack, the owl swooned and fell below. The crow had won. It was a victory gained by choosing the right time to strike.

Valluvar says that time and occasion must be reckoned with in order to defeat an enemy.

When Hitler's armies entered as far as Moscow, did not the people of the Soviet land wait until winter came and finally win? This Kural explains the value of timing:

> By day the crow defeats the owl; so too, to conquer a foe,  
> a king must have the proper time.

Chapter 49 — Knowing the Proper Time; Kural 481
