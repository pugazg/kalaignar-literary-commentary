He was recognized by Arignar Anna as an unfailing younger brother, an unerring younger brother, a younger brother who would not stray from his path—an able younger brother growing in Anna's way—and he grew in that manner. Today he has given *Kuraloviyam* to the country.

Valluvar gave us the Kurals; he gave us diamond-gems. He set those diamond-gems into a golden manuscript and arranged them in three divisions. When we look at the diamonds he gave—even though they are so beautiful—the eye is dazzled. We cannot look at them directly. For when we do, the brilliance of their intellectual light can make it impossible to gaze at the diamond itself.

To put it another way, when that light flashes so brightly, the beauty of the diamond is not seen; only the light is seen. But when that same diamond is set in gold together with emerald, hessonite and other stones of many colours that resemble diamonds, the diamond not only shines distinctly: it gives its full light in a way that allows its full beauty to be seen.

In the same way, by setting the Kural given by Valluvar within a picture and making the light of that diamond visible to our eyes, Kalaignar has completed a rare and great task, a task of great distinction—like the work a goldsmith performs by engraving the diamond and working the gold, but here by engraving Tamil and engraving its Kural, **[several words are unavailable in the audited Tamil source at this point]**, and performing it as an artist.

Because Valluvar's lines showed the way for life in its entirety, Bharathi sang that Tamil Nadu “gave Valluvar himself to the world and gained sky-high fame.” Those who have explained the Kural given by Valluvar in a way that our hearts can receive are not very few. Great commentators such as Parimelazhagar, Manap Pulavar, or more recent commentators such as Ka. Su. Pillai, Dr. Mu. Va., my beloved professor Dr. V. Sub., and many other teachers—Puratchi Kavignar Bharathidasan, Pulavar Kuzhanthai, Namakkal Kavignar—have, in different ways and colours, given commentaries on Valluvar's Kural. But when we see those commentaries, we see Valluvar from one angle through the commentators.

Whatever Kural Kalaignar touches upon to show Valluvar, he presents Valluvar in his complete form.

He shows Valluvar's beauty; he shows his face; he shows his anger; he shows him shining as a great intellectual genius; he shows Valluvar as a leader; he portrays the heroine who lived within Valluvar's heart; he shows Valluvar as a merchant; he shows Valluvar as a hunter.
