She imagines that in this world, at night, she alone is separated from her beloved and suffering in solitude. Yes—what concern has she for anyone else? Wherever she and her lover are together, that alone is the world to her. A place without him is harsher to her than even a wasteland.

That is why she withers like the tender shoot of a young banana plant scorched by embers. It is natural for someone to languish because there is no food to eat. Here, however, the food itself languishes because he has still not come to consume her.

It is because of that anguish that the night is first abused; then the same feeling turns into pity for the night.

“O night that puts every living being in the world to sleep! Now you have no companion except me. Your condition deserves pity.”

Thus she looks upon the night and expresses her compassion. Thinking again and again of the man who is as dear as life itself, she alone tosses upon the bed without sleep. If she too had fallen asleep, poor night—there would be no one at all to keep it company. How beautifully that maiden expresses the pain of separation she suffers at night!

Is it she who says this? It is Valluvar who makes her say it!

> O night that has put all living beings to sleep,  
> you have no companion except me.

Chapter 117 — Lamenting in Wasting Longing; Kurals 1168, 1169
