The elder endured their cruel acts and spear-sharp words like an ocean of patience. He did not lower himself to their level and strike back at those arrogant youths with either tongue or hand.

All the women who had come to the water tank pleaded with them to leave the elder alone. Yet the youths' hearts would not yield to compassion. They sought to increase their own pleasure by tormenting him.

After a little while, some government policemen happened to arrive there. The moment they saw the officers' headgear, the youths began trembling. They clearly understood that they had been trapped and could not escape.

The elder could have taken revenge. But his heart, rich in the fullness of refinement, had not fallen to that level. Instead, he made this appeal to the police:

“They are all my dear grandchildren. Playing with me in this familiar way is always a source of fun for them. It was wrong of them to behave like this in the middle of the street. I ask forgiveness on their behalf. Please let them go!”

Hearing the elder's words, the young men's hearts sank; they stood with heads bowed in shame.

“What a worthy noble man, what a patient elder, we have humiliated—and by doing so we ourselves stand humiliated! Though we pierced him with cruel words, is it not a flood of compassion that flows from his eyes? Even after we rose to the height of arrogance and scorched him with evil words, does he not stand shining like gold refined in fire? He has even chosen to protect us rather than hand us over! What can equal his forbearance, his full measure of generosity?”

Thinking all this, the youths were overcome; those thoughts flowed down their cheeks as tears.

See how the great Valluvar shapes that scene:

> Those who punish are held as nothing; those who endure  
> are treasured and kept like gold.
