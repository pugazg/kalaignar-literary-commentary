Mullai-kodi sobbed that the cruel one called dawn had come and ruined the night in which they had caressed and rejoiced.

Even in those sobs she possessed a beauty of her own, a beauty that drew him still more strongly. Seeing it, Mukilan sighed and embraced her. With his lips he stamped a seal upon hers, a shell that kept pearls hidden within. The tears flowing from her eyes wet his lips. To him those tears tasted sweet, not salty.

Mukilan suffered greatly before he could use all his strength to separate her body, which clung to his, and step outside.

The fishermen welcomed him with teasing. His catamaran too began to float upon the sea.

Mullai-kodi stood at the doorway of her hut looking out until her eyes could see no farther. All the catamarans went far into the sea and disappeared. She gave no thought to food that day. She had no need for supper either. The feast she needed was out at sea, on a catamaran, and would come seeking her only the next morning.

After she had remained awake for a long time, weariness and the deep darkness slowly closed her long eyelids. She fell asleep. Even that sleep was sweet to her.

A dream came to end her sorrow. Who else would come in the dream? Mukilan came. “Mullai-kodi!” he called. “Atthaan!” she replied. A rain of kisses! Pleasure swelled like a flood. There was a brief pause; even then he held her shoulder and pressed his chest against her beautiful face.

The weariness born of delight brought her another strange delight. Suddenly she awoke. The dream dissolved. She said to herself:

“When I slept, the one who appeared in my dream and rested upon my shoulder gave me joy. When I awoke, where did he go? He went nowhere. As soon as the dream dissolved, he hurried here and seated himself in my heart.”

This Kural is the casket that holds those beautiful lines:

> When I sleep, he rests upon my shoulder;  
> when I wake, he swiftly dwells within my heart.
