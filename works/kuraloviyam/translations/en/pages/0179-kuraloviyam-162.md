## Visual material

Large colour royal-court illustration occupying the upper part of the page: a crowned king points outward while two attendants stand behind him.

A great king who had always shown warm affection toward his minister one day spoke somewhat harshly and humiliated him. The reason was nothing other than false information a spy had given the king about the minister. The report conveyed to the ruler was that the spy had discovered the minister taking part in a conspiracy against the king. At first the ruler could scarcely believe it. He did not know that the spy disliked the minister, or that out of that hatred the man had sown, cultivated and successfully harvested such a monstrous lie.

The king decided that the actions of the minister, who had left with his head bowed after seeing the king's anger, should continue to be watched. He summoned the spy again and ordered him to observe every movement of the minister with the greatest care. The very same spy! The same spy who had worked to create a rift between king and minister! Spies too are human, are they not? Among them too there are some who fall prey to likes and dislikes. The spy departed to carry out the royal order.

After sending him away, the king's thoughts began to move rapidly. To discover whether what that spy said was really true, and also to determine whether he was acting honestly, the king concluded that it would be best to send another spy without the first spy knowing. Accordingly, another spy was given his instructions. The first spy's name was Othiyan. The man sent next was named Iniyan.

That night Othiyan wandered outside the minister's house in disguise. Without Othiyan knowing, Iniyan too moved about the same area in disguise. The minister's conversation inside the mansion could be heard outside—
