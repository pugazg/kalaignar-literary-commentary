## Visual material

Large upper illustration of a man handling bundles of palm-leaf manuscripts while several men observe.

Good heavens! What a sharp sword!

Its gleam steals the eye like lightning flashing through pitch darkness amid crashing thunder. What skill the smith who forged such a sword must possess!

An army equipped with weapons like that sword could never even be touched by defeat. If that blade merely whirled on the battlefield, the enemy would tremble. A mighty sword capable of tossing two or three severed heads into the air with a single sweep—there it is, raised in one hand, its beam of light tearing lines across the sky.

Palm-leaf manuscripts lie scattered in heaps.

Among those manuscripts sits a man. His eyes swarm over the leaves.

Akam, puram, the Five Great Epics—how many manuscripts there are!

Has he read every one of them and engraved them in his mind? Or is that merely the illusion he gives those who see him?

Books pouring beauty. Books fragrant with knowledge. Books teaching culture. Books proclaiming virtue.

He gathers them all into bundles, rises and walks with dignity.

Two men who see him speak to one another:
