## Visual material

Large upper illustration of a mother and young woman.

> Is not education a hill-grown plantain?  
> Go, my daughter—eat of it to your heart's content.

Singing this sweet, breeze-like verse of Bharathidasan—the poet praised as Paavendhar, Revolutionary Poet, treasure of classical Tamil and the poet of twentieth-century awakening—a mother sent her daughter to school.

> After combing your hair and adorning it with flowers,  
> your mother told you: “Go to school.”

The daughter went to listen to her mother tongue and gain mastery in it.

She studied eagerly.

She gathered the world's treasuries of knowledge and stored them in the chest of her heart.

She grew like a tender shoot, like a cool crescent moon, sweet in every way.

Her lips became red fruit.

Her body was like a statue dipped in molten gold.
