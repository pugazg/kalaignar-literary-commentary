Before she could begin to wonder whether that tree had sprouted two arms and whether those arms had embraced her just so, she realized that he had arrived. For a little while there was no speech between them. Both hearts throbbed.

“Did anyone see you coming?”

“No! No one but you saw me! Did anyone perhaps see you come?”

“No, my dear! Apart from the oil lamp that gives light in my house and this undying lamp that gives light in my heart, no one else knows that I came here.”

“Why would they not know? Here is the coconut tree! There is the punnai tree! And the moon above—do they not all know?”

“I only mean that neither my family nor the people of this village know of our love. Unless someone comes to know through you, that is?”

“Certainly no one will know.”

Their conversation turned in another direction. The moon-maiden covered her face with the cloth of clouds.

Dawn came. The love-meeting that the two of them had thought no one knew about had become known throughout the village.

Her friend came to her and told her in detail that five or six women had been speaking at the street-corner pavilion about the meeting in the coconut grove.

She replied in anguish:

“I met my lover for only one day—alas! And already the news has spread as widely as everyone comes to know when an eclipse grips the moon!”

It is said, is it not, that an eclipse occurs because a serpent swallows the sun or the moon? Astronomers have discovered that eclipses occur when the Earth, the sun and the moon come into a particular alignment in the sky. Even in ancient times there were scholars who studied the positions of the celestial bodies and revealed the subtleties of the heavens. An eclipse is one such truth that was observed and understood.

Just as news that an eclipse has gripped the moon becomes widely known throughout the village, so too, it is said, the event of her meeting her lover for a single day spreads swiftly through the whole village. Here is the Kural for it:

> We met but for a single day; yet gossip spread  
> as though a serpent had seized the moon.
