"If only I knew where he was, I would at least have sent some messenger to him.

"How many days has it been since I slept?

"Sleep comes only if one can forget something.

"What shall I forget?

"That he touched me?

"That my whole body burned then?

"That he poured a rain of cool kisses to calm that heat?

"That he asked whether I had no heart to return what he gave?

"That before he had even finished asking, he took it back himself?

"Or the story of delight that followed?"

Piling question upon question, she finally forgets herself and falls asleep.

She has the weariness of many sleepless days.

A dream comes.

In that dream—a swan.

It speaks to her.

Her lover, it says, sent it.

He has promised to return within two more days.

He has told her not to worry.

He has asked her to bite half a jackfruit bulb and send the other half to him.

Hearing the swan's words, she sinks into delight.

Still lying upon the bed, the young woman's hand reaches toward the bowl of jackfruit.

She wakes.

The swan disappears.

But the dream remains in memory.

"What feast can I offer this sweet dream that brought me the swan sent as my lover's messenger?

I do not know what hospitality I can give it, for it has given me such happiness!"

She releases a long breath of joy.

This Kural paints her longing:

> What feast shall I give the dream  
> that came bearing my lover's message?

Chapter 122 — Speaking of the Dream State; Kural 1211
