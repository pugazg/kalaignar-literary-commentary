“I firmly told you not to come after me, went to the edge of the bed and lay apart from you. You were the one who came close, touched me, kissed me and embraced me! So it was you who embraced defeat.”

“True. I embraced you. And you yourself admit that your name is ‘defeat,’ do you not?”

“Do not think you can win with words. You were the one who lost last night's lovers' quarrel—not I!”

“Do you know that the one who loses in a lovers' quarrel is the one who wins?”

“What new grammar is this? If that is so, why not give the kingdom and crown to the king who loses on the battlefield?”

“On the battlefield, a warrior wins only by making the enemy kneel. In the field of love, a man wins only by kneeling before a woman!”

“Your explanation is amusing!”

“It is not my explanation. It is Valluvar's explanation—and a truth I have learned in my own experience!”

“Oh! Has Valluvar himself called those who lose a lovers' quarrel victorious warriors?”

“Yes! The one who loses in the quarrel is the one who wins. The Kural says that this truth can be seen in the joy of union after the quarrel ends. In other words, only I know how, by surrendering to you and losing last night, I gained all the delight I desired and became the victor. What do you say? Shall I lose again tonight?”

“Go away!”

Burying her face in modesty, Thirumathi ran inside.

Comparing her complexion with the colour of the gold bangles shining in his hands, Ilamathi happily murmured Valluvar's Kural to himself.

Which Kural?

Here it is:

> In lovers' quarrel, the one who loses is the one who wins;  
> that truth is seen in the union that follows.

Chapter 133 — Joy of Lovers' Quarrel; Kural 1327
