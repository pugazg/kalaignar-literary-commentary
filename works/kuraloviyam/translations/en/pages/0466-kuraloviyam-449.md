## Visual material

Large upper illustration of embracing lovers beside an oil lamp.

An unexpected meeting. Then the event in which they lost one another.

The lover who has returned home cannot sleep. His heart surges with memories of her. He thinks her condition must be the same.

To ease the pressure upon his mind, he writes her a letter.

When the heart carries a burden, one must speak to someone to lighten it—or at least write about what weighs on the mind. In that spirit, this is the letter he writes to her:

“My honeyed Tamil! The festival crowd! In the light of the torches, your red-lotus face shone as though polished with gold. I stood tasting that sight with my heart.

“Then where did such a cyclone suddenly come from? I do not know! Yet it did us some good too. Every torch set up along the street went out in that wind. Complete darkness!

“The elephant brought for the festival broke free and ran. People screamed in fear, scattered in every direction and fell to the ground.

“What happened to you? Where were you? I did not know. I stumbled through the darkness searching for you.
