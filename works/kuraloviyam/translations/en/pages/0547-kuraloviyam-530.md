## Visual material

Large upper illustration of Athiyaman with Avvai.

Athiyaman Neduman Anji, who then ruled Tagadur, was a great warrior who could raise his sword and shake the world.

Even when he saw a spear flying toward his face, he possessed the courage not to blink.

The battlefield was the courtyard in which he had crawled and played.

Spears and arrows were the toys of his childhood.

He was a lord of valour who regarded battle wounds upon his chest as strings of pearls.

He was a victorious lamp of the Tamil people celebrated by the literature of the Purananuru, a people who considered showing one's back to the enemy a disgrace.

That Tamil hero, filled with zeal for war, also possessed a vast love for the language that had raised him.

Avvai, whose Purananuru verse casts light upon him, praises him in these lines:

> Bearing the victorious, keen-edged sword,  
> broad-armed and ankleted, crossing the field where foes fell—  
> lord of the Athiyars, rich in surging liquor.

Her praise does not stop there.
