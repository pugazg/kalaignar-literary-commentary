# Dr. V. Sub. Manickam

The great distinction of this *Kuraloviyam* is that Kalaignar began writing it thirty years ago. He began in 1956 and has written it until now. He must have been very young then—why, even now he remains very youthful.

If he began thirty years ago and kept writing without giving it up for thirty years, that shows his clarity, his firmness, and his determination to complete it somehow. What remarkable firmness of purpose he must have possessed to begin thirty years ago and bring it to completion.

Whether or not he believes in rebirth, and whether or not I believe in it even more than he does, the Tamil style that came to him in his thirtieth year is still with him now. His mature, practised style was already present by the time he was thirty.

Some acquire one style at thirty; another at thirty-five; by sixty it begins to totter.

But one who has sustained the same style for thirty years is one among the poets of Sangam-Tamil strength, one among the Sangam learned, one who speaks Sangam Tamil: Kalaignar.

In my view, one who has no interest in Sangam Tamil is merely a *panga-thamizhan*.

Sangam works such as the Thirukkural and the Tolkappiyam are the taproots and supporting roots of our Tamil. Only those who immerse themselves and revel in these Sangam literatures can understand the civilization and distinction of the Tamil people.

In that way, because he possessed a deep command of fine Sangam Tamil from his youth onward, the Tamil style found in his *Kuraloviyam* has become so forceful, beautiful and sweet.
