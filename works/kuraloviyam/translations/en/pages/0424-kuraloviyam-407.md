“Do you think I am one who worries about his own life? If my life or yours is lost on the battlefield and yet the suffering of our people does not disappear—if that fox-hearted man continues destroying the country—what use would our deaths have been? Think of that, my beloved warriors.

War will indeed rise again. We will indeed meet the enemy in battle. But first we must examine the four kinds of strength Valluvar speaks of, and only then enter the field.”

One commander asked humbly:

“My king! I have been trained to swing the sword in a field flooded with blood and play ball with enemy heads. I have not studied Valluvar. You said there are four kinds of strength. May I know what they are?”

The king of the mountain fort patted the commander's back, smiled, and replied:

“First, we must examine the strength of the action we intend to carry out and make that action strong. Next, we must understand our own strength in beginning the action; if it is insufficient, we must increase it. Third, we must never underestimate the enemy's strength. We must remember that deceit and intrigue too are weapons in his armoury. We must remember that he first defeated us by using the ignorance of the people as a weapon. Along with these, we must accurately calculate his military strength.

The fourth is most important. Which kings stand as his allies? Which kings stand as ours? We must understand the strength of that support. Yes—we need the support of powerful rulers. Only after examining all four kinds of strength will the decision we take bring us victory in war.

Do not fear. We are moving toward the crown of victory.”

The king's explanation of the Kural made everyone around him in the mountain fort think—and act.

They strengthened the undertaking.  
They worked to reduce the enemy's strength.  
They increased their own military power.  
They secured powerful allies.

War broke out. The new king fell. Good government returned. The people rejoiced.

> Weigh the strength of the deed, one's own strength, the enemy's strength,  
> and the strength of allies—then act.
