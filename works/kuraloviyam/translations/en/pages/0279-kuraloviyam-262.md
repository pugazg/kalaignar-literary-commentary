“After eating, you must have gone to the bedroom. Your wife must have come in. You would have lain turned away on the other side of the bed. After waiting and waiting, she would have touched your shoulder and pulled you toward her. Then the two of you would have quarrelled back and forth. And after that…”

“Hey, Nalli! Keep quiet! Lovers' quarrel, my foot! You are rushing ahead before I finish. I thought she would touch me, or place her hand on me as though turning in her sleep. I lay turned away pretending to sleep, thinking I could continue the quarrel a little longer and afterward the union would overflow with boundless delight. Not the slightest sign came from her. Then I thought: let me go outside and lie in the cold on the veranda. She will become distressed, come out and take me back inside. Trusting that, I put my plan into practice.”

“Oh! You are quite a fellow, Killi! Seeing you shivering on the veranda, your wife must have come running, embraced you and taken you inside!”

“She certainly came running! Do you know what she did? She bolted the bedroom door and went back to sleep!”

“Alas! What happened to you? What became of your lovers' quarrel?”

“What became of it? I wrapped my arms around myself against the blowing wind and suffered on the veranda all night. I caught a fine cold!”

Nalli laughed aloud. Killi's face showed his embarrassment. They walked talking toward the riverbank. The river was running in flood, overflowing its banks. Just then a man shouted, “I am going to swim in this flood!” jumped into the river and began swimming. Within moments Killi and Nalli saw the flood carry him away.

“What is this? Has he become such a fool? Even knowing the speed of the flood, he jumped in to swim and got caught in it. Poor man!” said Killi.

“This is just like your lovers'-quarrel story, friend! Have you not read the Kural in which Valluvar says that the condition of a man who tries to quarrel even though he knows the quarrel will not work is like that of someone who leaps into a river knowing the flood will sweep him away?” asked Nalli.

> Like one who leaps into a flood knowing it will sweep him away,  
> why do I quarrel, knowing the quarrel will fail?
