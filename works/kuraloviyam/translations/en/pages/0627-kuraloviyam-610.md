Roman rulers fought without sheathing the swords they had drawn, won battle after battle, and built an empire that seemed impossible to shake.

Wherever they marched with sword in hand and set foot, lands fell beneath their feet.

They returned only after raising the flags they carried upon the summits of enemy fortresses.

Many crowned rulers knelt before them.

Pearls and coral, offered as tribute, were piled in their royal halls.

The age in which the Roman Empire shone like the golden crown of the world was created by the strength of that country's warriors and by the disciplined leaders who guided them.

The fame of Caesar, the renown of Antony, and the power of Augustus flew like banners across the world.

Yet history also records how that empire, once a treasury of heroic history, slowly wore away and vanished under the rulers who came generation after generation.
