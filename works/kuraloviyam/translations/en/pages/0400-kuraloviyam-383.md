## Visual material

Large upper illustration of an Earth woman and two speculative visitors or robot-like figures.

Let us imagine something strange. In this twentieth century, through the achievements of science, man has landed on the moon, walked a little distance there, picked up rocks, photographed the regions he saw, and returned. As far as he has learned, no living beings have yet been reported as dwelling there. Let us continue our imagination from that point.

Suppose one or two human beings from the moon—or from some other world such as Mars—come to Earth with the aid of scientific craft they have invented. They are astonished to see a world unlike their own, laughing in the green beauty of nature. They lose themselves in wonder at the young grain swaying in the fields. They see mountains where eaglewood and teak flourish. They see people carrying mature sugarcane from the fields, crushing it in mills and tasting the jaggery. They see threshing floors, bundles of grain, and women of unbroken grace carrying them upon their heads. They marvel as golden paddy becomes rice shining like pearls. They watch that rice being cooked and people sitting together to eat with delight. A child bites and chews a tender cucumber; nearby a young man eats a mango while casting his gaze below the neck of a beautiful maiden standing before him.
