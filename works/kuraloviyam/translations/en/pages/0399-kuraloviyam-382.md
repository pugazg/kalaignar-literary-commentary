He wanted a group beside him to justify the wrongs he committed. He sought people who would praise his jungle rule as good government. He planned that those who had not studied books of justice—or who, even if they had studied them, did not live by them—should remain near him and sing his praise.

A person who studies a book of justice but does not live according to it belongs in the list of the “unlearned,” does he not? It was therefore easy for that king to obtain the support of such a group.

News reached Valluvar that the tyrant had surrounded himself with people who had not learned justice or right conduct. His face withered. A smile mixed with pain appeared.

“If a tyrannical government makes people who do not know what justice and right conduct are into its protective wall, there is no greater burden upon this earth than that.”

Though Valluvar spoke these words with a smile, his eyes were troubled.

Mother Earth sighed, as though saying: “At times I must serve not merely as the bearer of human beings, but as the bearer of mere lumps of flesh.”

> The tyrant who binds himself to the unlearned—  
> there is no greater burden upon the earth.
