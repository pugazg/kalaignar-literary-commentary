In that moment, the scene of her lover leaving her began to cast its shadow across her heart.

As time wrapped itself in a black blanket, he and she slipped beneath it together.

Thinking that the curtain of night had fallen so that no one could see them, they travelled to the very boundary of delight. As strings of pearls struck and struck against one another, as coral-red lips told the news of their union again and again, face joined face and heart joined heart in joy.

Night itself grew tired, trampled beneath their play. Unable to bear any more, time began to cast off its black blanket and put on a white cloth.

It is rare to see a tender creeper lying entwined around a hard palmyra tree; yet here that was how she lay embracing him.

The arrival of dawn tormented her. He would have to leave on account of trade—that was the cause of such pain! Before he could finish saying, “I shall go and return,” he gave her one kiss for every syllable he uttered and received two in return for each.

The sound of those kisses is still ringing in her ears.

The pleasure of the embrace still hums like a bee within the flower of her heart. But in her eyes alone a flood is rising. And through that flood her eyes are swimming, trying to go and see him!

She speaks to those eyes. What does she say?

“O my eyes! If you had the power to run, like my heart, along the inward path to the place where my lover who left me now lives, you would not have to swim like this through a flood of tears!”

The Kural from which this picture was painted is this:

> If my eyes could travel inward, like my heart, to where he is,  
> alas, they would not have to swim this flood of tears.

Chapter 117 — Lamenting in Wasting Longing; Kural 1170
