...when pigeons, sparrows, goats, cattle, deer and rabbits are killed for food? That question cannot help but arise!

As the world's population grows into crores upon crores, a condition has arisen in which grain alone is not enough and meat too has become, in practice, part of the food supply.

We live in an age when governments ask people to meet nutritional needs through meat as well.

The world now proceeds without treating meat-eating as a great cruelty, but by including meat among ordinary foods.

Valluvar, however, rejects killing and eating any living being with the same feeling by which we refuse to kill and eat creatures that have grown under our own hands.

He cannot bear the idea that one life should be destroyed in order to sustain another.

He composes a Kural saying:

"All living beings in the world will join their hands in worship before one who kills no life and refuses flesh."

> All living beings will join their hands and worship  
> the one who kills not and refuses flesh.

Valluvar goes a step further and criticizes even those who perform a thousand sacrificial rites pouring offerings into the fire.

He states decisively that refraining from killing one life and eating it is better—more beneficial—than performing a thousand sacrifices while pouring pot after pot of ghee.

There have been, and are, those who conduct sacrifices not only by pouring ghee as offering, but by cutting down goats, cattle, and even horses.

If Valluvar says that even sacrifices with ghee become unnecessary when compared with refusing to kill and eat one life, would he accept rituals carried out by killing many lives?

He would not.

He did not.

Though these verses in the chapter Abstaining from Flesh have been placed within the section on renunciation, are they not heart-moving ideas that householders too ought to think about?

> Better than a thousand sacrifices pouring offerings into fire  
> is not to kill one life and eat its flesh.

Chapter 26 — Abstaining from Flesh; Kurals 260, 259
