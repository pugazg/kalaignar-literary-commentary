For his explanation, Valluvar sees no place more fitting than a battlefield.

Flashes of sword-light.

Daggers flying from side to side without wings.

Battle-blades entering armour, striking shields, and scattering sparks.

These are what we see in the field.

Horses charge and trample with their hooves.

Elephants seize and fling men with their trunks.

The wheels of racing chariots roll across bodies.

Ayyo! Some soldiers, unable to bear the terrifying resistance, hide.

Some turn and flee.

Some kneel before the enemy.

Some, smiling at the thought of dying, carry spears, bear honourable wounds, and embrace heroic death in the field.

But the fearless commander who leads the army keeps advancing with the warriors who still follow him.

Even if some who entered the field beside him turn their backs, he bears that too.

Even if some who marched with him bow before the enemy, he does not let his resolve weaken.

Whatever side the weapons fly from, he moves forward with the firm mind that it is his responsibility to bear them himself.

Valluvar says that one who takes responsibility for preserving the greatness of his people and lineage must be like that fearless commander who bears the enemy's attack in battle.

> As the fearless bear the burden in the battlefield,  
> so among one's own people the burden rests upon those able to bear it.

Chapter 103 — The Work of Sustaining One's House and Line; Kural 1027
