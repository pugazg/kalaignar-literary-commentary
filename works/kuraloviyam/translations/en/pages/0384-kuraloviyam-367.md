## Visual material

Large upper illustration of a man asleep on a cot while a woman lies awake on the floor.

“How long has it been since work ended? Where have you been all these hours? Would you lose anything by running home, as soon as work ended, eager to see your woman? Going to the kitchen, coming back to stand at the doorway, casting my eyes all the way to the street corner to see whether you were coming—do you know how painful it has been for me?”

The wife sang this song of her heart to her husband as he entered the house holding a string of jasmine. Without understanding her state of mind, he replied with a slightly hardened face, “When work ends, do I have no other way to spend my time? Friends came. I went with them to the beach. I even bought this jasmine there.”

After that she said nothing. She moved like a statue suddenly brought to life—but a mute one. She spread the leaf, served the food, straightened and cleaned the bed. He lay down. She placed the fruit plate near him. Reclining, he bit into the fruits, tasted them and finished them. She also held out the bowl of milk.

Usually it was not like this. After eating they would talk for some time, swelling with happiness. She would lie in his lap and he in hers in turn, wetting each other's lips and travelling toward the city of delight—
