Gambling and intrigue increased.

Murder and robbery shocked the people.

The condition kept spreading until there seemed to be no department without deficiency and no hand among those who governed that remained unstained.

The ministers repeatedly announced that all the good schemes they had declared were functioning and that the country was prospering.

A new law every day. A new scheme every hour. Yet nothing remained steady or firm. Everything staggered.

Amid the sigh of the farmer, the long breath of the worker, and the fiery breath from the homes of ordinary people, that government stumbled and floundered.

Friends described such a government to a distinguished poet.

"I heard that they gathered ideas and planned to act in an orderly way," said the poet.

"True, poet! But without bringing any matter to a proper conclusion, they have turned the country into a wilderness," his friends explained in pain.

"They thought and announced things methodically, yet what they actually did remained incomplete and deficient. Do you know why? They lacked competence—the ability to carry out what they say and think."

With anger mixed with mockery, the poet said this and his eyes filled with tears.

That is exactly what Valluvar points out through this Kural:

> Though they plan in proper order, those without competence  
> carry out their actions without bringing them to completion.
