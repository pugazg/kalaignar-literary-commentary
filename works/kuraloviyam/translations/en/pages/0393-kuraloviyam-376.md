The place where he stands thinking is near the military camp where the forces of his king are arrayed while besieging the enemy's fort.

They have conquered many regions formerly under the enemy king and have laid this siege so that at last they may enter the fort, surround him and defeat him.

The enemy king cannot break the siege. Final victory will belong to the ruler whom this warrior serves. With that confidence, the warrior stands with shoulders raised.

“Husbands and wives enjoy a feast in the evening. Should I not provide a feast for my evening?”

Such a question rises in his heart. At once he gives the evening some good news. What news?

“O evening, wait! May my king press forward in the undertaking before him and win victory. After that victory I will run home, join with my beloved wife, and prepare a feast for you.”

What flavour there is in changing the idea from “husband and wife eat a feast in the evening” to “husband and wife prepare a feast for the evening”! What flavour!

Can we not understand the special charm in the difference between saying, “O bed, we will roll upon you in affection and delight,” and saying, “O bed, by rolling upon you in affection and delight we will relieve your sorrow”?

Valluvar has simply used evening in place of the bed. That is all.

Whether the feast is eaten or offered, the benefit belongs only to husband and wife—certainly not to the evening!

The richness of imagination in this idea cannot but astonish us.

> May the king engage in action and win; then, joining my wife at home,  
> we shall in the evening prepare our feast.

Chapter 127 — Longing for His Return; Kural 1268

அயர்கம் = we shall do
