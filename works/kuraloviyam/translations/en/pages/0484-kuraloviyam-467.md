But the heart's pouch expands the more intellectual food it receives. It keeps digesting what it has eaten and keeps searching for new nourishment.

A man who seeks books, obtains them and clearly learns their meaning finds that, with every new thing he comes to know, he marvels at the ignorance he possessed before.

Today, when he understands the meaning of a book and immerses himself in the stream of knowledge, he decides that the stream in which he bathed yesterday was poorer than this one.

As he keeps learning fresh meanings from books, he wonders what can be compared with the pleasure he feels.

Each time he unites with his beloved, does not the pleasure feel new—each day and each time—so new that what he enjoyed before seems second-rate beside it?

He decides that only that feeling can be compared to the joy of fresh understanding.

“Jewel of my eye! I have never known pleasure like what I have known today!”

“Nor have I, beloved!”

Each time the lovers are lost in pleasure like this, they feel as though they had never known it before.

So it is with the person who reads each new book and understands its meaning.

As earlier ignorance becomes visible with every new act of knowing, perhaps the flavours of union never learned before become visible in fresh ways each time lovers join and rejoice.

Thiruvalluvar says:

“Each time one joins the adorned woman and experiences pleasure, it is like realizing one's former ignorance each time one comes to understand the meaning of a book.”

What a beautiful comparison!

Here is that Kural:

> As each new learning reveals the ignorance that came before,  
> so is love each time one joins the jewel-adorned woman.

Chapter 111 — The Joy of Union; Kural 1110

செறிதோறும் = each time one joins and becomes one
