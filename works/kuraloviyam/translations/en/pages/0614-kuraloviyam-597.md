Her friend, standing there with a face drawn by sorrow, tried to comfort her.

"Do not worry. Even if your parents close the pupil of the eye behind the door of the eyelid, memories stay awake and wander outside. They do not seem to understand that."

Then the friend added:

"I know the pain you suffer because you are separated from Kathiron. I also understand how the mad elephant called unrestrained desire must trample and crush your heart."

At this, Thenmozhi smiled, though her eyes were wet.

Looking at her friend through tears, she said:

"Are you describing your own experience? I have nothing like that.

"Even if I am forced to live separated from the man I love, I keep thinking of him constantly. Because of that, the pain of separation cannot come near me. Even the assault of desire does not exhaust me; instead, it gives me a feeling of pleasure."

Her friend looked at her in surprise.

"What an extraordinary woman you are! Even the Kural shows us flock after flock of women lying in grief for the one who has gone away—their eyes pouring rain, their bangles slipping from their arms. But you..."

Before she could finish, Thenmozhi interrupted:

"Do not rush! Even in that Kural you can find women like me. I am one of them," she said, with both a sigh and pride.

Yes. Which Kural is that?

Here it is:

> However great it may be, love is sweet when one keeps thinking  
> of the beloved, for then no pain of separation can enter.

Chapter 121 — Lamenting the Absent Lover; Kural 1202

எனைத்தொன்று = however much / however great  
வீழ்வார் = the beloved / one who is loved
