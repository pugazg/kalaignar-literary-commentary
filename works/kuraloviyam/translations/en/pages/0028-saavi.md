# Editor Saavi

Many people have written commentaries on the Thirukkural. But, above all those commentaries, Kalaignar has brought honour to Valluvar.

I too have read many commentaries. I understand nothing. I have sometimes thought it would be good if someone wrote explanations for them.

Kalaignar has written them—and every illustration he has written—I say this sincerely: after Ramakrishna Paramahamsa and after Rajaji, when it comes to telling little stories, Kalaignar's equal is Kalaignar himself. The stories are highly fitting. He will take a story that happened now and tell it in a way that suits a Kural written long ago.

When I was at *Dinamani Kathir*, Kalaignar used to send in *Kuraloviyam*. Week after week, my assistant editors and I would wait for his *Kuraloviyam*, wondering when it would arrive.

In the newspaper office, instead of thinking, “This story has arrived,” we would wait asking when it was going to come—and as soon as it arrived, we would read it in the handwritten manuscript itself. There would not be a single place with a strike-out or correction. He would not rewrite it a second time.

Only Kalaignar writes like that. Those who have clarity of thought have no need for strike-outs or corrections.

He has built a splendid memorial for Valluvar, written *Kuraloviyam* too, and thereby brought honour to Valluvar.

Kalaignar has written this book not merely for the Tamils of Tamil Nadu, but on a scale capable of uplifting them wherever they may be in the world.

People speak of “a library for every home.” Even a library is unnecessary. If *Kuraloviyam* alone is there, that is enough; it can be useful at any time.
