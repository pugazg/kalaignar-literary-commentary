## Visual material

Large upper illustration of a tiger hidden among crops while people carrying spears approach.

In our youth we heard stories about a tiger that wrapped itself in a cowhide, entered a herd, deceived the cattle, killed them and ate them. Though it is a highly imaginative tale, it arose to teach us not to be deceived by trusting a wicked man merely because he acts like a good one. Even in plays, dialogue is sometimes written such as, “Look, there he comes—the tiger wrapped in a cowhide!”

When one animal disguises itself in another animal's skin in this way, it would seem fitting that a wicked animal should wear the skin of one considered gentle.

But the speed of Valluvar's thought, the great flood of his imagination, takes an entirely different form and leaves us amazed.

> They alone truly live who live by ploughing and eating;  
> all others follow behind, living by bowing to them.

In keeping with that Kural, a small landholder engaged in agriculture lived in a beautiful little village. Through the price of his sweat, the maiden of his cultivated field stood dressed in green silk. Yes, the rich green crops made his fields shine in beautiful array.
