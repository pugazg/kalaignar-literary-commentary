## Visual material

Large upper illustration of Periyar and Rajaji.

In the early period of India's freedom struggle, the two stood shoulder to shoulder and beat the drum together. Both wanted society in the future free country to advance in every way, but each chose a separate path to achieve that aim. When they explained their methods to the people, their ideas clashed with one another. They lived through those clashes of ideas until those venerable old men had reached almost ninety-five years of age.

One travelled across forests and rough ground, raining ideas upon the people. He undertook hard work—he spoke, he wrote. He was Periyar, a giant of the world of reason. The other was a master of politics. He went among people, spoke ideas they would accept, drew them slowly toward his path, and through writing, speech and labour sought to create the radiance of a new society. He was Rajaji, the elder statesman.

Idea against idea, principle against principle—the two charged against each other with sparks flying. Yet not even the slightest harm ever came to the friendship of their early days—
