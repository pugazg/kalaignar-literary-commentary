Yesterday, when he had touched and struck the bull, received the garlands of victory upon his shoulders, and was still approaching from afar, what haste possessed these very eyes? They broke through the doors of the eyelids, would not even wait at the gateway of the brows, flew out and fastened themselves upon his broad chest.

And today, because he has gone away saying, “I will be back soon,” instead of lying in an embrace all day, how furious they have become!

“Cry well, my eyes—cry! Your crying only amuses me. That day you ran so eagerly toward him and made me his. Today, separated from him, it is quite a sight to see you weep!”

So the woman before the mirror mocks her own eyes, even as her tears overflow.

Here is the Kural that explains it:

> They themselves rushed to look upon him, and now they themselves weep;  
> there is indeed something laughable in this.

Chapter 118 — Reproaching the Eyes; Kural 1173

கதுமென = swiftly

கலுழும் = weep
