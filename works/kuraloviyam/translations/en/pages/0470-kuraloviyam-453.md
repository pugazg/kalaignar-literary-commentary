## Visual material

Large upper illustration of a woman turning away while a smiling man watches.

“Bharathidasan sings, dear:

> I took up a poem to paint;  
> the sky said, ‘Write me.’

“When you take up the pen and begin to write, what—or who—comes and stands before you saying, ‘Write me’?”

the young woman asked, resting her face upon his shoulder.

“You already know, my dear, whom I take boundless delight in writing about, and what gives me immeasurable joy to write about!”

he said, stroking her lotus-soft cheeks.

“Tell me, beloved! Can you not say it? Am I not allowed to savour hearing it?”

She pouted. She pleaded with a spilling smile. “Hmm,” she cooed.

“Some who read old tales say there is something called the realm of the gods. There, they say, are celestial women named Urvashi, Rambha, Menaka and Tilottama, sweet as honey and jackfruit. They make even sages absorbed in silent penance become overcome with desire and drink the nectar of their lips.
