"Though the general word 'living beings' can include every form of life, caste quarrels do not arise among you because you do not possess the sixth faculty called rational discrimination. It is among humans—who claim to possess reason—that there are many castes, clans, and feelings of high and low.

"I wrote this for those able to read the Kural. You cannot read it. Why would I have written it for you? Therefore, in this Kural, when I say 'living beings,' I am thinking of human beings."

After giving this explanation, Valluvar continued along another path. There he saw two men arguing.

One said: "Even if the Kural says there is no high or low by birth, it also points out that excellence differs because the occupations people perform are different. Therefore I, as a musician, am superior to you, who work as a teacher."

The other replied: "No! I reject that! Teaching is the greater profession. Your musical profession cannot even be compared with it!"

Before their verbal battle could grow hotter, Valluvar intervened.

"Stop this war of words! I did not say that one occupation is higher and another lower, nor that the inherent worth of occupations differs. Every occupation differs according to the person who performs it.

"Take yourselves as an example. When one musician sings, people in town may refuse even to listen and turn to other business. Yet they may listen through the night with delight to the sweet music of another musician. Likewise, one teacher may become an object of ridicule among students, while another may possess a dignity that earns their respect.

"Thus I did not seek to distinguish one occupation as higher than another. Whatever the work may be, the excellence achieved in it differs according to the knowledge and ability of the person who performs it. That is how my thought is to be understood. It is not right for you to clash with one another."

Hearing the explanation of the sovereign who gave the Kural, the musician and the teacher rejoiced and bowed.

> Birth is equal to all living beings; distinctions in excellence arise  
> from differences in the work each person performs.
