## Visual material

Large upper illustration of an embracing couple.

There is an imaginative story that when the poet Pugazhenthi sang, “O jasmine, you are a white conch to the bee,” Ottakoothar rejected the very meaning of the verse.

Pugazhenthi's imagination was that the bee blows the jasmine flower like a white conch. To him, the sight of a bee sitting upon jasmine looked like someone blowing a white conch.

Do you know the argument Ottakoothar offered in rejection?

“Those who blow a conch place their lips at its rear end, do they not? But the bee is at the front of the jasmine. How can this comparison be fitting?”

So Koothar is said to have asked.

Pugazhenthi instantly replied:

“The bee, intoxicated by drinking nectar, is blowing without knowing which side of the jasmine-conch is front and which is back.”

The gathered poets are said to have rejoiced at Pugazhenthi's answer.

If the bee becomes so confused after drinking flower-honey, what will happen to men who have drunk the honey of love? How will they speak all topsy-turvy in their intoxication? Valluvar shows this through several Kurals.
