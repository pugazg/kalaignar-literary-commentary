Bharathi Pathippagam, fortunate to publish *Kuraloviyam*, bears the distinction of bringing out innovative works by renowned elders. Need one even speak of the Tamil service rendered by a publishing house that bears the name of the national poet Bharathi, who sang with overflowing feeling, “Long live classical Tamil”? The publication of *Kuraloviyam* is one such great service. A distinctive feature of this edition is that it gives large-scale visual art worthy of the word-pictures. Among the many beauties deserving special mention are the picture of a love-sick woman whose troubled heart has swollen so greatly that she scoops up sand with both hands and tries to fill the sea; the sorrowful picture of Periyar, after the elder statesman Rajaji entered his long sleep, sitting upon the cremation-ground earth with his walking stick resting at an angle; and the picture of the heroine with her cosmetic stick, showing that an imagined fault vanishes the moment she sees her husband. Kalaignar, who saw the flow of the Kural, also gave us Valluvar Kottam. It is therefore both natural and pleasing that Valluvar Kottam is represented from page to page. It is not enough merely to thank the publisher Chidambaranar, who has printed the incomparable Kalaignar’s pictured Kural as a work of visual art; I also offer the blessing: may your publishing house grow into a picture of fame.

> “Who said that Valluvar lived two thousand years ago? He is still living. He gave grammar to life. As long as the Kurals he bestowed remain, he will continue to live.”

These fulfilled words of Kalaignar are a pledge he has given to the Tamil people. For Kannagi, goddess of fidelity, Senguttuvan created an image in stone; Ilango created a word-picture called the *Silappathikaram*. The works that those two kings created separately have here been created for Thiruvalluvar, god of Tamil, by one man called Kalaignar. As Valluvar Kottam and as *Kuraloviyam*, he has made them endure for as long as the bloodstream called Tamil continues to flow.

What duty do we owe to Kalaignar, the great son of Tamil? Learn the whole Thirukkural from childhood; think of the Thirukkural in relation to the conduct of our own lives. May the Thirukkural adorn one of your hands and *Kuraloviyam* the other, like bracelets.

> “One who is skilled in speech, untiring and unafraid—  
> for anyone to defeat such a man in contention is difficult.”

With this Kural I offer my good wishes to Kalaignar, with whom I share an old friendship and deep esteem.

Kathiragam  
Karaikudi

Dr. V. Sub. Manickam
