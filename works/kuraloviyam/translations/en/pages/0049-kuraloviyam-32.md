“Who are you?”

That was my question.

“Do you not recognize me? I am the heart!” said the figure.

“Oh! The heart? Have you sprouted arms and legs, and gained eyes and a nose too? All right! All right! Whose heart are you? A man’s or a woman’s?”

“Can you not understand just by looking at my vine-like arms and legs that I am a woman’s heart?”

Everything became clear to me. I understood plainly that, because of some sorrow, a woman’s heart had come out like this. I continued questioning it.

“O woman’s heart! Why have you come all alone?”

“This is my lady’s command.”

“Your lady sent you alone? Fine! But did she also command you to take this sand and throw it into the sea?”

“Yes!”

“Why this pointless labour—for you and for your lady?”

“My lady called me. ‘My heart! There is no use telling the one who has no love for you about your great suffering and the pain of separation. An easier task than that is to scoop up sand and fill the deep sea! So go and do that work,’ she said. That is what I am doing now.”

When I heard this answer, my head spun. Alas! What kind of sorrow must that lady endure? It seems that filling the sea is easier than telling her suffering to her lover and making him love her.

I awoke.

I realized it had been a dream.

I looked closely at the Thirukkural manuscript lying on my chest. I read once again the Kural that had led me into this imaginative dream:

> O heart, tell your grievous pain to one who loves you not—  
> Or live on, and try instead to fill the sea with sand.

Chapter 120 — The Agony of Solitary Love; Kural 1200
