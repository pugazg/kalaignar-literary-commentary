The fondling, the playful intimacy, her teasing, his pleading—all of it flickers like shadows in her heart as she lies curled up, wrapped in sorrow, shielding herself from the chill wind called love-feeling. In that anguish her body grows thin, her shoulders waste away, and her bangles loosen and fall.

> With bangles slipping as my shoulders waste, I grieve—  
> grieve that they should call him cruel.

Seeing her waste away, the townspeople feel sorrow; her relatives are distressed; her friends too share her suffering. And so all of them call her lover “cruel.” “This woman has become so thin because she cannot bear the pain of separation! Is not the lover who gave her such suffering a cruel man?” they say. “Alas! Seeing my body waste away without him, they call him cruel!” laments that peacock-like woman. Does her sorrow write its final sentence there? Not at all.

“My eyes! My beloved eyes that he praises as kayal fish! Do you not understand what I am begging of you? You have wandered and wandered in search of him and have turned me into a crop withering in a rainless field. Enough! Please listen to the petition I place before you. If only my lover appears even in a dream, I shall tell him the news of how I remain alive. I shall say to him, ‘Beloved who does not come and show me love in waking life! It is only because I see you in dreams that my life still endures.’”

> My life still remains because in dreams I see  
> the one who grants me no love in waking.

“Only if that dream comes can I at least tell him this. And who is to blame for that dream not coming? My eyes! You are the reason! If only you would fall asleep as I beg you to, then the dream would come. My lover would come in the dream. I would tell him why I am still alive. That is why I plead with you—have mercy and sleep!”

It is this flower-maiden, writhing like a worm in pain, whom Valluvar brings before us in the chapters “Wasting Away of Bodily Beauty” and “Speaking of the Dream State.”

> O fish-like eyes, if at my pleading you would sleep,  
> I would tell my beloved how I still survive.

Chapter 124 — Wasting Away of Bodily Beauty; Kural 1236

Chapter 122 — Speaking of the Dream State; Kurals 1213, 1212
