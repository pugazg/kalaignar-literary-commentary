## Visual material

Large upper illustration of three men standing together.

Valluvar states firmly that people of honour “will give up life.”

Just as the kavari deer cannot live when its hair is removed from its body, people like that deer, he says, will surrender life itself if a situation arises in which their honour would be destroyed.

Such is the distinction he gives to the feeling called honour.

Under the broader subject of *kudi*—family, lineage and community—Valluvar speaks of the greatness of honour.

But do you know that under another chapter in the same field he also says how great a sacrifice one must be prepared to make in order to preserve and raise one's *kudi*?

The word *kudi* can mean one's family.

It can mean the lineage into which one is born.

It can also stand for the people or community to which one belongs.

When speaking of the family, honour is treated as something exalted.

When speaking of lineage, honour is praised as that which gives it radiance.

But when one acts to protect and advance one's people, the way honour is viewed must be considered with deeper thought.

Some people, wishing only to soak in a pond overflowing with wealth, leap into its muddy water without caring about honour at all.
