That young man of the ayar community was named Alagan. Alagan also had an Alaguvalli. Though their love had not yet fully blossomed, both had surrendered their hearts to it.

Each day, at the time when he drove the cattle home after grazing, Alaguvalli and her mother would come along that very path. They were accustomed to going to the neighbouring hamlets to sell milk, curd and butter and returning home before dusk.

For Alagan, who had grazed cattle beneath the fierce sun from morning onward, meeting her as evening dimmed was his greatest consolation. He had met her like this on many days. He would look at her with love overflowing. She, however, would look at him in an ordinary way and pass on, revealing no meaning at all through the language of her eyes.

She had boundless affection for him. Yet because her mother was beside her, she trembled even at the thought of conveying a sign through her eyes.

That day Alagan summoned a boldness he had never shown before and began a conversation with her mother. “How was business today?” he asked. Once Alaguvalli's mother began speaking, it was difficult to make her stop; her words came like pouring rain. Without caring what answer she gave, Alagan stood looking only at Alaguvalli's face.

Fortunately, Alaguvalli was standing behind her mother. With her long eyes she had the chance to rebuke him affectionately and, in silent language, command him to look at her mother's face while speaking. Alaguvalli's mother kept talking as she walked. Alaguvalli too turned back toward him, cast a secret sign with her eyes, and then followed her mother.

The cattle, assuming that their herdsman was coming behind them, had gone a long distance away. Alagan alone stood motionless. Heat spread through his veins and nerves with the speed of lightning. A light of hope shone upon his face.

“Ah! In that secret sign my beloved gave me as she went away, is there not a medicine mixed that will cure my sickness of love? Now my illness will leave.”

So saying to himself, he rejoices. It is to explain this that the following Kural is given:

> The secret sign the close-bangled maiden made as she passed  
> holds a medicine that can cure my deep distress.
