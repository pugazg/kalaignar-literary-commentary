## Visual material

Large colour illustration above the prose showing the prospective-bride household scene; a pair of stylised tearful eyes appears in the upper-right of the illustration.

There was a young poet who was unmarried. A friend eager to arrange his marriage told him of a beautiful woman. She lived in that same small town and had no one besides her elderly mother to support her. The friend said he had spoken to the mother and that she too had agreed. The young poet was delighted by the news. He mounted the chariot of poetry and began circling the streets of imagination, wondering what the maiden who might become his life-partner would be like: a swan learning to walk? A young gentle peahen? A pearl of laughter? A jasmine flower? Lips like a spring soaked in honey? The very boundary of delight?

Tormented by such imagination, the poet set out with his friend to see the woman in person. Eager to behold the beauty he had already shaped with his poetic imagination, he went with his friend to her home. The poet and his friend sat on a screw-pine mat spread in the hall. The girl's mother welcomed them with great respect. After a little conversation, the mother moved to create an opportunity for the young woman and the prospective groom to see one another face to face.
