## Visual material

Large colour illustration showing two men in court or ceremonial dress, one holding a chain or ornament.

A music hall that captivates the eye! The king who rules the land and the eminent men of his court are seated in state. The distinguished musician, proclaiming how the mother tongue that gave us number and script also gave grammar to music and thereby won fame reaching to the heavens, is seated on the stage and pouring down a rain of nectar. Is it the tumult of a waterfall? The strength of the breeze? A cuckoo's lullaby? A sindhu sung by a little stream? Or a flower-ball tossed in play by refined Tamil women? Wonder and question seemed to race one another throughout that musical performance. The richness of his voice was like the richness of a flute! Bend, curve, rise and fall—all were in perfect accord.

In the concert that began by greeting and worshipping Mother Tamil, endowed with youthful excellence, every song had sweetness in its opening, linkage and close.

> “We seek attar, civet, javvadu—  
> how many more fragrances still!  
> Yet unable to bear the smell that comes after death,  
> how many leagues we run away!”

When he sang that philosophical song with emotion overflowing, one might even say that the entire court lay spellbound.

Having sung that, the musician immediately continued:

> “To grieve that life is impermanent  
> and turn oneself into mere chaff is folly!”
