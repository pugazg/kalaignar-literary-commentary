They were people with a habit of wasting time by conducting "research" into everybody in the village.

That day too, they competed with one another in telling village stories.

Speaking contemptuously of someone was sweet entertainment to them.

If an enemy invasion came and the village army assembled, these men would run and hide.

When the danger passed and the village soldiers celebrated victory, those who had hidden would return and hold idle discussions about how the victory had been won.

They had no enthusiasm for public work.

They had no interest in heroic action to protect the village.

Their only great passion was spending time in empty, elaborate talk.

People passing the hall on their own business paid no attention to them.

Merchants passed that way.

Warriors walked there.

Beautiful women came along the road.

None gave the group any respect.

Each simply went about his or her own work.

But that day, for some reason, one warrior lowered his head as he passed the hall.

His steps faltered.

Old age did not trouble him; it had not yet begun to rule him.

Disease had not come near him.

He had mountain-like shoulders.

His eyes shone like rays of light.

He usually walked like an iron statue come to life.

On the battlefield he was a tiger that broke the enemy's waist.

Why, then, did that powerful warrior writhe like a worm as he passed the hall?

Why did a heart that treated storm-winds like straw make him slink like a cat?

A single wide-eyed glance from him would have scattered those people whose profession was insulting everyone.

Where, then, was that proud gaze?

Where was that majestic stride?

No one answered.

The slanderers gathered in the hall invented many stories because they did not know the reason for his weakened walk.

The true reason was spoken by his own conscience.

Though he possessed every kind of fame, he had not been blessed with a wife who wished to guard that fame.

That was why, when he passed before people whose occupation was contemptuous speech, his heart pierced him.

His stride weakened.

His head bent.

> Those without a wife who safeguards their honour  
> have no lion-like majestic stride before those who mock them.

Chapter 6 — The Worth of a Life-Partner; Kural 59
