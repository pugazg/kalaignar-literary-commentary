The young man, drenched in the waterfall of pleasure and delighting in the memory of the woman who drenched him, sees the anicham flower. He praises its softness and blesses it. At once he thinks of comparing the anicham flower with his beloved.

“You are soft! Live long! But my beloved is softer than you,” he says.

> Live long, O tender anicham flower; yet she whom I love  
> is softer even than you.

As he speaks thus to the anicham flower, his beloved comes before him. Her eyes leap upon him. She gives a slight smile and draws near. He takes her by the shoulder and embraces her.

As he embraces her, he says her body is a tender shoot. Holding her shoulder, he describes her as possessing the beauty of young bamboo. Seeing her smile, he swells with delight and says her teeth are pearls. As their nearness deepens, he praises her as a jewel-bearing woman who gives off a sweet fragrance. He marvels that the darkened eye which draws him in is like a spear.

What a wonder that the beloved whom he had praised as softer than the anicham flower now appears in his imagination as a woman whose eyes bear spears!

> Tender shoot her body, pearls her teeth, fragrance her breath;  
> spear-like her dark eyes, bamboo-like her shoulders.

Just as the bee drunk on nectar could no longer tell front from back in the jasmine-conch, the lover drunk on love seems not to notice the contradiction between calling his beloved softer than anicham and then describing her as possessing spear-like eyes.

Chapter 112 — Praising Her Beauty; Kurals 1111, 1113
