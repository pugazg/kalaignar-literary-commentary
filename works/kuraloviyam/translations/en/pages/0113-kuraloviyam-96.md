Only after the sound “chchu” does she start with surprise. She realizes that the one who has moistened her cheek is the king who had filled her thoughts—after that, there is no more speech!

That night, the two of them sing their praises to the moon as it goes in procession, and frolic in the bedchamber. Pulling one another close, covering one another, joining lip to lip, they partake of a feast of delight. And to make that feast of pleasure still sweeter, Thiruvalluvar offers them a rare fruit through the Book of Love!

That fruit is sweeter even than the celebrated three fruits! It is the first and primal fruit, older than the fruit of kisses. It is the fruit of love that lays the foundation for the fruit of sensual pleasure that gladdens the heart.

The name of this fruit is the “fruit of lovers' quarrel.” It is said that only after tasting this fruit, which brings the intoxication of pleasure, do young lovers fall into a blissful daze. The truth Valluvar discovered—and the book of experience offered by those who have found a partner in life—is that pleasure sought without tasting this fruit may indeed have flavour, but it has not reached “fullness.”

In the bedchamber, husband and wife, quarrel playfully with one another. Do you know why they should quarrel? So that they may come together; engage in lovers' quarrel! Why? For lovers' union! Is this not a beautiful truth filled with teasing? A playful quarrel—then the two of them apart! One or two nazhigai pass. Unable to bear even that much time, within that very interval each looks at the other's face in the dim light; both look at once and become shy before one another; they draw near; hands touch; comes the stern phrase, “Shoo, don't touch me”—yet words sweeter than sugarcane! She takes his hand away—and then she herself moves closer until her hand touches him. “Hey, why are you touching me?” he protests playfully.

“Then why did you touch me?” comes her voice of entitlement.

“You touch me too; I am touching you.” Each tries to outdo the other!

Then comes the flood of exhilaration brought by the fruit of lovers' quarrel! Valluvar beautifully writes and shows just how necessary this quarrel is—here is the Kural:

> Lovers' quarrel is delight in love; its delight is this—  
> to come together and embrace.

Chapter 133 — Delight in Lovers' Quarrel; Kural 1330
