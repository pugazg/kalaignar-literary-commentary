# Dr. Ma. Nannan

Dr. V. Sub. Manickam has provided a critical appreciation for the book *Kuraloviyam*. That appreciation is structured like the hawk's-eye view of something flying in the sky and photographing the objects below.

But in my speech, I think I may point out just one or two ideas, like a cuckoo entering a grove.

If one were to give an example of the beauty of Kalaignar's similes,

> “The round moon, like the mouth of a silver milk-pot”

is something Kalaignar says in one place.

Bharathidasan sang a great deal about the moon. Yet Kalaignar has given the moon a very beautiful simile that did not fall even within the gaze of the revolutionary poet Bharathidasan:

> “The round moon, like the mouth of a silver milk-pot.”

I wish to give an example of the excellence of Kalaignar's metaphor.

> “A woman is in sorrow because she is separated from her lover. Sleep will not come. Thinking that at least by closing her eyes she may forget her worries, she lowers the lids over her eyes like bolts. Those mischievous eyes knock at the doors of the eyelids and then peer out through the window of memory.”

Look and see whether such a metaphor exists anywhere, in any literature. As far as I have heard and known, I do not remember anyone having fashioned a metaphor like this.
