He yearned somehow to escape the eagle eyes of the white rulers. Officials of the colonial government sensed his agitation. They approached him, threatened him, tempted him. He agreed to betray his friends—Bhagat Singh, Rajaguru and Sukhdev. In court he pointed them out and testified that they were “conspirators!” and “murderers!” He saved himself. History speaks of him as lower than the dust beneath one's feet.

The death sentence for Bhagat Singh! He did not tremble. He welcomed it with joy. Rajaguru and Sukhdev felt that to perish together with their friend—the heroic Bhagat Singh, fighter for an ideal—would bring honour both to the cause they had pursued together and to the friendship they had shared. They begged no one for their own lives. In the struggle to redeem the nation, they kissed the gallows ropes together with their friend.

Even today the people of the country kiss those lines of history and press them to their eyes.

Only those who do not understand the greatness of friendship run away from a friend when hardship arrives. The contemptible Jeyagopal is an example.

Those who understand the excellence of friendship and live by it will not cut off their bond with a friend even when that friendship itself brings destruction.

The following Kural plants this feeling:

> Those who have stood with us from long ago do not abandon that bond  
> even when ruin itself arrives.

Chapter 81 — Old Friendship; Kural 806

தொல்லைக்கண் நின்றார் = those bound in long-standing friendship  
தொலைவிடத்தும் = even when destruction comes
