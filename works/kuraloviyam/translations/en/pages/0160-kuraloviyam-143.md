## Visual material

Large colour illustration above the prose showing an elephant with a fallen warrior at the front while a jackal attacks the elephant from behind.

An elephant enters a river to drink water. A crocodile catches hold of it there. The elephant struggles for its life and cries out. At last the elephant shouts, “Narayana! Narayana!” At once Narayana, Maha Vishnu, appears and frees the elephant from the crocodile. This is the story the Puranic storytellers call “Gajendra Moksha.”

To show that even an elephant as great as a mountain may be trapped by a crocodile once it sets foot in water, and likewise that a crocodile, powerless on land, gains enough strength in water to drag an elephant down, the Puranic imagination paints this dramatic picture. In the end it makes the elephant raise a cry for protection, summons Anantha-Sayanan there too, and places a full stop upon the story called Gajendra Moksha.

The elephant that entered the water, placed its foot in a crocodile's mouth, called upon Parandhaman and attained the supreme state—that is the elephant of the Puranas!

Now look: our great Valluvar introduces us to another elephant.
