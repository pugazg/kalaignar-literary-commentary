## Visual material

Large colour illustration occupying the upper page: Socrates seated in a stone-walled prison holding a cup, with another man standing behind prison bars.

“My dear and noble people of Athens!” the old man would call out in a long, ringing voice, and hundreds would gather around him—young and old, men and women alike. A pure white beard stretched toward his chest as though pointing to the courage in his heart. A broad forehead proclaimed the thinker in him. His striking eyes seemed to pierce into the future. Though his voice was slightly tremulous, his beautiful speech poured out like a waterfall of forceful, precise words. His body bent a little, like a bow before it is drawn to take aim. He possessed an untiring urge and eagerness always to be among the people.

What did he say to the crowd gathered around him? “Know yourself!”

“Do not be deceived by deceivers! Do not lose the wealth of thought! Raise the questions ‘Why?’, ‘For what reason?’, and ‘How?’, and make an intellectual revolution!”—this was the old man's counsel.

He wished to spread it through every corner of Greece. The beautiful streets of Athens and the raised platforms by the roadside all became stages for his campaign of reason.
