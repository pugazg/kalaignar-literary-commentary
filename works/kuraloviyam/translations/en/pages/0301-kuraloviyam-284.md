My friend asked in wonder, “Where do they live? Do you even know their address? On a street where chariots run? Or in the coastal settlements where ships sail?”

My firm reply was simply, “Come with me. I will show you where they live.”

The two of us walked through many streets.

At a forge, one man was raising a sledgehammer and striking red-hot iron. Another worked the bellows to supply air to the fire. A third moved the piece of iron this way and that. Sweat poured like rain over the bodies of all three. I stopped my friend there and said, “Look at this scene in the forge.” He too stood absorbed in it with feeling.

Then my friend turned to me. “Enough, enough! Why abandon the work we came for and watch this work? Come, let us go.” I moved on with him.

Next, a beautiful pavilion appeared along our way. We stopped there. I could have gone on looking endlessly at the beauty of that pavilion made of polished stone. But there was one thing: spiders had spun webs as though to destroy its beauty. Wasps had built nests. Bats had scattered their droppings across the pavilion floor as though sesame had been strewn there. There was no shortage of rubbish either. On one of the inner platforms of the pavilion, a handsome, well-built young man had only then risen from sleep and was yawning.

“Friend, look carefully at this pavilion and at that man,” I said.

“Tell me the details,” said my friend. “This pavilion is a memorial built for a great man who has died. That youth is the one entrusted with protecting it,” I explained.

My friend laughed painfully. “What splendid protection the idler is giving it!”

We walked on again.

“Shall we return home?” I asked. My friend was bewildered. “What kind of game is this? You said you would show me where Thirumagal and Moodevi live, and you have deceived me!” he said.
