There, visible in the distance, is the village public assembly hall.

It is in that hall that the people choose those responsible for the village through the kudavolai method.

Only those who meet the prescribed qualifications for public responsibility are entitled to stand for those duties.

Palm-leaf slips bearing the names of qualified persons are placed in a pot.

Before the people gathered in the hall, the pot is shaken.

Then a person who is not competing in the selection draws the slips one by one.

The village office-holders are appointed in the order in which the slips are drawn.

This kudavolai method of election appears in ancient Tamil inscriptions and literature.

When no such selection work is being carried out, many villagers gather in halls like this simply to pass the time.

Many ideas shine through their conversations.

But can one say that only good people gather in public halls?

And can one say that villagers always live without hostility toward one another?

On that particular day, those gathered in the hall belonged to one particular group.
