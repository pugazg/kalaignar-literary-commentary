## Visual material

Large upper illustration of the elderly Nallamma ill in bed, with the village elder Ulakanathan beside her.

“Nallamma! Nallamma!” called Ulakanathan, the respected elder of the village, as he entered the house overflowing with happiness.

His hair was grey and his body trembled, but the strength in Ulakanathan's voice had not diminished.

“What is it, sir? Why are you so delighted?” asked Nallamma. Out of respect she rose and sat up. She had been lying on a somewhat torn silk mat spread in the hall. Fever, cough, pain throughout her body—she was suffering from the torments of illness. Even amid that suffering, she still possessed the courtesy to show respect to the elder.

“Do not get up! You are ill. Just lie down!”

The elder sat beside her.

Nallamma's illness was not without cause.

Only a month earlier she had been forced to surrender her thirty-year-old son into the mouth of death. He was her only son; apart from him she had no support. Five years had passed since her husband had left her a widow. Now the son too had followed his father, leaving his mother alone.
