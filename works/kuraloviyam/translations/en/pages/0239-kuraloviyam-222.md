## Visual material

Large upper illustration of a drunken man facing an elder, with drinking imagery behind them.

His eyes were red. His speech was slurred. His intoxication kept rising. Why? Was it because he was filled with fierce anger to cut down an enemy? No. Was it the turmoil of his heart because some traitors had arisen to damage the principles he held? Not that either. Then why? He was a drunkard, a habitual drinker. Nothing seemed finer to his mind than scooping liquor from a pot and drinking it.

Staggering as he walked, singing and laughing, gathering with others to drink—these were his daily occupations. The townspeople spoke contemptuously of him, though not to his face. Those sitting from veranda to veranda mocked him and made jokes about him. If they saw him staggering down the street, they covered their mouths. Some even “praised” him. At most times his eyes could not tell the difference between abuse and praise. First a laugh, then immediately tears. If he entered the house, his wife's back would swell and her face would be torn. If his mother or father intervened, all they gained were titles such as “dog” and “jackal.” The children who cried “Appa!” were trampled beneath his brutal feet.
