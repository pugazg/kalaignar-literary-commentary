“My king! While our warriors were immersed in the joy of celebrating the Chithirai festival, the enemy king carried out these outrages. To bring down our flagstaff is an unbearable humiliation. If he is now invading again, what is there to deliberate? We must gather our forces, confront him and strike. Even if all of us are destroyed in this war, will not the honour of our soil be preserved?”

The minister interrupted the commander's spirited speech.

“Good, good, Commander! Is the king asking for advice so that he may be destroyed? No, no! So that the country may live! Is he not asking for advice so that our rule may continue? Your words and deeds overflowing with valour may preserve our honour. But will they preserve our crown? Therefore stop this speech of yours. My king! The way I propose is the better way. All the enemy king's anger is directed at you. There is only one way to cool that anger: you must renounce the crown. Then the question will arise—who will take charge of the government? It is a fair question. As an interim arrangement, if necessary, I shall bear this burden on my shoulders without regard for the hardship. If such a decision is taken, he has sent word that the enemy king is even prepared to remain friendly with us.”

Before the minister had finished, the commander sprang to his feet, his young moustache trembling, and raised a trumpet-like voice.

“My king! For this country to live, I must now reveal the secret I have kept hidden. Our minister has long maintained ties with the enemy king. The attack upon our flagstaff was a scheme devised jointly by this man and that king. Here is the conspiratorial letter this esteemed minister wrote to the enemy. Our spies have seized it. Read it!”

The king looked at the letter and was stunned. He was astonished. Sweat poured over his whole body.

“Ah! Rather than have beside me a minister who devises such wrongful and dangerous ways, it would be better even if seventy crores of enemies stood beside me. Those enemies would at least bring good. How much better they are than a minister who plots ruin!” cried the king. Here is the Kural for it:

> Better seventy crores of enemies standing at one's side  
> than a minister who plots one's ruin.

Chapter 64 — The Minister; Kural 639
