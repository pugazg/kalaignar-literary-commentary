Even so, a few people, attracted by the grandeur of the seven-storey mansion, left Vazhithunaiyan and placed themselves in the robber's hands.

The robber already carried the arrogance that naturally rises from enormous wealth and a seven-storey palace.

Added to it was the pride of having bought over some of the friends of the man across the street.

“So many clever men are willing to bow before me like servants. Yet that one neighbour alone refuses to bow his head!”

Anger boiled in the robber's hard heart.

He spoke every abuse that came to his mouth about Vazhithunaiyan.

Once or twice, when Vazhithunaiyan walked along the village lakeshore, he even sent his men to attack him.

From the guards at the robber's gate to the friends who sat closely chatting with him, everyone kept raining insults upon Vazhithunaiyan.

He did not worry about any of it.

“He has limitless wealth. He has men at his command. That is why he wanders about swollen with boundless arrogance. That very arrogance will punish him.”

Saying this, Vazhithunaiyan continued his work.

The villagers seethed as they watched him walk upright wearing patience like armour against degrading words, burning insults, violent attacks and songs of slander.

His patience itself became the weapon he received.

As the reward for that patience, the whole village lined up on his side.

Even then Vazhithunaiyan calmed them and sent them away.

With a few dear friends he hurried to build an embankment against floodwater breaking from the river.

At that moment the robber came to the front of his mansion laughing until his mouth hurt.

“What? Have all of you become errand-boys for the man across the street?”

he asked.

That was enough.

The people who had been returning quietly became a pride of lions.

They sprang upon the marble mansion.

The arrogant man's palace collapsed, shattered and turned to dust.

There in the distance the boastful man runs alone.

Vazhithunaiyan, meanwhile, has stopped the river flood and is returning home with the blessings of the villagers.

His story is an example showing that people who cause harm through arrogance can be defeated through one's own patience.

Here is the Kural:

> Those who do excessive evil through arrogance  
> may be conquered by one's own quality of patience.

Chapter 16 — Forbearance; Kural 158

மிகுதி = arrogance

மிக்கவை = evil deeds

தகுதியால் = by the quality of patience
