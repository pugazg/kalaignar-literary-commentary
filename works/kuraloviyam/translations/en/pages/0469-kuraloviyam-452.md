—Peraalan surrendered his mind to Vidiyan.

Peraalan had many friends. Those friends loved him sincerely. Vidiyan joined them and pretended that he too was taking care of all Peraalan's affairs.

Whenever those friends were absent, Vidiyan would pour some piece of slander into Peraalan's ear about them. He shaped circumstances so that he alone would possess special influence with Peraalan above everyone else.

Deceived by his plot, Peraalan drove away his dearest friends one by one.

Only a friend named Ilavenil remained close—a man who had stayed inseparable from Peraalan and had rescued him from grave danger once or twice.

One day, while Ilavenil was absent, Vidiyan began weaving his net around Peraalan.

“You know that Ilavenil? His mind is a volcano! Whenever he speaks about you to others, he pours out that burning resentment. He is consumed by jealousy.”

As Vidiyan was feeding these words into Peraalan's ear, Ilavenil rushed in, seized Vidiyan by the ear and twisted it, then slapped him across both cheeks in turn.

Had Peraalan not restrained Ilavenil, Vidiyan might never have risen after falling to the ground.

“Hey, fool! Do you know what gain the books of virtue speak of? It is far better to die in poverty than to live a false life by slandering others behind their backs!”

Ilavenil shouted.

Peraalan embraced Vidiyan protectively and scolded Ilavenil.

“I did not say it, my friend! I only repeated what the Kural says. I am leaving!”

Ilavenil said angrily, and walked out of the house.

What does the Kural say?

> Rather than live falsely by speaking slander behind another's back,  
> death brings the gain that virtue declares.

Chapter 19 — Refraining from Slander; Kural 183
