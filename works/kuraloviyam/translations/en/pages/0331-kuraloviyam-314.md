“Explain it a little more clearly, Grandfather! What bundle? What burden? Why is he carrying them for no reason? I cannot see any such load!”

“It cannot be seen by the eye. But it is a great burden that can be perceived by understanding. Listen and I will tell you. It is natural for a person to desire, or long for, something. But if he becomes ready to take any crooked path in order to obtain it, that desire makes him lose the respect of human society and then climbs onto his head as a heavy burden. That is the desire this man carries. People who knew the facts were amazed that he had become ready to lose even his self-respect for the sake of his personal desires and attachments. The anger he felt toward them grew into a mountain. He carries that great burden called anger too. He poured harsh words upon those who pitied him and wondered how such a man had come to this state. Those harsh words, like an elephant throwing earth upon its own head, became yet another burden upon him. How can he travel on the path of virtue carrying all these loads? That is why he has lost the path and wanders in distress.”

“Grandfather! Grandfather! Oh no—the man has fallen into the river! He cannot even swim. Look, Grandfather, he is sinking! Yes! I cannot see him at all now! The river has carried him away!”

“Do you know what river he fell into? That, my boy, is azhukkaaru—envy. No one who falls into it can escape. One might perhaps survive falling into any other river, but no one has fallen into this azhukkaaru and lived.”

“Grandfather! What does azhukkaaru mean?”

“It means feeling jealous when you see another person prosper, another person praised, or another person's intelligence, courage and firmness. That is called azhukkaaru. That is why Valluvar says that if one walks without carrying the four burdens of envy, greed, anger and hurtful speech, one can travel easily on the path of virtue.”

The grandson's eyes shone after hearing his grandfather's explanation.

> Envy, greed, anger and hurtful speech—these four  
> avoided without straying: that is virtue.
