## Visual material

Large upper illustration of a poet or lover holding a reclining woman, with a woman representing the moon in the background.

Beauty! Beauty! Beauty! That poet saw beauty everywhere and in everything. He worshipped beauty; he loved beauty. In his poetic imagination he gathered all the beauty of the world together and fashioned a maiden of loveliness.

A gathering of red-lotus blossoms! A cluster of jasmine buds! The radiance of the dawn sky! The blissful softness of the evening breeze!

That incomparable beauty, formed from all these together, lay in his lap upon the seashore, tasting with delight the kisses he gave her and returning each gift in kind.

To her, the gift of his lips was milk mixed with honey, easing the weariness born of the trance of union. Slowly she closed her eyelids and fell asleep.

Gently stroking the hair of that embodiment of beauty, the poet looked toward the sky. He could not see the moon that had been pouring down a rain of light. Yet light still filled the space around him. Wondering how light could pour without the moon, he looked all around.
