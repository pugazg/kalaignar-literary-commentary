...all of it should be used to make wetland and dryland fields fertile, shouldn't it? That is why I have planned to build a dam, store the water, and conserve it so that it can be supplied to the places that need it at the time they need it," said the builder of the dam.

The farmers rejoiced when they understood that the wild river's flood, finding its banks and its dam, could be made to bring fertility to the fields.

One family: a husband, a wife and two children. Monthly income: one thousand silver-gold coins. But husband and wife competed with each other in spending. Trips out of town! Pleasure outings! All kinds of clothing! Expenditure beyond their income. They could not repay their debts. They sold the house they lived in; they sold the ancestral paddy fields from their grandfather's time; after paying the creditors, they sat staring helplessly, hands on their cheeks.

Another family: likewise a husband, wife and two children. Their income was only eight hundred silver-gold coins. Without living as misers who denied themselves food and every need, they met necessary expenses, saved one hundred coins each month, and lived happily. There was no burden of debt. Not even the shadow of worry showed itself in their life.

If the foundation is narrow and the upper part is widened toward the sky, the tower collapses.

If river water is not stored, apportioned and used in measure, there will be no green crops to see.

Even if income is small, no harm comes if one ensures that expenditure does not become large.

That is what this Kural explains:

> Even if the channel of income is small, there is no harm,  
> so long as the channel of expenditure does not grow wide.

Chapter 48 — Knowing One's Strength; Kural 478

ஆகாறு = the channel of income  
அளவு இட்டது = small in measure  
போகாறு = the channel of expenditure
