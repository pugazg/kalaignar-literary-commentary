Her parents tried again and again to scold and persuade her to change her mind, for she stood firm that she would marry only Kathiron.

But their daughter Thenmozhi replied that she could not take back the heart she had already given him.

Her mother and father concluded that their wish could succeed only if they prevented the two from meeting.

"When will the day come when he and I may join—to dance in the waterfall of joy, to play together upon a soft mattress, to embrace and hold one another, forgetting everything in the world?"

Yearning in this way, the young woman rolled upon her bed through the night, fell asleep toward dawn, and woke to discover that the door of her room had been locked from outside.

"If they imprison me inside the house, will that stop the bee of love from swarming through the flower-garden of my heart?"

They would not let her go out, would not let her see radiant Kathiron, would not let her hear the sweet words of love he uttered.

From that day, she said, she had declared war upon the parents who had stopped her.

She poured out this anger to a friend who had come to see her and stood outside the window speaking with her.
