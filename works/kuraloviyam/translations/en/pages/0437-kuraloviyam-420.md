That night came a great storm, a raging sea, and floodwater throughout the village. The catamarans that had gone to sea were not seen for five days.

The people of the settlement panicked. Men and women cried and writhed in distress.

“O God! Is this too a kind of livelihood you chose and gave us?”

They shed tears enough to outdo the sea.

Five days later those who had gone out returned, looking like mere skeletons tied to pieces of timber after days without food or sleep.

The settlement erupted in celebration. Hearts found relief. Families embraced the men, blessed them, and led them home.

Only Selkendai stood by himself.

Kayalvizhi ran to find him.

“Come, little brother! You can eat at my house.”

“Sister! I have been hungry for five days. Bring the rice! I will settle five days of hunger by eating today.”

“How can that be possible? In the frenzy of hunger you may scoop up food and swallow it quickly. Even then, can five days' food be eaten in one day? You are only showing how fiercely hunger is throbbing inside you.”

Kayalvizhi teased him.

But Selkendai was a prankster. Would he stay quiet?

“Sister! When your husband had gone to sea leaving you for only two days, what did you say to your neighbour and friend Ayirai?”

“What did I say?”

“I was hiding and listening!”

“What did you hear? Tell me!”

“You said, ‘My husband who has gone away from me will return. When he comes, I will join him and drink him in until all my suffering sickness is cured.’ Did you say that or not? In the same way, I say I will cure five days of hunger in a single day.”

When he said this, Kayalvizhi fell silent. She only gently pinched his little cheek.

“All right, all right. Come and eat.”

She took him home. Only on her face, modesty kept swinging back and forth.

> Let my husband return; in a single day I shall drink him in  
> until all this wasting pain is gone.
