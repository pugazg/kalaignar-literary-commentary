Within the section on renunciation in the Book of Aram, presented for those who have taken up the ascetic life, the chapter called Non-Killing stands as a distinct heading.

Valluvar may well have shaped the Kurals into the three divisions called Aram, Porul and Inbam. But could he himself have given each chapter a title and arranged ten Kurals in sequence under every chapter? That is open to doubt. We have reason to understand that those who came after him divided and compiled the sections and chapters. Many counsels meant for ascetics are also guides that householders ought to accept and follow. Some of the ideas and norms spoken of in the Book of Porul, and some of the government's weighty duties in safeguarding the people, can also be found in the Book of Aram. One may even wonder why ideas that seem properly to belong to the Book of Porul have been placed in the Book of Aram.

Since the path of Aram is the foundation of everything, is it not only natural that ideas which seem to belong to the Book of Porul should also be found in the Book of Aram?
