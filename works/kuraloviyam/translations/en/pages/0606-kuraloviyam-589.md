Valluvar asked how and when such immense wealth had come to him.

"That is a long story," said Azhagiyan, but he told it briefly.

The newly rich man had once lived in deep poverty, without resources. Then one day he suddenly discovered a great buried treasure and made it his own.

Now prosperity smiled upon him in the form of mansions, halls and towers.

"Even before he became the owner of such wealth, there had been hostility between him and me. Now in this Palur he is the man who lives with the flag of dominance raised high," Azhagiyan concluded.

"Can his boundless increase of wealth alone defeat the wealth of knowledge you possess? Do not fear," said Valluvar.

But Azhagiyan leaned toward him and began speaking almost as though sharing a secret.

"Great one! I too began thinking deeply: why should I continue to nurture that rich man's hostility? My wife thought the same.

"In the meantime, an invitation came to me from him. I had no heart to reject it and went. But on the first day, the second day, the third day—three whole days—I waited and still could not meet him. Each day the answer was, 'Tell him to come tomorrow.'

"At last one day I obtained an audience.

"He ordered a man there to give me a tray full of gold coins. I accepted them. He has asked me to use my knowledge and ability in Tamil to write a rare and distinguished book about him."

Valluvar listened patiently and then looked at Azhagiyan.

"From the man you have described so far, I cannot understand anything clearly. First you began by calling him your enemy. Then you showed him as an enemy of Tamil too. Now you say he has showered gold coins upon you and asked you to write a book. Have you started writing it or not?"

he asked, opening his eyes wide.

"Great Valluvar! That is precisely why I seek your help. I want to know what I should take as the central idea of the book. Though he was my enemy, could I not write it on the basis of the broad-mindedness he has now shown toward me?"
