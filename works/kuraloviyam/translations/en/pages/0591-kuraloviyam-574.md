He gazed again and again at its gleaming body and felt transported with delight.

When it twisted around him and embraced him, his whole body thrilled with gooseflesh.

When it swayed and writhed, he leapt about in joy.

"Drink your milk, my dear," he would say, holding out a bowl.

"My golden one! I have even composed a lullaby for you. Listen to it and sleep in my lap," he would say, stroking it from head to tail.

That was how lovingly he raised the snake.

It lived with him in the same hut. Frogs would leap ashore from the pond beside the hut and end up in the snake's belly. Sometimes the snake hunted rats as well.

But the hut was where it rested. His own bed was where it slept, coiled beside him.

"People raise deer; they raise pigeons in lofts; they raise little parrots and pet birds; they raise dogs and cats. But no one raises a snake the way I do!

"Even if they do, they make a box for it...
