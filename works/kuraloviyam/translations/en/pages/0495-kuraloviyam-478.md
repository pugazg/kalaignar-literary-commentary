## Visual material

Large upper illustration of a mother holding a lamp at a doorway while her adult son, wife and child stand outside.

From the time she nursed him, raised him and delighted in cherishing him, through the days when she stood at the doorway sending him off to school and watched his retreating form with pride, the son had remained drenched in the rain of his mother's love.

When the first tender shoots of moustache appeared and the townspeople and relatives rejoiced that he had become a man—when good people praised him as one who possessed both firmness and courage—a green parrot came and perched upon his shoulder.

He surrendered his mind to that picture-perfect young woman of refined Tamil beauty.

Even then, his love for his mother and father did not diminish in the slightest.

But his father was not willing to accept as a daughter-in-law a worthy young woman who had not been born into a household rich enough to match their status.

He told his son so directly.

The son had no wish to give up the woman who had taken her place in his heart.

His father's threats therefore had no effect.

His mother suffered, helpless and uncertain what to do.
