When the moon in the sky is reflected in a clear pond, is there not a beauty in looking at it? In just that way, seeing upon the screen the shadowed fullness of Enthizhai's unclothed form, his eyes stood filled with sweetness.

She dressed quickly, adjusted her clothes, spread a leaf and served the meal. She sat beside him too. With youthful teasing and displays of love, the meal came to an end.

After smearing and cleaning the floor, she went to bolt the door.

“Do not bolt it, my gold! I have an unavoidable task. My friend Veeran will come in a cart drawn by two bulls... I must go away tonight and will return tomorrow night,” said Vengai.

She started; she was stunned.

She longed to pour out a thousand words and beg him not to leave.

The castles she had built in her mind, imagining that this would be an incomparable night of pleasure, crumbled to dust.

“My dear! I cannot bear to be separated from you tonight. Since morning my lips have been standing ready to battle with your lips, where the tender moustache has begun to spread. I had imagined that tonight, seeing us embrace perfectly from crown to sole until two bodies became one, even Kama himself would close his eyes in shyness. Is it right for you to go away contrary to all I had imagined? Do you not know that my body is burning like fire? Tell your friend, ‘I am not coming,’ and cool the passion of this woman; otherwise the fire of love will burn me to ashes.”

Enthizhai did not actually say all this to Vengai; she thought it. Yet her womanly reserve prevented her from letting those thoughts come out through her mouth. Then how did she express what she felt?

She kept looking at him. He too looked into her eyes. Her eyes spoke everything she could not say.

In place of her beautiful mouth, her eyelids and lips moved. The dark pupils of her eyes revealed her love and made her passion known.

In short, her eyes pleaded with him on her behalf. Valluvar describes the fact that she expressed her love-sickness only through her eyes—without putting it into words or expressing it by any other act—as lending still greater grace to her womanliness:

> They call it womanliness beyond womanliness when, drawing near,  
> she tells her love-sickness and pleads with her eyes.
