—when people heard that he was to sing, the royal hall filled up. Soldiers of Kolli Nadu, eminent poets and ordinary people gathered there.

Ezhini began to sing. After a line or two about the ruler of Kolli, he began pouring out music that abused Valanadu. He might at least have shown gratitude toward the land and people that had sustained him by refraining from singing about them. Instead, he made uncultured, useless words about Valanadu and its people into his song. The poets, soldiers and other people of Kolli Nadu themselves could not accept it.

Without caring about anyone, he closed his eyes and continued thundering out his “music of abuse.” Ezhini imagined that, pleased by his song, the ruler of Kolli would set aside the other poets and give him first place in the assembly. Ezhini knew that the guardian of Kolli possessed just such a nature.

While he stood with eyes closed hurling harsh poetic words at Valanadu, the entire royal hall emptied. At last he stopped singing and opened his eyes. No one remained in the hall. Even the king was no longer on the throne. He too had been the last to rise and leave for his private chamber.

Disappointed, Ezhini came out of the royal hall. Outside, groups of poets and soldiers stood mocking and laughing at his song.

“Did he come to praise Kolli Nadu, or to insult Valanadu?”

“What a performance just to win the king's favour and gain high office!”

“He was one of the reasons enmity arose between Valanadu and Kolli Nadu; now he comes here and acts so well—the ungrateful man!”

They spoke these insults loudly enough for Ezhini to hear. He walked with head bowed, sighing that after betraying the place where he had grown, he found no one to respect him even in the place to which he had come.

A man who speaks hateful, useless words that make many listeners resent him will be despised not only by his enemies but even by those among whom he has joined himself.

Here is the Kural for it:

> The man who speaks useless words that many resent  
> will be despised by everyone.

Chapter 20 — Refraining from Useless Speech; Kural 191
