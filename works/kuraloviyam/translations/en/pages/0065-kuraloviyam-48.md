Though it was a country that had faced many wars, as soon as one battle ended the government undertook the rare work of erasing even the scars of that war and giving the people a renewed life. Thirunadu's government acted as a shield that blocked enemies and, at the same time, as a sword that cut down the suffering and grief of its citizens. It regularly examined the people's appeals, did what was needed for them at the proper time, and removed their grievances. It was because the government functioned in this way that, however many invasions came, the country could withstand them and protect itself from ruin.

Here is another country. Its name is Valanadu—“the prosperous land.” Prosperous only in name: there is no richness in its soil and no happiness in the lives of its people. None of the schemes created by the Valanadu government for the people gives them the benefit meant for them. And yet Valanadu suffers not even the slightest trouble from enemies. Still it lies without prosperity. Why?

A broad river does indeed overflow both its banks in Valanadu. It is a country rich enough to multiply wealth through paddy, sugarcane, plantain and areca. Even so, because those fields and gardens are not properly organized, they do not yield their full benefit. The Valanadu government has no concern either to receive the people when they come seeking redress for their grievances or to go to them and remove those grievances.

Thirunadu not only bore the burden of repelling an invasion every month; it also ceaselessly sought out the people's needs and worked to remove them.

But the government of Valanadu did not examine day by day the wrongs occurring in its country and administer the corresponding remedy; it remained such a government and turned the land into a wilderness. If a government brings no benefit to its people, it must be said that such a government is losing parts of its country day by day. Yes—that is what Valluvar says so beautifully:

> The king who does not daily examine and render due justice—  
> day by day, his country will decay.
