A withered face. Lips upon which no smile was fastened. Eyes whose light had dimmed. Her friends standing across the field looked in astonishment at Malarkkodi, whose beauty was fading in this way.

“What is it, Malarkkodi? Why such sorrow? Ah! Pallor has spread all over your body. What happened?”

“Why should I be ashamed to hide it? Shame itself has left me! The moment I think of him, who has gone away to another town leaving me, pallor spreads over my body. My own body has revealed my shameless condition to the people of the village!”

“Is that all that worries you so much? Malarkkodi! Not only shame has left you. O queen of stolen beauty, it looks as though your beauty itself has been stolen!”

“Yes! It is true. He took away from me both the beauty of my body and the quality called modesty. In exchange he gave me the sickness of love and the colour of pallor, and then left me.”

When she struggles, unable to hide separation from her lover, modesty flies away. To confirm it, pallor lays siege to the body.

So is it not fitting when Valluvar explains that he took her beauty and modesty from her and, in exchange, gave her love-sickness and pallor?

> He took my beauty and modesty; in exchange he gave me  
> love-sickness and pallor, and went away.

Chapter 119 — Suffering from Pallor; Kural 1183
