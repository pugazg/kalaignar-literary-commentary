—but the judge ruled that he was no thief and that the very people who had called him a thief were the ones who killed him. Even after that ruling, those in government bent justice. The long march that began from Madurai—the city once ruled by Nedunchezhiyan Pandiyan, who ended his own life on seeing the sceptre of justice bend and fell with it upright again—made the people's voice echo before those who ruled the land.

Doubt, fear, wonder over whether it could be done—amid all of these, Valluvar's Kural lines rose proudly in the hearts of those who took part in the long march.

> Prosperity, asking the way, will go  
> to the abode of one who possesses unwavering resolve.

“Prosperity itself will ask the way, seek out the place of one whose resolve never tires, and go there to join him.”

Was not the meaning of this Kural enough for those marchers to undertake their historically renowned journey on foot?

How many obstacles along the way! Hardships! Illnesses!

The sun in the sky kept pouring down a rain of fire. The tar road beneath their feet burned and seemed to melt like black paste.

Those troops with hearts capable of bearing anything walked with chests held high, imagining even the burning sunlight as though it were the shore of a lake across which the moon floated and swam.

As they had resolved, they crossed countless troubles, completed their long journey, and took up residence in my heart. Do you know what picture of thought from the Kural shone then in my memory?

“Just as an ox pulls a cart through every obstructed place—mound, hollow, pit and depression—so the hardships that come upon a person of unceasing effort will themselves suffer hardship; that person will not falter because of them.”

> For one like the ox that pulls through every obstruction,  
> the hardship that befalls him is itself put to hardship.
