A street stretched, twisted and wound like a rat snake. Since darkness too had settled heavily over it, "like a rat snake" was a fitting comparison indeed. The street and the houses along it had a close relationship. That was why many things that ought to have been inside the houses lay out in the street as well. The uncleanness of the street existed inside the houses too.

To call them houses was itself a mistake. They were sparrows' nests—inhabited by human birds. But if they were birds, should they not have wings? They would send either toddy or arrack flowing through the blood vessels of their shoulders, sprout wings, lie beside the street and fly off in every direction. Here and there a few chimney lamps, baring their teeth with a flickering grin, danced in the wind while advertising the greatness of their light.

One had to pass through that street to reach a beautiful park beside a very broad avenue in that "great" metropolis. Pagalavan, returning home after work, and his wife Paavai wanted to go to that park so they could relax, talk cheerfully and enjoy themselves. On their way stood that "splendid street."

Drawing in less breath than usual and letting out rather more, the two walked down it. At the doorway of one hut stood a man about forty years old, a woman not yet thirty, and five children ranging from ten years down to two. They crowded around them like kittens rubbing against a cat, laughing, crying and making noise...
