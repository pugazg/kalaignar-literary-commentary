The striped tigers who once stood holding swords now lay holding the soft feet of women gentle as deer.

Those who had once gathered in military camps could no longer tear themselves away from women on cushioned beds. They spent their time whispering sweetly to them.

The couches themselves became laboratories for comparing pleasures:

Which tastes sweeter—the drink of wine, or the drink from a woman's lips?

Shoulders that had once collided with the enemy's now lay joined with a maiden's thighs.

In the heart, the fire of desire.

In the head, the dizziness of drink.

Loud laughter while clasping and embracing!

Dance.

Song.

Lovers' quarrel.

Union.

Terrified young women would flee with tearful eyes from men charging upon them like maddened rhinoceroses. Those men would console them:

"Only cowards wail on the battlefield and lament in the field of pleasure, my swan-like beauty!"

Then they would seize, squeeze and kiss them.

Kings who changed queens every day.

Queens who changed kings every hour.

Great beauties who bathed in donkey's milk to polish their bodies.

Men who drank rejuvenating potions to satisfy them.

The land once rich in courage became a hunting ground for those burning with lust.

The soil upon which great warriors had marched became an arena of amusement for kings of sensual pleasure.

Revelry!

Love!

Lust!

Wine!

Pleasure!

Frenzied play!

Amid all this, the place where the sense of duty had once stood could no longer be found.

Affairs of state piled up unfinished.

The rulers who should have crossed those heaps of work were instead sleeping upon beautiful women.

Singing "everything is made of pleasure," and believing that everything would happen by itself, the rulers rejoiced—and finally stood having lost everything.

This is the history of Rome rolling down from the summit of the sky to the ground.

Should not such history become a lesson for those who come after?

That is what Valluvar says with beauty:

"When, plunged into a sea of pleasure and swollen with pride, one forgets duty, one must remember those of earlier times who forgot duty in the same way and were destroyed."

Here is the Kural:

> When pride grows in one's pleasure, remember those  
> who perished before through neglect of duty.

Chapter 54 — Freedom from Negligence; Kural 539

இகழ்ச்சியின் = through neglect / forgetting duty
