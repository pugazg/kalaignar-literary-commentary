—and, for their sake at least, could you not have explained the true state of affairs and stripped away the disguise of those who deliberately cast a stain upon Malaiyan?”

When Kalingan asked this, Silamban gave no answer and turned his talk in another direction.

“They think as though I have neither attachment nor affection for Malaiyan, and as though only some others possess true love for him. Even if others do not know that I love Malaiyan more than my own life, Malaiyan himself knows it. I am not unaware that without Malaiyan this whole village would have fallen into ruin. Malaiya! I have another urgent task. I will come and see you later.”

Saying this, Silamban hurried away. As soon as he had gone, Kalingan looked at Malaiyan and asked:

“Yes! Whenever this Silamban comes, he keeps bringing you news about our village hunchback and dwarf. Why? If they say something wrong about you, should he not explain the truth to the people who are there? Instead of doing that, why must he keep coming to you again and again, finding fault with the hunchback and the dwarf?”

Malaiyan answered Kalingan's question patiently:

“Kalinga! You do not understand the nature of my friend Silamban. I have measured his nature correctly. He keeps bringing us complaints and accusations about those two neighbours, the dwarf and the hunchback, but I do not believe even half of them. Why? Because I have heard that Silamban goes about speaking behind my back even about me, his intimate friend. When a friend has the nature of speaking ill behind one's back like this, what is there for me to say about what outsiders may say of us? And that is not all, Kalinga! How am I even to believe that the hunchback and the dwarf spoke as he claims? Might he not himself have spoken ill of me behind my back and then placed the blame upon them when talking to me? If people have the habit of finding faults even in close friends and slandering them behind their backs, should we not think for ourselves and reach a conclusion about what they would say of outsiders? This is exactly what Valluvar asks as a question mixed with wonder. Listen—here is that Kural!”

> What will those who habitually proclaim even the faults of those close to them  
> do when it comes to outsiders?

Hearing this explanation from Malaiyan, whose face remained calm, tears welled in Kalingan's eyes. They revealed the depth of their friendship. They also revealed the truth of how patiently Malaiyan bore even friends who spoke ill behind his back.
