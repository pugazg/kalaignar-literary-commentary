## Visual material

Large colour illustration occupying the upper half: a couple seated closely together beneath a moon and stars, visually tied to the moon/maiden comparison in the prose.

A round moon like the mouth of a silver milk-pot. A star-filled sky that makes one wonder whether it is a pond of lilies. Having played honeyed music about all these, those two fine yaazhs sit motionless, forgetting the whole world. As though afraid to disturb the dream-world of those living yaazhs, the gentle breeze rocks them in a low register.

The stars in the sky, meanwhile, wander about in confusion, immersed in a great and perplexing inquiry. What is that inquiry?

They are trying to determine where they themselves are—and where the moon is.

Is her face being reflected in the mirror of the sky, or is the moon being reflected in the mirror of the earth? Such is the beauty of that moon-faced woman that the stars wander in confusion, unable to distinguish between her and the moon. Here is the Kural for it:

> Unable to tell the moon from the maiden’s face,  
> The stars wander bewildered in their stations.
