## Visual material

Large upper illustration of a bearded older man standing beside a seated turbaned ruler.

Veerarakkiyar was a man of imposing appearance and strong shoulders.

At one time he had served the country as its commander. During a war with an enemy kingdom he was captured and obtained his freedom only after a long imprisonment. His release came under a treaty of friendship reached between the two countries.

The king respected the sacrifice Veerarakkiyar had endured in prison, but he also recognized that Veerarakkiyar lacked the ability to defeat the enemy kingdom. So he removed him from command and gave that responsibility to another man.

After losing his post, former commander Veerarakkiyar spent his time writing explanatory and extended commentaries on old books.

Old age too began to embrace him.

As the years mounted, Veerarakkiyar began worrying about his family. A desire arose in him to accumulate enough wealth that his household could live in comfort and luxury during his old age and even after his death.

In his youth, his attachment to the soil had meant love of country. Now it had changed into love of landed property.

He longed to acquire gardens and wells, forest lands and the wealth of hills.
