“You keep cursing your beloved, who shows you heaven when he embraces you and hell when he goes away, because you cannot bear the separation. Is that not so, Kala?”

“If you cannot understand even this, how can you call yourself a woman? Is it not one woman who truly knows another?”

“Then why does this wretched Nature bind together a man and a woman when the man does not understand the woman? Does that not make you want to scold Nature itself?”

“Go away! Life itself seems beautiful to me—something I want to think of and cherish again and again, Thogai!”

“And when he is away, does not even the distance make itself felt?”

Kala did not answer that question. Her chest heaved with an uncontrollable deep breath and then subsided. It was a sight her husband should have been there to see! Yet it was only her friend who was there. What use was that? The green-parrot maiden, who had allowed a pause for the answer, said to Thogai:

“Thogai! I am not even so angry with him for leaving me to suffer in separation. But... but...”

“Then with whom are you angry, Kala?”

“You know how friends sometimes abandon those who are suffering in distress, do you not?”

“There are such friends too. I have heard of them. I have also seen friends who do not leave when trouble comes.”

“I am not speaking of those good people! I mean the friends who withdraw when pain arrives!”

“All right, all right! What have those friends to do with this now?”

“Thogai! I am suffering now, am I not?”

“Yes!”

“At a time like this, my own heart ought to remain with me. Instead, like friends who leave when trouble comes, it has gone away from me and followed him! You give the judgment! Is not my heart a wicked heart? That is why, Thogai, I am more angry with my heart—which ran after him instead of staying as my companion—than I am with him for going away from me!”

This Kural introduces us to a maiden who shows anger toward her own heart:

> O heart, are you among the friends of those brought low,  
> that you go after him just as you please?
