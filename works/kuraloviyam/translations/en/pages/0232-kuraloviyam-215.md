—she is ready for anything, issuing her challenge on the field of desire. Yes: she is a woman of the bedchamber. An elegant woman who puts herself up for “auction,” saying that this body is an arena for men who dance in the frenzy of pleasure. She is a beautiful jewelled pavilion built of sandalwood ground into paste and mixed with attar and civet. Every part of her is a recreation hall for Kama. The handsome young bull stretches out his hand to seize the lightning waist of the honey-tongued woman. With a sidelong glance she bewitches the man who has come to her shop to buy the taste of love. “My dear, you must give me a heap of gold merely to see my smile. My king, would even the whole world be equal payment for tasting the beauty before me, behind me—my beauty in its entirety?” Only after she has taken all his wealth does she drag him onto the bed to grant pleasure. “What need of clothes?” the enchantress asks while pressing her lips to his chin. She does not stop with the question. She acts. She embraces him, slips away a little, makes him ache, leaves him alone within himself. “My life!” she says—but there is no life in that call. “My eyes!” she says—but there is no light in that word. “My love!” she says—but there is no truth in that speech. Is “pleasure” the name for two bodies plunging into a play of animal sensation? No, no! Never!

She cultivates her wave-like body to be sold by night. Even if she murmurs a thousand endearments, mingles with him and becomes a pavilion offering the arts of lovers' union, Valluvar never agrees to accept that as true pleasurable feeling. He speaks with contempt: there is no difference between embracing a corpse in the dark and joining with a woman whose love is bought for a price.

> The false embrace of women who seek wealth  
> is like embracing an unknown corpse in a dark room.

Chapter 92 — Women of Mercenary Love; Kural 913
