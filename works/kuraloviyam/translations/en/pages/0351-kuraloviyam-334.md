—medicinal powders and herbs. The fever subsided. Though he could rise and move about, he was still greatly exhausted.

Four days flew by. Though the illness that had come to his body was gone, the love-sickness glowing in his heart showed no sign of ending. Thinking of Azhagarasi, he brushed his teeth and walked toward the pond. There, unexpectedly, Azhagarasi was bathing.

Their eyes competed to devour one another.

“When did you come, my delight!” the young man asked, overflowing with love.

The beauty with the palai-flower smile cooed, “I came only at dawn! But leave that aside—I heard you were unwell, with fever?”

“It's true! I got soaked in the rain and invited the fever. The doctor gave good medicine. The illness flew away!”

Saying this, he came closer to her.

Azhagarasi looked around to see whether anyone was watching them. He gazed only at her face, like a lotus floating on the water.

Then, in wonder, he asked her:

“Azhagarasi! Other things served as medicine to cure the fever that came to my body. But for the sickness of love that you created in my heart, are you not yourself the medicine! Have you noticed this strange wonder?”

he asked, a smile flashing.

“Beloved! Surely there must be something about this in a Kural you have read!”

“Certainly! When I was suffering with fever, that was the very Kural I read and savoured.”

“What Kural is that, beloved?”

This is the Kural he recited:

> For ordinary illness, other things are medicine; jewel-adorned maid,  
> for the illness you cause, you yourself are the medicine.

Chapter 111 — The Joy of Union; Kural 1102

பிறமன் = other things  
அணியிழை = a beautiful woman adorned with jewels
