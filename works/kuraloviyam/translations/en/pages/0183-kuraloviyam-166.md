As soon as that answer came out, the young creeper-like maiden moved away from the body of the man against whom she had been entwined and began to cry with a petulant sob. Not understanding the reason, her beloved was bewildered.

He touched her hand; she struck his hand away. He moved toward her to embrace her waist. She rose, went some distance away and sat down.

Going near her, he said, “I will give you a kiss.”

She covered her face and murmured.

“What wrong have I done, my dear? Why this lovers' quarrel?” he asked.

“Repeat once more the words you just said!” she replied, her face hardening.

“What did I say, my lovely one? I only said that I thought of you,” he pleaded and coaxed; but she outdid him.

“You said, ‘I thought of you.’ That is what splits my heart,” she cried.

“Is it wrong for me to think of you? Should I not think of you, my pearl? What fault do you find in that?” he asked, falling to his knees.

“If you suddenly thought of me, does that not mean you had forgotten me before? Had you never forgotten me, there would have been no need to think of me again, would there, my dear?” she replied.

He praised the reason his life-like beloved had chosen for beginning her quarrel. He marvelled at it, asked forgiveness and lay down in her lap. As though embracing a child, she too drew him tightly to her breast. Joy overflowed from the pleasure given by their lovers' quarrel.

The boat that had been resting closed its eyes as though asleep. As though the darkness of its shadow were not enough and it wished to help the pair, the moon too slipped inside a cloud.

Here is the Kural Valluvar painted for this quarrel:

> “I thought of you,” I said; “Then had you forgotten me?” she asked,  
> and would not embrace me, but sulked.
