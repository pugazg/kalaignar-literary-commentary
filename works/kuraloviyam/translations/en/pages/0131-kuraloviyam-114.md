Hearing his grandson's reply, the old man was astonished. He raised his hand near his forehead, strained his eyes and looked deeply.

“What is this, son? Are you making fun of me? I don't see any orchard there at all. There is only a crowd of people. Someone is moving his hands rapidly up and down. There are no trees. There are no unripe fruits. There are no ripe fruits. You have made me completely blind!”

The grandson laughed at his grandfather's reply. Thinking that if they went a little nearer the elder would understand the meaning of his answer, he led him toward the gathering. The loud speech of the man on the platform reached the old man's ears too.

“People! You must understand one thing. Those who oppose us are fools. I even have doubts about their very birth. Allowing them to move about in this country is itself a great crime. They will talk about setting idea against idea. No need. A knife must meet a knife. The heads of those dog-men must be cut off and thrown into the sea. Whoever speaks against me, I regard them as insects and worms.”

Grandfather and grandson listened to this speech. Though his hearing was somewhat dull, the old man covered his ears with his hands and muttered, “Shame! Shame! What degrading words. Have public forums fallen to this level?”

“Grandfather! You asked, ‘Where is the tree? Where is the unripe fruit?’ Can you see it clearly now?”

The grandson asked with a smile. Deliberately pretending not to understand, the elder looked at him and said, “What game are you playing, boy? Someone is speaking. That is all, isn't it? Where is he leaving ripe fruit and eating unripe fruit?”

“Grandfather! This morning our teacher explained a Kural by saying that when there are plenty of sweet words available, setting them aside and uttering words full of baseness and harshness is like refusing to eat ripe fruit when it is there and instead plucking and eating unripe fruit. This gathering is the perfect example,” the grandson said. The old man showered that tender young shoot with kisses. He rejoiced, thinking that the boy's future would follow the path laid down by Valluvar.

> When pleasant words are available, speaking painful ones  
> is like plucking unripe fruit while ripe fruit is at hand.
