“If you had invited me to that hall fragrant with Tamil, I would have come too,” she said, serving white rice soft as molten silver onto the leaf. The Siddha physician next door had said onions were good for the body, so she placed beside it the pachadi she unfailingly made every day. “They say you should not eat too much fried yam; taste just one or two pieces,” she said with a smile, placing those beside the pachadi. “Even if bitter gourd tastes bitter, you say it gives good results like the principle of rationalism; so I cooked it today,” she said, serving four or five pieces. With a smile that seemed to say, “You like curry with tender drumsticks, don't you?”, mischief dancing in her eyes, she poured the curry over the rice and then sat nearby to watch the beauty of her husband eating. This was her habit.

He mixed rice and curry, took a handful and put it into his mouth.

“How is the curry today?” she asked playfully.

“It is like the lovers' quarrel you staged last night,” the husband replied, offering a simile for the curry.

“Your simile is confusing, Atthaan! Explain it clearly!” she protested coyly.

“I told you I went to a Kural gathering on the way home. There an explanation was given about lovers' quarrel. Lovers' quarrel may indeed bring pleasure to lovers, but if that quarrel is prolonged beyond measure, it becomes like food with a little too much salt. Lovers' quarrel should be like salt mixed into food in the right measure! Valluvar has written about lovers' quarrel with such beauty. I came home with that explanation still in mind. That is why I said what I did about today's meal,” he replied with a bright face.

“Then is there too much salt in the curry?”

She asked with distress.

“Oh! Then do you admit that the quarrel you staged last night went a little beyond measure?” he teased her.

Thinking back on how she had indeed prolonged her quarrel too much the night before, the beautiful woman lowered her head in shame.

> Lovers' sulking is like salt: let it be in measure;  
> if it grows too much, do not let it lengthen.
