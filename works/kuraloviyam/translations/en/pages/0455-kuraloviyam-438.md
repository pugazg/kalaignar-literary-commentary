## Visual material

Large upper illustration of the court jester facing an armed challenger before the seated king.

He was the court's jester-poet. People in the capital greatly admired and praised his humour. The king, ministers and palace residents regarded the jokes he scattered through the royal hall as fine entertainment.

Some of his jokes carried meaning. Some were meaningless and fit only for ridicule. The king happily rewarded the jokes that revealed his talent. At times he would point out the jester's empty jokes, dismiss them and move on.

One day a powerfully built young man came before the king, slapped his shoulders in challenge, and declared that if anyone possessed skill equal to his in sword combat, he was ready to fight him.

Before the commander of that country could open his mouth to answer the warrior's challenge, the jester twisted his moustache, faced the warrior and announced that he himself was ready.

The king warned the jester:

“This is work you do not need!”
