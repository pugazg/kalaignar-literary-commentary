## Visual material

Large colour illustration above the prose showing a pair of tearful eyes, with a reclining woman integrated into the lower-left portion of the image.

She lay on a bed of silver sand like a spotted peahen. From afar he saw the figure of her resting there. The sight, pouring sweetness into his heart, made him praise and bless the eyes that had given him that vision.

In the cool breeze he felt a warmth of sensation mingled in. For a moment he became like a statue. When awareness returned, he moved. Then the moon-like maiden was no longer there. She had gone.

His heart throbbed and grew heavy. “Alas! Why did I look upon this beauty? If I had not seen her, there would have been no need to suffer like this,” he lamented.

He cursed and rebuked his own eyes for having caused the pain; he went home. Sleep would not come. Thinking of her again and again, he wept. Then a certain consolation arose in him.

“O eyes! You made a love-sickness torment me when I was minding my own business. Now you too cannot sleep and are weeping... that gives me great joy. As the saying goes, ‘What is done in the morning bears fruit by afternoon’—enjoy your punishment properly,” he muttered.

Read how the Valluvar of Muthamizh shaped that muttering into a Kural:

> Ah, how sweet to us! The eyes that caused this illness  
> have themselves now fallen into this very suffering.

Chapter 118 — Reproaching the Eyes; Kural 1176
