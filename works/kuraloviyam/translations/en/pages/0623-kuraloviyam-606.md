The chapter in the Kural called "The Work of Sustaining One's House and Line" does not refer only to a family.

It is a treasury of thought that must be understood in many senses: those around us, the people of a country, one's community.

Whether a man is a self-sacrificing warrior who labours for the country, the commander of a band of selfless people who do such work, or an idealist who rises to awaken his community and remove its humiliation—how many forms of opposition are aimed at him!

Arrows of abuse.

Words of slander.

Prisons.

Torture.

Obstacles intended to isolate him so that he cannot raise his head.

Plots.

Repression.

Acts of revenge.

Defamation.

Betrayals.

Only if a man can endure all this can he bring, to the extent of his power, some measure of advancement to the people and community for whom he labours day and night.

What is it like, Valluvar asks, when such a person bears the furious condemnations and obscene outpourings hurled upon him?

Valluvar himself gives the explanation.
