Azhagarkudi was a small village. For that little village there was a man named Sevathaan who was like a lion.

They say all the animals in a forest accept the lion as king and tremble before it. In the same way, the people of Azhagarkudi lived in fear of Sevathaan and served him submissively.

There was no cruelty he had not committed.

If a beautiful flower opened and smiled in a garden of the village, he could sleep only after smelling it, biting its petals, and spitting them out. Many young flowers of the season had had their stems broken and been crushed upon his bed.

He claimed ownership over everything accumulated by the labouring people.

If he entered a house, any door had to open before him of its own accord. If anyone walked in his presence wearing footwear, that too was treated as an insult to him, and the offender was punished with a thousand lashes.

If women bathing beneath waterfalls or in ponds caught his eye, they had to appear naked before him at once. He would seize their clothes and ornaments, sit upon a tree branch, and describe himself as a "Krishna of the Kali age."
