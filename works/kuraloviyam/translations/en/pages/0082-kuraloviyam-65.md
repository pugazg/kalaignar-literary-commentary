The heroine looking into the mirror suddenly forgets that the figure before her is her own image and covers her cheek. Only afterwards does she remember that what stands opposite is a mirror and what appears in it is her own form. She is satisfied that no one has seen her cheek. Even though she knows that every morning she must deal with an incident like this, at night she does not worry about it or take precautions. And so a circular patch of pallor rises on her cheek. Though she feels shy that someone may see it, she strokes it gently with her fingers and smiles to herself, remembering some event that has passed. After her lover has gone, the mark appearing in the mirror becomes a feast for her eyes and adds sweetness to her thoughts.

Thirukkural makes another beautiful woman like her speak. The lover has departed after taking leave of the beloved. As the sky grows dark when the moon has gone, pallor spreads over this slender woman too. She sees this colour spreading across her body. She grieves that it is caused by separation from her lover. Yet even within that pain, pride arises in her.

A woman who carries a mark on her cheek may suffer in embarrassment, yet rejoice that it was left by her lover. In the same way, this woman says that even the pallor caused by separation is a matter of pride because, “This colour spreading over my body was given by my beloved.”

This is the Kural:

> Because I can say, “He gave it,” this pallor  
> that creeps over my body is itself a distinction.

Chapter 119 — Suffering from Pallor; Kural 1182
