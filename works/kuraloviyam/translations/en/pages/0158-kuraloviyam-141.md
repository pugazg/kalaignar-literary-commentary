## Visual material

Large colour illustration above the prose showing the couple together indoors, with the woman seated in the foreground and the man behind her.

Maavengko was planning to travel to another country for trade and return. Yet he was tormented by his inability to carry out that plan. Was it because he lacked the wealth needed for trade? No! In his house stood a heap of gold, like a hill, amassed by his forefathers. The reason for his hesitation and anguish was none other than his companion Maangani. Though he himself could not bear to be separated from her, he also knew that if she were separated from him she simply could not remain alive. Meanwhile, his business partners kept pressing him, praising the advantages of going abroad, conducting trade and earning wealth. With no other way out, one day Maavengko finally agreed that he would set out on the journey with them.

That night he came home with the determination somehow to win Maangani's consent in the bedchamber.

Seeing weariness on his face, Maangani pressed her cheeks against his and eased away that weariness.

She invited him to eat. She spread a banana leaf and served many kinds of food. Maavengko sat there merely looking at them. Seeing that he did not scoop up and taste the food, the spotted peacock-maiden sprang near; with her kantal-flower fingers she took the dishes from the leaf, parted his lips and began to feed him—
