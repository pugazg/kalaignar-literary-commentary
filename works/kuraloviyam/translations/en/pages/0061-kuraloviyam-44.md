The Pandiyan who had seemed a tender shoot rose like a lion the moment he heard the war drum, faced several kings on a single battlefield and defeated them.

All these are treasures from Tamil Nadu's heroic history of the past.

It was held that even blinking when a spear came hurtling towards one was a stain upon valour.

Two warriors are said to have spoken with one another. They belonged to two enemy kingdoms.

One warrior: “Your king's sword can never strike our king in the back!”

The other warrior: “Yes! That is true! Our king is not accustomed to swinging his sword at those who turn their backs and flee.”

Does not this exchange itself show how deeply Tamil warriors were attached to and honoured their leaders, the kings?

In one Kural, Valluvar says that the finest army is one capable of standing united even if Yama himself comes against it in fury. He also defines what qualities the warriors of such an army must possess.

He declares that an army gains distinction from warriors who possess four special qualities: valour, honour, following the noble path handed down, and trustworthiness.

There is no use in valour merely dancing upon the tongue. It is something that can be determined only on the battlefield. The army must consist of those who possess such valour.

In continuation of that, honour too is insisted upon for the warrior. To rise with the conviction that death's mouth is preferable to falling at the enemy's feet—that is the sense of honour.

Likewise, soldiers need the desire and the quality of following in the path of those who, through valour and honour, made themselves worthy of history.

Along with these three qualities, they must also prove worthy of the commander's trust.

Valluvar's designation of these four qualities, found in the literature of *Purappadal*, as marks of excellence in an army is fitting for every age and every form of warfare.

Here is that Kural:

> Valour, honour, the noble path well followed, and trust—  
> these four alone are the safeguard of an army.

Chapter 77 — Excellence of the Army; Kural 766
