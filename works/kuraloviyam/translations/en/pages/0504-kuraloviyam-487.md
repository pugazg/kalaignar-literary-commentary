“—and you shamelessly told me that his lips had ploughed you from the crown of your head to the soles of your feet. Have you forgotten all that?

“Why has everything that tasted sweet as jaggery that day become bitter as neem today?”

Though sugarcane juice dripped from Thumbai's teasing words, Thogai gave her a playful shove.

“Yes, yes! Such a great beauty! A warrior the world has never seen! As far as I am concerned, I do not like him! I do not like him! I do not like him!”

She heated every word.

“Mother Thogai! Do not deceive me. Do not pretend you dislike him and keep finding fault.

“I am a woman too. I know.

“When a woman has surrendered her heart to someone and waits in anguish for his arrival, is it not natural among us to speak to him as though there were no love—so that others may not discover it—while storing an ocean of love inside the heart?”

“What are you saying, Thumbai?”

“Nothing much. I am saying that even though your outward speech about love sounds hostile, the truth will soon become clear: inwardly you are not angry with him at all, but bound to him by affection.”

Thogai blushed and turned aside, realizing that Thumbai, being a woman like herself, had measured the contents of her heart.

Then she walked away—and finally ran.

> Though one speaks as though estranged, the words of one not truly angry  
> will soon be understood.
