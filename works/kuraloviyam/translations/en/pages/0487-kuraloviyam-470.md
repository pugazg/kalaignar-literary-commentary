## Visual material

Large upper illustration of a sage beside the reclining Ahalikai in a hermitage setting.

The hermitage stood in the middle of a flowering grove.

Deep within the forest, a waterfall tumbling from the hills, the leaping of deer and the fragrance of flowers filled the hermitage with delight.

There, like a lamp hidden inside a pot, lived within that hut the very limit of beauty—Ahalikai.

Even a painted woman may have some flaw. Even a sculpted beauty may lack one element of grace.

But Ahalikai seemed born as proof that the very word “beauty” came into existence only after she was born.

Neither spear-like nor fish-like eyes could stand before hers.

Even the unopened plantain blossom and the lotus bud would kneel before her breasts.

The moon that could be compared with her radiant face had not yet appeared in this world.

If one sees a tender creeper clinging to a rock, does one not feel pity for the creeper?

So too was her life in that hut with a sage who had renounced everything.

One day Indran saw that limit of beauty beside the waterfall.

“I do not rule the earthly world alone—I rule the heavenly world too!”

He was a man swollen with pride whenever he counted the extent of the lands under his rule.

She merely looked at him with indifference and disappeared into the hut.
