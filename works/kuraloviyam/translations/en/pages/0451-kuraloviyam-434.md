Nallamma's son had been a strong young man. Not only had he died; disgrace had come to rest upon his name as well.

Driving a cart was his livelihood. One day a husband and wife boarded his cart to travel to another town. It was night. The road ran through the hills. Even the growls of wild animals could be heard in the distance. The passengers belonged to a wealthy household and were in the fullness of youth. The woman wore a great quantity of jewellery.

Two days later news arrived:

“Nallamma's son threatened the husband and wife who had travelled in his cart on the forest road, killed the man, violated the woman's chastity, robbed her jewellery and fled. The woman too was killed. Nallamma's son, after murdering both of them and escaping with the stolen jewels, lost his way on the mountain road, slipped, rolled down and died. The police recovered the jewellery.”

The mother screamed and collapsed on hearing this. Illness took hold of her afterward.

Her son had not merely died—he had died carrying the name of a wicked man. Nallamma kept lamenting: “Was this why I bore the pain and agony of giving birth to him and raising him?”

Ulakanathan said to Nallamma:

“Mother! What actually happened is altogether different. When your son was taking them through the mountain road in his cart, a band of robbers blocked the way. At once your son jumped down and confronted those villains. They surrounded him and tied him to the cartwheel. Then they seized the husband and wife. The husband was killed. They raped the woman and killed her too.

“They tied the jewellery she was wearing into a bundle. When one of the robbers tried to carry it away, your son freed himself from the cartwheel, leapt forward, snatched the bundle of jewels and ran toward the village. The robbers chased him through the darkness. Unable to see the path, your son stumbled and rolled down the mountain.”

Nallamma opened her eyes wide and looked at the elder.

“How do you know all this?”
