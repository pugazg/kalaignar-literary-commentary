If we say this to a young man, we must understand that the force of the statement is being given to education, not to seven births.

> The learning one acquires in a single birth  
> Has the power to safeguard him through seven births.

He says that education learned in one birth will be useful through seven births! This is an exaggeration of the greatness of education; it does not mean acceptance of seven births.

All children born into the world are tender shoots who go to school and learn! Are all of them children who learned nothing in a previous birth? If there were such a thing as a previous birth and they had learned then, there would be no need to learn now!

Valluvar merely took up the seven births—or rebirth—that people believed in during his time and used that idea for the sake of praising education. That is all.

Having referred to it in this way in the chapter called “Education,” what does Valluvar say about “births” in the chapter called “Realization of Truth”?

> If the mind, having examined, knows what truly is,  
> There is no need to think of birth again.

If the mind examines and firmly realizes the true substance, there is no need to think that there is rebirth.

If the mind becomes clear and stands firm, he says, there is no need even to think that birth will come again.

Education is needed for the mind to gain clarity and stand firm! Yes; is there not also an education that explains and makes known what clarity of mind is? That education is needed!

If one is educated, it will be useful through seven births!

If the education one learns enables the mind to examine the true substance and lights the lamp of certainty within it, then there is no need for the very thought that there will be rebirth! Yes! That thought itself will not arise!

The idea of “seven births” was used only to speak of the greatness of education!

To insist that a firm mind is needed to examine the true substance, he states decisively that for such people there is “no rebirth.”

Valluvar’s desire to make people understand the strength of realization of truth and the greatness of education—even by speaking in deliberate exaggeration—is what emerges through these two Kurals.

Chapter 40 — Education; Kural 398  
Chapter 36 — Realization of Truth; Kural 357
