If land and sea are regarded as Aram, then it is fitting to regard all that they yield as Porul.

A pearl necklace adorns the neck of a lovely woman.

Yet the pearls for that necklace are found only in the depths of the sea.

A beautiful woman smiles, decked in gold.

Yet it is Mother Earth who bears that gold in her lap.

Though pearl necklaces and golden ornaments shine in the realm of Porul, are not pearl and gold produced by the sea and land that are Aram?

Perhaps this is how some new thoughts that seem suited to the Book of Porul—revolutionary ideas that blossomed two thousand years ago in Thiruvalluvar's garden of thought—came to be gathered into the Book of Aram.

In the age of monarchy, that great elder and scholar gave Valluvar's wisdom to the world.

Even now, under the name of democracy, many countries are ruled by strongmen's regimes of the stick.

Democracy is government in which citizens choose those fit to govern them.

Counsels once given to monarchs apply equally to those who govern democracies today.

It is not enough merely for a government to be formed by the people. That people's government must produce what the people need, procure it, preserve it, and distribute what has been protected among all the people.

> To create and acquire, to guard, and having guarded,  
> to apportion—such is the capable government.

This Kural appears at the beginning of the Book of Porul, under the political chapter The Majesty of Kingship. That was an age of jeweled crowns. Hence Valluvar spoke of the ruler's duty through the word "king."

This is an age that celebrates the dignity of democracy, in which governments are to be chosen by the people.

Even so, the duty belonging to government is still being emphasized.
