Nelindhan was left speechless. Rubbing his aching cheeks, he went out with his head bowed.

The next day, when her husband Thelindhan returned, she told him how Nelindhan had behaved and broke down weeping. Anger surged in Thelindhan. He decided that his friend must be taught a fitting lesson. He thought that just as his own wife had suffered, Nelindhan's wife too should suffer. Though his name was Thelindhan—the clear-minded one—anger made him choose a path that ought not to have been chosen.

One day Nelindhan had gone out of town. Thinking this was the opportunity, Thelindhan went to Nelindhan's house.

Nelindhan had behaved wrongly toward his wife and wounded her heart, had he not? His plan was to wound Nelindhan's wife's heart in return.

When she saw Thelindhan, Nelindhan's wife welcomed him warmly: “Come in.” She asked, “Would you like some milk? Shall I bring it?” “Bring it,” he said, restraining his anger.

She held out the cup of milk to him. He seized her hands.

He expected her to panic, tremble and cry. Instead, she shyly drew herself in and said, “Wait,” as she went to close the door.

Shock and bewilderment seized him! He ran straight back to his own house. He told his wife what had happened. She condemned her husband's conduct. Even so, she expressed relief that he had escaped without becoming caught in his own trap. He said:

“I too know the Kural, my dear!”

> He who thinks it easy and transgresses into another's home  
> gains a blame that will stand forever without perishing.

“That is, even when such an act seems easy to accomplish, a man who strays from the right path toward another's wife incurs an undying disgrace.”

Hearing the Kural and the explanation given by her husband, the dark-haired woman was filled with great joy.

Chapter 15 — Not Desiring Another's Wife; Kurals 142, 145
