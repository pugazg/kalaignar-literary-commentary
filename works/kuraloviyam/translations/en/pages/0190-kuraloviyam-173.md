—protect itself. If a maiden walking with cotton-soft feet across the grass in a flower garden unknowingly steps upon a snake, she at once becomes prey to its poisonous fang.

I wish to recount an incident that took place in the life of my dear father, Muthuvelar. As in my own life, hardships too seem to have formed a continuing story in his. One day he was sitting quietly inside a tiled village house, occupied with some work. Above his head was the loft commonly found in village homes. There, of course, all the household odds and ends, mats and bedding would be piled up.

A cobra that had been writhing about in that loft somehow slipped and fell through a narrow opening. Instead of falling straight to the floor, it landed upon my father's shoulder. Had my father immediately shouted in alarm and tried to fling the snake away with his hand, it would have raised its head, spread its hood and, in the blink of an eye, driven venom into his body before escaping. But nothing of the sort happened. He realized that a snake had fallen upon his shoulder. At the same moment, he recalled the nature of the snake. He decided, “If we struggle and leap about trying to save ourselves, it will bite; therefore it is best to remain still.” Without giving way to the shock of a snake crawling over his shoulder, he sat without moving or stirring, like a statue. The snake on his shoulder slowly crawled down along his thigh, then reached the floor, twisted away and left. My mother, Anjugathammai, would often recount this incident.

Thus, however greatly suffering may come upon us like a flood, if we understand the nature of that suffering and act accordingly, it will do us no harm.

Valluvar's statement is this: “Even if the suffering that comes to a wise person is immeasurable like a flood, once he reflects upon the nature of that suffering, he will be freed from its pain.” That is, if we understand the nature of suffering, the suffering itself will perish.

Was it not because he both understood the nature of the snake and kept that understanding in mind that the danger posed by the snake passed away?

> Even suffering vast as a flood will perish  
> within the mind of one who understands it.
