# Preface

## Kalaignar M. Karunanidhi

The Thirukkural is the treasury of knowledge bestowed by Valluvar, who laid down a way of life two thousand years ago.

In this age, when science has spread and grown so greatly, we delight in the skill with which photographers, through the camera, depict a beautiful grove, the tower of a temple, or the statue of a river maiden from many different angles, according to their ability and imagination.

In the same way, from those times down to the present, many eminent scholars may be said to have studied the imperishably renowned Kural and presented it as word-by-word gloss, extended commentary and explanatory commentary.

Apart from the explanations given to the Kural by Parimelazhagar, Manakkudavar, Parithiyar, Kalingar, Paripperumal and others, eminent poets of later times too have offered rare explanations.

Kapilar, a poet of old who came to speak of the Kural's greatness, marvels and praises it:

> “A tiny drop of water, smaller than a millet seed,  
> when looked into, shows the measure of a tall palmyra......”

One morning Kapilar sees a blade of fresh green grass. At the tip of that small blade, lying close to the ground, he sees a drop of dew smaller than a millet seed. He looks closely at the dewdrop. Within that tiny drop, the whole palmyra tree rising tall nearby can be seen! The sight sets the wings of Kapilar's imagination in flight! “Ah! The entire palmyra tree beside it can be seen within a little dewdrop; in just the same way, within Valluvar's Kural lies compressed the great meaning needed by this world,” he says.

The Quran and the Bible are revered as religious scriptures. Countless millions who belong to those faiths exalt, worship and follow those books as beacons of grace that show them the proper path.

The Kural, rather than being a book that guides one particular religion or faith, stands as a book that lays down a common way of life.
