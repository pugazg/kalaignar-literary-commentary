There may be people with the strength of heart to accept even that suffering. Yet dying is suffering all the same. Valluvar's thought is that there is no suffering greater than it. But Valluvar also says that, at one particular moment, even that suffering becomes sweet! At what moment?

Let us return to Kumanan.

By his younger brother's command, Kumanan expects that the suffering called death will approach him one day—and soon. It is true that a human being must die one day, and that this cannot be changed; yet even if one were to go out of one's way and embrace death, the suffering it brings at that moment would still be cruel. While Kumanan, the ruler, lived in the forest awaiting the arrival of that suffering, a poet afflicted by poverty came to him.

His name, they say, was Saathanaar.

The sight of Saathanaar's poverty grieved Kumanan.

But something else distressed his heart even more than the poet's poverty. What was it? He lamented: “Alas! I have fallen into a state in which I cannot give anything to the poet who has come before me in need!”

> O rare poet of fine verse, you did not come that day;  
> today you have come and reached me in distress.  
> Take my head, carry it to my younger brother's hand,  
> receive its price and cure the sickness of your poverty!

Although he knew that there was no suffering greater than death, when the state arose in which he could give nothing to the poor man standing before him, even death became sweet to Kumanan. So he begs the poet to take his head to his younger brother, obtain the purse of gold, and free himself from poverty.

He himself comes forward to have his head cut off and given away.

Valluvar, who declared that there is no suffering greater than death, pours forth the rare thought that when one reaches a state in which nothing can be given to the poor, even dying is sweet. The life-epic of the generous Kumanan itself stands as a picture suited to this Kural.

> Nothing is more painful than death; yet even death becomes sweet  
> when one can no longer give.
