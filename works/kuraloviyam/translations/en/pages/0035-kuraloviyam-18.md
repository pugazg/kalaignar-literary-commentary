—thinkers of this age—are the ones that fall within our count and reckoning.

We also know that stars many times larger than those planets revolve and twinkle far beyond, at distances of hundreds upon hundreds of thousands of miles. How many such stars are there?

This Earth is but a fragment thrown off from the Sun, one of the intensely hot celestial bodies. This globe, once a ball of fire, cooled only after countless crores of years, acquired the resources of water, land, mountains, trees, plants and creepers, and reached a condition in which living things such as worms, insects and microbes could emerge.

Through bodily union among living beings, the animal kind, bird kind, human kind and other kinds came into being.

Rain fell from the sky—the wind blew—and the human being possessed enough development of the brain to marvel at all the wonders of nature. This development differed from that of other living beings. Concluding that so many transformations, on Earth as well as in space, could not take place without some power, humanity described that power as God.

Does it have hands and feet? Does it speak? Does it grant boons?

Those who did not allow room for such questions accepted that power.

Even if one cannot believe that a person sits somewhere and conducts all these wonders in the sky and on this Earth, even those who completely reject God cannot easily oppose the idea that everything moves through the operation of a power that is a fullness encompassing universe upon universe.

They called that power “Iraivan.” They spoke of it generally as “Irai.” Without being able to say whether it was male or female, they called it “Kadavul.” They called it “Deivam.”

After religions arose, each gave it names according to its own manner.

Valluvar did not fail to shape into his Kural the condition of the age in which he lived. He not only did not disregard the qualities attributed to Irai, Iraivan or Deivam; he wished human beings themselves to embody those qualities.

In the chapter called “Praise of God,” he said that just as the letter “A” is first among letters, the Primordial Being is the foundation of the world.
