He stood like a summit in intellect, a lion in action, a waterfall of love, and a heroic form that broke the chains of slavery.

In the radiant season of youth he entered many fields of struggle for the freedom of sacred India. He was a commander who stood in the line of Pandit Nehru and Netaji Subhas Chandra Bose. There was even a time when he escaped from prison custody.

Jayaprakash Narayan was the name of that celebrated hero.

Gandhi himself urged that courageous young man to marry. Though he first refused, he eventually agreed on one condition: until the country won freedom, husband and wife would live without physical relations.

Accepting that condition, a woman named Prabhavati married him.

The springtime called youth was already taking leave before India gained independence. And there had still been no physical union between the two.
