## Visual material

Large colour illustration occupying the upper page: a young man and woman facing one another indoors.

Love is a moon—it spreads the light of delight not only in a palace, but in a mud hut too. There, beside the river and near a banyan tree, stands a hut that, with the help of the white moon, looks as though it has been thatched with strips of silver. Inside it, a handsome young man paces back and forth like a civet cat. Do you know his name? Vengai! He is not one who lives by hunting and eating his kill. He works in agriculture, feeding himself and helping the hungry as well. He comes from a tradition that exalts the work of the plough, saying that the world itself stands behind it.

He is waiting for the return of his young wife. She has bathed in the river with her dear friends and, thinking that her man will be waiting, is hurrying home in her wet clothes. Only after bathing does she feel fresh enough to serve her beloved his evening meal!

He too takes a special pleasure in feasting upon the body that has just bathed. Her wet clothing revealed to him the rises and hollows of her beautiful form and threw his senses into confusion.

Yet a worry greater than that flood of feeling was turning his heart elsewhere. Not even a month had passed since their marriage.

The taste of youth's feast had not cloyed even for a moment.

“Have you come? I will spread the leaf in just a moment,” said that jewel of a woman. Yes—her very name was Enthizhai. She went near the lamp burning by the wall, drew a screen for privacy and changed her clothes. The lamplight fell upon her form, and her shadow moved across the screen. Vengai gazed in wonder: even her shadow, he thought, had the power to steal his heart.
