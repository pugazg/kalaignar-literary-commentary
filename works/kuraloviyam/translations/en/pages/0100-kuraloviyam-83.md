They were people from a small settlement belonging to that village. They tearfully fell at his feet and explained that a tiger from the hill forest near their settlement had entered among their homes. All of them had joined together with flaming torches and chased it away, but they feared that the tiger might return. They therefore begged him to go into the forest itself, hunt and kill it, and protect them and their children.

Urkkavalan reassured them and promised that very day he would go with his men and kill the tiger. He sent them away with that assurance.

The next morning, while he was eating breakfast, his wife asked him:

“Did you kill that tiger and save the settlement people as you promised yesterday?”

He answered:

“No! I had some other work yesterday. So I am going hunting today.”

“Alas... those people are living in such fear. Can you keep letting days pass like this? Protect them at once,” she urged him. He said, “Certainly,” finished breakfast and rose.

His wife asked permission to visit her mother, who was unwell, saying she would return the next morning. He nodded. As promised, she returned the following morning. Her husband was asleep. With a deep sigh she managed to wake him. The first question she asked was, “Have you killed that cruel tiger?”

She was alarmed when he replied, “Oh! I completely forgot about that hunt!”

“What is this? It was a task that should have been done the day before yesterday, and even then you delayed it. I thought you would have gone yesterday. Instead you are sleeping. I ask you today, and you say you forgot! Is this how one serves people who depend upon you? Enough—go now. Leave immediately,” she said loudly.

“I simply cannot go now. I am far too tired. I will go in the evening,” said Urkkavalan, curling himself up and lying down again.
