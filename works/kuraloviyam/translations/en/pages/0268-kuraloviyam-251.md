## Visual material

Large upper illustration of women at a public water tank confronted by youths while an elder intervenes.

There is a drinking-water tank at a street corner. Slender young women, tender as vines, come there carrying pots to draw water.

The pipe begins to trickle only at certain fixed times. Until then the women, blooming with beauty, place their pots on the ground in rows and wait.

Choosing precisely that time, some unruly young men arrive like unrestrained bulls, making a commotion and laying siege to the water tank. The women burn with irritation. Without caring in the least about their feelings, the youths mock them and shower them with degrading words.

One day an elder happened to pass that way. His heart was deeply troubled when he saw the youths' improper conduct.

“Brothers! It is not right for you to wound the hearts of those women. If someone mocked your younger or elder sisters in such a low manner, would you tolerate it? Would you not roar, ‘I will cut out the tongue of the man who spoke like that!’? What benefit do you gain by ridiculing and insulting others?”
