—but sleep would not come. The door seemed to sway gently. Carrying a clay lamp in her hand, she went and looked closely at it.

There was no movement in the door. Sighing that it was merely the result of her fear, she returned to the bed and lay down. She turned and turned. Thoughts came to her—the stirrings of youth's springtime. She was an unmarried flowering vine. Believing that even thinking such thoughts might bring harm to her chastity, she covered her eyes in shame and lay face down.

Suddenly there came a great crash! The door shattered and broke apart. The axe that had broken the door flew inside and fell near her bed. Screaming, she rose and tried to run out through the broken doorway. But what a wonder! When she reached the door, it was still closed with the bolt in place. Her body trembling as though she had bathed beneath a waterfall in winter, she sank to the floor. Her heart spoke to her.

“Woman! Unmarried maiden! Even if you guard the door called chastity by using me as the bolt called modesty, one day the axe called desire will break that door open.”

Hearing the voice of her heart, the event of that evening cast its shadow across her memory.

The lover she desired had caught her hand and drawn her toward him in the mango grove. She had run away in modesty to protect her chastity. Though the lover let her go then, desire would not let her go now. It broke through the door called restraint, bolted with modesty, and took possession of her. With the decision that the next evening, before he arrived at the same mango grove, she herself would cling to his shoulder, she remained awake the whole night.

> The axe of desire breaks the door called restraint,  
> bringing down the bolt called modesty.
