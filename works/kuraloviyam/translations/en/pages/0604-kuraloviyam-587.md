...when will it be?' Lying upon my lap, you set the arrows of your eyes and bend the bow of your brows!"

"Yes! So many people know about our love and the townspeople speak of it with contempt. My mother too tries to stop me with harsh words. But one thing, dear: don't you understand that the talk of the town becomes manure and my mother's opposition becomes water, making the crop of our love grow luxuriantly?"

"I understand very well."

"Then why still hesitate?"

She rose from his lap and moved away in a lovers' sulk.

"Do not move away! If you do, this golden flower will lose its fragrance," he said, following her, placing an arm around her waist and drawing the vine-like woman close.

"Hmm! Enough of charming me. When is the wedding?"

she asked, clinging to him in return.

> The gossip of the town is manure, and the mother's words are water;  
> fed by both, this malady grows.

Chapter 115 — Awareness of Gossip; Kural 1147

கெளவை = blame / slanderous gossip
