Valluvar also has two lovers—who spent the entire night embraced without even the slightest space between them, reaching the peak of pleasure and returning—tell us how lovers' quarrel itself ran away.

Morning blossoms; the bedchamber gives a deep sigh. The lovers, who from crown to sole had competed in exchanging kisses with the sound “ich,” come out of the room. They bathe in water and adorn themselves with golden ornaments and garments.

Her friend meets her. They are close companions who speak without hiding anything in their hearts.

“What happened last night, girl?” the friend asks, unfolding a smile.

The answer comes at once.

“Friend! I entered the bedroom determined that I would first sulk with him and only afterward unite. But my own heart deceived me! Forgetting my purpose, my heart went straight toward union with him.

> Friend, I went intending to sulk; forgetting that,  
> my heart went instead toward union.

When she recited this Kural, the friend said:

“True! Did Valluvar say for nothing that once desire has grown to the size of a palmyra, even a millet-seed's measure of quarrel will be of no use?”

The lover who had tasted all the sweetness of that beloved's body also meets his friend. Who is that friend? No one else—it is himself. Yes, he looks at himself in a standing mirror. His reflection in the mirror looks back and asks him: “She did not quarrel at all last night. Did you notice?”

The lover's heart explains to the reflection in the mirror. It describes the night's joyous play of love.

“At first, intending to quarrel, she began her displeasure only at the level of her gaze. But when feeling rose beyond control, she moved even faster than I did, forgot even the displeasure held in her eyes, and embraced me in agitation.”

Perhaps she who intended to quarrel realized that when desire swells to the size of a palmyra, even a millet-seed's measure of quarrel is useless. Thinking so, his heart danced with delight.

> She feigned anger with her eyes, then grew agitated;  
> in embracing, she longed even more swiftly than I.

Chapter 129 — Yearning for Union; Kurals 1282, 1284, 1290
