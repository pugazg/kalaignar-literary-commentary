Like disobedient children who will not submit to their parents, not only their eyes but also their hearts broke free of control and moved closer to one another.

That day, while sweeping the street, he saw Vaigai struggling a little because she could not lift the rubbish basket onto her head. What more opportunity did he need? He went to help.

He lifted the basket and placed it upon her head. He received the privilege of seeing her and the radiance of her body from very close. His gaze spread across her whole form. That gaze seemed to lift her bodily and seat her on the summit of delight.

She moved away with the basket. He thought her sidelong glance had commanded him to follow. Like a prisoner dragged in chains who cannot escape, Vadivazhagan, caught in her eyes, followed behind her.

She emptied the rubbish into a large pit and turned. Vadivazhagan's gaze was still gathering over her. Within herself she marvelled at the pleasure that gaze gave.

He took her fingers in his own. Now she followed him. Beside the rubbish pit was a thick bush.

He led her behind its cover. She lowered her head in modesty. He embraced her. Her tender body trembled. Her eyes circled all around. No one was there.

The two street-sweepers became veena and music.

Yet the thrill she had felt when he looked at her was not there when she lay in his embrace. Instead she wilted under sorrow. What sorrow?

“Ah! It delighted me when he devoured me with his eyes. But now that I am joined with him, the fear that he may leave me—does it not give me great pain?”

So she worries.

Here is the Kural:

> His gaze gives delight; yet union itself brings pain  
> because it fears the separation to come.

Chapter 116 — Inability to Bear Separation; Kural 1152

இன்கண் = delight
