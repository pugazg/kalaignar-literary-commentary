## Visual material

Large upper illustration of embracing lovers with a torch-bearing silhouette behind them.

They say that when a lover departs, pallor spreads over the beloved woman's body. Her shoulders grow thin. Her bangles slip loose and fall away. Valluvar says separation from the lover brings such severe torments upon the woman and makes her suffer.

Very well. We may agree that Tamil in the Kural plays and caresses like this in describing the cruelty of separation.

But look—does he not create yet another wonder as an event, turn it into a scene, and place it before us?

They say pallor can spread over a woman even while her lover is beside her!

How? Why?

He bolted the door. He sat upon the bed. He ate fruit. When his eyes signalled that the milk could be drunk later, she covered the vessel while it was still hot and set it aside.

She gave rest to the large lamp that had been burning and ordered only a small oil lamp to provide light.

She upon his lap, he upon hers—they leaned upon one another in turn.

A music without strung-together words arose between them, while their lips made uncounted kisses into beats of rhythm.

And what ingratitude toward the clothes that had adorned their bodies since morning—to fling them aside like this! Perhaps they were angry because the garments had constricted their bodies.

The clothes and ornaments that had heightened their beauty lay crumpled and scattered in the corners of the room, grieving that they had suddenly been cast aside.
