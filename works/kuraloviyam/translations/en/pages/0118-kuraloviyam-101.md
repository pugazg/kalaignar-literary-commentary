hid itself. Before Valluvar's cool gaze could fall upon the young deer, a hunter carrying bow and arrow hurried in and stood before him. In agitation he asked, “A deer ran this way! Did you see it? If you did, please tell me; it escaped my arrow! Could it perhaps have entered your house?”

Valluvar replied calmly: “A deer? None came this way! Was it not over there that a deer bounded past a short while ago?”

On hearing this answer, the hunter cried, “Is that so?” and began running in the direction Valluvar had pointed.

With great humility, the student looked at Valluvar and asked:

“Great one! You have explained to me many times that one must not lie—that it is a great evil. Yet today you yourself told that hunter a lie. Is that right?”

Valluvar answered with a smile.

“A false word that produces good without harm can attain the place that we call truth. Had I told the hunter the truth, that spotted deer would have lost its life, would it not? What I said was false, but because it brought about a good result, it is to be regarded as truth.”

After giving this explanation, Valluvar took up the palm-leaf and stylus before him. A Kural took shape.

> Even falsehood may take the place of truth  
> if it yields a good that is free of fault.

Then he continued to the student:

“Young man! Do not take this explanation and make a life out of telling lies! If a person hides a truth known by his own heart and lies either to protect himself or to bring harm upon another, then the very heart of the liar will burn and consume him. It is with that in mind that I wrote this Kural too.”

> Do not lie against what your own heart knows; once you lie,  
> your own heart itself will burn you.

Delighted by Valluvar's precious ideas, the student bowed at his flower-like feet and cried, “Long live Valluvam!”
