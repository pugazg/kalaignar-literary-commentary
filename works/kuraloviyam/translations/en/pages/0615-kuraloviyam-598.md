They took ministerial office after making declarations such as these:

A good government would be established.

All the people of the country would be freed from decline and given prosperous lives.

Schemes would be framed so that the bent might stand upright and the blind gain sight.

The strong few would be prevented from tormenting the weak.

Even the words hunger and starvation would be given no place to exist.

Government would become a close companion to the farmer and the labouring worker and carry out its duties on their behalf.

The world would be shown the greatness of faultless hands and a faultless government.

The state would stand as a protective fortress safeguarding the dignity of women.

After announcing all this, that group accepted ministerial responsibility.

To put their announcements into practice, they called together government officials and held discussions.

Senior officers of mature understanding explained the proper procedures by which the work ought to be carried out.

Yet all that existed in the country was an illusion that everything they had announced had already been implemented.

In reality, hardship had become cheap and plentiful.
