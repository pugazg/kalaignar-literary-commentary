The ruler immediately convened his council of ministers to discuss action to stop the danger and protect the people.

Only the poet failed to appear.

After waiting a long time, the ruler sent a man to summon him.

"What is this, poet? Have you forgotten your responsibility as a minister, arriving after such delay?" the ruler asked.

"I fell asleep."

That was the minister-poet's reply.

"What? You fell asleep? We summon you to discuss how to act to relieve the suffering of the people under our rule, and you say you were asleep. What sort of absurdity is this?"

The poet smiled.

"Your Majesty! Do you know what Valluvar says about performing action? He says, 'Delay when an action is to be done with delay.' In other words, he tells us to sleep for actions that are to be done sleeping. That is why I slept."

Having said this, the poet sat up proudly, convinced that he had demonstrated his command of Tamil to the ruler and the others.

The minor ruler looked at him in deep disappointment.

"Sir! Are you giving this meaning as a joke, or do you truly believe that is what it means?"

"There is no joke in it at all. The Thirukkural clearly says, 'Sleep—sleep and perform what is to be performed.' That is why I was asleep. Wild animals come only at night. Night is the time when we sleep. So the Kural means, 'For things to be done during sleeping time, sleep,'" said the poet.

The ruler regretted the action he had taken out of ignorance and haste. Still, he explained:

"'Delay when an action is to be done with delay' means that matters which require patient delay should indeed be done after due delay. To take it as meaning that one should simply go to sleep is a grave mistake.

"Never mind that now, poet. Recite the whole Kural once. Let us hear it."
