“At that moment something touched my toes like a flower. Afraid that I might crush the flower, I bent down even in that darkness and tried to pick it up.

“A soft hand pressed my fingers and immediately let go. Ah! What delight! What delight—in that single instant!

“I was afraid, wondering who it might be. Yet I thought there was nothing wrong in helping a woman who had fallen. I lifted you. Yes—you! It was you.

“I said, ‘My jewel, is it you?’ Recognizing my voice, you embraced me in the darkness. Fear flew away. Two pairs of fire-warm lips pressed tightly together. I even tried to swallow your jackfruit-sweet tongue soaked in a bowl of honey.

“By then the festival organizers came carrying seven or eight torches to give the people light and help them. We moved apart and stood like innocents who knew nothing.

“In my heart I held a celebration in honour of that cyclone.

“Then I suddenly woke up. My honey-vessel! I am writing this letter to you in sorrow because it was only a dream. Do not ask how a dream can come when I have not slept! Such an imagining! Such pleasure within it!”

He finishes the letter and takes up the Kural.

“My beloved, whose beauty fills the eye and whose shoulders are like bamboo, possesses in abundance the nature in which womanhood is fulfilled.”

Thinking this, he turns through the Book of Inbam. Valluvar has composed a Kural even for that!

> The maiden whose beauty fills the eye, whose shoulders are like bamboo—  
> abundant indeed is the fullness of womanhood in her nature.

Chapter 128 — Making the Sign Understood; Kural 1272

காம்பு = bamboo

நீர்மை = nature
