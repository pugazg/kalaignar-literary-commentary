# Guardian of the Official Language Ramalinganar

Wherever the Tamil invocation beginning “Neerarum Kadaludutha” is sung, whenever I hear that song, more than savouring the merit of the song itself, I think of the esteemed Kalaignar.

Likewise, whenever I think of Valluvar Kottam, whenever I pass that way, whenever I see it, I offer a salute to the esteemed Kalaignar.

These are all enduring deeds.

We have heard that there once was a city called Kaveripoompattinam. He developed what had become a small village, and there created Poompuhar, proclaiming its ancient greatness. Whenever I think of that too, it is Kalaignar whom I remember.

Thus Kalaignar is one who has captivated my heart, one who bears abundant affection for me.

*Kuraloviyam* is a book that educated people should praise. In my view, *Kuraloviyam* is a work of poetry.

Poetry itself, I would say, comes from the element *kolavu*. Poetry is that which takes hold of the heart—we call it song; it is something that strikes within the heart and is spoken so as to strike the heart of another. I see this quality of taking hold of the heart in this book.

People speak of new poetry. They speak of traditional poetry. We see debates over which is superior.

When debates ask whether poetry is only what is composed according to grammatical tradition, with alliteration and rhyme and without violating *asai*, *seer* and *thalai*, or whether what we call new poetry—composed without treating these as binding—is truly song, I reflected on the matter.
