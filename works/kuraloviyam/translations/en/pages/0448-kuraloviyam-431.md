## Visual material

Large upper illustration of two men in conversation.

“You have been blessed with two beautiful children, in proper measure. I cannot say that wealth lies piled high in heaps, but you live in comfort without want. You may not own all the cultivated fields as far as the eye can see, yet you are the head of a worthy household—able to welcome guests and find sweetness in life.

“And your wife, too, is not one to parade herself among women who display their charms; I see that she has become for you a beautiful lamp suited to the home. With all this, why does a shade of sorrow lie across your face?

“When I met you last time, your face was flourishing with laughter. What is the reason for this change now? A pleasant family! A life of happiness! What trouble has entered your heart in the midst of all this?”

So Tanikaimalai kept asking. He had spent some years abroad and had now returned to his homeland.

Arulappar was Tanikaimalai's dearest friend. On the previous visit, with heart and face both in bloom, Arulappar had been with his friend Tanikaimalai—
