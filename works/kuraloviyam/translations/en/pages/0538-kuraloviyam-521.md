She takes the vessel containing lime and pours it into the large pot.

Then she picks up the vessel containing milk and prepares to pour it into the same pot.

At that moment the milk seems to think:

“Even if I tell her that what she poured first was not milk but lime, will she understand?

“No. Not at all.

“Then I too shall simply remain as though I were lime.”

In one house we saw milk join milk, become warm and sweet, and give pleasure to many who drank it.

In another house we saw milk mixed into lime and treated as lime itself.

Perhaps Valluvar too saw scenes like these.

Before him, in place of milk, appeared people endowed with reason.

In place of lime appeared ignorant people who merely looked like the learned.

His thought took wing.

Before the wise, the wise should use their intelligence and engage in discussion.

Before those without knowledge, even the learned should remain silent as though they too were without knowledge.

That is the child of thought his reflection produced.

Before milk, milk remained milk. That was one household.

Before lime, milk treated itself as lime and remained quiet. That was another.

Valluvar writes that before the radiant—the intelligent—one should shine as intelligent; but before the ignorant, one should take on the white-lime appearance of one without learning.

Here is the Kural:

> Before the enlightened, be enlightened; before the ignorant,  
> take on the colour of white lime.

Chapter 72 — Knowing the Assembly; Kural 714

வான் = whiteness

சுதை = lime

ஒளியார் / ஒள்ளியார் = people of knowledge

வெளியார் = the ignorant
