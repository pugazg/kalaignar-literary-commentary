“My king, I said that I would walk carrying the mountain on my head. You have forgotten that. All of you join together right now, lift this mountain and place it on my head. Then you may see whether I walk through the whole country carrying it or not!” the young man explained with great humility.

“Arrest him!” the king roared like a lion.

“From now on, do whatever you wish with me. But there is one thing! I have fulfilled the vow I made to my friends and to my wife. I had vowed that I would live as an important guest in the palace for at least a month before returning. That vow has now been fulfilled.”

With a smile blossoming on his face, the young man answered without fear.

“Why! If that was your desire, you could have done something you were capable of doing and then feasted in the palace as its reward!” the king rebuked him.

“If a man says he will do only what he is capable of, who respects such a man, Your Majesty? Only when one says, ‘I shall accomplish marvels,’ do worshipful honours and all those affectionate welcomes come!”

Hearing the young man’s reply, the king sprang up in fury.

“Oh! So that is why you deceived us?”

he thundered.

“King of kings! The very moment I entered this country, I asked whether the Thirukkural was in circulation here. When I heard that even the king did not know the Thirukkural, I entered upon my attempt with confidence. Had you read the Thirukkural, neither you nor the people of your country would have accepted my claims!”

Thus the young man replied.

“What is there in that Kural, then?”

That was the king’s question.

This was the Kural the young man recited:

> Saying is easy for anyone; difficult indeed  
> Is doing the deed exactly as one has said.

To say merely with one’s mouth, “I shall complete a deed,” is very easy for anyone. But to complete that deed exactly as one said is not easy; it is difficult—this is the meaning of this Kural.
