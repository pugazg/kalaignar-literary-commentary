A frightened bull charged at him, trying to gore him and throw him down. At that moment two young men arrived.

The doctor who had mocked their courage now fell at their feet and begged for refuge.

The young men subdued the raging bull and saved the doctor, but they mocked him in proportion to his arrogance before sending him on his way.

The doctor had become like a madman. Staggering, he made his way toward his house. Guards stood around it.

The moment he saw the soldiers, the doctor said:

“Sirs! Warriors! Forgive me! In the arrogance of ‘I’, I made enemies of everyone. Thinking myself a great intellectual, I insulted everybody. Only now do I understand that my knowledge is less than a madman's!”

He said this while folding his hands and bowing before them.

His family, who had been protected by the soldiers, also advised him:

“At least from now on, do not go seeking everyone's enmity!”

Whether the doctor actually reformed is the one thing we do not know.

> More pitiable than a madman is the ignorant man  
> who, standing alone, makes enemies of many.

Chapter 88 — Knowing the Nature of Enmity; Kural 873
