Two thousand years ago, the Thirukkural appeared, shedding light like a universal sun. Today, as I paint this pictorial commentary upon that Kural, I am repeatedly filled with wonder when I think of how many conditions woven into people's lives today had already been examined and declared by Valluvar two thousand years ago.

Let us imagine that today we go to a hospital. In one room, a friend or relative of ours lies ill. We enter the room where he is lying. A physician—the person called a "doctor" in English—is examining the patient. We stand a little distance away and watch.

When the examination is over, the doctor goes close to the nurse standing nearby and says something softly enough for only her to hear. The nurse shows the doctor several medicine bottles and tablets. Then the doctor leaves. The nurse gently lifts the patient, places a tablet in his mouth, and gives him a little water.
