Whatever the illness may be, the system of medicine rests upon these four basic elements: the patient, the physician, the medicine, and the person who remains nearby and administers it.

We see this in our own time. Yet two thousand years ago, the Kural had already explained that medicine as a system is constituted by precisely this fundamental fourfold division.

> The patient, the healer, the medicine, and the one who tends nearby—  
> these four components together constitute medicine.

Here, "the afflicted one" means the patient; "the one who cures" means the physician; "medicine" means the remedy suited to the illness; and "the one who stays nearby" means the attendant who assists. Valluvar's teaching thus defines these four basic elements together as the medical system called medicine.

Do you know how elegantly, clearly and decisively the Kural says that most illnesses can be prevented simply by not eating beyond measure?

> The body needs no medicine if one eats only after making sure  
> that what was eaten before has been digested.

Its meaning is this: if one knows that the food eaten earlier has been digested, and only then eats again in proper measure, the body of one who follows such a habit will have no need for anything called medicine.

The method Valluvar prescribes for curing disease also turns out to be a method fit for resolving the many problems that arise in society.

Agitations, violence, struggles, repression—when such conditions grow day by day, what use is there in looking only at their surface and then deciding what to do?

There is no benefit in treating a disease merely by identifying its outward symptoms.

Valluvar says: examine what the disease is; examine the causes of that disease; examine the means by which it can be relieved; and then provide the proper treatment—that is, treatment suited to the body of the patient.

Is this not a Kural that applies not only to bodily disease, but to every kind of problem that arises in human society?

> Examine the disease; examine its root cause; examine the means  
> by which it can be relieved; then act in the fitting way.
