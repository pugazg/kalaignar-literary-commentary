“A story that ought to continue, my peahen! Shall we continue it? Shall we?”

“You do not know me. I can be very dangerous. Please go away!”

“I have understood you. You are not dangerous. You are one who does not say outwardly what is in your heart.”

“You came to the place where I was bathing. That itself was mischief—and hiding behind the bush... tch... tch...”

“Why? Are you ashamed?”

“No. I am disgusted!”

“Truly?”

“Did I not look at you beside the waterfall with sparks of fire in my eyes? Did I not speak with flames? After that, how did you find the courage to come here?”

“There may be harsh words, but behind them there is no cruel hostility. There may be a look as though it burns, but behind it there is no fiery hatred. Though she appears outwardly like an outsider, these are the signs of a beloved who holds love within. Valluvar has written so. Have you not read it? It is trusting that Kural, my dear, that I leapt and came here even after the harsh words you poured upon me beside the cool waterfall. It was only because there is love as sweet as jaggery that you scolded me so fondly. Is that not so?”

She stood motionless. Her eyes hovered over him as though they would swallow him whole. “My gold!” he said, touching her. There was no resistance. He embraced her. She slipped away. Modesty launched an invasion. “I will shout and call everyone,” her ears seemed to put on mouths and say. Is that not love's first consent? The five-faced standing lamp hid its face.

> Slight harsh words without true hostility, and a look as though she were an enemy—  
> appearing like an outsider, these are the signs of one who is bound in love.

Chapter 110 — Reading the Sign; Kural 1097

`seraadhu` = not joined with hostility  
`setraar` = enemy; `uraaar` = outsider
