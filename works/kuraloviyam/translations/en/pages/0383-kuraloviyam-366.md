“Is that so? Oh! Very well! Very well! How many came yesterday to have me hear their grievances? How many women?”

“Five hundred came yesterday. More than half were women. But today there were only two hundred altogether, and very few of them were women!”

“Yesterday is the past. Today is the present. Tomorrow is the future. If things continue like this, will there finally be no one left even to listen to my advice?”

“That is how it appears to this servant.”

“We must find a way to change this situation. What can be done?”

“You are already speaking about your own greatness yourself. What other way could there be?”

“Then why is my prestige fading day by day?”

“People have begun to look not only at what you say, but also at what you actually do.”

“Fool! It seems you yourself are becoming my enemy! Go! Go! Do not stand before me! I will continue speaking about my greatness and my righteous life. If people will not fall into my net, I will not hesitate even to buy them with money.”

Saying this, the great man strode away. The bodyguard walked off in another direction biting his lip, mockery showing on his face.

The schoolteacher's son, who had watched all this while hiding near the wall of the hall, ran to his father and described everything that had happened.

The teacher said to his beloved son:

“My boy! What you saw—that is called smallness.”

“Why do you say that, father? That man listed all his greatness!”

“My son! True greatness does not desire that others bow down and praise it. The very quality of greatness is to bow to others and behave with humility. It is smallness that marvels at itself and praises itself—and it will soon see the result of that. So the form you saw is not the form of greatness; it is the symbol of smallness.”

Delighted by the explanation, the teacher's son opened the Thirukkural:

> Greatness is always humble; smallness  
> adorns itself by wondering at and praising itself.
