In the kitchen of a house there were vessels suitable for cooking and serving food.

One day the housewife, Irangai, after attending without rest to all the household work, became so exhausted that she lay down and slept right there in the kitchen without even a mat or pillow.

Before she fell asleep, one worry about her family troubled her.

Irangai's husband, Ezhilko, was a man of sweet heart and compassionate nature. He did not even like speaking in a way that would hurt anyone's feelings.

He had a younger brother named Koventhako. He was capable and energetic. Yet he and his elder brother were not of one mind.

Koventhako, who by nature did not know how to control his anger, had somehow become a man who nursed hostility toward his elder brother Ezhilko.

This condition continually tormented Irangai. How could such internal enmity arise within a family that had lived in unity?

With that worry she lay in the kitchen, thinking and thinking.

That morning, while speaking to her, Koventhako had said:

"Sister-in-law! Don't worry. There is no hostility at all between my brother and me. Look at this vessel you use. Let this vessel be my brother. I am like the lid that belongs to it."

He offered the comparison by way of explanation.

But the explanation did not satisfy her mind.

Moving among those waves of thought, she fell asleep.

A dream spread before her.

It seemed that the vessel and its lid in the kitchen stood before her and began to speak to one another.
