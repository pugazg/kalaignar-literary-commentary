However fine the colours one gives a person, good paintings can be created only if that person possesses artistic skill. Without artistic skill, no matter what colours are given, paintings will not emerge.

There are many instances of alliteration and rhyme in this book. There are many fine ideas. I regard it as a work of poetry capable of captivating the mind.

The subject matter of *Kuraloviyam* is the Thirukkural. The Thirukkural is a common book for Tamils; even calling it a Veda would be fitting. It contains teachings that can help a human being live a good life, live honestly and with good conduct, and attain a divine state. Yet that book cannot easily be read and understood. It requires commentary. Many commentaries exist. But even those commentaries require commentary in order to be read.

This *Kuraloviyam* has been written in a way that ordinary people, including those in remote villages, can easily understand the ideas of the Kural.

When I was reading *Kuraloviyam* at home, my granddaughter, who was studying in the sixth standard, saw the cover, said it was beautiful, snatched the book from me and began reading it. After reading, she said, “There are lots of stories in this too, Grandpa.” I told her, “Keep reading, dear; all 354 Kurals will easily become memorized.”

I say this because *Kuraloviyam* has been written so that everyone who reads it can understand it easily.

For bodily health, it is difficult to eat dry ginger, pepper and long pepper just as they are. But when they are made into a *lehiyam* and mixed with honey, they can be eaten easily and health can be gained.

Just as good medicinal ingredients are given in the form of a *lehiyam*, so too has this *Kuraloviyam* been fashioned.

Kalaignar has created and given us this excellent book—a book capable of benefiting all the people of the world. We must honour and make use of this book, produced through thirty years of effort, and we must thank Kalaignar who created it.
