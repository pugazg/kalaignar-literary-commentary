The man moved little by little all the way toward the very top of the tree, continuing to snap off branches and throw them down. We were delighted. We had obtained the foliage needed to decorate the festival pavilion properly, and our excitement knew no bounds. Then the man called out from the top of the tree. Startled, we turned our eyes upward. He said:

“See how high I am? Now I am going to climb even higher than this.”

We who stood below cried out on hearing him.

“Hey! Hey! You are at the top of the tree. There is nowhere higher to go. Please come down,” we pleaded. But he was drunk. Our request did not reach his ears.

“Look here! This is the tip of the tree! I am going to climb above it!”

He kept saying this. If he had actually done what he said, he would not have gone above the top of the tree; he would have gone looking for a place beneath the earth. Somehow we managed to get him down. We tied up the foliage, carried it and returned to the festival pavilion.

> One who has climbed to the tip of a branch, if he tries to go beyond it,  
> will bring his life to an end.

How perfectly this Kural fits whenever I remember that event! If a person who has climbed to the tip of a branch tries to climb beyond even that, the effort itself will become the end of his life.

The tree, the tip of the branch, and the wish to climb beyond it can be compared with every field of life: one must know one's strength and act accordingly. That is the meaning of this Kural.

Chapter 48 — Knowing One's Strength; Kural 476
