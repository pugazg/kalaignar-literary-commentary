## Visual material

Large upper illustration of a young couple embracing in a forest beside a deer.

A natural forest spilling beauty everywhere! Herds of deer bounding about! Peacocks spreading their plumes and dancing! Seeing them, wild turkeys loosen their unruly wings and leap! Rabbits seem to fly across the earth like balls of silk-cotton! The monkeys too hold their own kingdom in that forest, springing from branch to branch! Clouds on the mountain peaks melt, and by nature's wonder streams like flowing ghee surge over the rocks—beauty upon beauty! Nearby a cuckoo sits and sings! A crow performs penance to learn that voice! What it produces is an absurd raga! And a boar listens in appreciation! To watch this spectacle, a green parrot and a blue dove circle there as regular visitors!

One of the rocks there gave Paavai shelter from anyone's eyes. Yes—Paavai was her name! Hidden behind the rock, whom was she searching for; whom else would she search for?

The young poet Poonkundran was to come at the appointed time. It was because he had not arrived that she was suffering. This was their customary meeting place for love.

If the trees, plants and creepers there, the mountain rocks, the cascading waterfall, the squirrel, parrot, dove, cuckoo and peacock had been blessed with the gift of writing, they would have created epics of love surpassing even Kamban and Ilango.
