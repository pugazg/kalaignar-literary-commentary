Hearing the young man say "Greetings," Adigalar opened his eyes and asked, "Who are you?... What do you want?"

When he learned why the youth had come, he was pleased. Yet before accepting anyone into his group of students, he usually conducted some kind of test. He wanted to try one with this young man too.

"Brother! See what I am lying with beneath my head? This is the manuscript called Good Life, the fruit of my rare labour. If a man reads this whole work, the fragrance of fame will fill his life. It teaches how one may live in such a way that no one despises him. From this very manuscript I give ideas to my students every day. You too may wait; from tomorrow onward you can hear them and gain clarity."

Saying this, he rested his head again upon the bundle of palm leaves. The young man began to think. Adigalar fell into deep sleep.

"Good heavens! How many days must I stay here learning lessons? If this manuscript itself came into my hands, I could read it and set my own life in order!"

His thought sprouted wings.

He looked at Adigalar. The teacher was turning in deep sleep. His head had slipped down from the bundle of leaves.

This was the moment. If he took the manuscript and ran, could he not learn, without depending on anyone else, how to live a life beyond contempt?

He moved softly. He lifted the bundle. He was about to slip out like a cat when—

A peal of laughter!

It was Adigalar laughing. He rose while holding the youth by the hands.

"Brother, you have taken exactly what you need. You took it for a good purpose too. But one thing: do you know what I have written as the very first thought in this manuscript? Here—read it! I have written down an idea from the Thirukkural."

Adigalar himself read it aloud:

"One who wishes to live without being despised by others must guard his heart so that he does not think of deceiving another and stealing anything at all."

The young man stood with bowed head and tears in his eyes. Realizing that attempting to obtain anything by deceiving another is itself worthy of contempt, he fell at Adigalar's feet and asked forgiveness.

Afterward he was accepted as one of his students.

> If one seeks freedom from contempt, let him guard his heart  
> from thinking of stealing anything whatsoever.
