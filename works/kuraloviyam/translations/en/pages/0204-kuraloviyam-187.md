When two warriors meet, whether in a banquet hall or in an arena where the eyes themselves speak, Tamil valour has never failed to take first place. Our tales of entering the battlefield show that turning one's back and fleeing, or bearing scars upon the back, bring disgrace to the land where Tamils live, peerless upon the earth.

A battlefield. Armies clash; elephants collide; a river of blood surges and runs. Spears and swords flash in the sky and scatter warriors' laughter. Warriors lying upon the earth embrace death with delight. The men of the Maravar clan pull out the swords that have struck their chests, kiss them, cry out in valour and die.

There, on that field, an incident occurs. A warrior hurls the spear in his hand at an elephant and confronts it. Then another elephant comes. He has no weapon with which to meet it. He searches for a spear or sword, but finds none! Suddenly he remembers: a spear has pierced his own chest. He pulls it out and tries to hurl it at the elephant. He rejoices that he has found a spear.

Do you know who this warrior is?

He is the battlefield “hero” who appears in the Thirukkural.

Here is the Kural, brimming with valour:

> Having sent his hand-spear after an elephant, the warrior  
> laughs as he pulls the spear from his own body!

Chapter 78 — Martial Pride; Kural 774
