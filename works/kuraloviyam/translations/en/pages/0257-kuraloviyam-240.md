The flames had completely engulfed the choultry. The people of the village struggled, unable to put out the fire. Just then a little girl arrived carrying a begging bowl. Without even worrying that the bowl had fallen from her hand and the rice in it had scattered on the ground, she ran toward the choultry crying, “Alas! Save my father! Save him!”

A young man who saw this stopped the child and, in a single leap, rushed into the choultry. The next moment he came running out carrying a man. It was the father of the little beggar girl. Everyone angrily asked him, “How could a man lie sleeping in the middle of a blazing fire? What would have happened to you if this young man had not been here?” Rubbing his eyes, still exhausted, he replied:

“You speak as though this fire were terrible. But the poverty that torments and tortures me is crueller than this fire. Without even knowing that the building had caught fire, I managed to sleep for a little while. But when poverty attacks me, I cannot sleep at all! Can you not see that? Why did you spoil my sleep? I could have slept here in the middle of this fire; by reminding me of my poverty, you have made it impossible for me to sleep again!”

So saying, he wept. That anguish finds expression in this Kural:

> One may even sleep within fire; but amid poverty,  
> no sleep of any kind is easy.
