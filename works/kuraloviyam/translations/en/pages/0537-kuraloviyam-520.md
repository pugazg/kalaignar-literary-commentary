## Visual material

Large upper illustration of one woman pouring from a small vessel into a pot while another woman walks away.

Milk has been placed in one vessel.

Beside it, milk is brought and placed in another.

The woman must combine the milk from both vessels into one larger pot, set it on the stove, boil it, take it down, add powdered rock sugar, cool it, and serve it to Kannan, her two precious children, her mother-in-law and father-in-law.

She brings a large vessel so that the milk from the two smaller ones can be poured together and heated.

The milk in the two vessels seems to look at one another in delight.

It is the happiness of milk joining milk.

Let us leave this kitchen and observe another household.

First, milk is brought and set down in one vessel.

The mistress of the house asks her mischievous son to bring another vessel of milk.

Wanting to play a trick, the boy dissolves white lime in water and brings it in a vessel so that it looks like milk.

The woman cannot tell which is milk and which is lime.

Because both are white, she assumes that milk and lime are the same.
