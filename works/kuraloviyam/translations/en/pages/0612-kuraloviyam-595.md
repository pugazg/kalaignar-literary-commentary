The poet could not recite the full Kural as the ruler had asked.

The ruler looked at him mockingly.

"Poet, I shall say it. Listen:

'Do not delay an action that must be done without delay.'

That is, one must not postpone work that has to be done immediately. Do you understand now?"

"I know all of this. I only did this to test whether the king knew it too," the poet said, trying to save himself.

At once the minor ruler issued his order:

"Our warriors must leave immediately to drive away the wild animals and protect the people. The council will work out the necessary methods.

"But before we drive those animals away, I am driving this poet out of our town."

Once the order had been given, what business did the half-learned poet have there any longer?

Here is the Kural used to draw this imagined scene:

> Delay the work that may rightly be delayed;  
> do not delay the work that must be done at once.

Chapter 68 — The Method of Action; Kural 672
