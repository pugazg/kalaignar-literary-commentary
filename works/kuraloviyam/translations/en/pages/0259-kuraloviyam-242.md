—what else could it be? The friend from the neighbouring house comes to tease her.

“What is this, girl? You keep closing and opening your eyes. You will say that your lover is right inside your eyes—but if you close them and blink like this, will the lover inside not suffer?”

Her friend asks with a smile. Just then the lover arrives. Wanting to hear what the two women are saying, he hides behind the stacked pots and listens. Her answer to the friend's question pours a rain of honey into his heart.

“My lover never leaves the inside of my eyes. Even when I close them and blink, he does not suffer, because he is so subtle.”

Having given this answer, she recites the Kural for it:

> My lover never leaves the inside of my eyes;  
> even when I blink he is not hurt—so subtle is he.

Her friend interrupts: “If he is so subtle, why does he so often leave you without thinking of your rare virtues?”

Not wishing to let his beloved be trapped by her friend's teasing, the lover immediately steps forward. Looking at the maiden from the neighbouring house, he gives a beautiful explanation.

“I know the power of my beloved's eyes, which wage war. I could think of the rare virtues of such a spear-eyed woman only if I first forgot them. But I have never forgotten those virtues at all!”

He too recites the Kural suited to his explanation:

> I could think of her only if I forgot her; but I know no forgetting  
> the virtues of the woman whose bright eyes wage war.

Ashamed that she had spoken critically of him to his beloved, the neighbouring friend skips away from the place.
