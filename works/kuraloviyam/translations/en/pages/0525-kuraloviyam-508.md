While inspecting the camp called Parani, one of the soldiers standing in formation fell at Arulalan's feet, his eyes filled with tears.

“What is it, son? What do you want?”

asked the compassionate Arulalan.

“I have served a long time as an ordinary soldier in the army. You alone must recommend me to the king for one of the deputy-command posts. Until then I will not let go of your feet.”

He clung to him firmly.

Although several men in the Parani camp possessed both the ability and qualifications required for such responsibility, Arulalan gave this soldier his promise and returned.

A deputy-command post carried greater rank and higher pay.

Five or six deputy commanders were selected.

One of them was the soldier who had fallen at Arulalan's feet.

Declaring that he would never forget this favour, the man circled Arulalan three times, fell at his feet and paid homage.

Arulalan even gave money from his own resources so that the man could have the proper deputy commander's clothing made.

He felt some special affection for the soldier.

He even told a barber to trim and improve the broom-like moustache that made the man's face look grotesque.

“You are always deceived because you show such pity to certain people,” the Pallava king once told Arulalan. “Will this man truly remain grateful to you, to me, and to the country?”

Arulalan confidently assured him that he would.

Days passed.

Unable to win his struggle against bodily weakness, the Pallava king died a natural death.

Some in the palace invented a story that he had been murdered, intending to take revenge upon a person they disliked.

The treacherous eyes of those fox-hearted schemers did not fail to fall upon Arulalan as well.

They accused him in the Pallava court of having caused the king's murder.

The whole Pallava land was shaken to see Arulalan drawn into a fabricated murder case over a king who had actually died of illness.

“What proof is there that it was murder?” the court asked.

“Here is the evidence. It was found beside the dead king!”

answered the hard-hearted accuser.
