## Visual material

Large colour illustration of an aged, tearful warrior reclining beneath a fire motif while another armed man looks away.

He was a guardian of the land, a patron of Tamil poets, a man whose powerful shoulders could add lustre to the pages of history. The dark cloud of confusion had never entered the house of his heart even for a moment. Though he had seen many battlefields, he possessed a heart that did not long to take revenge upon enemy lands. The sweep of enemy swords had drawn lines of valour upon his chest. That lion of a warrior clan, who had sent thousands of fierce enemies away with death as their farewell and had worn garlands of victory, now lay upon a bed reading the invitation sent from death's chamber. Perhaps Yama himself, afraid to come in person because even his messenger trembled, had sent the invitation letter to this mighty warrior! Those who had surrounded his capital with fine chariots, horses and vast armies had, by the skill of his warfare, been gathered and cast away the next morning like flowers that had smiled upon a bed and then withered.

Nature too must write an ending for him. Otherwise the human story would never reach its close. In the never-ending serial of human society, does not each individual human being have a story of his own? Death is now turning the pages to finish that story. The moment has come for the stylus to be taken up.
