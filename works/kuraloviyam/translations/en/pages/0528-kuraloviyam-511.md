The crowned ruler looked at him and asked:

“What poem have you composed about my government?”

“About your government? Then listen. Do you know what your rule is like?”

the disguised poet replied.

The king eagerly asked:

“What is it like?”

The man recited:

> Kannaa  
> Pinnaa  
> Mannaa  
> Thennaa

and stopped.

The king sprang at him in fury.

He drew his sword and raised it toward the disguised man's head.

At once the court poet leapt between them and restrained the king.

It was true that the young man had used those words to expose the condition of the country.

The court poet himself understood that.

But he was desperate to save the poor man wearing the disguise.

“Your Majesty! A beautiful poem! A wonderful poem! No better verse could have been chosen to praise you and win a reward!”

said the court poet.

“Is that so?”

The king stared in astonishment.

“Yes. ‘Kannaa’ means Karna, who was born through the cheek of Queen Kunti. ‘Pinnaa’ means the righteous Dharman, younger-born after that Karna. Then ‘Mannaa’ means O king! ‘Thennaa’ means ruler of the southern land!”

Hearing this explanation, the gullible king embraced the disguised poet, placed a pearl garland around his neck, gave him a purse of gold and sent him away in delight.

Later, when the king shared the happy news with his queen, the truth came out.

The queen was learned in literature.

She explained that the court poet had very cleverly saved the poor man.

The king flared up again.

The queen stopped him, pulled him down upon the soft bed and said:

“My king, it cannot be denied that because of your conduct the country has no peace.

“Forgive me. Think calmly for a moment.

“Even if critics use harsh words—words sharp enough to pierce the ear—only if a ruler possesses the quality of bearing them will the people remain beneath the shade of his umbrella.

“Otherwise the country itself will slip from our hands.”

The king remained silent.

In a sweet voice the queen sang a Thirukkural.

Not only her voice—the Kural too captivated him.

> The world remains beneath the umbrella of the king  
> who has the character to endure words bitter to the ear.

Chapter 39 — The Majesty of Kingship; Kural 389

கவிகை = umbrella

கைப்ப = bitter, hateful to hear
