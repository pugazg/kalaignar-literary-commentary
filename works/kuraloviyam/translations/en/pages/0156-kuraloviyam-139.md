## Visual material

Large colour illustration above the prose showing a knife-brandishing man beside a woman, surrounded by raised hands in a crowd.

A man fell face down and broke his nose. Everyone mocked him. Unable to bear their ridicule, he devised a scheme. One morning he looked up at the sky, raised his hands in worship and cried out with melting emotion, “Ah! God!”

The villagers gathered. “What? What is it?” they asked. “There—God can be seen in the sky,” said the noseless man.

“We cannot see him!” said the villagers.

“You can see him if you cut off your nose,” he said.

No one came forward.

Then four or five people cut off their noses and looked at the sky. No God appeared; they saw only dark clouds. Those four or five were overcome with shame. They feared that the others would mock them and wound them with ridicule. So, without revealing that they too had been deceived, they jumped about crying, “Yes! There is God! Ah! What a rare sight! Wonderful! Wonderful! What pride is ours in having seen the Lord! Pride indeed!”

One by one, everyone who wished to see the Lord mutilated his nose in the same way, until at last all the people of that village had become noseless.
