★ A fort's excellence includes having no condition in which anything essential is lacking, and containing warriors who spring up like lions the moment the war-drum sounds.

★ A fort is one of such excellence that the enemy cannot capture it—whether by siege, direct assault, or by weaving a net of intrigue.

★ Its greatness lies not only in being strong enough to withstand an enemy besieging its battlements, but in seizing those besieging forces and planting the flag of victory.

★ A fort gains distinction by devising methods of war that destroy enemies who break inside and fight within it.

In the chapter called The Fort, Valluvar explains the excellences and defining qualities of a fort through nine Kurals.

What, then, does he stress through the tenth?

In those nine verses he gives luminous ideas about how a fort ought to be—how it should serve as a defensive camp, a stronghold of soldiers, a well-supplied storehouse, and a treasury of strength.

Yet even if it possesses all this excellence and abundance, courage and valour that crush the enemy's pride, and every other greatness, if the fort is operated by people without ability, he concludes that it becomes useless.

Here is that tenth Kural:

> However great a fort may be in every excellence,  
> it is no fort in the hands of those without skill in action.
