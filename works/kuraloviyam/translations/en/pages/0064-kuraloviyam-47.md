It could almost be said that there was never a time when the neighbouring enemy kingdoms did not wage war in an attempt to conquer Thirunadu. Thirunadu was always seen in battle-readiness, prepared to face the assaults of its enemies at any moment. It never entertained thoughts of destroying the border fortifications of other countries or even seizing one of the small rivers belonging to them. Even so, unavoidable circumstances repeatedly arose in which Thirunadu had to withstand enemy attacks.

The crops of Thirunadu would be destroyed; its carefully laid canals damaged; its frontier forts broken; many warriors would have to be sacrificed. Though Thirunadu suffered because wars flared up again and again, even after all those invasions the country remained prosperous. No hungry person could be found there. Thirunadu continued to be a delightful grove where smiling women and broad-shouldered young men lived joyfully.

Children received education enough to fill their minds with knowledge. Farmers carried out their work with delight. Merchants, those trained in the arts, sculptors, painters—
