In that war he had lost one of his hands. He had also spent several years in the enemy's prison. This elder, who had undergone such severe sacrifice for his own country's victory, grew disgusted with the prince's conduct and, forgetting that he had retired, worked tirelessly once again.

Angered by this, the prince spoke in the public assembly about the elder in the most degrading manner. The citizens did not like the prince's conduct or his words in the least. Showing their disgust, they began to leave the assembly. Unable to bear that humiliation, the prince ordered soldiers to attack the citizens. Their bones were broken under the blows; some even lost their lives.

News of this reached the elder. Though he had been a dwelling place of patience, he rose in fury. He walked towards the place where the citizens were being brutalised. He reached the public assembly where blood was flowing like a flood. Seeing people running and crying, “Alas! Alas!”, he said:

“You have only one life, do you not? And it can leave only once. Why, before that, are you dying like this in fear? Stand against injustice! Come. I will lead you who are being beaten. From now on, let every blow that falls, fall upon me.”

The people who were being beaten straightened themselves. Those who had fled returned and formed ranks behind him. A sea of people took shape. Its turbulence was not visible outwardly; their eyes showed the anguish and storm surging within their hearts.

Led by the elder, that great procession moved towards where the prince stood. Soldiers with raised hands stood aside in astonishment. The elder came very close before the prince. His face was red like the rising sun. So too were the faces of the people behind him.

Realising that the situation had become dangerous, the prince began to flee. He could not stand before the firmness of the elder's heart. His army became powerless; his fortress did not help him; allied kingdoms did not come to his aid; even the immense wealth in his treasury could not save him. He exiled himself from his own country.

This shows that if distinguished and worthy great people are provoked to anger, even those who possess vast supports such as fortress, army and wealth cannot escape their wrath.

> Though one possess supports great beyond measure, one will not survive  
> if the distinguished and worthy are moved to anger.

Chapter 90 — Not Offending the Great; Kural 900
