Seeing the richness of those crops made the farmer's cow's mouth water. Somehow, it decided, it had to enter those fields and eat the tender crops to its heart's content. But it also feared that if the farmer saw it grazing, it would receive a severe beating. The cow began thinking of what trick it might use.

One evening, as the day was fading, the farmer finished his work at home and came toward the field to set right the channel-bunds. His two eyes delighted in the unripe green crop swaying in the breeze. Walking along the bund, he suddenly cried, “Ayyo!” He did not remain there. He ran from the field toward the street. “Ayyo! Ayyo! A tiger is destroying my whole field. A tiger is grazing on the crops!” he shouted. The villagers ran and gathered. “What sort of wonder is this? Even if a tiger were hungry, would it eat grass?” asked an old man. “There! Isn't it eating my crop?” the farmer wept. The villagers ran toward the fields. It was true: something looking like a tiger was grazing on the crop. Seeing the crowd approach, the tiger stood upright as though threatening them. Trembling with fear, they decided, “All right, all right! Don't do anything to it. Let it graze until it has had enough and leave. Can its stomach hold an entire field?” Yet there were fearless men in the crowd too. They hurled the spears and lances in their hands toward the tiger. Terrified, the tiger ran into the field, became trapped in the mud and fell. The fearless men did not stop. They went closer and looked. Lying in the mud was the farmer's own cow. Beside it lay a tiger skin!

Everyone marvelled at the cow's trick. Only the farmer grew furious and thrashed the cow soundly.

“Everyone would think, ‘A tiger does not eat crops,’ and be deceived. Even if someone saw it, they would fear coming near a tiger. That is why this cow devised such a grand plot!” said one of the village elders. Another elder explained, “Yes! Just as some people who cannot control their minds adopt the appearance of ascetics when they commit wrongdoing so that no one dares come near and stop them, this cow wrapped itself in a tiger skin and grazed upon the crop.”

Though it is an imaginative story, is not the tale of the cow wearing a tiger skin an entirely new story? This Kural contains that idea:

> The imposing guise of one who lacks inner strength  
> is like a cow grazing while wrapped in a tiger's skin.

Chapter 28 — False Conduct; Kural 273
