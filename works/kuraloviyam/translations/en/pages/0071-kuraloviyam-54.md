She is said to have worn soft anicham flowers without removing their stems. Unable to bear even the weight of those stems, her slender waist suffered.

The silver fish of the sky, seeing both the moon and the face of that shapely beauty, are said to have wandered in confusion asking, “Which is the moon? Which is she?” When the feather of a swan touches her feet, it is said to feel like the prick of a nerunji fruit—such are her rose-soft feet of silk.

> The anicham flower and even the swan's feather  
> are nerunji fruit to a woman's feet.

Chapter 112 — Praising Her Beauty; Kurals 1114, 1120

A young man wants to give her, who stands as the very definition of beauty, a home inside his own eyes. But to make room for the queen of beauty, must he not first evict the present occupant of that house of sight? So he goes to the one living “on rent” inside the eye and pleads, “Please vacate the house.”

Hear it in Valluvar's manner:

“Little image dwelling in the dark pupil of my eye! There is no room in my eye for this beautiful woman whom I love—so you must leave!”

He asks the image that gives him sight to go, so that he may make room in his eye for the maiden who gives a string of pearls. Here is the Kural:

> O little image in the dark pupil, depart;  
> there is no room for the fair-browed one I love.

Chapter 113 — Declaring Love's Excellence; Kural 1123
