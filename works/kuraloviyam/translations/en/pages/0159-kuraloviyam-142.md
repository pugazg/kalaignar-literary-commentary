—and finished feeding him. Losing himself, Maavengko leaned upon Maangani's lap. As though the sorrow of their being childless had been remedied, she kissed the crown of his head.

Leaving the place where they had eaten, the two walked as one toward the bedchamber, to mingle as melody mingles with the yaazh.

Maavengko took a cluster of mullai flowers and placed it upon Maangani's hair.

She, in turn, showed him a whole garden of mullai blossoms through her smile.

“Do we need a lamp?” he asked.

“We do not need a lamp, but I do need an explanation. What is the reason for that fallen look on your face?” the maiden asked playfully.

“My face is an alli flower! When you are the moon beside it, why should it ever wither?” Maavengko said with a smile.

There was no lamp-light in that room! Nor was there the sound of speech! In the sky, moon and cloud embraced. The young breeze gathered the flower garden into its arms. Drops of dew upon the buds danced to the rhythm of the gentle wind.

“My dear!”

“Maangani!”

Here, in the bedchamber, silent pictures began to speak.

“I will ask for one thing. Will you give it to me, Maangani?”

“Was everything I gave you until the edge of dawn not sweet enough, my dear?”

“Now I am asking you for something bitter to you.”

Maangani's lips did not move. Like a drop of dew upon arugam grass, tears appeared along her eyelids.

“For the sake of trade, I will go abroad just this once and return, my love! Give me your consent without hesitation!”

“If you will not leave me and go anywhere, tell me that. If not—if you must go—then take your leave now from those who will still be alive when you return. As for me, once you have left me, I will not be alive when you come back.”

After such an answer, could his journey abroad possibly have taken place?

What a beautiful Kural this is for expressing the inability to bear separation!

> If you can stay, tell me so; otherwise speak of your difficult return  
> to those who will still be alive.

Chapter 116 — Inability to Bear Separation; Kural 1151
