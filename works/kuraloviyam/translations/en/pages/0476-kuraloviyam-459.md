## Visual material

Large upper illustration of a young man and woman facing one another.

He was the son of a poet wasting away in poverty, a young man just past twenty.

The wealth of imagination overflowed in him, but at home they had to wander about borrowing money merely to light the stove for a single meal.

The young man was handsome too.

Does the wild river called love pause to measure the boundary between poverty and prosperity before it swells and rushes forward?

The young poet was caught in that flood.

She was springtime itself in a prosperous household.

She became a devotee of the young poet's colourful Tamil.

As usual, love blossomed without either family knowing, and soon began to spread its fragrance.

As the days passed, a tremor arose in the poet's young heart.

“You are a star shining in the sky. I am a piece of charcoal lying on the ground. We may be suited to one another in love and marriage—but who will agree that such a match is fitting? Above all, will your parents, who live immersed in great wealth, accept it?”

He asked her to forget him.

She started in shock.

She stared at him.

“Are these words really coming from you? You asked me in poetry, ‘Are these eyes a net that scoops up fish—or fish that leap within the net?’ You turned my eyelids into lips and kissed them. How did you find the heart now to soak those very eyes in tears?”

She wept.

He sat speechless.
