"...and recognize your noble qualities. My soldiers and I left the king of Nilaa Naadu alone in the field and fled back to our Malainaadu.

"Forgive me. Regard my jeweled crown as lying at your feet and show me grace."

The letter of the Malainaadu ruler was read aloud in court.

At once the court poet rose with a mocking smile.

The emperor looked at him.

The poet spoke:

"Emperor! The Malainaadu ruler who sent this letter is not merely a man who cares nothing for honour. He rules having forgotten every virtue required of government.

"If, forgetting the kings who stood beside you, you pardon the Malainaadu ruler, people will think you are doing something contrary to virtue.

"That king has always made it his occupation to join whichever side is winning and worship at its feet.

"He has no connection with courage. That is why, unlike the Nilaa Naadu king, he lacked the resolve to die in battle. He abandoned even that king and fled.

"To accept him would not be righteous."

Ignoring the poet's counsel, the emperor ordered his minister to send a reply saying that the Malainaadu ruler was forgiven and accepted.

The poet could not endure it.

Burning with anger, he thundered at the emperor:

"Emperor! I do not regard either you or the minor ruler who has written begging your pardon as kings at all.

"Do you know why?

"You have departed from the moral discipline proper to government and carry out what is not virtuous.

"Treating the rulers who stood beside you as insignificant, you embrace a man whose very occupation is betrayal.

"This is not virtue.

"Only one whose honour never diminishes in courage is a king. By that measure the Malainaadu ruler does not qualify in the least.

"Therefore, according to Valluvar's thought, neither of you are kings!"

Throwing these words like sparks, the poet left the royal hall.

"What was the Valluvar principle the poet referred to?" the emperor wondered, turning through the Thirukkural.

His eyes fell upon a Kural whose meaning was:

"The best ruler is one who does not depart from the path of virtue proper to government, removes what is not virtuous, and possesses honour that does not fail in courage."

The emperor sat motionless upon his throne.

Here is that Kural:

> A true ruler departs not from virtue, removes what is not virtuous,  
> and possesses honour that does not fail in courage.

Chapter 39 — The Majesty of Kingship; Kural 384
