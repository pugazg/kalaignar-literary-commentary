“Your black hair lies curled in wave upon wave! Your budding eyebrows shine with beauty! Your long nose has driven my heart into a frenzy of feeling! Your mango-like body seems created by the divine craftsman solely to enchant mankind. O creeper of delight! Do you desire me as I desire you?”

“Certainly! You are the Manmatha suited to this Rathi, the Arjuna suited to this Subhadra.”

“Is that so? Today I have received the fruit of being born! My jewel, I have given myself entirely to you.”

“Your heart overflows with love!”

“And yours does too, does it not?”

“Would I have come with you without love? But... but...!”

“What? What are you saying? Why ‘but’?”

“How much wealth will you give me to stay with you tonight? It is not enough merely to promise. You must give it first before touching me.”

“Shame! Are you a courtesan? Good thing! Though I fell into the sweep of your eyes, I have escaped before stumbling into the next disaster.”

Saying this, he hurried away. Without the least concern, she turned elsewhere to cast her net of glances.

Struck by disappointment, he walked beside a grove. One man slapped him on the back saying, “Brother! Are you well?” Another stood before him laughing and saying, “I'm coming, my friend—no shortage here!” Both were known to him: deceitful thieves who had come to seize what he carried. To save himself and his possessions, he drew the knife tucked at his waist and sprang at them. The two thieves panicked and fled.

He sighed in relief, thinking how both he and his property had escaped the courtesan and the thieves.

He went home. A friend sat on the veranda.

“When did you come?” he asked.
