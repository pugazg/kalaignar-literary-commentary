He went to the king and begged for a ministerial post, asking that at least his intellectual ability be put to use.

The king answered with silence.

Some time later the king died, and his younger brother, Senthamizhvel, came to power.

Veerarakkiyar went to him too and begged for office. Taking pity on the old man, the young king Vel appointed him head of the ministers' advisory council.

Using that post, Veerarakkiyar collected as many bribes as he wished and plunged his family into wealth and luxury.

Then, for some reason, the ruler of the neighbouring country suddenly invaded. With the help of officials close to Vel, he seized Vel's kingdom.

Until then Veerarakkiyar had sung Vel's praises and enriched himself through office. Now he began proclaiming the greatness of the neighbouring ruler.

Vel, defeated through the treachery of those in the war, began preparing an army to recover his country.

Veerarakkiyar piled accusation upon accusation against the very Vel who had given him office. By doing so he won the affection of the enemy ruler and clung to the same position.

Vel had lost his country and was gathering forces from camps set up in different regions. Some of his commanders went to him and reported Veerarakkiyar's conduct.

“People who make no plan that says, ‘This is how one ought to live,’ but instead decide that they may live by any means, can obtain wealth that does not last. But they can never obtain fame that endures.”

Vel said this, then looked at his commanders and asked:

“Do you know what Valluvar has said about such people?”

He explained it:

“They carry their bodies through a life without fame; even the land that bears such a body will lose its rich yield.”

Here is that Kural:

> The rich yield of blameless land will diminish  
> when it bears a body without fame.

Chapter 24 — Fame; Kural 239
