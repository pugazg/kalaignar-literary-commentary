—before long, a skin would form upon the milk in the bowl, while the clothes upon them would disappear. The lamp itself, ashamed, would tightly cover its face.

Everything would be delight! Only afterward would they drink the milk. That was their habit. But today she gave him the bowl of milk, spread a mat on the floor and lay down there.

She expected her husband to come near. He would say, “My love! Are you angry?” He would gather her up in his arms. “From now on, the moment work ends I will run to see you,” he would say. His lips would press and play from the crown of her head over her whole body with the sound of kisses. Then they would throw away their quarrel and their bodies would entwine.

Painting such an imagined epic, she rolled on the mat with eyes half closed, half open. He, meanwhile, drank the milk, followed it with a belch, and yawned on the cot without caring about her, lying below and yearning.

“What is this great anger? Are you going to come and lie down properly or not?”

That was the method he used that day to resolve her sulking.

She did not speak. He fell asleep. Her eyes, wandering sleepless through the whole night, were red in the morning. Tears kept rolling down her cheeks.

Angry that she had not come even when he called, he left for work without even eating breakfast.

In the evening he returned home. She lay groaning with a high fever. He ran and brought a doctor. The doctor wanted to know the details. Because the physician was a close friend, the husband described what had happened the previous night. The doctor too had studied and understood the Kural.

“If you fail to understand and end the sulking of one who has quarrelled in love, and fail to show affection, it is like cutting the very root of a creeper already withered.

Do you know Valluvar has written so? So, my friend, the medicine for this illness is in your hands. Give it, and the fever will stop...”

Saying this, the doctor left.

> Failing to understand and end the sulking of one already pining  
> is like cutting the root of a withered creeper.
