## Visual material

Large colour battlefield illustration of a commander with a spear piercing his torso.

In the sea of blood made by spear and sword, warriors float on with terrifying breaths. Seeing broken pieces of bone and severed strands of sinew journeying along, kissing the heads of those warriors, he does not sing Mukhari, nor does he turn his back. Instead he sounds the war-drum—“Forward! Forward!”—filling the hearts of the remaining warriors with courage. Never, even by mistake, forgetting the battlefield primer which says that it is unbecoming of warriors to count the seconds at the very crest of death, wondering, “From which side will a spear fly at what moment? When will an enemy arrow strike?”, he leads his army with the strength of mind that says, “Death is a bouquet of flowers given to the great warrior; sweeter than a maiden's lips.” He alone is the great warrior, the commander of the Maravar host—the man who has read the special invocation of that camp-epic.

Under his command stands the fourfold army in formation.
