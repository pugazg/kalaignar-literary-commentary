## Visual material

Large colour forest illustration showing a Kumanan-like ruler and a bearded poet conversing among trees.

King Kumanan, through the scheming of his younger brother, was forced to live in the forest. Thinking that even leaving his elder brother alive in the forest—deprived of country and city and reduced to hardship—would be a danger to his own rule, the younger brother announced that a purse of gold would be given to whoever brought him his brother's head.

The news reached Kumanan too.

The king who had once sat in his palace with ornamented chariots, horses and a vast army was now made to wander in the wilderness; and even then suffering did not leave him. It began to pursue him in the form of death.

Even if death is something warriors on the battlefield may accept with a smile, Thiruvalluvar did not fail to compare the suffering brought by death with other sufferings.

That is why he said, “There is nothing more painful than death.” In other words, he stresses that nothing is more painful than dying.
