## Visual material

Large upper illustration of a man eating while a woman serves him, with another man leaving through the doorway.

Anbumani and Inbamani were friends from early childhood. Because there was no school in the village where they were born, every day they would walk four miles, study in a neighbouring village and return.

Inbamani was the only son of a wealthy father. Anbumani too was the only child in his home, but he had been born into a middle-class family.

Each day, when school ended and they returned to their village, it was the friends' habit to go to the small eatery on the way and eat murukku or appam. Yet not even for a single day did Inbamani give the shopkeeper the money for what they ate. Every day Anbumani paid for Inbamani's food as well as his own. Inbamani was that miserly.

Their school days and the days of playful youth flew by. In search of work, Anbumani went toward the capital. Inbamani, using the property his father had accumulated, moved to the country's port city to run a large business.

One day, after loading all the goods produced in his factory onto ships bound abroad and sending them off, Inbamani was returning home happily when suddenly, in the market street, he met his childhood friend Anbumani—
