“Today, anyone who debates against him in the assembly will turn his back and flee! There is no doubt.”

The learned man casts a smile at those who praise him and walks away.

Now let us return to the first scene.

A hand had lifted that sharp battle sword. The man to whom that hand belonged went toward the battlefield carrying it.

The enemy looked at him and laughed. He was not ashamed. He swung the sword.

Then an elephant casually knocked the sword away with its trunk and stood staring at him.

The man trembled at the sight, cried “Alas!” and ran.

“Coward! Coward!”

the warriors on the battlefield shouted, laughing.

Now let us return to the second scene.

The man who had marched noisily into the assembly carrying bundles of palm-leaf manuscripts looked once at those who were to debate him.

“Ah! He has come carrying all the works of Tamil literature. Victory will be ours today!”

his supporters said happily.

He held the great Tamil books in his hands.

But the opposing side carried their ideas within themselves. They spoke, citing evidence from those very books.

Suddenly there was uproar.

“The palm-leaf man is missing! He has run away from the assembly!”

The hall shook with laughter.

Our inward eyes keep turning these two scenes before us: the man who went to face the enemy with a glittering sword, and the man who went to face an assembly with many books.

Without realizing it, we laugh too.

Valluvar also looks at the man who fears the assembly and laughs. He wonders whom to compare him with.

The coward who fears an enemy comes to mind, and Valluvar shapes the comparison into a Kural.

This is it:

> A bright sword in a coward's hand amid enemies—  
> such is the learning of one who fears an assembly.

Chapter 73 — Fearlessness in the Assembly; Kural 727
