We frequently use the word “folly,” or the word “fool.” When we use some words, we do not stop to investigate what their proper meaning actually is. Because a word has remained in common use for a long time, we do not develop the curiosity to learn its meaning. When we are angry with someone, we say, “Let that fool lie there!” Yet how clear a meaning Valluvar gives to the words “fool” and “folly” through the Kurals he has fashioned!

He points out that “folly” is the name for taking up what is harmful to oneself and abandoning what is beneficial. To put it briefly: the state of not knowing good from bad! That does not mean one can simply call a fool a person of good heart. Valluvar's view is also that fools have a special liking for doing what lies outside the bounds of proper conduct. Here the poet of unfailing speech places before us a beautiful comparison about a fool.

A man in the street has gone mad and is babbling. Children surround him in groups and mock him. He rants, “I am the king of this entire town! Where is my royal sceptre? Call the minister!
