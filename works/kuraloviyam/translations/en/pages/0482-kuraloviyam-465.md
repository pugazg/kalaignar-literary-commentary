Crystal-clear water pours from the top of a rock. Men and women bathe and rejoice beneath the fall.

The cold waterfall is a feast for the eyes, like molten silver being poured.

When the water splashes across golden bodies, the young women laugh like flowers in their swelling joy.

Leaving the rock and striking the ground, the clear water runs like glass.

Then it leaps into clay soil.

The water that had appeared like crystal and glass loses its former nature and takes on the colour of the black soil it has entered.

Valluvar expresses this beautifully:

“According to the nature of the land it joins, water changes and takes on the character of that land. In the same way, the understanding of people takes on the nature of the company they join.”

Here is the Kural:

> Water changes according to the nature of the soil it joins;  
> so does human understanding take the nature of one's company.

Chapter 46 — Avoiding Mean Company; Kural 452
