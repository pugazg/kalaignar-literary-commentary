## Visual material

Large upper illustration of three young women in a rural setting.

Along a narrow field-bund path came the maiden with a waist that seemed ready to snap. In the fields the young seedlings shivered in the breeze, like the rippling waves in a pond when kayal fish leap and play. Her feet moved in a gentle dance over patches of green grass. Silver anklets, looking like two twisted ropes, moved upon her legs, stained with mud. Her garment revealed beauty up to the knee; her half-sari fluttered in the wind, barely touching her shoulder. She was a sculpture carved for that little village.

From a distance, men and women alike drank in her beauty. The man who had drawn near, pressed heart to heart with her, and made the field and threshing floor into a soft bed, becoming hers alone, was Alagan. Not only in name but in appearance too he possessed sculpted beauty, and that flower-creeper whose bud had opened had made him her own.

Now she comes alone, without him. Even the anril birds may sometimes be separated, but until now the great sinner called separation had never shown its head between these two.
