## Visual material

Large colour illustration above the prose: a reader with an open book radiating light, surrounded by books and scroll-like forms.

When praising a person’s ability, we say, “There is no one in the whole world who can defeat him.” We know only as far as our own town. We do not know whether such another capable person exists somewhere in the world or not! Even so, when praising this man, we set the whole world against him and speak in deliberate exaggeration.

A man is caught in a river flood and escapes. Looking at him, we say, “From now on, even if you are rolled down from the Himalayas, you will not die!” We praise him in that way because he survived being submerged in the river; but how do we know that he would not die if he were rolled down from a mountain? Even so, we exaggerate the strength of his life.

When we exaggerate in this way, we sometimes make effective use of things that suit our point even though we ourselves do not believe in them.

“For the help he has done you, even if you take seven births you will never finish repaying him with gratitude!”

When we speak like this, we are not accepting seven births! That is, we do not accept rebirth itself! Yet we still make the exaggerated statement.

It is clear that the fear of rebirth was shown in order to prevent human beings from engaging in evil deeds.

This condition existed in Valluvar’s time as well. That is why, when he came to speak of the excellence of education, he praised its necessity beyond ordinary limits.

“The education learned in one birth is of such a nature that it helps a person not only in that birth but through seven births.”

“Brother! The learning you are studying will be useful to you for seven births!”
