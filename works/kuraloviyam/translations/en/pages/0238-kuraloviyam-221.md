“Moon, why have you come here? What do you want?” the poet asked.

“My beloved poet! While I am here, while honey rests upon my lips, is it right that you should love her?” the moon-woman complained.

The poet looked at the face of the maiden sleeping in his lap. Then he looked at the moon-woman's face.

“Moon! You are indeed a treasury of great beauty. I do not deny it. But the reason I love her more than you is that the blemish upon your face is not upon hers!

> Has a woman's face any blemish  
> like the scar upon the bright, full moon?

Have you forgotten what Valluvar wrote? There is no use in grieving because I love this living picture whose face bears not even that blemish,” the poet replied in a sweet voice.

The moon-maiden folded her hands in supplication, her eyes filling with tears, and pleaded with him.

“My beloved poet! Then do I have no right to love you?”

The poet smiled gently and answered.

“If you can shine like the face of this beauty sleeping with her head in my lap, then you too will gain the worthiness to claim a right to my love, just as she does. Do you not know that Valluvar has painted even this as a Kural?

Listen; I shall tell you that too:

> O moon, if you can shine like a woman's face,  
> then live on, worthy of love.

A blemished face has no right to win the poet's love, so unequal is it to the radiance of his beloved's face!” Hearing this explanation, the moon-maiden lowered her head in shame, rose and, without even taking leave of him, flew toward the sky. Hiding behind a curtain of dark cloud, she shed tears.

Raindrops fell upon the face of the maiden sleeping on her lover's lap. She awoke. She awoke! The two left the seashore and walked quickly toward home. Until they reached their house, the sky-maiden moon did not show her face outside again.
