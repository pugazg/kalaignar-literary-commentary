Valluvar utterly rejects the violence of killing.

He holds firmly that no living being should be killed.

He does not stop with the explanation that sentencing vicious criminals to death can be the ruler's duty.

The struggle over whether the death penalty is necessary seems already to have risen in his mind.

That is why, while speaking of a king's duty, he also offers a warning through another Kural to prevent killings from happening at all.

A man is sunk in poverty and wasting away.

It is impossible to say how many diseases have laid siege to his body and tormented him.

His body has become a warehouse of illness.

Relatives avoid even looking at him.

He lies like a worm writhing in an evil life.

Valluvar sees him.

He seems to think:

“By showing this man to the world, perhaps I can stop people from ever entering the trade of killing.”

He announces:

“People of the world, listen to what I say!

“Do you know who this man is—this person wandering in deprivation as a nest of terrible diseases?

“He is one who once committed many murders.

“This is the punishment he now suffers for those cruel acts.”

We do not know whether that man actually killed anyone.

Then why does Valluvar say so?

Do all those who kill and commit cruelty end up as destitute people afflicted by unbearable disease?

Do not many instead live joyfully like Kubera himself?

But the warning Valluvar gives here is advice arising from a good heart—an attempt to deter people from terrible acts such as murder.

Rather than saying:

“If you engage in killing, poverty will strike and diseases will surround you,”

he says, with great skill:

“Those who suffer in poverty and disease are said by the wise to be people who once killed and removed lives from bodies.”

In this way he seeks to prevent others even from thinking of cruel acts like killing.

Here is that Kural:

> Those who live in diseased bodies, crushed by poverty,  
> are said to be those who once removed life from bodies.

Chapter 33 — Non-Killing; Kural 330

செயிர் = disease

செல்லா = worn down by poverty
