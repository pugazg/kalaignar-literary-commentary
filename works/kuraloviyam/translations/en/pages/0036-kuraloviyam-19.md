By calling him “Vaal-arivan,” Valluvar said that he is one who stands as the very form of pure knowledge, and pointed out that learning itself is of no use if one does not worship at his sacred feet.

“Venduthal vendaamai ilaan”—that is, he indicated that those who seek the shade of one who is without likes and dislikes will have no suffering.

“Tanakku uvamai illaathaan”

“Aravaazhi anthanan”

In all these ways, he defined the qualities associated with what is called God.

Valluvar thought that a person should live in this world possessing all these excellences attributed to the divine.

That is why he announced one idea to the world in unmistakable terms.

“So many rare virtues are said to belong to the divine! One who lives in this world standing in the path of virtue as life ought to be lived will be honoured as equal to the deity said to dwell in heaven.”

Here is the Kural for that:

> One who lives in this world as life ought to be lived  
> will be placed among the gods who are said to dwell in heaven.

Chapter 5, Domestic Life; Kural: 50

(Note: the explanation of the chapter “Praise of God” is also connected here.)
