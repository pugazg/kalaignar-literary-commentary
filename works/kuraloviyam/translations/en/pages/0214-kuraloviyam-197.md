He would surely come in a little while; he would not let her wither any longer; he would gather her into his arms and rain kisses like a monsoon. With that thought, she stroked the young mango tree against which she leaned with her golden hand. Seeing it, the other trees thrilled. They cast jealous looks at that young mango tree. In longing that the sandalwood-statue maiden had not leaned against them, they seemed to sigh deeply. Her eyes, meanwhile, were wild with watching the path by which he would come.

There! There! In the dim light, the figure of that man was moving toward her. The pure white clothes he usually wore! A shirt covering him down to the wrists! A handsome turban upon his head! It was for the arrival of that figure that the young parrot-like maiden had yearned and suffered. The moment she knew he was coming, her swelling heart grew full; when he came near, it leapt and danced.

“Atthaan!” she cried. How much anguish of separation lay in that single sound!

Without saying anything, he came and stood before her. “Why so late, Atthaan?” she asked; before an answer could come, she sprang upon him, embraced him, and fell to the ground together with him.

“Oh! Such haste?” he asked, bursting into laughter.

Poonguzhali was startled. The reason was that the voice speaking to her was not a man's voice, but a woman's!

And it was the voice of her dear friend Iniyaval. Poonguzhali pushed her away and stood up. Iniyaval removed the man's clothing she had been wearing and stood before her. Though Iniyaval was her closest friend, Poonguzhali lowered her head in shame at having revealed her longing and passion so openly before her. Iniyaval asked forgiveness for deceiving Poonguzhali. Then the two spoke freely with one another.

Poonguzhali said to Iniyaval:

“What am I to do? Even if he forgets me, I cannot forget him. Joined to this unworthy foolish heart of mine, I too have forgotten shame.”

Iniyaval replied:

“Yes, yes! Seeing how, the moment you saw him, you threw yourself into an embrace and began composing an epic of love, I understood at once that you had indeed forgotten the fine quality called modesty,” she teased.

> I forgot modesty too, bound to my unworthy foolish heart  
> that cannot forget him even if he forgets me.
