He shouted this in a loud voice. Accordingly, four men stood one upon another's shoulders, leaning against the steep rock. Stepping on them, he climbed upward, caught hold of the fourth man's shoulder and easily reached the summit.

The villagers raised joyful cheers and praised him. “If the four of us had not been here, could you have reached the top?” asked the young men who had lent their shoulders.

He laughed at them dismissively. “I could have done this without your cooperation,” he declared, thumping his chest.

The four grew angry. “You are making enemies of us without knowing the measure of your strength,” they warned.

“I need no one's help. The world knows my strength. Do you see where I am? I have now climbed the steep rock you said I could not climb. From here I look down upon all of you. I am on the height; you are in the hollow below!”

He began speaking such arrogant words. The villagers who had surrounded and praised him with cheers moved away, murmuring that pride had gone to his head.

“Do not speak as though whatever you decide is already done. Reduce your arrogance. Practise restraint,” pleaded a few who cared for him.

“There is no one to advise me. I am everything. I know my own strength. Go without speaking!” he drove them away.

Everyone moved to a distance and kept watching him. From above he addressed them in a booming voice:

“You said I climbed only because of you. Look now—I will give you proof of my strength of mind and body. I am going to jump down from this steep rock. Then you will understand my courage and valour!”

Saying this, he jumped from the rock. Instead of leaping as one jumps onto the ground, he dived headfirst as though plunging into water. His head struck the earth and shattered. His body, soaked in a flood of blood, twitched and finally lay still. Poor man. Thinking too highly of himself, refusing to work in harmony with others, refusing even to respect them, and failing to understand his own strength, the man who wandered about proclaiming himself a hero destroyed himself for nothing.

This is the Kural Valluvar fashioned for people like him:

> He who does not act as befits his situation, knows not his measure,  
> and marvels at himself will swiftly perish.
