Hearing the long-eyed woman's answer, the sword-bearing lover lost his composure. Saying he could not see the face of that peacock-plumed beauty while she lay across his shoulder, he lowered her onto his thigh. She kept devouring him with her eyes without blinking.

“What is this, my beauty? You stare without blinking! Storytellers claim that even the gods who dwell above do not blink. Are you trying to make that tale come true?”

he asked.

She replied:

“My handsome beloved! My loving beloved! I did not apply kohl because it might hide you, who dwell within my eyes. Now I do not even wish to blink, because for that tiny instant you would disappear. If you were separated from me even for that fraction of a moment, the people of this town would say of you, ‘What sort of man is he? He has no love for her.’”

There was no counting the kisses received by the eyes that gave that explanation. Nor was there any measure to the kisses the sculpted beauty gave back to the giver. There was no boundary at all to the flood of feeling that rose between them.

> Because my beloved dwells within my eyes,  
> I will not line them, knowing the kohl would hide him.

> I know that if I blink he will be hidden even then;  
> yet this town says, “He is without love.”

Chapter 113 — Declaring Love's Excellence; Kurals 1127, 1129

கரப்பாக்கு = hiding

ஏதிலர் = one without love
