## Visual material

Large upper illustration of Nagan and Nallaan.

Even a day might fail to keep its appointment, but Nagan never failed to meet Nallaan.

Nallaan was one of the great landowners of that village. His fields stretched as far as the eye could see. They were river-irrigated lands lying beside the riverbed—rich soil capable of producing three harvests a year.

There was never a time when the granary in his house was not filled with ruby-like grains of paddy.

Nagan was his friend. Nallaan loved him more than his own life.

It was Nallaan's nature to trust people easily, show them boundless affection and friendship, and give whatever help they needed.

Nagan, who moved closely with him, had secured an honoured place in Nallaan's heart.

When Nagan's younger sister was married, Nallaan helped him with money and goods so that the ceremony could be completed well.

Every week, rice white as thumbai flowers was sent from Nallaan's mansion to Nagan's house according to the household's needs.

Nagan had lived in a hut. Nallaan had built a beautiful stone house and given it to him as a gift.

Whenever Nagan met Nallaan, he thanked him for all the help. He continually praised Nallaan's intelligence, ability to act, sweetness of character, good conduct, and the way he stood as a model of friendship.

Nagan praised him so much that even Nallaan grew tired of hearing it.
