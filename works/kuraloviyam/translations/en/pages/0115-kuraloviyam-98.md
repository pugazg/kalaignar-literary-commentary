“Painter! What is coming toward us is no colourful parrot; it is a handsome young man, a sturdy young bull. Surely you can see that?”

“I can see it clearly. I regret that there are no poets nearby to sing the praises of this young man in honey-scented Tamil. I marvel at his gait. See that graceful peacock of a youth coming toward us—ah, how much charm there is in his face! His fine young moustache curves like the bent tip of a coloured brush. Did Mother Nature take the petal of a red lotus and make it the skin of his body? Sculptor! I have read with delight about so many handsome men—Arjuna, Manmatha, Kannan and Murugan—and I have painted those beauties too. But in my view, none of them equals the loveliness of the man I see today.”

The sculptor, who had been listening quietly to the painter's hymn of praise, began to speak with a smile.

“All your praise is fitting! But there is one thing. That figure of beauty you now see is not a human being; it is a doll very skilfully made of clay.”

At this, the painter was taken aback.

“What game are you playing? I am no fool who will believe what you say! Would a doll walk toward us like this? He is coming straight toward us—look! Here is a little stream, and he is even leaping across it. A doll, indeed! You are making a fine joke!”

“Painter! Not a doll—yes, he really is a human being! I know him well. But there is one thing: whoever else may agree to call him human, Thiruvalluvar is not prepared to do so.”

“What do you mean? Explain it so I can understand, sculptor!”

“Valluvar has said that a man who lacks subtle, penetrating and carefully discerning intelligence, however much beauty and charm he may possess, is equal to a doll fashioned out of clay. This fellow is the perfect example.”

Even as they spoke, the handsome young man came very near them. The sculptor looked at him and asked, “Well, handsome one! Are you well?” Then he introduced the painter to him.

“Oh! So you are a painter? Have you ever painted a handsome man like me? Of course you could not have!”

The moment those words fell upon the painter's ears, he looked at the sculptor and agreed: “What you said is one hundred percent true.”

> The beauty of one without subtle, penetrating discernment  
> is like a finely fashioned doll of clay.
