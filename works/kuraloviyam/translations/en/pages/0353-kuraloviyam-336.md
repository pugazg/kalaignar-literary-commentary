Purudothaman patiently listened to everything Kudilan said. At last he turned to his guards and ordered them to arrest Kudilan.

Kudilan trembled.

“King! Is this the punishment for me, who came to help you?” he cried.

Purudothaman looked at him and said:

“Kudila! My commander and I possess enough spirit and courage to fight openly and meet the Pandya king on the battlefield. You dared to abandon and betray your own king when hardship came to him. How can I trust that, when hardship comes to me another day, you will not do the same thing? That is why you receive this punishment!”

Kudilan is bound and brought before the Pandya king. The Pandya understands Kudilan's treachery. Friendship arises between the Chera and the Pandya—and thus *Manonmaniyam* ends happily. Through these characters, the author of *Manonmaniyam* brings out a rare idea from the Thirukkural.

“One should not even entertain thoughts that make the spirit shrink. Likewise, one should not accept the friendship of those who abandon you when hardship comes.”

Because Purudothaman understood Kudilan, he acted according to the words of the Kural.

> Think not thoughts that make the heart shrink; accept not  
> the friendship of those who abandon you in adversity.
