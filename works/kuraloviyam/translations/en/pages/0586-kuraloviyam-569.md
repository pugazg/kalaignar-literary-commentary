"Where did these flowers bloom?"

they asked.

"These? How beautiful these flowers are! But do you notice that they have not even a little fragrance? They must have bloomed in cold hill regions. Flowers growing in cold-soaked soil do not have fragrance."

That was the old man's explanation.

Until then, a stout man had been sitting in another corner of the platform smoking a cheroot. He now walked toward the old man and asked, laughing loudly:

"Hey, old fellow! What are you supposed to be—a great research scholar?"

The old man looked at the children around him and said:

"This man was not born into a good lineage. He was born into a base one."

At once the stout man sprang forward to strike the old man. But the children surrounded him, stood up to him, and drove him away.

One of the boys looked at the old man and asked:

"Grandfather! How did you find out so quickly that he was born into a low lineage?"

"Brother! We can look at this paddy stalk, this mango and this cluster of flowers and identify the kind of soil from which they grew, can't we? In the same way, a person's words reveal what kind of lineage he comes from."

Here is the Kural that explains the old man's point:

> The sprout reveals the nature of the soil in which it grew;  
> a person's spoken words reveal the lineage into which he was born.

Chapter 96 — Nobility of Birth; Kural 959

கால் = the sprouting of a crop
