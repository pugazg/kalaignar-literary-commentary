## Visual material

Large upper illustration of a richly adorned woman with a young man behind her.

A young man whose heart has grown heavy and full under the burden of youth sees a woman carrying beauty itself.

Having seen her, should he not simply go on his way? It seems he will not sleep unless he first finds some fault with that beautiful woman. Human nature has become like that.

A man looks at the moon moving across the sky and angrily says, “Moon! Why do you burn me like this?”

He sees a stream rushing noisily along, cutting through the earth, and gives voice to his envy. With a poet's heart he pours abuse upon it: “When I am coming this way, must the stream go singing like this?”

It is just such a young man whom Valluvar the great places before our eyes—forgive me—before the eyes of a young woman, and builds a story around him.

“If only those cruel brows would not bend and would block them, her eyes would not leap upon me strongly enough to make me tremble!”

His youthful heart, it seems, is struck and scattered by her gaze. In that sweet torment he laments: Ah! Had the dam been built properly and held firm, the flood of longing would not have drowned me!

That is the thought behind his plea: “Could not her brows have stood like a wall and blocked those beautiful eyes?”

Read it in Valluvar's own movement:

> If her cruel brows would not bend but hide them,  
> her eyes would not inflict this trembling pain on me.

Chapter 109 — The Bewildering Power of Beauty; Kural 1086

நடுங்கு அஞர் செய்யல = would not cause the suffering that makes one tremble
