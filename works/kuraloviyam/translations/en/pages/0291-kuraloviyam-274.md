“How many naazhigai are there in one day?”

Without the slightest hesitation Ponni replied, “There are four hundred and twenty naazhigai in a day.” Thamarai was astonished.

“What is this, Ponni? Are you setting me a riddle? Alli and I were arguing about this only a little while ago. Alli said there are fifty naazhigai in a day. I said sixty. I am sure that what I said is right. Still, I thought I would casually ask you too, and now you say outright that there are four hundred and twenty!”

Ponni let out a smile mixed with pain.

“For you, a day is reckoned as sixty naazhigai. But for me, the reckoning I gave is the one that fits.”

Thamarai did not understand. She wanted an explanation from Ponni.

“You are giving a calculation no one else gives. If someone says there are four hundred and twenty naazhigai in a day, everyone would ask whether that person had lost their senses!”

Hearing this, Ponni put her hand on Thamarai's shoulder and began speaking again with another deep sigh.

“Thamarai! Have you not read in the Kural that, for a woman who longs and longs for the day when her lover who has gone to a distant land will return, one day feels like seven? That is exactly my state. For me, who languishes for my beloved who has gone far away abroad, one day is four hundred and twenty naazhigai—is it not seven days?”

Tears now ran from Ponni's eyes. Thamarai consoled her.

> For those who languish, fixing their hope on the day  
> when one gone far will return, one day passes like seven.
