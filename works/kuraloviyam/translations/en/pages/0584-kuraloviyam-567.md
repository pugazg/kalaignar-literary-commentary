"...and if no such thing exists, let us create it here, my dear," she said, letting her lips wander over his body.

The union of those two love-doves was the special spectacle of the flower grove that day.

"Is this body made of ivory, my dear?"

"I was born only to be given to you, beloved!"

"May I pluck the mangoes?"

"Why not taste them while they are still on the tree?"

Endearments! A million delights! The grove itself was their bridal chamber. Arms around waists, not even room for a thread to pass between them, they reached the height of pleasure.

"My beloved," he said.

"I will never part from you!" she replied.

"In this birth, we shall never part!"

He held her even closer. But she began to weep.

She carried the unknowing belief that there are many births. That was why she cried.

Feeling tears wet his cheek as they lay embraced, he started and asked, "Why this hiccup while eating millet flour?"

"There are so many births! Yet you say only that in this birth you will not part from me!"

She said it and wept. She rose. Like the wind she moved toward the waterside, picked up the clothes lying there, and prepared to dress.

Only after he accepted the idea of rebirths for her sake and said that in every birth he would never leave her did she put aside her lovers' quarrel and become ready for union again.

> When I said, "In this birth we shall not part,"  
> her eyes filled with tears.

Chapter 132 — Subtleties of Lovers' Quarrel; Kural 1315
