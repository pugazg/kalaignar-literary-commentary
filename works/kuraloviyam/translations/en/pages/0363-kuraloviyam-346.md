—they floated in the lamps! Those beauties, believing their intoxicated eyes to be magical weapons made expressly to bring men down, placed kings in their laps and held governments in their grip.

Kings no longer needed ministers to advise them. If only the grace of wine-cups moistened by a beauty's lips continued, they said, that was equal to wondrous counsel delivered by God himself in person—while stroking the woman's waist. Crowds of subjects would come saying, “Resolve our grievances, O king!” Rolling on the bed and pressing her flower-like feet to his cheeks, the king would ask, “What is your grievance? Tell me, graceful one!” The man responsible for government, with no trace of where his clothes and ornaments had gone on the luxurious bed, would chant the mantra that she alone was the world. She would embrace him, cook him in the fire of passion until he collapsed in exhaustion, and let him fall asleep. Then the sceptre of rule was in her hand. The state treasury opened at her command.

The king would wake after the stupor cleared. She would fill her own mouth with liquor and then fill his mouth with it. Under the double intoxication he would curl up again. Whatever the beauty laid down became law; whatever she uttered became an order. She cared nothing for love, affection, devotion or the welfare of a husband. With the splendour of her body and the excellence of her beauty as capital, she had to make both him and his government dance to her tune. She had to heap up riches. It did not matter who embraced her for pleasure. She had to make the luxuries of the palace her own.

Under the glances of such women of luxury, many rulers of Rome lost all shape and identity. The result: Rome itself declined and shattered. Through the amorous dalliance of women of pleasure held lower than ordinary citizens, a vast empire itself became a mound of earth. History teaches us this.

And here is what Valluvar teaches:

“Those without the strength to keep the heart steady and unmoving,  
who embrace the shoulders of public women who hold some other purpose  
in their minds while speaking words of enchantment,  
will perish.”

> Those without steadfast hearts will embrace the shoulders  
> of women who cherish another purpose within as they join them.

Chapter 92 — Women of Mercenary Love; Kural 917
