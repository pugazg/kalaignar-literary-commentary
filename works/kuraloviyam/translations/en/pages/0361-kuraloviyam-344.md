Is not the purpose of taking birth to gain the great fame of being a servant even to the servants of the Lord who wears the crescent moon, rather than becoming entangled in life's troubles as son, grandson, and the wife of a cherished son?”

Hearing the ascetic's discourse of grace pouring like a waterfall, the devotees became bees drunk on honey. They received the sacred ash he gave them, placed it upon their foreheads, bowed, and received his blessing. After completing his counsel, the ascetic left the temple hall of the Lord's presence carrying staff and water-pot.

A young man followed him without the ascetic knowing it. The ascetic entered the monastery where he stayed. The young man too followed, hidden from his sight. The monastery door was bolted. The holy man knocked on the door of a room. A young woman with a full-moon face ran out and embraced the ascetic.

“Slender waist—  
swan-like gait—  
sugar-sweet speech—  
my jewel!  
Were you waiting?”

the ascetic asked. Pressing cheek to cheek, she clasped him close, her full breasts giving pleasure, and answered him with kisses.

“I have just returned after proclaiming that one must live without attachments. Even then, I kept this twenty-four-carat gold pressed within my heart.”

The peacock-like woman did not let the ascetic speak further. Leaping as though a bedchamber epic were about to pour forth, she pushed him onto the cot.

The young man could stand there no longer. He left the place.

The words of a Kural came to the young man's mind:

“Who is more merciless than the deceivers who have not truly renounced in their hearts, yet live as though they had renounced?”

He wrote that Kural at the entrance of the monastery and went away.

> None is more hard-hearted than those who have not renounced in the heart,  
> yet live deceitfully as though they had renounced.

Chapter 28 — False Conduct; Kural 276

வன்கணார் = the merciless / hard-hearted
