## Visual material

Large upper illustration of an elderly chief minister with a younger ruler near a waterside landscape.

“Ah! We must not let him go unpunished. My shoulders are throbbing. The sword has already left its sheath. He has hurled at me words of contempt unknown even among the lowest men. I will go at once and teach him a lesson, proving that mine is the Tamil tradition that holds honour itself to be the good life.

“He may possess a great army. Compared with his, mine is smaller in number. But in strength it is greater. Warriors of firm heart, brave men who consider life itself insignificant, stand arrayed behind me. Around the man who insulted me there is only an army that fights for its meal.

“So, honoured minister, do not stop me. I am not in a state to accept your opinion. Forgive me for that.”

Before the elderly chief minister, grey-haired with age, the young king of that country argued with fire in his eyes.

The great former king had died. The young man, newly crowned—young in age but great in strength—burned to set out for war because the ruler of a neighbouring land had insulted him.

The minister was an elder of long experience. He had held that great responsibility since the young king's father's time. His wisdom of experience therefore rose like a dam against the young ruler's surging impulse.

It was not that he had no desire to confront the neighbouring king and humble his arrogance.
