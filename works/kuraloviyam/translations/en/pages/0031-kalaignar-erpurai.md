# Kalaignar's Response

I have noted in the book's preface that I began writing *Kuraloviyam* in 1956. Our dear friends here have also explained that.

Through nearly thirty years of labour, 300 Kuraloviyam pieces have been drawn for 354 Kural couplets, and they have now been published in book form.

They spoke in praise; they spoke appreciatively. None of you should think that, by way of thanking them for all this, I ought to speak at length. I should not speak at length either.

For a mother who has carried a child for ten months, suffered labour pains, writhed in those pains and finally given birth, when the child is lying beside her and she is looking at it with joy overflowing from her eyes, her friends may all come near her—the neighbours may come, elderly women may come—and on seeing the child say, “Ah, what a beautiful child! It shines like a diamond! It is like gold!” Whether or not that is literally so, that is what they will say. At such a moment, the mother who gave birth will not immediately look at them and begin telling all these stories: “Do you know how much I suffered to give birth to this child? Do you know that I carried it for ten months? Do you know all that happened in the third month and the fourth month? Do you know how many mangoes I needed?” Whether they are neighbours, elderly women, older people, even her husband, uncle or aunt—while everyone is praising and rejoicing over the child, the exhausted mother will answer only with a sweet smile. Even that smile is not cast at them; she smiles while looking at the child.

In the same way, all of you have come—as elderly women, neighbouring women, friends, elders, as my mothers and fathers—and praised my child. I smile while looking at that child. That is the gratitude I show and express.
