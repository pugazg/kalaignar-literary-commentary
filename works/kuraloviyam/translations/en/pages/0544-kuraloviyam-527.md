Before I could reach them, a boy with a fishing hook scooped them up, dropped them into his vessel and ran away.

My beautiful eyes!

Today I cannot see your dancing and leaping.

Was the commotion you made back then any small thing?

In the evening I would stand at the entrance of the house drawing a kolam, jasmine in my hair and kumkum shining upon my forehead.

He would appear at the far end of the street.

Leaving me standing alone, you—my two eyes—would run to him.

You would welcome him, bring him into the house and cling to his broad shoulders.

You would keep drinking in the beauty of his moving lips.

When he sat to eat, you would gaze at the very grace with which he ate and lose yourselves completely.

When he reclined on the bed, I would gently take the book from his hand and bring a bowl of milk near his lips.

By then you would already be playing games of longing with his eyes.

Have you seen the reward your revelry has brought you now?

Just as the cat carried away the sparrow in the guava tree, separation has isolated both him and me.

You writhe like fish that have leapt onto the bank.

And now tears! Tears!

You deserve this—and more!

“Eyes that once delighted in seeing the beloved they desired—may the tears within them now dry away through grief upon grief, unable even to sleep.”

So a woman, unable to bear the pain of separation, curses her own eyes.

> Eyes that once rejoiced to see the one they longed for—  
> may their inner tears dry away through grief upon grief.

Chapter 118 — Reproaching the Eyes; Kural 1177

உழந்துழந்து = grieving again and again
