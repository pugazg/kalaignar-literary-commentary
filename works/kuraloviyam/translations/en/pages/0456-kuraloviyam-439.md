The jester, however, would not back down.

“O king! This is a question of my honour! I can no longer withdraw the challenge I have issued. I will face him and see what happens!”

he declared, and went forward.

The sword fight began in the royal hall. The warrior's sword flashed faster than lightning. The jester dropped his sword and looked wildly around, wondering which way he could run. The assembly clapped and laughed.

The king looked at the jester and asked:

“You said it was a question of honour. What will you do now?

> Like the kavari deer that cannot live when its hair is lost,  
> people of honour give up life when honour is at stake.

“Do you know the meaning of this Kural?

“They say that the kavari deer cannot live if the hair on its body is lost. In the same way, when honour is destroyed, people of honour give up their lives like that deer. This is what Valluvar says. What is your decision?”

The king asked this with a mischievous smile.

“O king! Just watch!” said the jester, and ran out of the royal hall.

Everyone was bewildered.

A little while later the jester returned to the court with his head completely shaved. Everyone stared at him in astonishment.

“O king! I have changed one letter in the Thirukkural. By changing it, I have found a solution to my problem of honour!”

said the jester. Then he recited his altered Kural:

> Like the kavari deer that cannot live if life is lost,  
> people of honour give up their hair when honour is at stake.

Hearing this corrected-by-the-jester Thirukkural, everyone—including the master swordsman—clapped and laughed.

“It is fine entertainment for the people of this country to have a jester like you,” said the king, and presented him with a silk turban to cover his shaven head.

> Like the kavari deer that cannot live when its hair is lost,  
> people of honour give up life when honour is at stake.
