The flame of the little oil lamp danced in the sweet breeze. So did their bodies.

“What if our two bodies had been created as one body, beloved? Then we could remain joined like this without ever separating, could we not?”

That was her question.

“If there really is someone who created us, we are now showing him how we ought to have been created.”

He answered and embraced her even more tightly.

The beautiful flame of the oil lamp, its face reddened with shyness, whirled around as though trying to escape by some route, bumping here and there, but it could not. With no other way out, the little lamp remained there enjoying the scene upon the bed.

With his lips he caressed her from crown to sole. Without lifting her lips from where they rested upon his sole, she poured pleasure all the way up to the crown of his head and received pleasure in return.

Is not the bedchamber a place where principal and interest are repaid at once without delay? Is it not also a place where, if one records a gift, the return is recorded threefold?

It was not merely that “she embraced him; she embraced him.” She embraced him! Embraced him! Lay there embracing him without end!

Only now did the wonder occur.

Yes—the lovely forehead of the woman turned pale.

Soaked in nectar, held close by vessels of nectar—what quarrel could possibly have arisen while they lay entwined?

Then why did pallor begin to spread across her forehead?

Why should the illness that raises its head in separation step into lovers who are lying joined together?

Oh! Oh! The reason is now very easy to understand.

What is the reason?

“They say the lover slightly loosened the arms with which he was embracing her. Unable to bear even that tiny separation, pallor rushed in and spread across the forehead of the maiden wearing green bangles!”

Here is that Kural:

> When the arms that embraced her loosened,  
> the forehead of the green-bangled maiden turned pale.

Chapter 124 — Wasting Away of Bodily Beauty; Kural 1238

முயங்கிய = embraced

ஊக்க = loosened
