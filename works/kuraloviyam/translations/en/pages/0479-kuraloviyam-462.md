## Visual material

Large upper illustration of two contestants before an elderly judge holding a garland.

In ancient times a contest was held in a small town. A great crowd gathered to watch.

It was announced that a grey-haired elder of the town would serve as judge, declare the winner's name, and place the victory garland upon him.

Should we not know what kind of contest had been arranged?

The elder would examine those who came wearing the most costly and valuable ornaments and decide who among them was the winner.

The wealthiest man in that town was Veerasingam. No one in the surrounding region possessed greater riches or more ornaments than he did. He had already announced that he would take part in the contest.

“I too will enter the contest,” another man announced.

His name was Anbanandan. He was the next wealthiest man after Veerasingam.

People around Veerasingam advised him that Anbanandan was extremely eager to win and might spend a large portion of his wealth to appear in unmatched ornaments. Therefore, they said, Veerasingam must be cautious.

With victory as his only aim, Veerasingam gave goldsmiths the money to prepare ornaments of priceless value.
