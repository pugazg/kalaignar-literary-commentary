In the blue sky, the stars hold the ropes and slowly draw the moon's chariot.

Unable to hide the mountain slopes with her waterfall-sari, the maiden Earth buries her face in the flourishing groves out of shyness.

The lover takes honey from imagination and writes out the wonder of nature; as his reward, he receives the honey of his beloved's lips.

In that grove patterned with flowers, as though challenging nature itself—"Is this beauty greater than ours?"—the lover, dear as an eye, and the beloved, precious as the pupil of that eye, lie clasped in a full embrace.

He runs his fingers through her curling hair; she leans upon his broad chest. He is a bee intoxicated by the beauty of her eyes; she longs to join her lips to his. From crown to sole he caresses her with his fingers, and to savour her lying there lost in that pleasure is heaven to him.
