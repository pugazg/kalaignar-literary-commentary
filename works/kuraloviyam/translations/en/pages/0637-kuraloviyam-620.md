"He frowned that even the garment I wore, thin as incense-smoke, hid my beauty.

"In the end, only so that his face would not fall, I accepted defeat. Even in that defeat there was an unfading delight for me. I have not forgotten it.

"On the open terrace at the top of the house there was no one.

"Even the moon-maiden wandering in the sky seemed to have drawn a curtain of cloud across the window of her own house and gone to sleep.

"So, 'There is no need for shyness, my dear! My beauty! We can stage our festival of pleasure right there,' he said and pulled me toward him.

"Though I shook my head and said 'No' out of modesty, in the end he was the one who won.

"And I celebrated that victory with joy. I will not deny it.

"Do you remember how he asked what it would be like for us to roll clasped together over dry leaves that rustled beneath us, to bite and taste honeyed lips like sugar-candy dissolved in sweetness, while the anklets upon your feet made music suited to us?

"And then he conducted a sacrifice of delight there in the shade of the mango grove.

"Ah! Feeling climbed from the crown of my head to the soles of my feet!"
