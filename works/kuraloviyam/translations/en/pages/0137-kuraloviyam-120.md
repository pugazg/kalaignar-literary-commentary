She was imprisoned. What had become of her sweet beloved—the commander of that land—she did not know. Thinking of him, she wept and lamented. The enemy king repeatedly pressed that radiant Tamil picture, now carried away as a prisoner, to marry him; he pleaded and threatened, but to no avail. So he decided that a beauty useless to him might as well be destroyed. He ordered that unplucked fruit to be taken to the execution ground and cut down. That embodiment of delight, that treasure-chest of great beauty, was made to stand at the place of execution. Enemies approached with cruel swords to kill her. She trembled not only at the thought of losing her life; she also grieved that she would die without seeing the beloved who was her very life. The killing swords were raised high. Then came a voice: “Stop!” Everyone turned. The princess's lover came forward and stood before them in heroic fury.

“My lord!” she cried.

“Do not fear! The enemy king who brought you here as a prisoner now lies in prison himself. When that worthless fellow unexpectedly encircled our land and won, I slipped out of the fortress by strategy, came here with our country's forces, and have defeated the enemy,” he said, and embraced her. The killers were taken prisoner. Leaning on the victorious warrior's shoulder, that peahen returned to her land. The wedding took place as she desired. The commander assumed royal responsibility and became king. Days of happiness followed. From time to time, affairs of state required him to travel to neighbouring lands for a week or two. When he returned from one such journey, she ran to him and clung to his shoulder.

“My dear, did the separation trouble you very much?” he asked.

“My lord! Do you know how every evening came to me when you were not beside me?”

“How did it come?”

“Do you remember how the enemies came toward me with raised swords at the execution ground that day? Every evening came toward me just like that.”

“My golden one!” he said, laying her across his lap and showering her with kisses.

> When the beloved is away, evening comes  
> like enemies on an execution ground.

Chapter 123 — Lamenting at the Sight of Evening; Kural 1224
