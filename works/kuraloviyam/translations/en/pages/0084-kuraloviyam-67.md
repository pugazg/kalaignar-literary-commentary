“The enemy king's military strength could not be underestimated. When the elephant corps of both sides collided, it looked like masses of black clouds crashing into one another. The clash of chariot forces was like hills striking each other and shattering. I will not say the horses merely charged and fought—they flew as they fought! Warriors bearing spears and swords surged against one another with a fury surpassing even storming seas.

“At one stage—yes, the final stage of the battle—the enemy king and I met face to face. When the spear he threw came racing directly towards my chest, had I delayed even for the blink of an eye, I would have embraced a hero's death. But before that, I not only stopped it; with this very spear I took his life. The next instant after the enemy king fell struck by my spear, his battle line began to break and flee. Our warriors raised the flag of victory. Great one! Look at the tip of this spear. See how the enemy king's red blood has dried upon it.”

Deep in the exhilaration of victory, the king held the spear out towards Valluvar. Without touching it, Valluvar looked at him and said:

“O king! I praise the victory you have won. I honour the strength of your army. I do not deny that this spear which brought down the enemy king stands as a symbol of your triumph. It is true that kings cannot always avoid war, and victory in war can bring joy. But do not forget one thing, ruler! What brings a government the victory praised by the world is not the spear that defeats enemies, but the sceptre that governs its citizens. And that sceptre must be an unbending, just sceptre. Give your people such righteous rule and win enduring victory!”

The king, captivated by Valluvar's wise words, threw down the spear in his hand, bowed to him, mounted his chariot and departed towards the capital. Valluvar stood smiling. With the stylus of thought he inscribed the following Kural upon his heart. Here it is:

> It is not the spear that gives a king victory;  
> it is his sceptre—if that sceptre does not bend from justice.

Chapter 55 — Just Rule; Kural 546
