## Visual material

Large colour illustration above the prose showing an angry prince or ruler in front of assembled men, with another figure partly visible at right.

The prince of a country delivered a highly charged speech in the capital's public assembly. He had to answer a succession of questions that had arisen in the country about his interference in governance. Because his answers contained neither clarity nor truth, waves of anger surged through his speech. He spoke contemptuously of learned elders and continued in the same vein. He treated experienced ministers as though they were his slaves. He ordered the petty rulers subject to that government to behave as his lackeys. With no other course open to them, the ministers and petty kings endured all these humiliating actions.

The reason for the prince's overbearing conduct was the protection on which he relied. Yes! There was a fortress strong enough to prevent enemies entering. There was a large and powerful army. Wealth lay heaped up beyond what the treasury could contain. The government also enjoyed the friendship of several emperors. With all these supports, the prince was arrogant enough to think, “Who can do anything to me?”

An elder said that if this situation continued, it would be good neither for the country nor for the government. In his youth, that elder had been one of the country's commanders and had now retired. While serving as a commander, he had fought an enemy country and defended the land of his birth.
