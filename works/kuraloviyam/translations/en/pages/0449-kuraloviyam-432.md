—talking with him, going out walking, gathering relatives and friends for feasts and rejoicing. But this time there was no light in his eyes and no enthusiasm in his speech. It was because Tanikaimalai saw this great change that he pressed question after question.

Arulappar explained in a soft voice:

“Sometimes even a prosperous condition causes greater suffering than the pain of poverty and want. Do you know when? When one must live under a tyrannical government that acts without justice. Has not Valluvar said exactly this? Today that Kural has become the voice of the heart of people like me.”

Hearing Arulappar's explanation, Tanikaimalai said:

“Yes. How beautifully Valluvar has pictured the heart of citizens under an unjust government. It is true, my friend! A good life does not end with a good household. Surely only a life lived under good government can truly be called a good life!”

This is the Kural underlying the two friends' conversation about good government:

> Possession is worse than poverty  
> when one falls beneath the sceptre of a king who rules without justice.
