The aim of those few was to use those very principles to build mansions for their own selfish interest.

They succeeded. One among them was even raised to the seat of government as a king.

The people of principle rejoiced: one who had been in their movement had now become ruler!

They expected the support of government to help their principles spread widely and awaken the people.

But that ruler, in order to secure his own authority, cast to the winds the principles he had claimed to accept.

He had not shed a single drop of blood to nurture those principles. He had never entered the field of struggle. He did not even understand the full form of the ideology.

Those who had been ready to give their lives for those principles, those whose bodies bore a thousand scars for them, now watched those principles being dug into pits and buried.

The man beneath the royal umbrella had armed strength. But what could his army do against those whose weapon was principle?

It could cut them down. It could shoot them. But could it shake the resolve of the warriors of principle?

They rose like volcanoes.

They marched like storm winds.

Before the army of principle, the ruler's cowardly army could not stand. Whether cavalry, elephant corps or chariot corps, all the many forms of his military fled defeated before the people's army gathered by the warriors of principle.

The government without principle lost its umbrella and fell. That is what the Kural says. How?

> If those who uphold lofty principles rise in anger,  
> the king will be broken midway and lose his kingship.

Its meaning is that when people of lofty principle and firm resolve are provoked to anger, a king will lose his royal office midway and be ruined.

Chapter 90 — Not Offending the Great; Kural 899
