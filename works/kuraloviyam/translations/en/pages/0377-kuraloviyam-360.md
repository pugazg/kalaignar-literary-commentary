The monastic head's eyes sprang toward the waterfall. He did not lower and close his eyelids to bar their way. He let his eyes fly freely. More powerful than they, his heart fitted itself with strong wings and flew toward the waterfall. The rudraksha beads in his hand stopped rolling. His fingers became still. His lips no longer uttered the name of God.

“Hey! Stop the palanquin! We must rest a while. Sleep is embracing my eyes,” he ordered the bearers. The bearers stopped. They too looked toward the waterfall. They understood the monastic head's state of mind. They lowered the palanquin. He kept staring at the waterfall. One of the bearers ground his teeth, but could not show his anger openly.

“My lord! Rest as you wish. Here, I will draw this silk curtain so that the sun does not fall inside.”

Saying this, he pulled the silk curtains on both sides of the palanquin and closed them firmly. The monastic head was deeply disappointed. “The fool has blocked such a rare sight!” he muttered.

Seeing people moving nearby, the young women immediately left. The monastic head could neither sleep nor rest. He removed the silk curtain and looked. The golden statues were no longer there. The palanquin bearers sat beneath a tree, turned away from the waterfall without looking toward it, reading some manuscript.

The monastic head growled, “Hey, what are you reading after leaving me here?”

“We are reading the Thirukkural, lord! It says that even those who sit above are not truly superior if they possess no noble character; and even those who stand below, like us, are not low if they possess no base qualities. That is Valluvar's thought.”

So said the palanquin bearers. The monastic head's face withered. The palanquin journey resumed.

> Those above are not truly above if they lack nobility;  
> those below are not truly below if they lack baseness.
