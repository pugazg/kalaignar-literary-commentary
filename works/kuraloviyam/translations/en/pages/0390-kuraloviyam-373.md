## Visual material

Large upper illustration of a teacher-like elder speaking with a young boy, with a large bearded portrait behind.

A student asked his teacher whom one should approach to learn the qualities that belong to a country.

“To learn about a country, approach Valluvar,” said the teacher.

“Where is he now?” the student asked with longing.

“The Kural itself is Valluvar in book form. In the *Porutpaal* there is a section called Fortification. It contains ten verses concerning the country. Every one of them is a pearl.”

The teacher's face shone as he spoke of the greatness of those Kurals.

Two kinds of water—the abundance of springs and the wealth of rain—a well-situated mountain range, the river wealth that comes from that mountain, and a strong fort are the components of a country.

> Two waters, a fitting mountain, the waters flowing from it,  
> and a mighty fort are the limbs of a country.

So says that great Tamil sage.

“And that is not all, my boy. Valluvar also declares firmly that a country worthy of the name is one that functions without being approached by cruel hunger, unending disease, or ruinous invasion.”
